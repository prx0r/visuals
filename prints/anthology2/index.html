Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.1

Homage to the World-Formed

प्रपञ्चोत्तीर्णरूपाय नमस्ते विश्वमूर्तये । सदादिव्यप्रकाशाय स्वात्मनेऽनन्तशक्तये
prapañcottīrṇarūpāya namaste viśvamūrtaye | sadādivyaprakāśāya svātmane'nantaśaktaye
Homage to the form that has crossed beyond the manifest — to the self of the world's form; to the ever-divinely-shining, to the self that is unending power.
1
The Guide

Homage to the World-Formed

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.1 · v.1
The first word of the day: bowing to what was here before you woke.
The logic

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā opens not with a request but with a naming. Three appositions do the work: he has passed beyond the manifest yet IS the world's form; he always shines with divine light; he is your own self and consists of inexhaustible power. The logic is identity, not distance — you salute the world-form because you address what already shines.

How to perform it

Say it sitting up before your feet touch the floor. Three clauses, three slow bows of the head. Feel the room as svātmane'nantaśaktaye — not out there but as your own body's brightness. Don't visualise a figure; visualise light that has nowhere to go. One round, then stand.

Dehastha v.1

The Body-Temple Offering

देहो देवालयः प्रोक्तः स्वात्मा देवः सनातनः । त्यजेदज्ञाननिर्माल्यं सोऽहं भावेन पूजयेत्
deho devālayaḥ proktaḥ svātmā devaḥ sanātanaḥ | tyajedajñānanirmālyaṃ so'haṃ bhāvena pūjayet
The body is declared God's temple, the own self the eternal God. Casting off the sweepings of ignorance, let him worship with the feeling: I-am-He.
2
The Guide

The Body-Temple Offering

Dehastha v.1 · v.1
The morning rite in one verse: the body is the shrine, so'ham is the worship.
The logic

Dehastha (the Indwelling) compresses a whole pūjā into four clauses. Temple, God, sweeping, worship: a temple needs cleaning before its God is honoured, and the 'sweepings of ignorance' are exactly the ordinary assumption that the body is mere matter. The worshipper and the worshipped collapse — so'ham bhāvena, with the feeling I-am-He.

How to perform it

Wake and stay horizontal. Sweep an imaginary hand from head to toe three times — that is casting off the sweepings. Then place attention at the heart and let so'haṃ, so'haṃ rest with the breath for twelve rounds. The feeling to aim for is warmth at the chest, not a thought: the body as lit, not the body as object. Then rise — and the day is the pūjā.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.10

The Three States as His Rays

जाग्रतस्वप्नसुषुप्त्याद्या धारयंश्च निजाः कलाः । स्वेच्छया भासि नटवन् निष्कलोऽसि च तत्त्वतः
jāgratasvapnasuṣuptyādyā dhārayaṃśca nijāḥ kalāḥ | svecchayā bhāsi naṭavan niṣkalo'si ca tattvataḥ
Waking, dreaming, deep sleep and the rest — you hold them as your own portions. By your will you shine like an actor, yet you are partless in truth.
3
The Guide

The Three States as His Rays

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.10 · v.10
The three states are costumes; the one who shines is partless.
The logic

The verse splits experience from experiencer: the states are kalāḥ, portions or costumes — things held; the holding is by svecchā, your own will; and the actor is partless though he plays many parts. Play and partlessness are asserted in one breath, so no state can claim you.

How to perform it

Recite on waking, naming each state as you come out of it: 'that was a costume.' The actor-idea to hold is literal — you played sleep for seven hours. Feel the partlessness as the fact that nothing about you has edges. Keep it memorised: it is the fastest exit from a bad dream.

Svabodhasiddhi v.4

Wake the Knowledge That Frees

जयन्त्य् अपारसंसारसारासारविभागदाः । कैवल्यज्ञानसंबोधहेतवश् शम्भवाङ्घ्रयः
jayanty apārasaṃsārasārāsāravibhāgadāḥ | kaivalyajñānasaṃbodhahetavaś śambhavāṅghrayaḥ
Victorious are Śambhu's feet — they that tell the endless wheel of becoming from its essence and its refuse, and are the cause of the waking knowledge that frees.
4
The Guide

Wake the Knowledge That Frees

Svabodhasiddhi v.4 · v.4
A dawn verse whose whole grammar is waking.
The logic

The verse stacks three functions on the feet: they discriminate (saṃsāra's sāra against asāra — essence against refuse), they cause the knowledge that awakens (saṃbodhi-hetu), and they are victorious. Liberation is not granted but caused — hetu, cause — through a knowledge that must wake, exactly like you.

How to perform it

Say it once after the body-temple verse. The word to weight is saṃbodha — waking. Picture a lamp being lit rather than a person bowing. A useful feel-test: does the line make the room sharper? Repeat it at any hour when you have been awake but not waking.

Paramārthasāra v.35

Waking, Dreaming, and the Fourth

जाग्रद् विश्वं भेदात् स्वप्नस् तेजः प्रकाशमाहात्म्यात् । प्राज्ञः सुप्तावस्था ज्ञानघनत्वात् ततः परं तुर्यम्
jāgrad viśvaṃ bhedāt svapnas tejaḥ prakāśamāhātmyāt | prājñaḥ suptāvasthā jñānaghanatvāt tataḥ paraṃ turyam
Waking is the universe, through difference; dreaming is splendour, through the greatness of light; the knower is the state of sleep, through the massing of knowing; beyond that, the fourth.
5
The Guide

Waking, Dreaming, and the Fourth

Paramārthasāra v.35 · v.35
Abhinavagupta's census of the states — each credited with what it does best.
The logic

Each state is honoured for its real contribution instead of being ranked: waking gives the many (bhedāt), dream gives light (tejaḥ), sleep gives massed knowing (jñāna-ghana). Only then is turya named — beyond the three, not as a fourth state but as what was never covered by them. The census itself dismantles the hierarchy.

How to perform it

Use it as a morning inventory: touch each clause with a memory — this room, that dream, that blankness. Then stop at tataḥ paraṃ turyam and don't try to produce a fourth; the verse says beyond, not within. Feeling-tip: after the third clause leave a half-beat of silence — that gap is closer to turya than any image.

Dehastha v.5

The Sense-Goddesses Offer Bhairava

आत्मीयविषयभोगैरिन्द्रियदेव्यः सदा हृदम्भोजे । अभिपूजयन्ति यं तं चिन्मयमानन्दभैरवं वन्दे
ātmīyaviṣayabhogairindriyadevyaḥ sadā hṛdambhoje | abhipūjayanti yaṃ taṃ cinmayamānandabhairavaṃ vande
With feasts of their own sense-objects, the sense-goddesses ever worship, in the heart-lotus, him — the consciousness-made bliss-Bhairava: I praise.
6
The Guide

The Sense-Goddesses Offer Bhairava

Dehastha v.5 · v.5
Every taste, sight and sound is already a pūjā happening inside you.
The logic

Dehastha inverts the usual charge against the senses: indriya-devyāḥ, the senses are goddesses, and their bhoga (enjoyment) of their own viṣaya (objects) is continuous worship of the Bhairava made of consciousness. Eating is therefore not the interruption of practice but its oldest form — the offering is already being made in the heart-lotus.

How to perform it

At the first bite, feel the heart-lotus (just behind the centre of the chest) as the altar and the flavour as the flower landing on it. Do this for three bites only — overdoing it kills the taste. The feeling to cultivate is gratitude with heat: the sense is honoured as a goddess, not supervised as a thief.

Vijñāna Bhairava v.72

From Eating and Drinking

जग्धिपानकृतोल्लासरसानन्दविजृम्भणात् । भावयेद् भरितावस्थां महानन्दस् ततो भवेत्
jagdhipānakṛtollāsarasānandavijṛmbhaṇāt | bhāvayed bharitāvasthāṃ mahānandas tato bhavet
From the swell of joy made by eating and drinking — from the blooming of bliss-rasa — one should imagine the full state; great bliss then may arise.
7
The Guide

From Eating and Drinking

Vijñāna Bhairava v.72 · v.72
Take the fullness of a meal and turn it into mahānanda.
The logic

The dhāraṇā uses a given fact — eating and drinking already produce rasa and ullāsa, savour and lift — and asks you to make that the object of bhāvanā: not the taste itself but the state of being filled (bharitāvasthā). The logic is alchemical: ordinary satiety is the raw material, great bliss the product, provided attention stays on fullness instead of sliding to the next want.

How to perform it

Practise at the meal itself, not before. Eat to comfortable fullness, then pause with eyes half-open. Take one more spoonful of air, hold, and imagine the fullness radiating from the belly upward — filling the chest, then the head. Release. Three rounds. Don't chase ecstasy: the graded signal is that thinking quiets while the body stays warm. If drowsiness comes, stand up — the verse asks for fullness with alertness.

Vijñāna Bhairava v.74

Wherever the Mind Is Content

यत्र यत्र मनस् तुष्टिर् मनस् तत्रैव धारयेत् । तत्र तत्र परानन्दस्वारूपं सम्प्रवर्तते
yatra yatra manas tuṣṭir manas tatraiva dhārayet | tatra tatra parānandasvārūpaṃ sampravartate
Wherever the mind is content, there precisely the mind should be held; there, there, the own-form of supreme bliss proceeds to arise.
8
The Guide

Wherever the Mind Is Content

Vijñāna Bhairava v.74 · v.74
Supreme bliss follows attention to contentment, not effort.
The logic

A conditional with a promise: where tuṣṭi (contentment) already exists — any place, object or moment — dhāraya, hold the mind there, and parānanda's own form arises. The verse locates bliss as already in motion (sampravartate) at the site of contentment; the practice is staying, not seeking.

How to perform it

Scan for the most content spot of your day — a chair, a stretch, the moment after work — and return to it deliberately. Hold with soft gaze 30–60 seconds, breathing shallow and slow. Feeling-tip: contentment has a body-map; find it (jaw, shoulders, belly) and rest attention there. The sign of success is that you stop cataloguing other wants. Twice daily: midday and after dinner.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.13

Whence Invocation for the Full?

पूर्णस्यावाहनं कुत्र सर्वाधारस्य चासनम् । स्वच्छस्य पाद्यमर्घ्यञ्च शुद्धस्याचमनं कुतः
pūrṇasyāvāhanaṃ kutra sarvādhārasya cāsanam | svacchasya pādyamarghyañca śuddhasyācamanaṃ kutaḥ
For the full, whence invocation? For the all-supporting, whence a seat? For the clear, foot-water and welcome-water? For the pure, the sipping?
9
The Guide

Whence Invocation for the Full?

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.13 · v.13
The litany begins: the rites cannot apply to what already contains them.
The logic

Verses 13–19 ask, item by item, how each rite of pūjā can be addressed to a fullness that lacks nothing: āvāhana (invocation) presumes absence, āsana (seat) presumes sitting elsewhere, pādyārghya (foot- and hand-water) presumes a journey, ācamana (sipping) presumes impurity. Each question is the recognition of a perfect absence of need. The litany is not scepticism — it clears the ground for the real worship of v.20.

How to perform it

Say it before any pūjā, or before you begin your own ritual of work. Let each 'whence?' land as a shrug, not an argument — the feeling is amusement, not doubt. Practise by remembering each rite as you name it: the water, the seat, the thread. Then go and do nothing for one beat — that beat IS the answer, and it makes any ritual you later perform unforced.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.15

Flowers for the Desireless

निरालम्बस्योपवीतं पुष्पं निर्वासनस्य च । अघ्राणस्य कुतो धूपश्चक्षुर्हीनस्य दीपकः
nirālambasyopavītaṃ puṣpaṃ nirvāsanasya ca | aghrāṇasya kuto dhūpaścakṣurhīnasya dīpakaḥ
The sacred thread for the supportless, flowers for the desireless; incense for the noseless, lamp for the eyeless — of what use?
10
The Guide

Flowers for the Desireless

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.15 · v.15
The offering-items are matched to what the Lord has none of — and the joke is the doctrine.
The logic

The litany names the objects of worship — upavīta (thread), puṣpa (flowers), dhūpa (incense), dīpaka (lamp) — each with a deity stripped of the faculty it serves: no support, no desire, no nose, no eye. The mismatch is the teaching: worship addresses not a being lacking things but the emptiness of lack itself. What looks like futility ('of what use?') is the precise applicability of each gift.

How to perform it

If you use an altar, offer each item while naming its mismatch: thread for the one who supports nothing — place it; flower for the one who desires nothing — let it fall. With no altar, do it in air with the hand: four gestures, four lines. The feeling to find is tenderness toward an absence rather than a presence — worshipping emptiness, not a shape.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.17

Waving-Lights for the Self-Shining

स्वयं प्रकाशमानस्य कुतो नीराजनं विभोः । वेदवाचामवेद्यस्य कुतः स्तोत्रं विधीयते
svayaṃ prakāśamānasya kuto nīrājanaṃ vibhoḥ | vedavācāmavedyasya kutaḥ stotraṃ vidhīyate
Whence the waving of lights for the self-lit Lord? How is a hymn enjoined for him whom the Veda cannot say?
11
The Guide

Waving-Lights for the Self-Shining

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.17 · v.17
He lights himself — so the lamp is for you; he is unsayable — so the hymn is for you.
The logic

Two paradoxes: an offering of light presupposes darkness in the recipient, and speech presupposes an object speech can reach. The Lord is svayam-prakāśa (self-lit) and vedavācāmavedya (unknowable by the Veda's words). The litany's logic turns in every such verse: the rite's use falls on the offerer's lighting, not the Lord's.

How to perform it

Say this one at dusk, with a lamp or a screen-lit face. Watch what light does to the object it falls on — the verse makes you the lamp-bearer. Feeling-tip: the wave of light should move from your hand to your own chest, not outward to a statue; then you will notice the chest brightening on its own. For the hymn: hum, don't articulate — you have just honoured the Vedavācāmavedya.

Dehastha v.8

Śāmbhavī's Flowers of I-Making

कुरुते भैरवपूजामनलदलस्थाऽभिमानकुसुमैर्या । नित्यमहङ्कृतिरूपां वन्दे तां शाम्भवीमम्बाम्
kurute bhairavapūjāmanaladalasthā'bhimānakusumairyā | nityamahaṅkṛtirūpāṃ vande tāṃ śāmbhavīmambām
Who performs Bhairava's worship, standing in fire's petal, with flowers of I-making — ever of I-making form, her, the mother Śāmbhavī, I praise.
12
The Guide

Śāmbhavī's Flowers of I-Making

Dehastha v.8 · v.8
The real offering: flowers of 'I', made and placed by Śakti herself.
The logic

Dehastha's inner pūjā continues: Brāhmaṇī worships with flowers of resolve (v.7), Śāmbhavī with abhimāna-kusuma, flowers of I-making — the very sense of mine and I. Standing in the petal of fire, the Mother arranges a worship where the offering is the ahaṃkāra that is normally the problem. The rite converts the fault into the flower.

How to perform it

This is a contemplation, not a chant with a count. Sit and take your strongest 'I' of the day — pride, offence, fear — and see it as a flower being picked and placed. Literally imagine it: the thought becomes an object in the hand, laid at the heart's fire. Feeling-tip: the moment of placement should feel like relief, like putting down a bag. Three 'flowers' are enough; the fire (warmth at the solar plexus) confirms it.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.19

Pardon for the Unseen

पूर्णस्य दक्षिणा कुत्र नित्यतृप्तस्य तर्पणम् । विसर्जनं व्यापकस्याऽप्रत्यक्षस्य क्षमापणम्
pūrṇasya dakṣiṇā kutra nityatṛptasya tarpaṇam | visarjanaṃ vyāpakasyā'pratyakṣasya kṣamāpaṇam
Where is the fee for the full? Libation for the ever-content? Dismissal for the all-pervading? Pardon for the unseen?
13
The Guide

Pardon for the Unseen

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.19 · v.19
The litany's last question — and the ritual closes with forgiveness.
The logic

The four closing rites of any pūjā: dakṣiṇā (fee to the priest), tarpaṇa (libation to ancestors), visarjana (formal dismissal of the invoked), kṣamāpaṇa (apology for ritual faults). All four presuppose want, debt, departure and fault in one who is full, content, all-pervading and unseen. The kṣamāpaṇam for the apratyakṣa flips apology into recognition: you apologise to what was never absent.

How to perform it

Use it as the close of any practice, and as a daily evening line. Weight the last word: kṣamāpaṇam — pardon. Bow once. The feeling to find is the end of accounting: no tally of errors, no debt. If guilt is present, name it as one of the litany's items — 'where is the pardon for the unseen?' — and notice that the guilt expects a god who could be offended; the verse denies that god exists, and the guilt dissolves with him.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.20

The Answer: Yoke the Mind

एवमेव परा पूजा सर्वावस्थासु सर्वदा । ऐक्यबुद्धया तु सर्वेशे मनो देवे नियोजयेत्
evameva parā pūjā sarvāvasthāsu sarvadā | aikyabuddhayā tu sarveśe mano deve niyojayet
Just so is the highest worship — in every state, at all times: with a mind of oneness, one should yoke the mind to God as the Lord of all.
14
The Guide

The Answer: Yoke the Mind

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.20 · v.20
After seven questions, the Mahopadeśa gives the instruction.
The logic

The litany's payoff. Parā pūjā is neither abolished ritual nor no ritual: it is ritual re-specified — sarvāvasthāsu sarvadā (all states, all times) instead of the fixed occasion, and aikyabuddhi (oneness-mindedness) instead of the duality of giver and gift. The verb is niyojayet, yoke: the mind is a draught-animal that must be hitched, and the yoke is identity.

How to perform it

Memorise this one — it is the verse the other eight are for. Three yokes to practise: on waking (hitch the mind to 'his waking'), at any task (the doing is his), before sleep (the night is his). The feeling test for aikyabuddhi: the boundary of your skin thins. If it sharpens instead, you are thinking oneness, not yoking it — drop to the breath and restart.

Svabodhasiddhi v.5

The Senses Turn Back

मनो बुद्धिर् अहं प्राणास् तन्मात्रेन्द्रियजीवनम् । यं दृष्ट्वा विनिवर्तन्ते तम् उपास्यम् उपास्महे
mano buddhir ahaṃ prāṇās tanmātrendriyajīvanam | yaṃ dṛṣṭvā vinivartante tam upāsyam upāsmahe
Mind, thought, ego, breaths — the subtle elements, senses and life-principle — when they see the one worth worshipping, they turn back; that, we worship.
15
The Guide

The Senses Turn Back

Svabodhasiddhi v.5 · v.5
Not restraint but re-sighting: everything returns when it sees.
The logic

Svabodhasiddhi's list is the whole apparatus of going-out (manaḥ, buddhi, ahaṃkāra, prāṇas, tanmātras, indriyas, jīva), and the verb is vinivartante — they turn back of themselves, on dṛṣṭvā, seeing. The practice cannot be control (they are not restrained) but redirecting what they look at. The upāsyam/upāsmahe doublet: the seen is the object of worship and the seeing is the worship.

How to perform it

When you notice the mind scattering — mid-conversation, mid-screen — don't pull it back; give it something worth seeing. The chosen object: the breath's origin (just under the nostrils) or the pulse at the temple. Hold 5–8 seconds. Feeling-tip: 'seeing' here means interest, not staring; if the body leans slightly toward the object, you've got it. Three redirects per sitting, no more — exhaustion turns practice into force.

Svabodhasiddhi v.6

The Knife That Cuts the Knot

ममाहम् इति हृद्ग्रन्थिच्छेदनैककृपाणिकाम् । स्पन्दास्पन्दकृतग्रासां नौम्य् अहं शुद्धसंविदम्
mamāham iti hṛdgranthicchedanaikakṛpāṇikām | spandāspandakṛtagrāsāṃ naumy ahaṃ śuddhasaṃvidam
I bow — I — to pure awareness: the knife that alone cuts the heart's knot of 'mine' and 'I', the one that has swallowed both the throb and its stillness.
16
The Guide

The Knife That Cuts the Knot

Svabodhasiddhi v.6 · v.6
Even the bowing 'I' is offered to the knife.
The logic

The verse performs its own logic twice. The knot (granthi) is mamāham — 'mine' and 'I', two threads. The instrument is a kṛpāṇa, a knife, and only one does it: pure awareness. And the knower's own domain — spanda and aspanda, throb and stillness, i.e. all experience — has already been swallowed by that knife. 'I bow — I' stages the surrender: the pronoun survives long enough to do the bowing, then is cut too.

How to perform it

Recite with a hand flat over the sternum. Weight the first aham: feel where your 'I' sits in the body (throat? chest?), and on 'naumy ahaṃ' press gently and let that place soften. The image is a blade, but the feeling should be a release of grip, not a strike — knots untie. Use it whenever possessiveness or self-defence flares; it works fastest on small knots (a slighted email), slower on old ones.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.8

Enough of Difference-Talk

अलं भेदानुकथया त्वद्भक्तिरसचर्वणात् । सर्वमेकमिदं शान्तमिति वक्तुं च लज्जते
alaṃ bhedānukathayā tvadbhaktirasacarvaṇāt | sarvamekamidaṃ śāntamiti vaktuṃ ca lajjate
Enough of difference-talk — chewing the sap of devotion to you: all this is one, at peace; and even to say so feels shy.
17
The Guide

Enough of Difference-Talk

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.8 · v.8
The verse stops out of modesty — and the shyness is the proof.
The logic

Bheda-anukathā, the narration of difference, is cut (alam) — not refuted but abandoned mid-chewing of bhakti-rasa, devotion's sap. What remains: sarvam ekam idam śāntam, one, at peace. The final clause — that even to say so feels shy — guards the verse from proclaiming non-duality as a thesis: the oneness is so ordinary that stating it embarrasses, like saying aloud that a family is at peace.

How to perform it

Use it when a disagreement has the mind re-telling separation stories (them, me, wrong, right). Stop talking — literally if possible — and say it once inwardly. Feeling-tip: shyness is a subtle heat in the face or a slight looking-away; let the body do that, it disarms argument. It also works before sleep as a day-closer: review nothing, just this.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.11

From Your Waking, His Waking

त्वत्प्रबोधात् प्रबोधोऽस्य त्वन्निद्रातो लयोऽस्य यत् । अतस्त्वदात्मकं सर्वं विश्व सदसदात्मकम्
tvatprabodhāt prabodho'sya tvannidrāto layo'sya yat | atastvadātmakaṃ sarvaṃ viśva sadasadātmakam
From your waking comes his waking; from your sleep comes his merging. Hence all is of your nature — the world, both real and unreal.
18
The Guide

From Your Waking, His Waking

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.11 · v.11
Your sleep does not stop the world — it feeds it.
The logic

The verse links two transfers: prabodha (waking) passes from you to him, laya (dissolution) passes from his sleep to the world. Because his waking enlivens the world and his sleep dissolves it, everything — real and unreal — is tvad-ātmaka, of your nature. Sleep is therefore not the end of practice but its most global act: when you sleep you participate in his laya, and the world is laid down with you.

How to perform it

Last line of the day. Lie down and take one awake minute: you are about to do what he does at night. Don't stay awake doing this — the point is to fall asleep inside the thought. Feeling-tip: as the eyes close, feel the day's edges (tasks, persons) get soft, 'unreal' — that softening is literally sadasadātmakam. If anxious at night, this verse dissolves the day rather than reviewing it.

Vijñāna Bhairava v.75

In Sleep, When the Outer Range Perished

अनागतायां निद्रायाम् प्रणष्टे बाह्यगोचरे । सावस्था मनसा गम्या परा देवी प्रकाशते
anāgatāyāṃ nidrāyām praṇaṣṭe bāhyagocare | sāvasthā manasā gamyā parā devī prakāśate
In the not-yet-come sleep, when the outer range has perished — that state, to be gone to by mind, the supreme Goddess shines forth.
19
The Guide

In Sleep, When the Outer Range Perished

Vijñāna Bhairava v.75 · v.75
The instruction for the moment before sleep: go there by mind.
The logic

Three temporal conditions: anāgatāyām nidrāyām — sleep that has not yet arrived; praṇaṣṭe bāhyagocare — once the outer field of sights and sounds has died. In that gap, gamyā (to be gone to) by mind, parā devī, the supreme Goddess, prakāśate — shines, not is-made. The verse locates practice in the dying of the outer senses, where consciousness is neither awake nor asleep, and names what appears there as Śakti herself.

How to perform it

Practise at lights-out, lying on your back, one hand on the belly. Wait for the outer field to fade — sounds go dull, images slow. Then instead of following sleep passively, send the mind as an arrow to the heart-centre, once. Then release. The Goddess-feel is a hum or warmth behind the chest; if images swarm you have grasped — release the arrow and let the hum carry. No repetitions: this is a single-shot practice.

Paramārthasāra v.34

Creating, Keeping, Dissolving

सृष्टिस्थितिसंहारा जाग्रत्स्वप्नौ सुषुप्तम् इति तस्मिन् । भान्ति तुरीये धामनि तथापि तैर् नावृतं भाति
sṛṣṭisthitisaṃhārā jāgratsvapnau suṣuptam iti tasmin | bhānti turīye dhāmani tathāpi tair nāvṛtaṃ bhāti
Creating, keeping, dissolving; waking, dreaming, deep sleep — these shine in him, in the fourth abode; and unveiled he shines, not covered by them.
20
The Guide

Creating, Keeping, Dissolving

Paramārthasāra v.34 · v.34
The states are lights in a room; the room is not covered by them.
The logic

Abhinavagupta maps the macro (the three acts: creation, maintenance, dissolution) onto the micro (the three states), both placed tasmin, in turīya, the fourth abode. The load-bearing clause is tathāpi tair nāvṛtam — yet not veiled by them: turīya is not a fourth experience added to the series but what remains unveiled while the others shine. That is why it is called the fourth and not the first.

How to perform it

Recite as a sleep primer, slowly: the three acts — pause — the three states — pause — then hold nāvṛtam (unveiled) as the last word you hear. The feeling to cultivate: a screen behind the mind's pictures that stays clear while pictures change. Don't try to enter turya; the verse only points at what is not covered. Sleep then arrives without touching the unveiled.

Vijñāna Bhairava v.80

Gaze Down, Mind Unsupported

स्थूलरूपस्य भावस्य स्तब्धां दृष्टिं निपात्य च । अचिरेण निराधारं मनः कृत्वा शिवं व्रजेत्
sthūlarūpasya bhāvasya stabdhāṃ dṛṣṭiṃ nipātya ca | acireṇa nirādhāraṃ manaḥ kṛtvā śivaṃ vrajet
Of the gross-formed state — fixing the gaze downward, and swiftly: making the mind unsupported, one may go to Śiva.
21
The Guide

Gaze Down, Mind Unsupported

Vijñāna Bhairava v.80 · v.80
The last practice before sleep: eyes down, mind unsupported, go.
The logic

Vijñāna Bhairava's simplest night instruction. The bhāva is the gross (sthūla) — the day's residue, the body's weight; the gaze is set firm and lowered — not soft, but settled; and the mind is made nirādhāra, without support: no image, no thought to rest on. Three conditions, one result — śivaṃ vrajet, one may go to Śiva. 'Go' is not travel; it is sleep without a holder.

How to perform it

Lie down. Eyes half-open, lowered toward the pillow, then closed once the gaze has found its floor. Deliberately remove the mind's supports one by one: drop the plan for tomorrow (support removed), drop the commentary on the day (removed), drop even the wish to sleep (removed). What's left is a warm weight behind the sternum with no object — that is nirādhāra. Feeling-tip: if the mind grabs a support, don't fight it; note 'support', and set it down like an object.

Paramārthadvādaśikā v.10

I Am He — the Waking Light

ये ये के ऽपि प्रकाशा मयि सति परमव्योम्नि लब्धावकाशाः । काशा ह्य् एतेषु नित्ये महिमनि मयि ते निर्विभागं विभान्ति । सो ऽहं निर्व्याजनित्यप्रतिहतकलनानन्तसत्यस्वतन्त्रध्वस्तद्वैताद्वयारिद्वयमयतिमिरापारबोधप्रकाशः
ye ye ke 'pi prakāśā mayi sati paramavyomni labdhāvakāśāḥ | kāśā hy eteṣu nitye mahimani mayi te nirvibhāgaṃ vibhānti | so 'haṃ nirvyājanityapratihatakalanānantasatyasvatantradhvastadvaitādvayāridvayamayatimirāpārabodhaprakāśaḥ
Whatever lights there are — abiding in me, having found room in the highest sky — they shine in me, in eternal glory, undistinguished. I am He: unhypocritical, eternal, unbroken, infinite, true, free — the beginningless waking light that dispels the darkness made of the enemy-pair, one and not-one.
22
The Guide

I Am He — the Waking Light

Paramārthadvādaśikā v.10 · v.10
The last verse of the Paramārthadvādaśikā: a sleep-proclamation in one breath.
The logic

Three moves: (1) all lights depend on my space (mayi sati, labdhāvakāśāḥ) — without me they have no room; (2) in my eternal glory they shine undivided — no hierarchy of experiences; (3) so 'ham — the confession, heaped with attributes ending in prakāśa, light, modified by 'dispelling the darkness of the duality-pair'. The verse ends not in an image but in what does the seeing: light that was awake before dark and light were distinguished.

How to perform it

Only the last arm is for use: so'ham ... prakāśaḥ is too long to recite in bed — memorise the short form, 'so'haṃ — I am the waking light', and give it three rounds, one for each attribute-group (true, free, light). Feeling-tip: light WITHOUT source — no lamp, no sun, just visibility. As drowsiness comes, let the pronoun soften first and the light stay. This is the volume's last word because it is the only one that works while asleep.
