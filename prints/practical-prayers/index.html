How this book works Nine sections, one for each turn of the day. Every prayer faces its explanation: the prayer page is for the mouth, the guide page for the mind. Take the prayer at the moment it names — before food, at the threshold, when the negativity arrives — and read its guide once, so the words land with their logic. All seventy-two prayers are Trika: Kashmir Shaivism's own corpus, from Abhinavagupta and Utpaladeva to Vasugupta, Kṣemarāja, Somananda, and the anonymous masters of the Vijñāna Bhairava and the Dehastha. Nothing here needs belief. The verses were built to be said — at the sink, at the desk, in the queue, in the dark.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.1

Homage to the World-Formed

प्रपञ्चोत्तीर्णरूपाय नमस्ते विश्वमूर्तये । सदादिव्यप्रकाशाय स्वात्मनेऽनन्तशक्तये
prapañcottīrṇarūpāya namaste viśvamūrtaye | sadādivyaprakāśāya svātmane'nantaśaktaye
homage to the form that has crossed beyond the manifest — to the self of the world's form; to the ever-divinely-shining, to the self that is unending power.
1
The Guide

Homage to the World-Formed

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.1 · v.1
The first word of the day: bowing to what was here before you woke.
What is happening

Three appositions do the work: he has passed beyond the manifest yet IS the world's form; he always shines with divine light; he is your own self and consists of inexhaustible power. The logic is identity, not distance — you salute the world-form because you address what already shines.

How to use it

Sitting up before your feet touch the floor. Three clauses, three slow bows of the head. Feel the room as svātmane'nantaśaktaye — not out there but as your own body's brightness. Don't visualise a figure; visualise light that has nowhere to go. One round, then stand.

Dehastha v.1

The Body-Temple Offering

देहो देवालयः प्रोक्तः स्वात्मा देवः सनातनः । त्यजेदज्ञाननिर्माल्यं सोऽहं भावेन पूजयेत्
deho devālayaḥ proktaḥ svātmā devaḥ sanātanaḥ | tyajedajñānanirmālyaṃ so'haṃ bhāvena pūjayet
the body is declared god's temple, the own self the eternal god. casting off the sweepings of ignorance, let him worship with the feeling: i-am-he.
2
The Guide

The Body-Temple Offering

Dehastha v.1 · v.1
The morning rite in one verse: the body is the shrine, so'ham is the worship.
What is happening

Dehastha compresses a whole pūjā into four clauses. Temple, God, sweeping, worship: a temple needs cleaning before its God is honoured, and the 'sweepings of ignorance' are the ordinary assumption that the body is mere matter. The worshipper and the worshipped collapse — so'ham bhāvena, with the feeling I-am-He.

How to use it

Wake and stay horizontal. Sweep an imaginary hand from head to toe three times — that is casting off the sweepings. Then place attention at the heart and let so'haṃ, so'haṃ rest with the breath for twelve rounds. The feeling: warmth at the chest, not a thought — the body as lit. Then rise: the day is the pūjā.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.10

The Three States as His Rays

जाग्रतस्वप्नसुषुप्त्याद्या धारयंश्च निजाः कलाः । स्वेच्छया भासि नटवन् निष्कलोऽसि च तत्त्वतः
jāgratasvapnasuṣuptyādyā dhārayaṃśca nijāḥ kalāḥ | svecchayā bhāsi naṭavan niṣkalo'si ca tattvataḥ
waking, dreaming, deep sleep and the rest — you hold them as your own portions. By your will you shine like an actor, yet you are partless in truth.
3
The Guide

The Three States as His Rays

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.10 · v.10
The three states are costumes; the one who shines is partless.
What is happening

The verse splits experience from experiencer: the states are kalāḥ, portions or costumes — things held; the holding is by svecchā, your own will; and the actor is partless though he plays many parts. Play and partlessness are asserted in one breath, so no state can claim you.

How to use it

Recite on waking, naming each state as you come out of it: 'that was a costume.' The actor-idea is literal — you played sleep for seven hours. Feel the partlessness as the fact that nothing about you has edges. Keep it memorised: the fastest exit from a bad dream.

Svabodhasiddhi v.4

Wake the Knowledge That Frees

जयन्त्य् अपारसंसारसारासारविभागदाः । कैवल्यज्ञानसंबोधहेतवश् शम्भवाङ्घ्रयः
jayanty apārasaṃsārasārāsāravibhāgadāḥ | kaivalyajñānasaṃbodhahetavaś śambhavāṅghrayaḥ
victorious are śambhu's feet — they that tell the endless wheel of becoming from its essence and its refuse, and are the cause of the waking knowledge that frees.
4
The Guide

Wake the Knowledge That Frees

Svabodhasiddhi v.4 · v.4
A dawn verse whose whole grammar is waking.
What is happening

Three functions stacked on the feet: they discriminate (saṃsāra's sāra against asāra — essence against refuse), they cause the knowledge that awakens (saṃbodhi-hetu), and they are victorious. Liberation is not granted but caused — through a knowledge that must wake, exactly like you.

How to use it

Say it once after the body-temple verse. Weight the word saṃbodha — waking. Picture a lamp being lit rather than a person bowing. Feel-test: does the line make the room sharper? Repeat at any hour when you have been awake but not waking.

Paramārthasāra v.35

Waking, Dreaming, and the Fourth

जाग्रद् विश्वं भेदात् स्वप्नस् तेजः प्रकाशमाहात्म्यात् । प्राज्ञः सुप्तावस्था ज्ञानघनत्वात् ततः परं तुर्यम्
jāgrad viśvaṃ bhedāt svapnas tejaḥ prakāśamāhātmyāt | prājñaḥ suptāvasthā jñānaghanatvāt tataḥ paraṃ turyam
waking is the universe, through difference; dreaming is splendour, through the greatness of light; the knower is the state of sleep, through the massing of knowing; beyond that, the fourth.
5
The Guide

Waking, Dreaming, and the Fourth

Paramārthasāra v.35 · v.35
Abhinavagupta's census of the states — each credited with what it does best.
What is happening

Each state is honoured for its real contribution instead of being ranked: waking gives the many, dream gives light, sleep gives massed knowing. Only then is turya named — beyond the three, not as a fourth state but as what was never covered by them. The census dismantles the hierarchy.

How to use it

A morning inventory: touch each clause with a memory — this room, that dream, that blankness. Stop at tataḥ paraṃ turyam and don't try to produce a fourth; the verse says beyond, not within. Feeling-tip: after the third clause leave a half-beat of silence — that gap is closer to turya than any image.

Dehastha v.6

The Play of the Dawning

उदयावभासचर्वणलीलां विश्वस्य या करोत्यनिशम् । आनन्दभैरवीं तां विमर्शरूपामहं वन्दे
udayāvabhāsacarvaṇalīlāṃ viśvasya yā karotyaniśam | ānandabhairavīṃ tāṃ vimarśarūpāmahaṃ vande
the play of the dawning, shining forth, and chewing over of the universe, which she performs unceasingly — bliss-bhairavī, her, whose form is reflection, i praise.
6
The Guide

The Play of the Dawning

Dehastha v.6 · v.6
The dawn is her play: she chews the universe into light, unceasingly.
What is happening

Bhairavī's līlā is udaya-avabhāsa-carvaṇa — the dawning, shining and chewing of the universe, performed without pause; her form is vimarśa, reflective awareness. The morning light is not physics but her appetite: she is already chewing the day into being when you open your eyes.

How to use it

At first light, before anything else: watch the room brighten and name it her chewing. One line. Feeling-tip: the day is being eaten into existence for you; you arrive mid-meal. Gratitude, not effort.

Svabodhasiddhi v.1

Bow to the Giver of Freedom

नमः सकलकल्याणमयकैवल्यदायिने / शिवाय शमिताशेषभवाध्वध्वान्तभानवे

bow to the giver of freedom, made of every blessing — to śiva, the sun that burnt away the darkness on every road of becoming.
7
The Guide

Bow to the Giver of Freedom

Svabodhasiddhi v.1 · v.1
A morning bow to the sun that burns the roads dark.
What is happening

The verse stacks blessings — sakalakalyāṇa-maya, made of every good — with the one gift that matters, kaivalya, freedom, and calls Śiva the light that has already burnt the darkness on every path of becoming. You don't light the road at dawn; you bow to the one who burned it.

How to use it

At the window, sun on the face: one bow, palms together. The darkness on the road is already burnt — say it as fact, not wish. Feeling-tip: the day's route looks pre-cleared; you're just walking it.

Anubhavanivedana v.1

The Mudrā of the Waking Hour

अन्तर्लक्ष्यविलीनचित्तपवनो योगी यदा वर्तते दृष्ट्या निश्चलतारया बहिरसौ पश्यन्नपश्यन्नपि । मुद्रेयं खलु शाम्भवी भवति सा युष्मत्प्रसादाद्गुरो शून्याशून्यविवर्जितं भवति यत् तत्त्वं पदं शाम्भवम्
antarlakṣyavilīnacittapavano yogī yadā vartate dṛṣṭyā niścalatārayā bahirasau paśyannapaśyannapi | mudreyaṃ khalu śāmbhavī bhavati sā yuṣmatprasādādguro śūnyāśūnyavivarjitaṃ bhavati yat tattvaṃ padaṃ śāmbhavam
when the yogin abides with mind-wind dissolved in the inward aim — he, seeing yet not seeing outside with unmoving-pupil gaze — this indeed becomes the śāmbhavī mudrā, by your grace, o guru: devoid of void and non-void becomes that reality, the śāmbhava state.
8
The Guide

The Mudrā of the Waking Hour

Anubhavanivedana v.1 · anu-p01 v.1
Half-awake, seeing-not-seeing: the first mudrā of the day.
What is happening

The verse catches the yogin at the waking edge: the mind-wind dissolved inward, pupils steady, seeing the outside yet not seeing it — paśyan apaśyan. That in-between is the śāmbhavī mudrā, and the state it opens is beyond even void and non-void. The hypnopompic moment is already the practice.

How to use it

In bed, eyes half-open before rising: hold one minute in seeing-not-seeing. Don't grasp the state; the verse only names where you already are. Feeling-tip: the room is visible and not-yet-real; stay a breath longer than comfortable.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.13

Whence Foot-Water?

पूर्णस्यावाहनं कुत्र सर्वाधारस्य चासनम् । स्वच्छस्य पाद्यमर्घ्यञ्च शुद्धस्याचमनं कुतः
pūrṇasyāvāhanaṃ kutra sarvādhārasya cāsanam | svacchasya pādyamarghyañca śuddhasyācamanaṃ kutaḥ
for the full, whence invocation? for the all-supporting, whence a seat? for the clear, foot-water and welcome-water? for the pure, the sipping?
9
The Guide

Whence Foot-Water?

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.13 · v.13
The litany begins at the washbasin: the rites cannot apply to what already contains them.
What is happening

The first questions of the deconstructive litany: āvāhana (invocation) presumes absence, āsana (seat) presumes sitting elsewhere, pādyārghya (foot- and hand-water) presumes a journey, ācamana (sipping) presumes impurity. Śiva lacks nothing and is never impure — so the washing is for you, not him. The recognition is a perfect absence of need.

How to use it

At the basin, water on the hands: let each 'whence?' land as a shrug. The water is real, the impurity imagined, the washing both. Feeling-tip: lightness, not solemnity — the joke is the doctrine, and the hands are clean either way.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.14

What Bath for the Stainless?

निर्मलस्य कुतः स्नानं वस्त्रं विश्वोदरस्य च । निर्लेपस्य कुतो गन्धो रम्यस्याभरणं कुतः
nirmalasya kutaḥ snānaṃ vastraṃ viśvodarasya ca | nirlepasya kuto gandho ramyasyābharaṇaṃ kutaḥ
for the stainless, what bath? for the world-bellied, what garment? for the unanointed, what scent? for the lovely, what jewel?
10
The Guide

What Bath for the Stainless?

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.14 · v.14
Shower, towel, scent, jewel — all addressed to one who needs none.
What is happening

The litany names the bathroom itself: snāna (bath), vastra (garment), gandha (scent), bharaṇa (jewel) — each with a deity stripped of the lack it serves: stainless, world-bellied (all-containing), unanointed, lovely. What looks like futility is the precise applicability of each gift: you bathe the unblemished because bathing is what love does, not what dirt requires.

How to use it

In the shower: name each item as you use it — water for the stainless, towel for the world-bellied, scent for the unanointed. The mismatch is the smile. Feeling-tip: the water becomes an offering rather than a chore; three items, three small bows.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.15

Flowers for the Desireless

निरालम्बस्योपवीतं पुष्पं निर्वासनस्य च । अघ्राणस्य कुतो धूपश्चक्षुर्हीनस्य दीपकः
nirālambasyopavītaṃ puṣpaṃ nirvāsanasya ca | aghrāṇasya kuto dhūpaścakṣurhīnasya dīpakaḥ
the sacred thread for the supportless, flowers for the desireless; incense for the noseless, lamp for the eyeless — of what use?
11
The Guide

Flowers for the Desireless

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.15 · v.15
At the mirror: thread, flower, incense, lamp — for the one who has none of the faculties they serve.
What is happening

The next movement of the litany names the offering-objects — upavīta (thread), puṣpa (flower), dhūpa (incense), dīpaka (lamp) — each matched to a deity stripped of the relevant faculty: no support, no desire, no nose, no eye. The mismatch is the teaching: worship addresses the emptiness of lack itself. What looks like futility is the exact fit of each gift.

How to use it

At the mirror or altar: offer each item while naming its mismatch — thread for the one who supports nothing, incense for the one with no nose. With no altar, do it in air with the hand: four gestures, four lines. The feeling: tenderness toward an absence rather than a presence.

Dehastha v.4

The Stainless Guru

यद्धीबलेन विश्वं भक्तानां शिवपथं भाति । तमहमवधानरूपं सद्गुरुममलं सदा वन्दे
yaddhībalena viśvaṃ bhaktānāṃ śivapathaṃ bhāti | tamahamavadhānarūpaṃ sadgurumamalaṃ sadā vande
by whose mind-strength the universe shines as śiva's path for devotees — him, intent in attention, the stainless true guru, always i praise.
12
The Guide

The Stainless Guru

Dehastha v.4 · v.4
After the water: the guru is attention itself, made stainless.
What is happening

The verse identifies the guru not primarily as a person but as avadhāna-rūpa — the form of intent attention — by whose dhībala (mind-strength) the whole universe shines as Śiva's path for devotees. The guru is what makes the world a road rather than a mess. The stainless one (amala) mirrors the freshly washed body.

How to use it

Towel in hand, after the bath: one bow to the clarity that is seeing — the attention that has just been rinsed into readiness. Feeling-tip: the room looks slightly sharper; that sharpness is the guru's path appearing. Then dress as devotee on that path.

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 17.26

This Body for Worship

अन्तरुल्लसदच्छाच्छभक्तिपीयूषपोषितम् / भवत्पूजोपयोगाय शरीरम् इदम् अस्तु मे
antarullasadacchācchabhaktipīyūṣapoṣitam / bhavatpūjopayogāya śarīram idam astu me
may this body of mine — nourished by the clear, bright nectar of devotion rising within — be fit only for your worship.
13
The Guide

This Body for Worship

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 17.26 · 17.26
The body leaves the water consecrated: today's uniform is worship.
What is happening

The body is not renounced; it is consecrated. Nourished by the clear nectar of devotion rising in the heart (antar ullasad acchāccha-bhakti-pīyūṣa), it becomes fit for pūjā — and then it is the offering itself. This is the Trika answer to body-denial: the body is the Lord's instrument, and devotion makes it luminous.

How to use it

Dressed, before leaving the bathroom: say it once and feel the freshly washed body as anointed rather than merely clean. The day ahead is the pūjā; the body is the instrument. Feeling-tip: a slight uprightness in the spine — the instrument picking itself up.

Waving-Lights for the Self-Shining

स्वयं प्रकाशमानस्य कुतो नीराजनं विभोः । वेदवाचामवेद्यस्य कुतः स्तोत्रं विधीयते

whence waving-lights for the self-shining lord? how is a hymn enjoined for him whom the veda cannot say?
14
The Guide

Waving-Lights for the Self-Shining

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.17 · v.17
The mirror, the lamp: he lights himself — so the light is for you.
What is happening

Two paradoxes: an offering of light presupposes darkness in the recipient; speech presupposes an object speech can reach. The Lord is self-lit and beyond the Veda's words. The rite's use falls on the offerer — you wave the lamp so your own eyes open; you sing because the singing lights you.

How to use it

At the bathroom mirror, hand raised with the light: you are the lamp-bearer, and the face that brightens is the audience. Hum, don't articulate — you have just honoured the Vedavācāmavedya. Feeling-tip: the chest brightens before the mirror does.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.18

Oblation for the Difference-Free

अन्तर्बहिश्च पूर्णस्य कथमुद्वासनं भवेत् । भेदहीनस्य विश्वत्र कथं च हवनं भवेत्
antarbahiśca pūrṇasya kathamudvāsanaṃ bhavet | bhedahīnasya viśvatra kathaṃ ca havanaṃ bhavet
how is there sending-off for the full, inside and out? and how is there oblation for the difference-free, everywhere?
15
The Guide

Oblation for the Difference-Free

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.18 · v.18
You can't send off or burn what's already everywhere.
What is happening

Udvāsana (formal dismissal) and havana (oblation into fire) both require edges — a thing to dismiss, a thing to burn. The full has no inside or out; the difference-free is everywhere, so nothing can be offered into a fire that isn't already him. The washing has no residue to send away.

How to use it

At the end of the bath, hand under the tap: the water dismisses nothing and burns nothing; it just falls. Let the litany's answer be that shrug. Feeling-tip: no residue to send off — the day starts clean because it was never stained.

Gaze Down, Mind Unsupported
