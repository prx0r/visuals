Bhagavad Gītā 4.24 (Lakshmanjoo mealtime yajña)

The Meal Offering

ब्रह्मार्पणं ब्रह्म हविर् ब्रह्माग्नौ ब्रह्मणा हुतम् । ब्रह्मैव तेन गन्तव्यं ब्रह्मकर्मसमाधिना ॥
brahmārpaṇaṁ brahma havir brahmāgnau brahmaṇā hutam | brahmaiva tena gantavyaṁ brahmakarmasamādhinā ||
the offering is brahman / the oblation is brahman / poured by brahman into the fire of brahman / brahman alone is reached / by one absorbed in the action that is brahman
1
The Guide

The Meal Offering

Bhagavad Gītā 4.24 (Lakshmanjoo mealtime yajña) · Gītā 4.24
The meal prayer: water in the palm, one verse, and eating becomes worship.
What is happening

Lakshmanjoo read brahman throughout as Lord Śiva. Four things are named — the offering, the oblation, the fire, the goal — and all four are the same substance. What you take is offered to Śiva; what you offer is itself Śiva; the stomach is Śiva's residence. The logic collapses the distance between eater, food and eaten into one taste.

How to use it

Before the first bite: put a little water in the palm, look at the food, and say the verse once. Then eat as if the meal is being offered into a fire — slowly, without hurry. The feeling to find: gratitude with heat. Nothing extra; the verse does the whole work.

Somananda, Śivadṛṣṭi 7.103 (mealtime lore)

Śiva Bows to Śiva

अस्मद्रूप समाविष्टः स्वात्मनात्म निवारणे । शिवः करोतु निजया नमः शक्त्या ततात्मने ॥
asmadrūpa samāviṣṭaḥ svātmanātma nivāraṇe | śivaḥ karotu nijayā namaḥ śaktyā tatātmane ||
śiva, having entered the form that is myself, bows — by his own power — to śiva, for the removal of the obstacle that is himself.
2
The Guide

Śiva Bows to Śiva

Somananda, Śivadṛṣṭi 7.103 (mealtime lore) · Śivadṛṣṭi 7.103 (provisional)
The obstacle on the plate is also Śiva. Say it before food.
What is happening

The whole Trika in one bow: the one who bows, the one bowed to, the obstacle being removed, and the power doing the removing are all Śiva. Nothing is outside the recognition — not even the thing that blocks it. Lakshmanjoo taught this verse alongside the mealtime practice.

How to use it

Say it silently as the food arrives. The 'obstacle' can be anything — the craving, the guilt, the person you're eating with. Name it, then let the verse dissolve it. Feeling-tip: the moment of placing should feel like relief, like putting something down.

Dehastha v.5

The Sense-Goddesses Offer Bhairava

आत्मीयविषयभोगैरिन्द्रियदेव्यः सदा हृदम्भोजे । अभिपूजयन्ति यं तं चिन्मयमानन्दभैरवं वन्दे
ātmīyaviṣayabhogairindriyadevyaḥ sadā hṛdambhoje | abhipūjayanti yaṃ taṃ cinmayamānandabhairavaṃ vande
with feasts of their own sense-objects, the sense-goddesses ever worship, in the heart-lotus, him — the consciousness-made bliss-bhairava: i praise.
3
The Guide

The Sense-Goddesses Offer Bhairava

Dehastha v.5 · v.5
Every taste is already a pūjā happening in the heart.
What is happening

Dehastha inverts the usual charge against the senses: the senses are goddesses (indriya-devyāḥ), and their enjoyment of their objects is continuous worship of the Bhairava made of consciousness. Eating is the oldest form of practice — the offering is already being made in the heart-lotus.

How to use it

At the first bite, feel the heart-lotus (just behind the centre of the chest) as the altar and the flavour as the flower landing on it. Three bites only — overdoing it kills the taste. The feeling: gratitude with heat. The sense is honoured as a goddess, not supervised as a thief.

Vijñāna Bhairava v.72

From Eating and Drinking

जग्धिपानकृतोल्लासरसानन्दविजृम्भणात् । भावयेद् भरितावस्थां महानन्दस् ततो भवेत्
jagdhipānakṛtollāsarasānandavijṛmbhaṇāt | bhāvayed bharitāvasthāṃ mahānandas tato bhavet
from the swell of joy made by eating and drinking — from the blooming of bliss-rasa — one should imagine the full state; great bliss then may arise.
4
The Guide

From Eating and Drinking

Vijñāna Bhairava v.72 · v.72
Take the fullness of a meal and turn it into mahānanda.
What is happening

The dhāraṇā uses a given fact — eating already produces rasa and ullāsa, savour and lift — and makes that the object of bhāvanā: not the taste itself but the state of being filled (bharitāvasthā). Ordinary satiety is the raw material; great bliss the product, if attention stays on fullness instead of sliding to the next want.

How to use it

At the meal, not before. Eat to comfortable fullness, then pause with eyes half-open. Take one more spoonful of air, hold, and imagine the fullness radiating from the belly upward — chest, then head. Release. Three rounds. Don't chase ecstasy: the graded signal is that thinking quiets while the body stays warm. If drowsiness comes, stand up.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.16

Food-Offering for the Ever-Content

नित्यतृप्तस्य नैवेद्यं ताम्बूलं च कुतो विभोः । प्रदक्षिणमनन्तस्याऽद्वितीयस्यय कुतो नतिः
nityatṛptasya naivedyaṃ tāmbūlaṃ ca kuto vibhoḥ | pradakṣiṇamanantasyā'dvitīyasyaya kuto natiḥ
food-offering for the ever-content, betel for the lord — whence? circumambulation for the endless, obeisance for the non-dual — whence?
5
The Guide

Food-Offering for the Ever-Content

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.16 · v.16
The litany asks: what can you offer to one who lacks nothing?
What is happening

The four closing rites of pūjā — naivedya (food offering), tāmbūla (betel), pradakṣiṇa (circumambulation), nati (obeisance) — all address a being who could lack something. Śiva lacks nothing. So the offering is not for him but for you: the act of offering is what turns eating into worship, not the food itself.

How to use it

Use it as the shrug before a meal: 'what can I offer the one who lacks nothing?' — then offer anyway. The humour is the doctrine. Feeling-tip: lightness, not solemnity. The verse makes the ritual joke that is also the deepest point.

Waking

on rising
Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.1

Homage to the World-Formed

प्रपञ्चोत्तीर्णरूपाय नमस्ते विश्वमूर्तये । सदादिव्यप्रकाशाय स्वात्मनेऽनन्तशक्तये
prapañcottīrṇarūpāya namaste viśvamūrtaye | sadādivyaprakāśāya svātmane'nantaśaktaye
homage to the form that has crossed beyond the manifest — to the self of the world's form; to the ever-divinely-shining, to the self that is unending power.
6
The Guide

Homage to the World-Formed

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.1 · v.1
The first word of the day: bowing to what was here before you woke.
What is happening

Three appositions do the work: he has passed beyond the manifest yet IS the world's form; he always shines with divine light; he is your own self and consists of inexhaustible power. The logic is identity, not distance — you salute the world-form because you address what already shines.

How to use it

Sitting up before your feet touch the floor. Three clauses, three slow bows of the head. Feel the room as svātmane'nantaśaktaye — not out there but as your own body's brightness. Don't visualise a figure; visualise light that has nowhere to go. One round, then stand.

Dehastha v.1

The Body-Temple Offering

देहो देवालयः प्रोक्तः स्वात्मा देवः सनातनः । त्यजेदज्ञाननिर्माल्यं सोऽहं भावेन पूजयेत्
deho devālayaḥ proktaḥ svātmā devaḥ sanātanaḥ | tyajedajñānanirmālyaṃ so'haṃ bhāvena pūjayet
the body is declared god's temple, the own self the eternal god. casting off the sweepings of ignorance, let him worship with the feeling: i-am-he.
7
The Guide

The Body-Temple Offering

Dehastha v.1 · v.1
The morning rite in one verse: the body is the shrine, so'ham is the worship.
What is happening

Dehastha compresses a whole pūjā into four clauses. Temple, God, sweeping, worship: a temple needs cleaning before its God is honoured, and the 'sweepings of ignorance' are the ordinary assumption that the body is mere matter. The worshipper and the worshipped collapse — so'ham bhāvena, with the feeling I-am-He.

How to use it

Wake and stay horizontal. Sweep an imaginary hand from head to toe three times — that is casting off the sweepings. Then place attention at the heart and let so'haṃ, so'haṃ rest with the breath for twelve rounds. The feeling: warmth at the chest, not a thought — the body as lit. Then rise: the day is the pūjā.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.10

The Three States as His Rays

जाग्रतस्वप्नसुषुप्त्याद्या धारयंश्च निजाः कलाः । स्वेच्छया भासि नटवन् निष्कलोऽसि च तत्त्वतः
jāgratasvapnasuṣuptyādyā dhārayaṃśca nijāḥ kalāḥ | svecchayā bhāsi naṭavan niṣkalo'si ca tattvataḥ
waking, dreaming, deep sleep and the rest — you hold them as your own portions. By your will you shine like an actor, yet you are partless in truth.
8
The Guide

The Three States as His Rays

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.10 · v.10
The three states are costumes; the one who shines is partless.
What is happening

The verse splits experience from experiencer: the states are kalāḥ, portions or costumes — things held; the holding is by svecchā, your own will; and the actor is partless though he plays many parts. Play and partlessness are asserted in one breath, so no state can claim you.

How to use it

Recite on waking, naming each state as you come out of it: 'that was a costume.' The actor-idea is literal — you played sleep for seven hours. Feel the partlessness as the fact that nothing about you has edges. Keep it memorised: the fastest exit from a bad dream.

Svabodhasiddhi v.4

Wake the Knowledge That Frees

जयन्त्य् अपारसंसारसारासारविभागदाः । कैवल्यज्ञानसंबोधहेतवश् शम्भवाङ्घ्रयः
jayanty apārasaṃsārasārāsāravibhāgadāḥ | kaivalyajñānasaṃbodhahetavaś śambhavāṅghrayaḥ
victorious are śambhu's feet — they that tell the endless wheel of becoming from its essence and its refuse, and are the cause of the waking knowledge that frees.
9
The Guide

Wake the Knowledge That Frees

Svabodhasiddhi v.4 · v.4
A dawn verse whose whole grammar is waking.
What is happening

Three functions stacked on the feet: they discriminate (saṃsāra's sāra against asāra — essence against refuse), they cause the knowledge that awakens (saṃbodhi-hetu), and they are victorious. Liberation is not granted but caused — through a knowledge that must wake, exactly like you.

How to use it

Say it once after the body-temple verse. Weight the word saṃbodha — waking. Picture a lamp being lit rather than a person bowing. Feel-test: does the line make the room sharper? Repeat at any hour when you have been awake but not waking.

Paramārthasāra v.35

Waking, Dreaming, and the Fourth

जाग्रद् विश्वं भेदात् स्वप्नस् तेजः प्रकाशमाहात्म्यात् । प्राज्ञः सुप्तावस्था ज्ञानघनत्वात् ततः परं तुर्यम्
jāgrad viśvaṃ bhedāt svapnas tejaḥ prakāśamāhātmyāt | prājñaḥ suptāvasthā jñānaghanatvāt tataḥ paraṃ turyam
waking is the universe, through difference; dreaming is splendour, through the greatness of light; the knower is the state of sleep, through the massing of knowing; beyond that, the fourth.
10
The Guide

Waking, Dreaming, and the Fourth

Paramārthasāra v.35 · v.35
Abhinavagupta's census of the states — each credited with what it does best.
What is happening

Each state is honoured for its real contribution instead of being ranked: waking gives the many, dream gives light, sleep gives massed knowing. Only then is turya named — beyond the three, not as a fourth state but as what was never covered by them. The census dismantles the hierarchy.

How to use it

A morning inventory: touch each clause with a memory — this room, that dream, that blankness. Stop at tataḥ paraṃ turyam and don't try to produce a fourth; the verse says beyond, not within. Feeling-tip: after the third clause leave a half-beat of silence — that gap is closer to turya than any image.

Sleeping

at lights-out
Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.11

From Your Waking, His Waking

त्वत्प्रबोधात् प्रबोधोऽस्य त्वन्निद्रातो लयोऽस्य यत् । अतस्त्वदात्मकं सर्वं विश्व सदसदात्मकम्
tvatprabodhāt prabodho'sya tvannidrāto layo'sya yat | atastvadātmakaṃ sarvaṃ viśva sadasadātmakam
from your waking comes his waking; from your sleep comes his merging. hence all is of your nature — the world, both real and unreal.
11
The Guide

From Your Waking, His Waking

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.11 · v.11
Your sleep does not stop the world — it feeds it.
What is happening

Two transfers: prabodha (waking) passes from you to him, laya (dissolution) from his sleep to the world. Because his waking enlivens the world and his sleep dissolves it, everything is tvad-ātmaka, of your nature. Sleep is not the end of practice but its most global act: when you sleep you participate in his laya.

How to use it

Last line of the day. Lie down and take one awake minute: you are about to do what he does at night. Don't stay awake doing this — fall asleep inside the thought. Feeling-tip: as the eyes close, feel the day's edges get soft, 'unreal' — that softening is sadasadātmakam. If anxious at night, this dissolves the day rather than reviewing it.

Vijñāna Bhairava v.75

In Sleep, When the Outer Range Perished

अनागतायां निद्रायाम् प्रणष्टे बाह्यगोचरे । सावस्था मनसा गम्या परा देवी प्रकाशते
anāgatāyāṃ nidrāyām praṇaṣṭe bāhyagocare | sāvasthā manasā gamyā parā devī prakāśate
in the not-yet-come sleep, when the outer range has perished — that state, to be gone to by mind, the supreme goddess shines forth.
12
The Guide

In Sleep, When the Outer Range Perished

Vijñāna Bhairava v.75 · v.75
The instruction for the moment before sleep: go there by mind.
What is happening

Three conditions: sleep that has not yet arrived; once the outer field has died. In that gap, to be gone to by mind, parā devī, the supreme Goddess, shines — not is-made. The verse locates practice in the dying of the outer senses, where consciousness is neither awake nor asleep, and names what appears there as Śakti herself.

How to use it

At lights-out, on your back, one hand on the belly. Wait for the outer field to fade — sounds go dull, images slow. Then instead of following sleep passively, send the mind as an arrow to the heart-centre, once. Then release. The Goddess-feel is a hum or warmth behind the chest; if images swarm you have grasped — release and let the hum carry. Single-shot: no repetitions.

Vijñāna Bhairava v.80

Gaze Down, Mind Unsupported

स्थूलरूपस्य भावस्य स्तब्धां दृष्टिं निपात्य च । अचिरेण निराधारं मनः कृत्वा शिवं व्रजेत्
sthūlarūpasya bhāvasya stabdhāṃ dṛṣṭiṃ nipātya ca | acireṇa nirādhāraṃ manaḥ kṛtvā śivaṃ vrajet
of the gross-formed state — fixing the gaze downward, and swiftly: making the mind unsupported, one may go to śiva.
13
The Guide

Gaze Down, Mind Unsupported

Vijñāna Bhairava v.80 · v.80
The last practice before sleep: eyes down, mind unsupported, go.
What is happening

The bhāva is the gross — the day's residue, the body's weight; the gaze is set firm and lowered; the mind is made nirādhāra, without support: no image, no thought to rest on. Three conditions, one result — śivaṃ vrajet, one may go to Śiva. 'Go' is not travel; it is sleep without a holder.

How to use it

Lie down. Eyes half-open, lowered toward the pillow, then closed once the gaze has found its floor. Remove the mind's supports one by one: drop the plan for tomorrow, drop the commentary on the day, drop even the wish to sleep. What's left is a warm weight behind the sternum with no object — that is nirādhāra. If the mind grabs a support, note 'support' and set it down.

Paramārthasāra v.34

Creating, Keeping, Dissolving

सृष्टिस्थितिसंहारा जाग्रत्स्वप्नौ सुषुप्तम् इति तस्मिन् । भान्ति तुरीये धामनि तथापि तैर् नावृतं भाति
sṛṣṭisthitisaṃhārā jāgratsvapnau suṣuptam iti tasmin | bhānti turīye dhāmani tathāpi tair nāvṛtaṃ bhāti
creating, keeping, dissolving; waking, dreaming, deep sleep — these shine in him, in the fourth abode; and unveiled he shines, not covered by them.
14
The Guide

Creating, Keeping, Dissolving

Paramārthasāra v.34 · v.34
The states are lights in a room; the room is not covered by them.
What is happening

The macro (creation, maintenance, dissolution) is mapped onto the micro (the three states), both placed in turīya, the fourth abode. The load-bearing clause: tathāpi tair nāvṛtam — yet not veiled by them. Turīya is not a fourth experience but what remains unveiled while the others shine.

How to use it

A sleep primer, slowly: the three acts — pause — the three states — pause — then hold nāvṛtam (unveiled) as the last word you hear. The feeling: a screen behind the mind's pictures that stays clear while pictures change. Don't try to enter turya; the verse points at what is not covered. Sleep then arrives without touching the unveiled.

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 9.14

At Your Cool Feet

अशेषविषयाशून्यश्रीसमाश्लेषसुस्थितः / शयीयम् इव शीताङ्घ्रिकुशेशययुगे कदा
aśeṣaviṣayāśūnyaśrīsamāśleṣasusthitaḥ / śayīyam iva śītāṅghrikuśeśayayuge kadā
when will i lie down — free of every object, settled in the embrace of undiminished glory — at your two cool lotus feet?
15
The Guide

At Your Cool Feet

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 9.14 · 9.14
The sleep prayer: the cool feet are the peace after the fever of wanting.
What is happening

Utpaladeva asks when he will lie down free of every object (aśeṣaviṣaya — no grasping left), settled in undiminished glory (aśūnya śrī), at the two cool lotus feet (śītāṅghri-kuśeśaya). 'Free of every object' is not blankness but the rest that remains when the mind stops reaching. The cool feet are the peace of Śiva.

How to use it

In bed. Say it once, then let the image of the two cool feet form under the mind's eye — no details needed, just the coolness. The feeling to find: the fever of the day dropping. If the mind races, return to the coolness, not the words.

IV

Waiting

in the in-between
Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 16.8

I Don't Mind

विषमस्थो ऽपि स्वस्थो ऽपि रुदन्न् अपि हसन्न् अपि / गम्भीरो ऽपि विचित्तो ऽपि भवेयं भक्तितः प्रभो
viṣamastho 'pi svastho 'pi rudann api hasann api / gambhīro 'pi vicitto 'pi bhaveyaṃ bhaktitaḥ prabho
in crisis or at ease, weeping or laughing, grave or unhinged — by devotion, lord, let me be so.
16
The Guide

I Don't Mind

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 16.8 · 16.8
The api-chain refuses resolution: everything is allowed.
What is happening

The verse piles up opposites with 'api' (even): viṣama/sthāna (crisis/peace), rudann/hasann (weeping/laughing), gambhīro/vicitta (grave/unhinged). Devotion is not the absence of any of them; it is what holds them. The prayer does not ask for calm — it asks to be devotion through whatever arrives.

How to use it

Use it when waiting has turned into agitation — a queue, a delay, a message that won't come. Say it once inwardly. Don't wait for composure: the very mess is the place where devotion can be. Feeling-tip: the shoulders drop half a millimetre; that's the api doing its work.

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 1.1

Methodless Devotion

न ध्यायतो न जपतः स्याद् यस्याविधिपूर्वकम् / एवम् एव शिवभासस् तं नुमो भक्तिशालिनम्
na dhyāyato na japataḥ syād yasyāvidhipūrvakam / evam eva śivabhāsas taṃ numo bhaktiśālinam
i bow to that devotee who is glorified with the devotion of lord śiva and to whom the appearance of lord śiva takes place without conducting meditation or recitation of any kind.
17
The Guide

Methodless Devotion

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 1.1 · 1.1
No method needed — the waiting itself is the devotion.
What is happening

The opening verse of the Śivastotrāvalī: the devotee to whom Śiva appears without dhyāna or japa (avidhipūrvakam — methodless). The bow is to a state where nothing is being done. Waiting is exactly that state: nothing to do, nowhere to go, and yet not nothing.

How to use it

In a queue, on a train, waiting for news: say 'I bow to that devotee' and recognise yourself as the one described. No technique. The waiting is already the practice. Feeling-tip: the hands can rest; the attention does not need to grasp.

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 17.19

In Every Action

अनुभूयासमीशान प्रतिकर्म क्षणात् क्षणम् / भवत्पूजामृतापानमदास्वादमहामुदम्

o lord, may i experience, moment by moment, in every action, the great joy of drinking the nectar of your worship.

The Guide

In Every Action

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 17.19 · 17.19
The in-between moment becomes the practice itself.
What is happening

Utpaladeva prays for the taste of worship inside every action — not more ritual, but the nectar of pūjā in the ordinary. 'Moment by moment' (kṣaṇāt kṣaṇam) is the whole method: the repeated return of attention. Waiting is full of kṣaṇas — this verse makes them the site of the drink.

How to use it

At any transition or pause: say it once, then take one breath as if that breath were the nectar. Don't manufacture joy — notice the small lift that a pause already gives. Three pauses, three sips, and the waiting has changed colour.

Abhinavagupta, Anuttarāṣṭikā v.1

No Method

संक्रामो ऽत्र न भावना न च कथायुक्तिर् न चर्चा न च ध्यानं वा न च धारणा न च जपाभ्यासप्रयासो न च । तत् किं नाम सुनिश्चितं वद परं सत्यं च तच्छ्रूयतां न त्यागी न परिग्रही भज सुखं सर्वं यथावस्थितः
saṃkrāmo 'tra na bhāvanā na ca kathāyuktir na carcā na ca dhyānaṃ vā na ca dhāraṇā na ca japābhyāsaprayāso na ca | tat kiṃ nāma suniścitaṃ vada paraṃ satyaṃ ca tacchrūyatāṃ na tyāgī na parigrahī bhaja sukhaṃ sarvaṃ yathāvasthitaḥ
there is no crossing over here — no contemplation, no discussion, no visualization, no steadying, no effort of japa. so what is decided? speak the supreme truth: not a renouncer, not a possessor — enjoy happiness, all of it, staying exactly as you are.
19
The Guide

No Method

Abhinavagupta, Anuttarāṣṭikā v.1 · v.1
The anupāya: when every method is denied, waiting is the answer.
What is happening

The verse takes away every method — bhāvanā, kathā, carcā, dhyāna, dhāraṇā, japa — and leaves one instruction: stay as you are (yathāvasthitaḥ), enjoy happiness, neither renouncing nor grasping. Waiting is yathāvasthitaḥ in its purest form: nothing to do, nothing to fix.

How to use it

When waiting becomes restless, say the verse — or just its last line, 'stay exactly as you are.' The trying to become something is the only distance. Feeling-tip: the restlessness is allowed to sit; you don't argue with it; you just stay.

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 12.25

Don't Control the Swift Mind

मनसि स्वरसेन यत्र तत्र प्रचरत्य् अप्य् अहम् अस्य गोचरेषु / प्रसृतो ऽप्य् अविलोल एव युष्मत्परिचर्याचतुरः सदा भवेयम्
manasi svarasena yatra tatra pracaraty apy aham asya gocareṣu / prasṛto 'py avilola eva yuṣmatparicaryācaturaḥ sadā bhaveyam
i don't want to control my mind because the mind is so swift, you cannot catch it.
20
The Guide

Don't Control the Swift Mind

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 12.25 · 12.25
Let the mind roam in the waiting; skill within, not restraint.
What is happening

Lakshmanjoo's gloss: the mind is too swift to catch, so the verse does not ask for control. The practice is skill within roaming (svarasena pracaraty api) — not catching the mind but being with it as it moves. Waiting is when the mind roams most; this verse blesses the roaming.

How to use it

In the waiting, watch the mind go where it goes — plans, memories, fantasies — without steering. The skill is staying with it, not against it. Feeling-tip: a slight amusement at how fast it moves; that amusement is already the 'worship-skilled' state the verse asks for.

Abhinavagupta, Bhairavastava 5

I Never Fear

इत्थम् उपोढभवन्मयसंविद्दीधितिदारितभूरितमिस्रः । मृत्युयमान्तककर्मपिशाचैर् नाथ नमोऽस्तु न जातु बिभेमि
ittham upoḍhabhavanmayasaṃviddīdhitidāritabhūritamisraḥ | mṛtyuyamāntakakarmapiśācair nātha namo'stu na jātu bibhemi
thus, with the great darkness torn open by the rays of the consciousness become you — lord, namas; i never fear, not from death, from yama, from the ender, from the ghosts of karma.
21
The Guide

I Never Fear

Abhinavagupta, Bhairavastava 5 · bh-p05
Fearlessness as salutation, not as courage.
What is happening

Awareness has already torn the darkness; what remains is not courage but salutation. The devotee still names death's ministers — Mṛtyu, Yama, the ender, the karma-ghosts — and bows to them, because they too are Śiva's play. 'I never fear' is the natural temperature of recognition.

How to use it

Recite when fear arrives — not to banish it but to salute it. The fear is named, bowed to, and left standing. Feeling-tip: the chest opens rather than tightens; the bow is physical as well as verbal. Do it at night, or in the moment before the difficult thing.

Abhinavagupta, Bhairavastava 3

No Fear-Talk

स्वात्मनि विश्वगये त्वयि नाथे तेन न संसृतिभीतिकथास्ति । सत्स्व् अपि दुर्धरदुःखविमोहत्रासविधायिषु कर्मगणेषु
svātmani viśvagaye tvayi nāthe tena na saṃsṛtibhītikathāsti | satsv api durdharaduḥkhavimohatrāsavidhāyiṣu karmagaṇeṣu
with you as lord, all-pervading in own self — therefore no talk of transmigration-fear. even though hard-to-bear suffering, delusion, terror-causing karma-hosts are present.
22
The Guide

No Fear-Talk

Abhinavagupta, Bhairavastava 3 · bh-p03
The suffering is conceded; the fear-talk is not.
What is happening

The verse does not deny suffering — it concedes the karma-hosts (durdharaduḥkhavimohatrāsavidhāyiṣu) — but denies the fear of saṃsāra, because the Lord is all-pervading in the self. Suffering can be present without being feared; the fear-talk is what stops.

How to use it

When negative, say it and let the concession stand: yes, this is hard; and the fear of it is separate from the pain itself. The verse splits them. Feeling-tip: the pain stays, the story about the pain thins. That thinning is the verse working.

The Cloud Lifts

गुरोर्वाक्याद्युक्ति-प्रचय-रचन-उन्मार्जन-वशात्समाश्वासाच्छास्त्रं प्रति समुचितादपि कथितम् । विलीने शङ्काभ्रे हृदयगगनोद्भासिमहसः प्रभोः सूर्यस्येव स्पृशतु चरणान् ध्वान्तजयिनः
gurorvākyādyukti-pracaya-racana-unmārjana-vaśātsamāśvāsācchāstraṃ prati samucitādapi kathitam | vilīne śaṅkābhre hṛdayagaganodbhāsimahasaḥ prabhoḥ sūryasyeva spṛśatu caraṇān dhvāntajayinaḥ
from the guru's word — by the force that wipes away the fabric of heaped-up reasonings — and from the assurance told, fittingly, out of regard for scripture: when the cloud of doubt has dissolved, let me touch the feet of the lord, whose splendor shines in the sky of the heart — like the sun, the conqueror of darkness.
23
The Guide

The Cloud Lifts

Abhinavagupta, Pañcaślokī 4 · pan-p04
For doubt: the cloud dissolves, the sun is already there.
What is happening

The verse traces doubt to 'heaped-up reasonings' (pracaya-racana) that the guru's word wipes away, and promises that when the cloud of doubt (śaṅkābhra) dissolves, the feet of the sun-like Lord — shining in the heart-sky — become touchable. Doubt is weather, not the sky.

How to use it

When doubt is heavy, say it and picture the cloud as literal weather over the heart. The sun is not summoned; it is uncovered. Feeling-tip: the image of touching the feet — a small act of trust — is the whole practice. Repeat the last line as a refrain.

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 2.8

Saṃsāra and Beyond

संसारैकनिमित्ताय संसारैकविरोधिने / नमः सम्साररूपाय निःसंसाराय शम्भवे
saṃsāraikanimittāya saṃsāraikavirodhine / namaḥ samsārarūpāya niḥsaṃsārāya śambhave
to the one who is the sole cause of saṃsāra and the sole opponent of saṃsāra; to śambhu, who is the form of saṃsāra and is beyond saṃsāra, namas.
24
The Guide

Saṃsāra and Beyond

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 2.8 · 2.8
No enemy outside: bondage and release are the same Lord.
What is happening

A paradox of the recognition: the one who causes saṃsāra also destroys it; Śambhu is its form and also beyond it. There is no enemy to defeat outside the self. When you feel negative about the world, this verse says: the world's very form is Śambhu.

How to use it

When the world feels like the problem, say it. The split between 'spiritual' and 'worldly' is the only saṃsāra. Feeling-tip: the irritation with the world softens into recognition; the same world, seen as his form, is no longer an attack.

Svabodhasiddhi v.6

The Knife That Cuts the Knot

ममाहम् इति हृद्ग्रन्थिच्छेदनैककृपाणिकाम् । स्पन्दास्पन्दकृतग्रासां नौम्य् अहं शुद्धसंविदम्
mamāham iti hṛdgranthicchedanaikakṛpāṇikām | spandāspandakṛtagrāsāṃ naumy ahaṃ śuddhasaṃvidam
i bow — i — to pure awareness: the knife that alone cuts the heart's knot of 'mine' and 'i', the one that has swallowed both the throb and its stillness.
25
The Guide

The Knife That Cuts the Knot

Svabodhasiddhi v.6 · v.6
For guilt and possessiveness: one cut, and the knot is gone.
What is happening

The knot (granthi) is mamāham — 'mine' and 'I', two threads. The instrument is a kṛpāṇa, a knife, and only one does it: pure awareness. And the knower's own domain — throb and stillness — has already been swallowed by that knife. 'I bow — I' stages the surrender: the pronoun survives long enough to bow, then is cut too.

How to use it

Recite with a hand flat over the sternum. Weight the first aham: feel where your 'I' sits in the body, and on 'naumy ahaṃ' press gently and let that place soften. The image is a blade, but the feeling is a release of grip, not a strike — knots untie. Fastest on small knots (a slighted email), slower on old ones.

o maheśa, may this lovely speech of mine flash forth at your door, faultlessly, again and again: victory, victory to maheśa! may this auspicious speech be giver of bliss to me, unceasingly. o lord, o giver of refuge, make me — whose protector is maheśanātha — to have a protector.

The Guide

Victory at the Door

Abhinavagupta, Pañcaślokī 2 · pan-p02
The prayer of rejoicing: only speech to offer, and it flashes at the door.
What is happening

The devotee has nothing but speech, and asks that it flash at the Lord's door, faultless, repeating 'victory, victory to Maheśa'. Then the real request: make me to have a protector. Śaraṇāgati is not becoming strong but being held — and the rejoicing is the holding already underway.

How to use it

When something good has happened — or when you want to rejoice anyway — say it aloud if possible. The repetition of 'jaya jaya maheśa' is the celebration. Feeling-tip: the smile arrives before the words; let it. The verse is permission to be happy in his name.

Mad with the Nectar of Devotion

Devotion as intoxication: the positive state has a name.

The Stone Pillar Worships

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 20.8 · 20.8
When devotion itches, even a stone pillar becomes worship.

When the positive urge to connect arrives — the itch — don't damp it. Say this and let the itch be the offering. The feeling: a restless sweetness that doesn't need a destination. Even waiting with the itch is already the pillar being worshipped.

He Alone Wins

Kṣemarāja, Spandasandoha 1 · v.1
A one-line victory song for the moment everything is right.
What is happening

Kṣemarāja's condensation of the Spandakārikā: Śiva alone wins — he who possesses the pulse (spanda), is self-established, and makes the inner face taste. 'The inner face' is attention turned back on itself; to taste it is the whole of the practice. The verse is already in the register of victory.

The Wheel of Powers

यस्योन्मेषनिमेषाभ्यां जगतः प्रलयोदयौ । तं शक्तिचक्रविभवप्रभवं शङ्करं स्तुमः

The Wheel of Powers

Vasugupta, Spandakārikā 1.1 · Spandakārikā 1.1
The opening hymn: the world blinks, and we sing to the one who blinks it.
