Bhagavad Gītā 4.24 — the meal prayer
Why these prayers Swami Lakshmanjoo compiled his Wisdom verses in the summer of 1987 and told his devotees: "These verses are for your daily recitation. Maybe sometime you will experience these stages." Four of the six hymns in his recitation appendix live in these pages — the Krama Stotra, the Anuttarāṣṭikā, the Bhairava Stotra and the Dehastha — alongside the mealtime yajña he taught, the breath-japa of the Vijñāna Bhairava, and the dawn-dusk junction of the Tantrāloka: "the Lord must be worshipped at the point of the meeting of day and night." Ten sections, one for each moment the day turns. Forty prayers, each facing its explanation — when to do it, how, why, and what it does. At the close: the two hymns chanted at his Weekly Sangha, the Aghora Mantra and the Śrī Bahurūpa-Garbha Stotra, with the notes from his own teaching. Nothing here needs belief. The verses were built to be said — at the sink, at the desk, in the queue, in the dark.
Practical Prayers
Reading a spread Each prayer sits beside its explanation. The left page is for the mouth — Devanagari big enough to fill the eye, the sound beneath it, the sense beneath that. The right page is for the mind: what the verse is actually doing, how to perform it, and what it is for. Read the guide once when you meet the prayer. After that, only the prayer page matters — you'll know what lies behind it. Take the one the day hands you: waking, bathing, social, work, food, waiting, negative, sick, positive, evening. Every source is linked from its guide — the translations come from our Kashmir Shaiva corpus, the sangha hymns from the Lakshmanjoo Academy.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.1

Homage to the World-Formed

प्रपञ्चोत्तीर्णरूपाय नमस्ते विश्वमूर्तये । सदादिव्यप्रकाशाय स्वात्मनेऽनन्तशक्तये
prapañcottīrṇarūpāya namaste viśvamūrtaye | sadādivyaprakāśāya svātmane'nantaśaktaye
homage to the form that has crossed beyond the manifest — to the self of the world's form; to the ever-divinely-shining, to the self that is unending power.
1
The Guide

Homage to the World-Formed

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.1 · v.1
The first word of the day: bowing to what was here before you woke.
What is happening

Three appositions do the work: he has passed beyond the manifest yet IS the world's form; he always shines with divine light; he is your own self and consists of inexhaustible power. The logic is identity, not distance — you salute the world-form because you address what already shines.

How to use it

Sitting up before your feet touch the floor. Three clauses, three slow bows of the head. Feel the room as svātmane'nantaśaktaye — not out there but as your own body's brightness. Don't visualise a figure; visualise light that has nowhere to go. One round, then stand.

Dehastha v.1

The Body-Temple Offering

देहो देवालयः प्रोक्तः स्वात्मा देवः सनातनः । त्यजेदज्ञाननिर्माल्यं सोऽहं भावेन पूजयेत्
deho devālayaḥ proktaḥ svātmā devaḥ sanātanaḥ | tyajedajñānanirmālyaṃ so'haṃ bhāvena pūjayet
the body is declared god's temple, the own self the eternal god. casting off the sweepings of ignorance, let him worship with the feeling: i-am-he.
2
The Guide

The Body-Temple Offering

Dehastha v.1 · v.1
The morning rite in one verse: the body is the shrine, so'ham is the worship.
What is happening

Dehastha compresses a whole pūjā into four clauses. Temple, God, sweeping, worship: a temple needs cleaning before its God is honoured, and the 'sweepings of ignorance' are the ordinary assumption that the body is mere matter. The worshipper and the worshipped collapse — so'ham bhāvena, with the feeling I-am-He.

How to use it

Wake and stay horizontal. Sweep an imaginary hand from head to toe three times — that is casting off the sweepings. Then place attention at the heart and let so'haṃ, so'haṃ rest with the breath for twelve rounds. The feeling: warmth at the chest, not a thought — the body as lit. Then rise: the day is the pūjā.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.10

The Three States as His Rays

जाग्रतस्वप्नसुषुप्त्याद्या धारयंश्च निजाः कलाः । स्वेच्छया भासि नटवन् निष्कलोऽसि च तत्त्वतः
jāgratasvapnasuṣuptyādyā dhārayaṃśca nijāḥ kalāḥ | svecchayā bhāsi naṭavan niṣkalo'si ca tattvataḥ
waking, dreaming, deep sleep and the rest — you hold them as your own portions. By your will you shine like an actor, yet you are partless in truth.
3
The Guide

The Three States as His Rays

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.10 · v.10
The three states are costumes; the one who shines is partless.
What is happening

The verse splits experience from experiencer: the states are kalāḥ, portions or costumes — things held; the holding is by svecchā, your own will; and the actor is partless though he plays many parts. Play and partlessness are asserted in one breath, so no state can claim you.

How to use it

Recite on waking, naming each state as you come out of it: 'that was a costume.' The actor-idea is literal — you played sleep for seven hours. Feel the partlessness as the fact that nothing about you has edges. Keep it memorised: the fastest exit from a bad dream.

Dehastha v.6

The Play of the Dawning

उदयावभासचर्वणलीलां विश्वस्य या करोत्यनिशम् । आनन्दभैरवीं तां विमर्शरूपामहं वन्दे
udayāvabhāsacarvaṇalīlāṃ viśvasya yā karotyaniśam | ānandabhairavīṃ tāṃ vimarśarūpāmahaṃ vande
the play of the dawning, shining forth, and chewing over of the universe, which she performs unceasingly — bliss-bhairavī, her, whose form is reflection, i praise.
4
The Guide

The Play of the Dawning

Dehastha v.6 · v.6
The dawn is her play: she chews the universe into light, unceasingly.
What is happening

Bhairavī's līlā is udaya-avabhāsa-carvaṇa — the dawning, shining and chewing of the universe, performed without pause; her form is vimarśa, reflective awareness. The morning light is not physics but her appetite: she is already chewing the day into being when you open your eyes.

How to use it

At first light, before anything else: watch the room brighten and name it her chewing. One line. Feeling-tip: the day is being eaten into existence for you; you arrive mid-meal. Gratitude, not effort.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.14

What Bath for the Stainless?

निर्मलस्य कुतः स्नानं वस्त्रं विश्वोदरस्य च । निर्लेपस्य कुतो गन्धो रम्यस्याभरणं कुतः
nirmalasya kutaḥ snānaṃ vastraṃ viśvodarasya ca | nirlepasya kuto gandho ramyasyābharaṇaṃ kutaḥ
for the stainless, what bath? for the world-bellied, what garment? for the unanointed, what scent? for the lovely, what jewel?
5
The Guide

What Bath for the Stainless?

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.14 · v.14
Shower, towel, scent, jewel — all addressed to one who needs none.
What is happening

The litany names the bathroom itself: snāna (bath), vastra (garment), gandha (scent), bharaṇa (jewel) — each with a deity stripped of the lack it serves: stainless, world-bellied (all-containing), unanointed, lovely. What looks like futility is the precise applicability of each gift: you bathe the unblemished because bathing is what love does, not what dirt requires.

How to use it

In the shower: name each item as you use it — water for the stainless, towel for the world-bellied, scent for the unanointed. The mismatch is the smile. Feeling-tip: the water becomes an offering rather than a chore; three items, three small bows.

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 17.26

This Body for Worship

अन्तरुल्लसदच्छाच्छभक्तिपीयूषपोषितम् / भवत्पूजोपयोगाय शरीरम् इदम् अस्तु मे
antarullasadacchācchabhaktipīyūṣapoṣitam / bhavatpūjopayogāya śarīram idam astu me
may this body of mine — nourished by the clear, bright nectar of devotion rising within — be fit only for your worship.
6
The Guide

This Body for Worship

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 17.26 · 17.26
The body leaves the water consecrated: today's uniform is worship.
What is happening

The body is not renounced; it is consecrated. Nourished by the clear nectar of devotion rising in the heart (antar ullasad acchāccha-bhakti-pīyūṣa), it becomes fit for pūjā — and then it is the offering itself. This is the Trika answer to body-denial: the body is the Lord's instrument, and devotion makes it luminous.

How to use it

Dressed, before leaving the bathroom: say it once and feel the freshly washed body as anointed rather than merely clean. The day ahead is the pūjā; the body is the instrument. Feeling-tip: a slight uprightness in the spine — the instrument picking itself up.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.17

Waving-Lights for the Self-Shining

स्वयं प्रकाशमानस्य कुतो नीराजनं विभोः । वेदवाचामवेद्यस्य कुतः स्तोत्रं विधीयते
svayaṃ prakāśamānasya kuto nīrājanaṃ vibhoḥ | vedavācāmavedyasya kutaḥ stotraṃ vidhīyate
whence waving-lights for the self-shining lord? how is a hymn enjoined for him whom the veda cannot say?
7
The Guide

Waving-Lights for the Self-Shining

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.17 · v.17
The mirror, the lamp: he lights himself — so the light is for you.
What is happening

Two paradoxes: an offering of light presupposes darkness in the recipient; speech presupposes an object speech can reach. The Lord is self-lit and beyond the Veda's words. The rite's use falls on the offerer — you wave the lamp so your own eyes open; you sing because the singing lights you.

How to use it

At the bathroom mirror, hand raised with the light: you are the lamp-bearer, and the face that brightens is the audience. Hum, don't articulate — you have just honoured the Vedavācāmavedya. Feeling-tip: the chest brightens before the mirror does.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.19

Pardon for the Unseen

पूर्णस्य दक्षिणा कुत्र नित्यतृप्तस्य तर्पणम् । विसर्जनं व्यापकस्याऽप्रत्यक्षस्य क्षमापणम्
pūrṇasya dakṣiṇā kutra nityatṛptasya tarpaṇam | visarjanaṃ vyāpakasyā'pratyakṣasya kṣamāpaṇam
where is the fee for the full? libation for the ever-content? dismissal for the all-pervading? pardon for the unseen?
8
The Guide

Pardon for the Unseen

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.19 · v.19
The ritual closes with forgiveness — and no account to settle.
What is happening

The four closing rites: dakṣiṇā (fee), tarpaṇa (libation), visarjana (dismissal), kṣamāpaṇa (apology for faults) — all presuppose want, debt, departure and fault in one who is full, content, all-pervading and unseen. The kṣamāpaṇa for the apratyakṣa flips apology into recognition: you apologise to what was never absent.

How to use it

Use it at the end of the bath as the day's closing already: one bow, weight the last word — pardon. The feeling is the end of accounting: no tally of errors, no debt. Guilt arrives, is named as one of the litany's items, and dissolves with the god it expected to offend.

Bhairavānukaraṇa v.7

The Speech-Goddess

स्वात्मप्रभां विधाय वाग्देवीं विषयकुसुमकॢप्तार्चः । प्रथमोन्मेषं गुरुम् अथ वन्दित्वा हृदयम् आविश्य
svātmaprabhāṃ vidhāya vāgdevīṃ viṣayakusumakḷptārcaḥ | prathamonmeṣaṃ gurum atha vanditvā hṛdayam āviśya
having made the speech-goddess one's own light, with sense-flowers arranged as offering — bowing to the guru, first unfolding, then entering the heart.
9
The Guide

The Speech-Goddess

Bhairavānukaraṇa v.7 · bhair-p01 v.7
Before you speak: make vāk the light, not the weapon.
What is happening

The verse sequences the art of speech: svātmaprabhāṃ vidhāya vāgdevīṃ — make the speech-goddess your own light; arrange the sense-flowers as the offering; bow to the guru, the first unfolding (prathamonmeṣa); then enter the heart. Speech is not emitted from the mouth but lit from the heart and offered with the senses.

How to use it

Before a difficult conversation, a speech, a message you'll regret: one breath, hand on the throat. Make the speech a light — not a blade. Offering the sense-flowers means: don't weaponise what you've seen and heard. Then speak from the heart-entered place, not the throat.

Anubhavanivedana v.3

Every Sound Is Mantra

शब्दः कश्चन यो मुखादुदयते मन्त्रः स लोकोत्तरः संस्थानं सुखदुःखजन्मवपुषो यत्कापि मुद्रैव सा । प्राणस्य स्वरसेन यत्प्रवहणं योगः स एवाद्भुतः शाक्तं धाम परं ममानुभवतः किन्नाम न भ्राजते
śabdaḥ kaścana yo mukhādudayate mantraḥ sa lokottaraḥ saṃsthānaṃ sukhaduḥkhajanmavapuṣo yatkāpi mudraiva sā | prāṇasya svarasena yatpravahaṇaṃ yogaḥ sa evādbhutaḥ śāktaṃ dhāma paraṃ mamānubhavataḥ kinnāma na bhrājate
whatever sound arises from the mouth — that mantra is transcendent. whatever posture of the pleasure-pain-born body — that indeed is mudrā. whatever flowing of breath by its own rhythm — that indeed is wonderful yoga. the supreme śākta abode — while i experience, what indeed would not shine?

The Guide

Every Sound Is Mantra

Anubhavanivedana v.3 · anu-p03 v.3
Every word out of your mouth is already a mantra. Use that.
What is happening

Abhinavagupta's experience-report: any sound from the mouth is lokottara mantra — transcendent; any posture is mudrā; any breath-flow by its own rhythm is yoga. Nothing needs to be Sanskritic to be practice. The verse abolishes the distinction between talking and chanting, fidgeting and ritual, breathing and kriyā.

How to use it

Use it after you've said something — ordinary, even stupid. That was mantra. The recognition flips the whole day: the meeting, the small talk, the complaint — all of it is mantra, mudrā, yoga, shining. Feeling-tip: the mouth becomes a temple; be careful what you put on the altar.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.8

Enough of Difference-Talk

अलं भेदानुकथया त्वद्भक्तिरसचर्वणात् । सर्वमेकमिदं शान्तमिति वक्तुं च लज्जते
alaṃ bhedānukathayā tvadbhaktirasacarvaṇāt | sarvamekamidaṃ śāntamiti vaktuṃ ca lajjate
enough of difference-talk — chewing the sap of devotion to you: all this is one, at peace; and even to say so feels shy.
11
The Guide

Enough of Difference-Talk

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.8 · v.8
Knowing when to stop talking: the oneness embarrasses speech.
What is happening

Bheda-anukathā, the narration of difference, is abandoned mid-chewing of bhakti-rasa: all is one, at peace. And even to say so feels shy (lajjate) — the verse guards non-duality from becoming a thesis. The oneness is so ordinary that stating it out loud is like announcing a family is at peace: true, and slightly vulgar.

How to use it

Use it when an argument about views has you mid-sentence — or when you're about to explain your practice to someone who doesn't need it. Stop. The shyness is the right register. Feeling-tip: looking away slightly, a small heat in the face — let the body do the disarming.

Abhinavagupta, Pañcaślokī 3

When Your Words Are Not Heard

ब्रूषे नोत्तमाङ्ग पश्यसि न मामेतादृशं दुःखितं विज्ञप्तिं बहुधा कृतां न शृणुषे नायासि मन्मानसे । संसारार्णवगर्तमध्यपतितं प्रायेण नालम्बसे वाक्चक्षुःश्रवणाङ्घ्रिणादिरहितं त्वामाह सत्यं श्रुतिः
brūṣe nottamāṅga paśyasi na māmetādṛśaṃ duḥkhitaṃ vijñaptiṃ bahudhā kṛtāṃ na śṛṇuṣe nāyāsi manmānase | saṃsārārṇavagartamadhyapatitaṃ prāyeṇa nālambase vākcakṣuḥśravaṇāṅghriṇādirahitaṃ tvāmāha satyaṃ śrutiḥ
you speak not, o best-limbed one; you look not upon me, wretched as i am. my entreaty, made in many ways, you hear not; you come not into my mind. fallen amid the pit of the ocean of transmigration, i am mostly left unsupported — yet scripture truly declares you devoid of speech, sight, hearing, feet and the like!
12
The Guide

When Your Words Are Not Heard

Abhinavagupta, Pañcaślokī 3 · pan-p03
The complaint: you don't speak, look or hear — and scripture says you can't. Ouch.
What is happening

Pañcaślokī 3 is the reproach: the devotee accuses Śiva of silence — no speech, no glance, no listening, no arrival in the mind — then undercuts himself with scripture's own claim that the Lord is devoid of speech, sight, hearing and feet. The complaint and the theology cancel: you're angry at a god who, by definition, never had a mouth to answer with.

How to use it

Use it when you've spoken and nobody heard — human or divine. Say the whole complaint with feeling; the scripture-clause at the end is the punchline that dissolves it. Feeling-tip: the irritation turns to laughter mid-verse; that laugh is the prasāda.

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.20

Yoke the Mind

एवमेव परा पूजा सर्वावस्थासु सर्वदा । ऐक्यबुद्धया तु सर्वेशे मनो देवे नियोजयेत्
evameva parā pūjā sarvāvasthāsu sarvadā | aikyabuddhayā tu sarveśe mano deve niyojayet
just so is the highest worship — in every state, at all times: with a mind of oneness, one should yoke the mind to god as the lord of all.
13
The Guide

Yoke the Mind

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.20 · v.20
The instruction after the litany: work is the highest pūjā.
What is happening

Parā pūjā is ritual re-specified: all states, all times instead of the fixed occasion, and aikyabuddhi — oneness-mindedness — instead of the duality of giver and gift. The verb is niyojayet, yoke: the mind is a draught-animal that must be hitched, and the yoke is identity. The desk becomes the temple by the hitching, not the décor.

How to use it

Before opening the laptop: one line, then begin. The test for aikyabuddhi: the boundary of your skin thins. If it sharpens, you're thinking oneness, not yoking it — drop to the breath and restart. Each task is one yoke; re-hitch as often as needed.

Achieve God in Activity

Utpaladeva, Śivastotrāvalī 1.14 · 1.14
Lakshmanjoo's work verse: find God in the activity, not by shunning it.
