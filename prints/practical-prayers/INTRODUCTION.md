# INTRODUCTION — Why These Prayers

*A living compilation from Swami Lakshmanjoo's teachings (Lakshmanjoo Academy / Universal

## 1. For daily recitation — not for research

Swami Lakshmanjoo began compiling his Wisdom verses in the summer of 1987, distributing
one or two ślokas to his devotees each Sunday. Eighty-five verses in all. Of them he said:

> **"These verses are for your daily recitation. Maybe sometime you will experience these
> stages."**

And when asked about their source:

> **"It is not for research, it is for your daily recitation … it will be helpful to you in
> your spiritual practice."**

*(Lakshmanjoo Academy, "The Wisdom of Kashmir Shaivism",1987–88.)*

**Note:** the recitation appendix of that book contains six things: the Wisdom Verses
themselves, the **Krama Stotra**, the **Anuttarāṣṭikā**, the **Bhairava Stotra**, the
**Dehasthadevatācakra Stotra**, and the **Sixteen Amṛtas**. Four of the six are already in
this book — the corpus we build from is Swamiji's own recitation list. *Still to source:
the Eighty-Nine Wisdom Verses (the complete recitation list) and the Sixteen Amṛtas.*

## 2. The hymn he died with

The Bhairavastava (here: *Refuge in Bhairava*) was composed by Abhinavagupta in992–993 CE
on Kṛṣṇa Daśamī of Pauṣa — the Padyanavakam, the "Nine Verses". Tradition holds that when
Abhinavagupta left this world he walked into the Bhairavaguhā with twelve hundred disciples,
entering alone **while singing this hymn**. The Lakshmanjoo Academy: Swamiji revered these
verses above all, asked his devotees to recite the Bhairava Stotra regularly, and it was
always recited **at the end of any major spiritual function** at his ashram in Kashmir.

## 3. The mealtime practice

At mealtime, water in the palms, two verses are recited (see Food, this book):
**Gītā 4.24** (brahmārpaṇaṁ) and the closing verse of **Somananda's Śivadṛṣṭi** (7.103) —
Śiva bows to Śiva, and the obstacle is Śiva too. Swamiji's gloss on the Gītā: read *brahma*
throughout as **Lord Śiva** — what you take is offered to Śiva, what you offer is itself
Śiva, the stomach is Śiva's residence, and the act of offering, the offerer and the offering
are Śiva's play. *(Lore transcript,2026-11-01, after Lakshmanjoo teaching.)*

## 4. Real japa is the breath itself

From the Vijñāna Bhairava (vv.155–156), as taught by Swamiji:

> **"Japa is automatic recitation of breath. It is available to everybody,24 hours a day."**

Out goes *sa*, in comes *ha* — haṁsa, so'ham — twenty-one thousand six hundred times a day,
already happening. But, says Swamiji, that alone accomplishes nothing; **real japa** shortens
the breath: classify one breath into sixteen *tuṭis*, then recite giving it "more time and
less space" — fifteen tuṭis, then fourteen, thirteen… "And afterwards? You will be born anew,
you will get some substantial progress. That is real japa." *(Manual for Self Realization,
VBT155–156 talk.)*

**Sequencing note (Practice page, Academy):** beginners should start with **cakrodaya**
(long breaths *with* sound) before attempting **ajapa-gāyatrī** (silent, soundless) — "if
in the beginning you attempt ajapa-gāyatrī you will fail and subsequently fall."

## 5. When to worship: where day and night meet

Abhinavagupta, Tantrāloka (as Swamiji taught it):

> **"Do not worship the Lord during the day, do not worship the Lord during the night; the
> Lord must be worshipped at the point of the meeting of day and night."**

Dawn — when the Goddess of dawn meets day — and dusk, when the sun "seems to kiss and sink
into the horizon." Swamiji: **"I swear by Absolute Reality that if you practice meditation
in this manner you will never fail."** This is why the book opens with Waking and closes
with Evening: the two junctions are the two seats.

## 6. The Aghora Mantra — the Sangha's opening chant

In the summer of1989, after a direct vision of **Svacchanda Bhairava**, Swamiji wrote to
John Hughes about the Aghora Mantra and ended the letter with a directive:

> **"You should spread this message all over the world."**

The mantra (`aghorebhyo 'tha ghorebhyo…`, Yajurveda) is chanted **at the beginning of every
Weekly Sangha** to this day. What it invokes, per Swamiji's Tantrāloka3.257–259 teaching:
the three energies of Lord Śiva —

- **Aghorā** — carries you to God-consciousness at once, "even without your wish" (cit-śakti);
- **Ghorā** — if you desire entry, makes you wait: "Don't hurry, just wait" (icchā-śakti);
- **Ghoratarī** — when you desire the blissful state, carries you down instead (jñāna-śakti).

**Protection:** Svacchandanātha's eighteen arms are the eighteen tattvas, outgrowths of the
five great śaktis (cit→manas; ānanda→buddhi+ahaṃkāra; icchā→five vāyus; jñāna→five
jñānendriyas; kriyā→five karmendriyas) — created **for the protection of the individual**,
but protection requires divine discipline: the yamas and niyamas. The final and supreme
niyama is **Īśvara-praṇidhāna** — love and devotion to God: "If you love Lord Śiva and are
devoted to Him, it is not possible for Him to neglect you." *(Self Realization in Kashmir
Shaivism, ch.4; Secret Supreme ch.13; letter1989.)*

## 7. The Bahurūpa-Garbha Stotra — the hymn that carries the mantra

An ancient hymn that expands the Aghora Mantra and elucidates Svacchanda Bhairava — shared
by the Academy for protection and healing "considering the recent events in the world."
The hymn itself states its effects (see the appendix spreads):

- **When:** at the beginning and end of every yajña; in daily ritual (nitya), occasional
  rites (naimitika), desired rites (kāmya); for oneself or for others.
- **How:** recitation of the hymn **or** japa of the Aghora mantra. One hundred recitations
  purify even great sinners; fifty destroy inadvertent sins; twenty-five, minor sins; **a
  single recitation** removes wrongs done by body, speech or mind — whether through
  inattention or knowingly.
- **Why / effect:** "There is no mantra greater than this stotra, no hymn superior to it" —
  the queen of all samaya-vidyā (knowledge of the precepts); it pacifies all sins, removes
  all sorrows, grants bhoga and mokṣa, bestows all accomplishments and all good fortune.
- **Viniyoga:** Ṛṣi Vāmadeva; meter Anuṣṭupa; devatā Śrī Bahurūpabhaṭṭāraka (Svacchanda
  Bhairava, Aghoranātha); purpose — removal of sins committed by speech, mind and body, and
  attainment of the fourfold goals (dharma, artha, kāma, mokṣa).
*(Academy recitation & meaning PDFs, © Hughes Family Trust — paraphrased here; Sanskrit
text ancient.)*

## 8. The prerequisites (Practice page, Academy)

Before any of it: an absolutely clean mind with **sameness for everyone** — no over- or
under-expressed love, no animosity; without this "your efforts to achieve the Truth … will
be totally wasted, like carrying water in a wicker basket to nowhere." Then the seat: sit
**"absolutely motionless like a rock"**; concentration on the **guru-shabda**, the resonance
of unlimited I-consciousness found in the junction (sandhi). Finish the day's routine, get
full sleep, free the mind of preoccupations — then meditate without deviation.

## 10. The reality of Svacchanda Nātha (Aghora part 2, Academy blog)

Two verses with Swamiji's own commentary (Secret Supreme ch.13 material):

> **viśvaikarūpaviśvātma viśvasargādi-kāraṇam |
> paraprakāśa-vapuṣaṁ stumaḥ svacchanda-bhairavam || 35 ||**
> "We bow to Svacchandanātha of the five heads — who is one with the whole universe
> (viśvaika-rūpa), the embodiment of the universe (viśvātma), the cause of its creation,
> protection, destruction, concealing and revealing with his five acts — and who is
> **para-prakāśa-vapuṣa, the embodiment of supreme light**, the light beyond the three
> lights of fire, moon and sun."

> **madhya-prāṇa-niviṣṭa-haṁsa... | tān mantrātmatayā vilomayati yaḥ svacchanda-nāthaḥ
> paraḥ | devo 'sau vidadhātu bhairava-vapuḥ tejaḥ paraṁ śāśvatam || 36 ||**
> "In the central vein of breath lives the supreme haṁsa, in the wells of the hair-pores —
> three and a half crore pores (35 million), openings of breath from the madhyā nādi in
> suṣumna. May that Svacchandanātha reverse them into mantra-form, and may that God
> bestow the supreme, immortal light (tejaḥ param śāśvatam) of Bhairava's body."

**Swamiji's key lines (part 2 talk):**
- **"Whatever is residing in the universe, that you'll find in your own body. This is the
  mystery, this is the secret..."** — asked how the35 million vijñānākalins connect to the
 35 million prāṇas.
- "Even in one tiny insect there are thirty-six elements... he has the capacity of residing
  in one hundred and eighteen worlds" (ekaikatrāpi tattve'pi ṣaṭtriṁśat-tattva-rūpatā).
- The pure creation (māyā → Sadāśiva) **only rises** — "there is no fear of decreasing; it
  has the tendency to rise." The impure scale (earth → māyā) rises AND falls. This is why
  the protection works upward.
- "It is very easy for Him to do if he so wills, wishes." (on bestowing the immortal light.)
