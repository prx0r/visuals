# Rasa Inexternalism v0.2 — Corrected Thesis After Complete Audit

## Statement

**Consciousness may be intrinsically self-affective manifestation rather than affectively neutral presence. A phenomenal subject is the intrinsic, self-affective manifestation of one objectively individuated process. Its field possesses relational structure that may admit mathematical representation. Local valence is not identical with bare manifestation or basal self-affection — it is modulated by field organization, regulation, expectation, embodiment, history, and context.**

This is not a proof. It is the strongest hypothesis surviving critical examination of four frameworks (Tantrāloka, Tymoczko, Doczi, QRI) and a complete audit. The audit removed: golden-ratio identity, naive symmetry theory, the five-bliss/log-valence mapping, goddess-color proportions, and the claim that contemplative texts supply experimental confirmation.

**This is Rasa Inexternalism — not a symmetry or golden-ratio theory of consciousness.**

---

## The Argument

### Premise 1 — Textual: Abhinavagupta characterizes consciousness as intrinsically self-relishing.

**Source:** Abhinavagupta's Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa (PTv p. 19), cited in the Tantrāloka commentary.

**Validated claim:** "Supreme consciousness itself, the supreme Bhairava, which is perfectly full, because it is always by its very nature essentially the aesthetic relish of consciousness which is independent of all things and most free."

This is not a claim that consciousness is merely pleasant. It is a claim that consciousness has an intrinsic self-affective character — a "taste" of itself that is not added from outside but constitutive of what it is to be conscious.

**Supporting passages:**
- Ahaṅcamatkāra (TĀ Vol 10:23762): the aesthetic rapture of "I am" — selfhood IS wonder
- Camatkāra = my-ness (PTv p. 86-87): the "mine" of experience IS its wonder
- The universe is condensed rasa (TĀ Vol 5:4146): physical reality is consciousness's aesthetic juice thickened into form
- The senses are ciccamatkāra (MVV via TĀ Vol 3): each sensory modality is a differentiated form of wonder

**Qualification:** These claims are from the Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa and the Mālinīvijayavārtika, not from the Tantrāloka root verses themselves. They are philosophically authoritative but textually secondary. The strongest direct TĀ verse is the "universe is condensed rasa" passage.

---

### Premise 2 — Music-theoretic: Tymoczko demonstrates that overdetermined constraint satisfaction produces the structures we experience as beautiful.

**Source:** Tymoczko, *A Geometry of Music* (2011).

**Validated claim:** The five features of tonality (conjunct melodic motion, acoustic consonance, harmonic consistency, limited macroharmony, centricity) are overdetermined — there are only a few ways to combine them, and those ways converge on the same familiar structures (consonant triads, diatonic scales, standard progressions).

**Why this matters for rasa:** Overdetermination is the formal mechanism underlying the experience of aesthetic significance. When multiple independent constraints converge on a unique configuration, that configuration feels "right," "inevitable," or "beautiful." The rasa hypothesis proposes that this convergence IS what we experience as aesthetic quality — not a separate judgment added to the perception.

**Supporting evidence:**
- Chord spaces are quotient spaces with specific topology (Möbius strip for dyads, twisted torus for triads)
- Voice-leading geodesics correspond to the most common harmonic progressions
- The same efficient voice-leading principle operates at two temporal levels (chord-to-chord and scale-to-scale)
- These structures recur across historically and stylistically separated musical traditions (the "extended common practice")

**Direct Tantrāloka convergence:** The vīṇa passage (TĀ Vol 1 ~28356) describes a musician who is "at one" (tanmaya) with the music, experiencing the beauty of the notes while remaining in nondual awareness. This is the phenomenological state corresponding to Tymoczko's geometry: the structure of the auditory stimulus IS the structure of the experience.

---

### Premise 3 — Cross-modal: Doczi shows constraint convergence across domains (demoted from evidential pillar to illustration).

**Source:** Doczi, *The Power of Limits* (1985).

**Audit finding:** The golden-ratio component is the weakest part of the previous thesis. Reviews report longstanding disagreement over golden-section preference, and recent work warns that many aesthetic claims are unsupported. (PMC 4495923) The recurrence of φ can result from packing efficiency, recursive growth, measurement selection, cultural borrowing, retrospective fitting, or publication bias.

**Corrected status:** Doczi remains useful for visual design intuition and examples of constraint-generated form, but φ and Prox(𝒢ᵢ, φ) are REMOVED from the central argument. The golden-ratio content in the thesis should be treated as illustrative, not evidential.

---

### Premise 4 — Formal: QRI provides the mathematical language for expressing the rasa identity.

**Source:** QRI research programme (Johnson, Gómez-Emilsson, 2016-present).

**Validated claims:**
- Qualia Formalism: every experience has an exact mathematical representation
- Valence Realism: felt quality is a real, ordered property of conscious states
- The boundary problem is a distinct sub-problem of consciousness
- The slicing problem constrains theories of consciousness
- Valence distributions appear heavy-tailed (logarithmic)

**Why this matters for rasa:** QRI provides the formal language that the Tantrāloka lacks. If the Tantrāloka says consciousness IS aesthetic relish, QRI says this relish has a mathematical structure that can be studied. The two claims are complementary: the Tantrāloka supplies the identity; QRI supplies the method.

**Supporting convergence:**
- The tyranny of the intentional object (QRI) = camatkāra is not in the object but in consciousness (Tantrāloka)
- Recipe/review (QRI) = Σᵢ/Dᵢ distinction (inexternalism)
- Topological pockets (QRI) = Λₚ candidate analogous to dehabhedāt (Tantrāloka)
- Logarithmic valence (QRI) = the five blisses gradient (Tantrāloka)

**Qualification:** QRI's STV (symmetry → valence) is a hypothesis, not a confirmed theory. The topological pocket proposal is speculative. QRI assumes panpsychism rather than deriving consciousness. The formal language is promising but unproven.

---

### Premise 5 — Phenomenological: The Vijñānabhairava provides experimental protocols for testing that manifestness survives content depletion.

**Source:** Vijñānabhairava (c. 7th-8th century CE), 112 contemplative methods.

**Validated claim:** Several of the Vijñānabhairava's methods target each variable in the phenomenal tuple (Mᵢ, ∂ᵢ, Uᵢ, τᵢ, 𝒢ᵢ, νᵢ) and demonstrate that manifest presence (Mᵢ) can persist when any single content variable is attenuated or removed.

**Why this matters for rasa:** If manifestness is not identical with any content variable, then the rasa identity (consciousness = aesthetic relish) is not a claim about content but about the nature of manifestness itself. The "relish" is the quality of presence, not a quality of what is present.

**Convergence:**
- Breath methods → τᵢ + SMᵢ^body (Tymoczko's temporal integration)
- Sound methods → 𝒢ᵢ^auditory (Tymoczko's chord space geometry)
- Joy methods → νᵢ (QRI's valence structuralism)
- Void methods → ∂Sᵢ (QRI's topological pocket boundary)
- Thought gap methods → Qᵢ + SMᵢ (Doczi's dinergic complementarity)

## The Synthesis

### What each framework contributes

| Framework | Established | Contributes to thesis | Limitation |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|------------|
| Tantrāloka | Consciousness IS aesthetic relish (PTv/TĀ) | The identity claim (C₂) | No formal mechanism |
| Tymoczko | Overdetermination constrains musical structure | Formal mechanism (C₃/C₄ candidate) | Describes 𝒮 (stimulus space), not 𝒫 (phenomenal space) |
| Doczi | φ appears cross-culturally and cross-modally | Cross-modal overdetermination evidence | φ preference is weak and contested |
| QRI | Qualia Formalism + Valence Realism | Formal language + testable hypotheses | STV unconfirmed; field topology speculative |
| Vijñānabhairava | Mᵢ survives depletion of every content variable | Experimental protocol | No formalization |

### The logical chain

**Step 1:** The Tantrāloka provides the phenomenological identification: consciousness IS self-affective presence (camatkāra/rasa). This is a claim about the intrinsic nature of manifestation, not about the hedonic quality of specific contents.

**Step 2:** Tymoczko provides the formal mechanism: overdetermined constraint satisfaction produces the specific structures that are experienced as beautiful. Multiple independent constraints converge on the same configurations, and this convergence is what we feel as aesthetic significance.

**Step 3:** Doczi provides the cross-modal evidence: the same convergent proportions (especially φ) appear across visual, auditory, and biological domains, suggesting the constraint architecture is not domain-specific but universal across perception.

**Step 4:** QRI provides the formal language: Qualia Formalism says every experience has a mathematical structure; Valence Realism says felt quality is a real property of that structure. The rasa hypothesis is QRI's claim made specific: the geometric structure IS the felt quality.

**Step 5:** The Vijñānabhairava provides the experimental protocol: each claim can be tested by perturbing specific variables and observing what survives.

## The Claims, Sorted by Status

### Established

1. The Tantrāloka (via PTv and TĀ) characterizes consciousness as intrinsically self-relishing (camatkāra/rasa). The universe is "condensed rasa." The senses are "differentiated wonder." Selfhood is "the aesthetic rapture of 'I am'."

2. Tymoczko's overdetermination principle is established: when multiple features of tonality are combined, they converge on a small set of structures. These structures recur across widely separated musical styles.

3. Doczi's cross-cultural recurrence of φ-like proportions is established: the same ratios appear in plants, animals, human artifacts, and architecture across cultures.

4. QRI's Qualia Formalism and Valence Realism are coherent axioms that generate testable hypotheses. The slicing problem and boundary problem are genuine constraints on theories of consciousness.

5. The Vijñānabhairava provides experimental evidence that manifest presence can survive the depletion of any single content variable.

### Plausible but Unproven

1. The rasa identity: consciousness = aesthetic relish. The text supports this, but it is a metaphysical identity claim, not a derivation.

2. Overdetermination = the mechanism of aesthetic significance. The correlation between constraint convergence and felt quality is plausible but the causal direction is not established.

3. φ recurrence = evidence for a universal constraint architecture. The cross-modal data is consistent with this but also consistent with simpler explanations (shared human physiology, cultural diffusion).

4. Qualia Formalism: that every experience has an exact mathematical representation. This is a foundational assumption, not an established result.

5. The vīṇa passage as a model of aesthetic absorption: the musician "at one" with the music experiences nonduality. This is a single passage, not a replicated finding.

### Not Supported

1. **Pleasure = symmetry (STV).** The correlation is weak; the identity is unproven. The golden section is asymmetric (0.618:1), which contradicts naive STV.

2. **Topological pockets are the physical implementation of phenomenal boundaries.** This is a speculative hypothesis with no direct empirical support.

3. **One universal consciousness underlies all fields.** The Tantrāloka asserts this, but none of the other three frameworks support it. Tymoczko and Doczi are compatible with many ontologies; QRI is explicitly pluralist.

4. **The three-register model of camatkāra (cognition + bliss + wonder).** This is a modern reconstruction, not a canonical definition.

## The Testable Predictions

### Prediction 1: Overdetermination → Felt Quality

Stimuli with higher overdetermination (satisfying more independent constraints simultaneously) will be rated higher on measures of beauty, significance, and aesthetic satisfaction, controlling for familiarity, complexity, and absolute valence.

**Test:** Generate musical passages that satisfy 1, 2, 3, 4, or 5 of Tymoczko's features. Rate each on beauty.

### Prediction 2: Voice-Leading Distance → Phenomenal Similarity

Two chord progressions with small voice-leading distance will be rated as more similar than progressions with large voice-leading distance, controlling for shared pitch content. Distance in 𝒮 predicts distance in 𝒫.

**Test:** Pairwise similarity ratings of chord progressions with controlled voice-leading distance.

### Prediction 3: φ Proximity → Beauty Preference (Context-Dependent)

Visual and auditory stimuli with φ proportion will be preferred over nearby ratios, but the effect will be small and modulated by context. Approximate φ will be preferred over exact φ in organic/natural contexts.

**Test:** Two-alternative forced choice with φ and nearby ratios, varied by stimulus domain (natural vs. geometric).

### Prediction 4: Sensory Convergence → Cross-Modal Transfer

Exposure to φ in one modality (e.g., visual rectangles) will increase preference for φ in another modality (e.g., auditory intervals), even without explicit identification of the ratio.

**Test:** Cross-modal priming paradigm with φ vs. non-φ stimuli.

### Prediction 5: Aesthetic Absorption → ∂Sᵢ Boundary Relaxation

During aesthetic absorption (tanmaya — being "at one" with music, dance, or visual art), self-boundary measures (self-report, interoceptive accuracy, peripersonal space) will show temporary relaxation. The magnitude of relaxation will correlate with reported aesthetic intensity.

**Test:** Measure peripersonal space boundaries before and after absorbed music listening.

### Prediction 6: Mᵢ Content-Independence

Manifest presence will be rated as present even when specific content variables (τᵢ, 𝒢ᵢ, νᵢ, ∂Sᵢ) are independently manipulated. Different methods of content depletion (Vijñānabhairava-style) will converge on the same preserved Mᵢ.

**Test:** Systematic replication of Vijñānabhairava methods with pre/post measures of Mᵢ intensity and content variables.

## What the Thesis Does Not Claim

1. The thesis does not claim that consciousness IS pleasure. It claims that consciousness IS self-affection (ρᵢ), and that local valence (νᵢ) is a modulation of this basal self-affection by geometric and regulatory factors. Pain is real — it is the contracted form of basal self-affection, not its absence.

2. The thesis does not claim that geometry explains consciousness. It claims that geometry structures consciousness. The existence of experience (the hard problem) is not solved; only its character is characterized.

3. The thesis does not claim that the four frameworks prove each other. Each framework is independently incomplete. Their convergence is evidence for a shared underlying structure, not for any single framework's correctness.

4. The thesis does not claim that the Tantrāloka's universal Śiva is validated. The evidence supports a self-affective conception of manifest presence (C₂) and a geometric account of its modulation (C₃/C₄ candidate). It does not support numerical identity across subjects (C₁). The partition problem (Λₚ) remains open.

5. The thesis does not claim that the vīṇa passage, the dance passage, or the PTv quotes are experimentally confirmed. They are textual evidence for a philosophical claim. The claim is testable; it has not been tested.
