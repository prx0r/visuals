# LOGICVID SESSION — Branch AD: śānta = the felt of the inward direction — unifying rasa with the master-key and the Solms/valence bridge

*The pay-off. The rasa-derivation (session: rasa-śānta) showed śānta = the camatkāra without a
screen, the common ground of all rasas. The master-key (session: masterkey-direction) showed
bondage/liberation = the direction of self-apprehension. The felt-session (Branch X) showed
the direction is felt. The stirring question now: is śānta the *felt of the inward
direction*, and are the other eight rasas the felt of the various outward turnings? If so,
this unifies the ENTIRE system — the rasa-derivation IS the direction-of-self-apprehension IS
the valence. Method: derive, force the answer, check against the text.*

---

## ROUND 0 — the two threads we hold

**Thread 1 (rasa):** camatkāra = the primary characteristic of consciousness = the act of
appearance = the self-relishing of consciousness. The rasas are the one camatkāra under
various content-relations. **Śānta = the camatkāra without a screen** (the content-free
self-relishing, "void of outer objectivity, equated with blissful deep sleep").

**Thread 2 (master-key + felt):** bondage/liberation = the direction of the one
self-apprehension — outward into the object/ego (contracted powers) vs. inward into itself
(the pure aham). The direction is FELT: outward = lack/separation/compulsion; inward = wonder/
bliss (svānubhūtimātra, "the dense wonder of the supreme bliss").

---

## ROUND 1 — the synthesis: is śānta the felt of the INWARD direction?

**The question:** Śānta = the content-free camatkāra. The inward self-apprehension = the
direction into the pure aham, free of the object. Are these the same?

**The derivation:**
- The inward direction is self-apprehension turned into itself — free of the object.
- Śānta is the camatkāra without a screen — free of the object.
- Both are the self-relishing of consciousness **freed from the object/ego**.
- Therefore **śānta IS the felt of the inward direction.**

And the text confirms the register: śānta is equated with blissful deep sleep (suṣupti), and
the text says the inward aham "abides alone as the one, unique and pure self-awareness" (Q7).
Both are the content-free, object-free state of consciousness resting in itself.

> **PENETRATION 1:** Śānta = the felt of the inward direction. The inward self-apprehension
> (free of the object) and the content-free camatkāra (free of the screen) are the SAME state
> under two descriptions: the direction-thesis describes it structurally (inward turn); the
> rasa-thesis describes it phenomenally (peace, the unscreened wonder). **Peace is the felt
> of the self-apprehension turned inward.**

---

## ROUND 2 — the full map: the eight rasas as the felt of the outward turnings

**The question:** If śānta = the inward direction's felt, what are the other eight rasas?
They must be the felt of the various OUTWARD turnings.

**The derivation (using the master-key's structure):**
The master-key: the outward turn is the self-apprehension directed into the object/ego — the
contracted powers (āṇava=will-as-deficiency, māyīya=knowledge-as-duality, karma=action-as-
binding), each felt as lack/separation/compulsion. The eight emotional rasas map onto the
*degrees and modes* of the outward turn:

| Rasa | The outward turn it IS (the felt of) | Relation to the bliss (screen) |
|------|--------------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **Śṛṅgāra** (love) | the outward turn *seeking* its object (the will reaching out) | bliss-seeking through the other |
| **Vīra** (heroism) | the outward turn *asserting* against obstruction (action) | bliss asserted through overcoming |
| **Adbhuta** (wonder) | the outward turn *astonished* by the object (the shock of appearance) | closest to the ground — wonder at the appearance itself |
| **Karuṇa** (grief) | the outward turn *losing* its object (the will's lack made explicit) | bliss *screened by felt-loss* |
| **Raudra** (fury) | the outward turn *rejecting* its object | bliss obstructed by the ego's defense |
| **Bhayānaka** (fear) | the outward turn *threatened* | bliss obstructed by danger |
| **Bībhatsa** (disgust) | the outward turn *repelled* | bliss obstructed by revulsion |
| **Hāsya** (laughter) | the outward turn *collapsing* its object (the fixity dissolves) | bliss released by dissolution |

**The pattern:** the rasas are the felt of the self-apprehension *turned outward to varying
degrees and modes*. Adbhuta (wonder) is the closest to śānta (it is the wonder of the
appearance itself, barely screened); the emotional ones are progressively more "screened"
(turned outward into the object's loss, danger, etc.). **The outward turn = the screen; the
screen = the content-relation that makes the eight rasas what they are.**

> **PENETRATION 2:** The eight emotional rasas are the felt of the OUTWARD turnings of the
> self-apprehension (seeking, asserting, losing, rejecting, fearing). Each is the one
> camatkāra *screened* by its outward content-relation. Adbhuta (wonder) is the least screened
> (the astonishment at appearance itself); the emotional rasas are progressively more
> screened by loss/danger/revulsion. **The outward turn = the screen; śānta = the unscreened
> inward turn. The whole rasa-system IS the direction-of-self-apprehension, mapped onto its
> felt register.**

## ROUND 3 — the valence: the rasas as the felt of V, and śānta as its zero

**The question:** The felt-session (Branch X) showed the direction of self-apprehension =
the direction of valence (outward = lack/drive, inward = resolution/wonder). Do the rasas
map onto this?

**The derivation:**
- The outward turn (the eight emotional rasas, screened) = the drive = the *restless
  self-maintenance* = the "negative" side of valence (V decreasing / the need for resolution).
- The inward turn (śānta) = the resolution = the wonder = the "positive" side (V increasing /
  the drive released).

But note the subtlety from the text: **the eight are not all "negative"** — adbhuta (wonder)
and śṛṅgāra (love) are positively-savouring. The valence is not a simple positive/negative
split. Rather, the rasas are the felt of the self-apprehension's *movement* — its outward
reaching (which can be joyful in seeking) and its inward returning (śānta). The cleanest
formulation:

> **Śānta is the valence-ZERO of the rasa-system — the resting state of the self-apprehension
> turned fully inward, free of all outward drive. The eight rasas are the felt of the
> self-apprehension in its various outward movements (reaching, losing, asserting, fearing);
> each is a deviation from śānta by the degree and mode of its outward turn. The rasa-system
> is the felt landscape of the direction-of-self-apprehension, with śānta as its ground-zero.**

This is EXACTLY the Solms/valence structure: V = −dF/dt. The outward turnings (the emotional
rasas) are the drive (the self-apprehension seeking to resolve its outward tension); śānta is
the resolution (V = 0, the free-energy minimized, the rest). **The rasa-system IS the felt of
the valence, with śānta as its zero-point.**

> **PENETRATION 3 (the unifying gem):** The rasa-system IS the felt of the
> direction-of-self-apprehension = the felt of the valence. The eight emotional rasas are the
> felt of the outward turnings (the drive, the deviations from rest); śānta is the felt of the
> inward turn = the valence-zero = the resolution of all outward drive. **This unifies the
> Tantrāloka's rasa doctrine, the master-key (direction), and the Solms/valence thesis
> (V = −dF/dt) into one structure: the felt of the self-apprehension's direction, with peace
> as its ground-zero.** The "peace that underlies all rasas" is the felt of the self-
> apprehension at rest in itself.
