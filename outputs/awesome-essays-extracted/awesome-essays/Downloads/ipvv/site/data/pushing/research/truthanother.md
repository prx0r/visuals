The first deep pass produces a cleaner result than the earlier unified framework: the traditions do not merely offer different answers to one question. They disagree about **what requires explanation in the first place**. The source-neutral framework is useful, but its nodes must remain contestable rather than functioning as hidden Pratyabhijñā premises. Your uploaded draft already establishes the right methodological discipline: separate textual claims, reconstructions, empirical findings, correspondences and hypotheses. 

# Recognition Enquiry — Comparative Research Dossier 01

## Central question

> What, if anything, remains invariant through changing cognition, and what explanatory work does that invariant perform?

This question divides into six progressively stronger contentions:

1. Is conscious occurrence intrinsically self-manifest?
2. Does self-manifestness include first-personality or ownership?
3. Does ownership persist through temporally separated experiences?
4. Does unity through change require one enduring subject?
5. Does such a subject possess genuine freedom rather than complex causal flexibility?
6. Why identify this subject with one universal consciousness?

The chain must remain:

[
A\not\Rightarrow B\not\Rightarrow C\not\Rightarrow D\not\Rightarrow E\not\Rightarrow F.
]

No source is permitted to inherit a later conclusion merely by establishing an earlier one.

---

# 1. Utpaladeva — the most developed recognition argument

## Textual target

The *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā* and Utpaladeva’s own *Vṛtti* are the primary argument engine. The surviving digital text is revised against Torella’s critical edition, which reconstructed the complete *Vṛtti* from a unique Malayalam manuscript and documents Utpaladeva’s sustained engagement with Buddhist epistemology.

The opening already states the programme:

> Recognition is to be demonstrated because the Lord, though self-evident, is not firmly apprehended under the obscuration of Māyā.

Utpaladeva treats knowledge and action as the life of sentient beings and claims that one’s own cognition is immediately known, while another being’s cognition is inferred through embodied action.

This means that Pratyabhijñā begins with three linked problems:

* self-manifest cognition;
* agency;
* other minds.

It does not begin with the 36 tattvas.

## Argument A — non-inert manifestation

The decisive contrast is between:

* an inert object receiving a form;
* a cognition apprehending something.

Utpaladeva’s claim is not yet that cognition is infallible or universal. It is:

> A conscious occurrence cannot be reduced to the passive presence of differentiated structure.

### Strong result

[
\boxed{
\text{conscious presentation}
\neq
\text{inert registration}.
}
]

### Unresolved issue

A modern dynamical theory can agree completely and explain cognition through recursively integrated prediction, action and regulation. Utpaladeva must still show why active organization requires intrinsic manifestation rather than merely complex causal responsiveness.

---

## Argument B — memory and synthesis

The *Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi* explicitly introduces the Buddhist stream theorist who rejects one synthesizing subject because all entities are momentary. Utpaladeva’s response is organized around *anusaṃdhāna*: connection or synthesis across cognitions.

The genuine contention is:

[
\boxed{
\text{causal trace persistence}
\neq
\text{remembered first-personal ownership}.
}
]

The Buddhist can explain:

[
C_1\rightarrow\text{disposition}\rightarrow C_2.
]

Utpaladeva asks why (C_2) presents:

> “I experienced that,”

rather than merely reproducing information causally descended from (C_1).

### What survives

A theory of memory must explain at least three different things:

1. retention of content;
2. attribution of that content to an earlier occurrence;
3. attribution of that earlier occurrence to the present subject.

### What does not survive automatically

The phenomenology of remembered ownership does not prove numerical identity of an enduring metaphysical subject. A later system could reconstruct ownership from:

* bodily continuity;
* source monitoring;
* narrative self-modelling;
* causal traces;
* present attribution.

The strongest contemporary verdict is therefore:

[
\boxed{
\text{Utpaladeva identifies a real explanandum,}
}
]

but:

[
\boxed{
\text{his preferred owner is not yet uniquely required.}
}
]

---

## Argument C — freedom and form-assumption

This argument is independent of memory.

Consciousness can manifest:

* absent objects;
* impossible combinations;
* future possibilities;
* alternative courses of action;
* different contents without becoming identical with any one content.

Pratyabhijñā interprets this as *svātantrya*: consciousness’s freedom to take forms while remaining itself.

### Strong result

[
\boxed{
\text{cognition possesses productive and counterfactual capacity.}
}
]

### Countermodel

Predictive and generative systems can construct absent or impossible representations without a persisting metaphysical owner.

Therefore the real contention is:

> Is form-generation merely computational flexibility, or does genuine agency require first-person authorship?

Pratyabhijñā needs a criterion distinguishing:

[
\text{complex generative flexibility}
]

from:

[
\text{free self-manifest agency}.
]

It does not yet provide a scientifically discriminating one.

---

## Argument D — universalization

The *Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi* moves from one non-inert synthesizing knower toward the supreme knower. This is precisely where translation and argument mapping should concentrate.

The move appears to be:

1. cognition requires non-inert apprehension;
2. cross-temporal synthesis requires unity;
3. finite cognitions are forms assumed by that unity;
4. the unity is not exhausted by any finite form;
5. therefore the true knower is universal consciousness.

The first four claims do not entail the fifth without an additional premise.

A pluralist Nyāya realist can accept enduring subjects while denying universal identity. A phenomenologist can accept for-me-ness while individuating it by subject. A process theorist can explain continuity through dynamically maintained organization.

The missing premise is:

[
\boxed{
\text{Every locally manifest unity must be numerically one unity.}
}
]

That remains unproved.

---

# 2. Utpaladeva’s three short treatises

## Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi

### Main contribution

A compact confrontation with the view that a causal stream can perform all the explanatory work of a subject.

### Framework answers

* **Manifestation:** requires a non-inert knower.
* **Reflexivity:** cognition is not external to its own manifestation.
* **Continuity:** synthesis points beyond isolated moments.
* **Universalization:** asserted through the move from synthesizer to supreme subject.
* **Weak point:** local synthesis does not entail cosmic numerical identity.

### Translation priority

Highest.

Translate each verse with four annotations:

* opponent reconstructed;
* precise explanandum;
* suppressed premise;
* cheapest viable countermodel.

---

## Sambandhasiddhi

The text survives in Muktabodha’s electronic corpus as one member of the *Siddhitrayī*.

Its importance is architectural: **relation** is the hidden primitive in nearly every part of our framework.

Questions it must answer:

* Can subject and object differ without becoming independent substances?
* Is relation something added between finished relata?
* Can unity include internal difference?
* How can the finite subject be identical with the universal without erasing finitude?
* Is recognition identity, relation, or transformation of relation?

The research hypothesis is that Pratyabhijñā needs relation to be internally constituted by manifest consciousness rather than externally linking independent things.

### Translation priority

Second, because it may expose the exact machinery hidden by phrases such as:

> “the same consciousness under a different mode.”

---

## Īśvarasiddhi

This should be treated as the missing universalization dossier.

The critical question is:

> Does Utpaladeva independently establish Lordship, or does he rename the unified powers of cognition as the Lord?

The text should be interrogated for:

* transition from agency to divine agency;
* transition from local unity to universal unity;
* other minds;
* cosmological creation;
* whether explanatory unification is being presented as deduction.

### Translation priority

Third.

# 3. Somānanda — the doctrine before Utpaladeva’s refinement

Somānanda’s *Śivadṛṣṭi* is not merely a mystical precursor. Recent scholarship reconstructs it as a sustained argument against Dharmakīrti concerning perception, error, exclusion, convention, momentariness and overarching unity. Its ontology is described as hyperpersonalist, realist and monist.

This is important because it overturns the idea that Utpaladeva independently imported Buddhist problems into an otherwise scriptural system. Somānanda was already attempting to defeat:

* nonlinguistic perception;
* *apoha* as the basis of universals;
* momentariness without unity;
* Buddhist accounts of perceptual error.

## Framework result

Somānanda’s primitive appears to be less:

> minimal for-me-ness,

and more:

> a personal, active Śiva whose powers constitute both cognition and objects.

This means that universal consciousness is likely a prior metaphysical commitment in Somānanda, which Utpaladeva later attempts to make philosophically demonstrable.

## Research value

Compare:

* Somānanda’s explicit premises;
* Utpaladeva’s later inferential repairs.

This identifies which Pratyabhijñā arguments are genuine discoveries and which are retrospective justifications of inherited theology.

## Philological warning

The text and Utpaladeva’s partial commentary still lack a fully reliable complete edition and translation; Torella explicitly describes the textual state as unresolved.

Therefore the *Śivadṛṣṭi* is a high-value but high-risk translation target.

---

# 4. Nyāya and Udayana — the strongest non-idealist rival

Udayana’s *Ātmatattvaviveka* systematically defends an enduring plural self and Nyāya realism against Buddhist momentariness, no-self and idealist pressures.

This is not a minor rival. It directly blocks Pratyabhijñā’s universalization strategy.

## Nyāya answer

Memory, recognition, desire, effort and cognition belong to enduring individual selves.

Other minds are inferred from embodied behaviour.

Objects exist independently and cause cognitions.

No universal consciousness is required.

## Udayana’s attack on momentariness

Udayana argues that causal efficacy does not require instantaneous destruction. A condition may persist while awaiting cooperating conditions before producing an effect.

He also argues that momentariness destabilizes:

* causality;
* universals;
* identification;
* memory;
* error correction;
* determinate individuals.

## Framework comparison

### Shared with Pratyabhijñā

* rejects anonymous momentary streams;
* defends persistence;
* takes memory seriously;
* treats subjecthood as real.

### Rejects Pratyabhijñā

* subjects are many;
* objects are independently real;
* consciousness is a property of selves, not universal reality;
* relation does not require monistic identity.

## Decisive pressure

If Nyāya can explain:

* ownership;
* memory;
* action;
* recognition;
* other minds

with plural enduring subjects, Pratyabhijñā must show why one universal subject is explanatorily necessary.

At present:

[
\boxed{
\text{Nyāya pays the substance-self cost but avoids the universal-consciousness leap.}
}
]

The *Ātmatattvaviveka* is therefore the essential control text for the entire project.

---

# 5. Spanda — unity as dynamism

Dyczkowski’s *Stanzas on Vibration* provides the root text with four major commentaries: Kallaṭa, Rāmakaṇṭha, Bhagavadutpala and Kṣemarāja.

Spanda is not mechanical oscillation. It names Śiva’s dynamic conscious power through which manifestation, maintenance and withdrawal occur without literal spatial movement.

## Framework contribution

Spanda changes the invariant question.

Instead of asking:

> What static entity remains the same?

it asks:

> What dynamic pattern remains itself through expansion, contraction and transformation?

Possible answer:

[
\boxed{
\text{the invariant is not a substance behind change but the power of self-renewing transformation itself}.
}
]

## Does it avoid Pratyabhijñā’s subject?

Probably not in its mature commentarial form. Kṣemarāja interprets Spanda through the later nondual synthesis.

The useful test requires comparing the commentaries:

* Kallaṭa: earliest, potentially less systematized;
* Rāmakaṇṭha: philosophically substantial;
* Bhagavadutpala: broader synthesis;
* Kṣemarāja: mature Pratyabhijñā absorption.

## Exact enquiry

> Can dynamic continuity explain unity without positing one numerically persisting witness?

Spanda may offer a process-like vocabulary, but the existing tradition still identifies the pulse with universal Śiva-consciousness. It does not obviously eliminate the universal subject; it redescribes its mode of operation.

---

# 6. Krama — cognition as sequential self-consumption

The *Mahānayaprakāśa* is unusually promising because it analyzes cognition through temporality, desire, manifestation and the dissolution of subject–object duality. The publicly available text explicitly describes cognition as formed through the manifestation of time and the object as marked by the activity of desire; both dissolve into a unified condition in the cycle of manifestation.

## Framework contribution

Krama may answer the invariant question differently:

> The invariant is not an owner standing outside cognitive phases; it is the complete cycle through which manifestation arises, stabilizes, dissolves and returns to an unobjectifiable ground.

This could provide:

* temporal process without Buddhist atomism;
* nonduality without a static witness;
* unity as completion of a cycle;
* recognition as seeing each phase as one power.

## Critical uncertainty

The “fourth” or unnameable phase may simply reintroduce an invariant ground under another name.

The translation project must therefore ask:

1. Is the fourth a separate substrate?
2. Is it the transition structure common to all phases?
3. Is it the total cycle reflexively apprehending itself?
4. Does it require numerical identity across cognitions?
5. Is it local to a cognitive sequence or universally one?

This is likely the most valuable original research frontier after the Pratyabhijñā core texts.

---

# 7. Bhartṛhari — unity before sequence

The *Vākyapadīya* begins with beginningless, endless *śabda*-reality manifesting as objects and the world process. It describes one reality appearing differentiated through multiple powers while remaining inseparable from them.

Bhartṛhari’s theory of *pratibhā* treats sentence meaning as a unitary flash of understanding rather than the final mechanical sum of independently meaningful words.

## Framework contribution

Bhartṛhari attacks a different atomicity:

> How can sequential elements yield unitary meaning?

Possible answer:

* sequence belongs to expression;
* meaning is grasped holistically;
* parts become intelligible only within an already operative whole.

This directly bears on:

* episodic memory;
* recognition;
* synthesis;
* *vimarśa*;
* relation;
* whole–part dependence.

## Recognition parallel

Ordinary recognition may resemble sentence comprehension:

* components are already present;
* no wholly new content is added;
* an integrating relation suddenly discloses their unity.

The relevant invariant may be:

[
\boxed{
\text{unitary significance rather than a persisting subject-substance}.
}
]

## Danger

Bhartṛhari’s linguistic unity cannot simply be imported as universal consciousness. The project must test whether:

* unitary comprehension requires an ontological whole;
* or merely a higher-level cognitive integration process.

---

# 8. Buddhist positions — several rivals, not one

“Buddhism” cannot be represented as one anonymist theory.

## Dignāga/Dharmakīrti reflexivity

Cognition is reflexively manifest, but no enduring owner is required.

Possible structure:

[
\text{manifest cognitive moment}
+
\text{causal continuity}
\rightarrow
\text{memory and conceptual attribution}.
]

This accepts Claim A while rejecting B and C.

## Buddhist momentariness

No entity persists numerically through change. Continuity is causal and constructed.

Its strength is ontological economy.

Its weakness is explaining:

* remembered ownership;
* cross-temporal synthesis;
* stable reference;
* coordinated agency.

## Madhyamaka anti-reflexivism

Some Madhyamaka traditions reject intrinsic reflexive awareness, while contemporary analysis suggests that limited versions of self-awareness may still be defensible conventionally.

This pressures the very first step:

[
\text{manifest experience}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{intrinsic mineness}.
]

## Framework result

The most important Buddhist contribution is not “there is no self.”

It is the discipline:

> Do not reify an explanatory function into an independently existing subject.

Every proposed invariant must show that it is more than:

* a causal pattern;
* a conceptual attribution;
* a pragmatic designation;
* or a structure imputed over dependent processes.

---

# 9. Contemporary reflexivism — the closest live analogue

Zahavi and Kriegel defend *for-me-ness* as the first-personal manner in which experience occurs, not a separate object or reflective thought.

Zahavi’s recent formulations explicitly deny that experiential life is ownerless or anonymous: pain, jealousy or perception are immediately lived from a perspective rather than first appearing neutrally and later receiving ownership.

## What this supports

[
\boxed{
\text{minimal first-personality may be constitutive of experience}.
}
]

## What it does not support

* an enduring substance-self;
* numerical identity through all temporal changes;
* identity between subjects;
* universal consciousness.

## Live objection

The transcendental argument for universal mineness remains disputed; critics argue that the impossibility of a perceptual model of self-awareness does not establish robust mineness as a universal phenomenal feature.

Recent neurophenomenological work attempts to describe embodied mineness as a stable formal feature across changing experiential contents, but this remains a philosophical and empirical proposal rather than settled fact.

## Exact result

Zahavi gives Pratyabhijñā a modern ally only for:

[
A=\text{intrinsic first-personality}.
]

He provides no route to:

[
C=\text{one cosmic subject}.
]

---

# 10. Science — what it can genuinely test

## Multiscale agency

Science can investigate:

* how local controllers form larger controllers;
* how higher-level goals constrain components;
* how boundaries and action repertoires form;
* how organisms construct stable worlds.

It cannot infer phenomenal unity merely from control integration.

## Predictive processing

It can explain:

* perceptual stabilization;
* self-models;
* source attribution;
* counterfactual action;
* memory reconstruction.

This supplies strong countermodels to Utpaladeva’s claim that ownership requires one enduring subject.

It does not independently explain why the process is manifest.

## Information closure

Information Closure Theory proposes that consciousness belongs to coarse-grained processes forming non-trivial informational closure relative to their environment.

This is especially relevant to the boundary problem:

> Why is experience individuated at one scale rather than at the level of neurons, cells, organs or groups?

But informational closure remains a proposed criterion, not an established derivation of first-personality.

## Neurophenomenology

The scientifically tractable prediction is:

> If first-personality is constitutive rather than reconstructed anew with each content, it should remain identifiable across major changes in content and self-model.

Tests might compare:

* perception;
* emotion;
* thought;
* silence;
* dreaming;
* contemplative absorption;
* disturbances of ownership.

Even a positive result would establish only content-invariant local first-personality, not universal consciousness.

# 11. Comparative verdict

## What each tradition treats as invariant

### Buddhist causal-stream theory

Causal continuity; no numerically persistent owner.

### Nyāya

One enduring individual self underlying changing qualities and cognitions.

### Zahavian phenomenology

The first-personal manner of each subject’s experience.

### Bhartṛhari

Unitary meaning articulated through apparent sequence.

### Spanda

Dynamic self-renewing power through expansion and contraction.

### Krama

The complete cognitive cycle and its unobjectifiable consummation.

### Pratyabhijñā

One universal self-manifest agency freely assuming finite forms.

These are not interchangeable answers.

---

# 12. Current eliminations

We can provisionally reject:

## Passive-copy cognition

Perception and cognition are active, temporally extended processes.

## The autobiographical ego as origin

The narrative self is a constructed element within cognition.

## Trace persistence as a complete memory theory

It does not alone articulate remembered ownership.

## For-me-ness as proof of universal consciousness

Local first-personality does not entail cosmic numerical identity.

## Finite-agent constraints as evidence for idealism

Bounded knowledge, action, value, time and causality are compatible with every major ontology.

## Tattva taxonomy as the proof of Pratyabhijñā

The tattvas explain finite manifestation after the ontology is accepted; they do not establish universal consciousness.

## Decorative formalism

No operator or equation should be used unless it generates a checkable implication, excludes a countermodel or identifies a non-entailment.

# 13. The uppermost frontier

The debate now has three gates.

## Gate 1 — intrinsic first-personality

[
\boxed{
\text{Is experience intrinsically for someone?}
}
]

If no, Pratyabhijñā and Zahavian reflexivism fail near the beginning.

## Gate 2 — diachronic ownership

[
\boxed{
\text{Does temporally extended mineness require one persisting subject?}
}
]

If no, Utpaladeva’s memory argument fails.

## Gate 3 — universalization

[
\boxed{
\text{Why should multiple locally reflexive streams be modes of one subject?}
}
]

Neither memory, reflexivity, productive imagination nor finite-agent architecture currently proves this.

The strongest Pratyabhijñā route is abductive:

> One form-assuming universal consciousness offers a unified account of manifestation, determination, continuity, agency and apparent externality.

The strongest objection is equally precise:

> One explanation covering many phenomena is not evidence that numerically distinct subjects are actually one subject, especially when plural realism, process theories and construction models remain viable.

# Final checkpoint

What can currently be said with maximum confidence is:

> Conscious experience is not adequately modelled as passive reception by an atomic ego. Cognition is active, temporally extended and dependent upon nested bodily and environmental processes. Information continuity, experiential ownership, diachronic identity and universal consciousness are four distinct problems. Utpaladeva correctly forces causal-stream theories to explain ownership and synthesis, but his arguments do not yet uniquely require a persisting subject, and even a persisting subject would not entail one universal consciousness. Nyāya supplies the strongest pluralist realist alternative; Zahavian reflexivism supplies the strongest contemporary defence of local first-personality; Spanda, Krama and Bhartṛhari supply alternative accounts of unity through dynamism, sequence and meaning.

The single enquiry governing all future translations is:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{What explanatory work requires numerical identity,}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{and what can be explained by continuity, structure, relation or process alone?}
}
]

That is the point where Pratyabhijñā either becomes necessary—or loses its claim to necessity.

This first dossier narrows the project decisively: **translate the exact universalization transitions, not entire texts indiscriminately.**
