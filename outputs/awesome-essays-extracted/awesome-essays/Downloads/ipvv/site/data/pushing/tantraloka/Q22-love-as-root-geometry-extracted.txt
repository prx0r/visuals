In the course of his discussion of the sixth obstacle to the realisation of
rasƘ, viz., the lack of some predominant factor (apradhanata), he
observes: All these permanent mental states (when portrayed in a drama)
are predominantly pleasurable. For, the essence of compact light (prakasa)
consisting in the aesthetic relish of one’s own consciousness is
transcendental delight. To explain: in actual life women when they are
completely absorbed in the rumination of their consciousness characterised
or circumscribed by profound sorrow also find full rest or repose in their
own heart. For happiness consists in complete rest or full repose without
any obstacle. Pain, on the contrary, is nothing but absence of complete rest
or full repose. It is for this reason alone that the Samkhyas who explain
pain or sorrow as a property of rajas declare that restlessness is the very
essence of pain or sorrow. All the rasƘs thus consist essentially in
transcendental delight.12 But some of them, on account of the vibhƘvƘs,
and such like, by which they are coloured are affected by a certain touch
