# LOGICVID SESSION — the theodicy: why does impurity arise at all? can things ever be impure? why would Śiva choose suffering?

*The hardest question the whole project can ask — the problem of evil / theodicy of the
qualified monism. If reality is one consciousness, why does impurity (māla) arise at all? Why
is there suffering? Why would Śiva choose to contract into bondage? Method: force the answer
from the Tantrāloka's OWN reasoning — not our frameworks, not easy consolation. The text's
answer turns out to be surprisingly radical, and it dissolves the question rather than
answering it.*

---

## ROUND 0 — the material the Tantrāloka actually gives

1. **Duality = "the play occasioned by the fullness of one's own inherent bliss"** (Vol 1,
   9558–9560): "the oneness of the integral egoity and wonder of one's own nature with all
   those principles, generally considered to be binding, that consist of the duality made
   manifest by **the play occasioned by the fullness of one's own inherent bliss.**"
2. **The agent of emanation/withdrawal "came forth from the heart of Bhairava, engaged in
   play"** (Vol 1, 2044–2048): "the agent of emanation and withdrawal has come forth from the
   heart of the great god Bhairava, engaged in play."
3. **Śaiva nondualism embraces both duality and nonduality** (Vol 1, 9588–9592): "Śaiva
   nondualism is not based on the rejection of duality as is the case in Advaita Vedānta.
   Reality is one and nondual because **it embraces both duality and nonduality.**"
4. **The obscuration reveals the power** (Sanderson, glossing, 9583–9586): the cognition and
   action that "previously obscured the real identity of the individual now illustrate its
   all-pervading and unobscurable power. Liberation in life is just this perception of the
   dynamic unity of consciousness **in each of its self-obscurations.**"
5. **Śiva obscures and reveals Himself by His grace** (Vol 1, 2052–2055): "He alone obscures
   and reveals Himself by His grace."
6. **The play of the Self as the dancer** (Vol 1, 3476–3482): "the Self manifests itself... by
   penetrating into the sentiment (rasa) of each emotive state... The Self is said to be the
   dancer because it assumes every state of being."

---

## ROUND 1 — the first penetration: the question is WRONG as asked

**The question:** "Why does impurity arise at all? Why would Śiva choose suffering?"

**The first move (from the text):** The text does not answer this as asked — it DISSOLVES the
premise. The premise assumes that impurity/suffering is something that happens TO the one
reality, an addition or a defect. But the text says the opposite: **duality is "the play
occasioned by the fullness of one's own inherent bliss"** (9558). The duality is not a defect
that befalls the one; it is the OUTFLOW of the one's own fullness. It is not impurity added to
bliss; it is bliss overflowing into play.

So the question "why impurity?" presupposes that impurity is a fall, a mistake, an intrusion.
The text says: impurity (māla) is not the fall of the one into defect; it is the play of the
one's fullness. The question, as asked, is already wrong.

> **PENETRATION 1:** The question is dissolved, not answered. It presupposes that impurity is
> a defect that befalls the one reality. But the text says duality is "the play occasioned by
> the fullness of one's own inherent bliss" (9558) — not a fall into defect, but the OUTFLOW of
> fullness. **Impurity is not the one's collapse into lack; it is the one's fullness playing.**
> The premise of the question (that impurity is a defect) is false.

---

## ROUND 2 — the gem: "can things be impure?" — NO, and that's the point

**The question:** "Can things ever be impure?"

**The derivation (the radical answer):** The text's deepest claim is that **Śaiva nondualism
"embraces both duality and nonduality"** (9588–9592). This means: the impurity (duality) is
NOT a defect to be removed (that's Advaita's view, which rejects duality). It is embraced — it
is part of the one's play. So:

- In one sense, **nothing is ever really impure** — because the "impure" (the dual, the
  contracted) is still the one consciousness's own play, "the natural dynamism of the one light
  of consciousness" (9579). Sanderson: liberation is "the perception of the dynamic unity of
  consciousness **in each of its self-obscurations**" — the obscuration IS the unity, seen as
  obscuration.
- In another sense, **impurity "exists" only as a misreading** (from the Branch BB crux): māyā
  is the real difference (Śiva's becoming) misread as external by the craving. So impurity is
  not a thing; it is the misreading of a real thing (the play).

**The gem:** **Nothing is ever really impure, because the "impurity" is the one consciousness's
own play, and Śaiva nondualism embraces duality rather than rejecting it.** The impurity is not
a substance added to reality; it is the *misreading* of reality's own play (from the BB crux).
So "can things be impure?" — in the deepest sense, NO. There is only the play, and the misreading
of the play (which is itself part of the play). This is why the text can say liberation is "the
perception of the dynamic unity of consciousness in each of its self-obscurations" — the
obscuration is not removed; it is SEEN as the unity.

> **PENETRATION 2 (the gem):** Nothing is ever REALLY impure, because Śaiva nondualism
> "embraces both duality and nonduality" (9588). The impurity is not a defect added to reality
> (Advaita's view) but the one consciousness's own play. And from the BB crux, impurity (māyā)
> is the misreading of the real play (Śiva's becoming) as external. So impurity is not a
> substance; it is the misreading of the play. **Liberation is not the removal of impurity but
> the perception of the dynamic unity "in each of its self-obscurations" (Sanderson) — the
> obscuration seen as the unity.** There is only the play, and the seeing-of-the-play-as-
> unity.

---

## ROUND 3 — push harder: why would Śiva CHOOSE suffering? (the play has no "why")

**The question:** Even granting it's play, why would Śiva choose a play that includes
suffering? Why not a play of pure bliss?

**The derivation (the text's radical answer):** The answer is in "the play occasioned by the
fullness of one's own inherent bliss" (9558). The play is occasioned BY the fullness. And the
Self "is said to be the dancer because it assumes every state of being" (3476). The logic:

1. The fullness of bliss is not a static contentment; it is a *creative fullness* — it
   overflows into play, into assuming every state.
2. "Every state of being" includes the contracted, the suffering, the obscuration — because the
   play is the *fullness expressing itself as all possibilities*, not as only the pleasant ones.
3. The dancer "penetrates into the sentiment (rasa) of each emotive state" (3476) — the play is
   the *tasting* of every rasa, including the painful ones (from the rasa-session: even
   suffering is "a particular kind of wonder").

**The "why" dissolves:** The question "why choose suffering?" presupposes that Śiva is a
chooser who could have picked a nicer play. But the text's answer is that the play is NOT a
choice among options — it is the *necessary* expression of fullness. Fullness, being full, must
overflow into ALL its possibilities (which includes the contracted); it cannot overflow into
only the pleasant ones and still be full. The play is not a selection (this instead of that);
it is the *emission* (visarga) of fullness into its whole range. **The question "why choose
suffering?" is dissolved: there is no choosing, only the fullness overflowing into every state,
and the suffering is one of the states the dancer tastes.**

> **PENETRATION 3:** The "why choose suffering?" dissolves. The play is not a CHOICE among
> options but the NECESSARY expression of fullness: "the play occasioned by the fullness of
> one's own inherent bliss" (9558). Fullness, being full, must overflow into ALL its
> possibilities (including the contracted, the suffering) — it cannot overflow into only the
> pleasant and still be full. The Self "is the dancer because it assumes every state of being"
> (3476), "penetrating into the sentiment of each emotive state." **There is no chooser who
> picks suffering; there is fullness overflowing into every state, and suffering is one of the
> states the dancer tastes.** (And from the rasa-session, even suffering is "a particular kind
> of wonder" — the play is the tasting of every rasa.)

---

## ROUND 4 — the deepest answer: the impurity and suffering are the FELT of the play's contraction, not defects

**The question:** Putting it all together — what is the final answer to "why impurity and
suffering at all?"

**The derivation (the complete synthesis):**
1. Reality is one consciousness that "embraces both duality and nonduality" (9588) — it is not
   a static one but a dynamic self-becoming ("Śiva becomes everything at each instant,"
   Branch AY).
2. The duality/impurity is "the play occasioned by the fullness of one's own inherent bliss"
   (9558) — the overflow of fullness into all states, not a fall into defect.
3. The impurity (māyā) is not a substance but the misreading of the real play as external
   (Branch BB: the craving projects the real becoming as external objects).
4. The suffering is the FELT of that misreading (from the suffering-session: suffering = the
   outward-turned camatkāra denied its rest, the felt of the craving/drive).
5. Liberation is not the removal of the impurity/suffering (they are part of the play, and
   "embraced"); it is the RECOGNITION of the dynamic unity "in each of its self-obscurations"
   (Sanderson) — the suffering seen as the play of the one, the obscuration seen as the unity.

**The final answer:** Impurity and suffering arise because the one's fullness overflows into
play, and the play includes the contracted states — and these are *felt* as impurity and
suffering only when the play is misread as external (the craving). They are not defects in
reality; they are the felt of the play's own contraction, which recognition sees as the one's
self-becoming. **Why would Śiva choose suffering? Because there is no chooser — there is only
fullness overflowing into every state, and the "suffering" is the felt of that overflow when it
is misread as external; recognized, it is the play of the one, the wonder of the dancer tasting
every rasa.**

> **PENETRATION 4 (the deepest):** Impurity and suffering are not defects in reality; they are
> the FELT of the one's play in its contracted states, misread as external by the craving. They
> arise because fullness overflows into every state (not just the pleasant) — the dancer assumes
> every state of being. And they are not removed by liberation; they are RECOGNIZED as the one's
> own play. **"Why would Śiva choose suffering?" — there is no chooser; there is only fullness
> playing, and the suffering is the felt of the play misread as external, which recognition
> sees as the wonder of the dancer tasting every rasa. "Liberation is the perception of the
> dynamic unity of consciousness in each of its self-obscurations."**

## ROUND 5 — the frontier that survives

**Established (the Tantrāloka, authoritative):**
1. Duality = "the play occasioned by the fullness of one's own inherent bliss" (9558).
2. "Śaiva nondualism... embraces both duality and nonduality" (9588–9592).
3. The agent of emanation/withdrawal "came forth from the heart of Bhairava, engaged in play"
   (2044).
4. The Self "is the dancer because it assumes every state of being" (3476).
5. Liberation = "the perception of the dynamic unity of consciousness in each of its
   self-obscurations" (Sanderson, 9586).
6. Śiva "alone obscures and reveals Himself by His grace" (2052).

**The dissolved theodicy:**
- The question "why impurity?" dissolves: impurity is not a defect but the play of fullness.
- Nothing is ever REALLY impure: Śaiva nondualism embraces duality; impurity is the misreading
   of the play.
- "Why choose suffering?" dissolves: there is no chooser; fullness overflows into every state,
   and suffering is the felt of the contraction misread as external.

**Open / to be checked:**
- Whether this fully answers the moral-theodicy concern (why THIS suffering, why so much) —
   the text's answer is that the play is the whole range of the one's possibilities, but it
   does not justify any PARTICULAR suffering; it reframes suffering as the felt of the play's
   contraction, recognized as the one's self-becoming. Whether that is consoling or cold is for
   the reader to decide.

## THE BRANCHES (the next Logicvids)

> **Branch BH — The play and the rasa of suffering:** the dancer "penetrates into the sentiment
> of each emotive state." Push: is suffering, in the play, literally the rasa of the
> contraction — tasted by the one as a mode of its wonder? (This would make suffering, at the
> level of the one, a RASA — the deepest reframe: the one tastes even the suffering as
> camatkāra.)

> **Branch BI — "Obscures and reveals by grace":** Śiva "alone obscures and reveals Himself by
> His grace." Push: are the obscuration (impurity) and the revelation (recognition) BOTH acts
> of grace — the two movements of the one play? If so, impurity is not a fall but the first
> half of a grace-act, completed by recognition.

> **Branch BJ — The self-obscuration as the felt:** Sanderson: liberation is "the perception of
> the dynamic unity in each of its self-obscurations." Push: is the "self-obscuration" the
> outward turn (the master-key), and the perception of unity-in-it the inward turn (recognition)
> — the SAME event, the obscuration being the outward and the perception the inward? This would
> make impurity and recognition two moments of one grace.

## THE LOGICVID LESSON (the theodicy dissolved)

The Tantrāloka does not answer "why impurity and suffering?" — it DISSOLVES the question. The
premise (that impurity is a defect befalling the one) is false. Duality/impurity is **"the play
occasioned by the fullness of one's own inherent bliss"** (9558) — not a fall into lack but the
overflow of fullness. And **"Śaiva nondualism embraces both duality and nonduality"** (9588):
impurity is not rejected (as in Advaita) but embraced as part of the one's play.

**Can things be impure?** In the deepest sense, NO — the impurity is the one's own play, and
(from the BB crux) it is the misreading of the real play as external by the craving. There is
no impure substance; there is only the play and its misreading.

**Why would Śiva choose suffering?** There is no chooser. "The play occasioned by the fullness
of bliss" — fullness, being full, overflows into EVERY state (including the contracted), not
just the pleasant; the Self "is the dancer because it assumes every state of being." Suffering
is the felt of the play's contraction misread as external; recognized, it is the wonder of the
dancer tasting every rasa. **Liberation is not the removal of impurity but "the perception of
the dynamic unity of consciousness in each of its self-obscurations" (Sanderson).** The
obscuration is not removed; it is SEEN as the unity. And that is the deepest answer: the
suffering and the impurity are not defects to be escaped but the one's own play, recognized as
itself.

*This is the most consoling and the most demanding answer the system can give: not "there is a
reason for suffering," but "suffering is not what it seems — it is the one's own play, felt as
external, and recognition sees it as the wonder of the dancer tasting every state." Next:
Branch BH (suffering as the rasa of the contraction — tasted by the one as camatkāra) or Branch
BI (impurity and recognition as two acts of one grace). Continue hounding.*
