# 3. The relation to IIT is exact at the critical point

IIT explicitly begins from the phenomenological claim that experience has definite borders and a definite spatial and temporal grain. It then introduces exclusion as a physical postulate: among overlapping candidate substrates, only the maximally irreducible one counts as the conscious complex. IIT 3.0 calls this a local maximum of (\Phi); IIT 4.0 retains the same architecture through the “principle of maximal existence” and selection of the maximal substrate.

The logical structure is:

[
\text{experience is definite}
]

therefore:

[
\text{its physical substrate must be definite}
]

therefore:

[
\text{select one maximum among overlapping candidates}.
]

But the last step is not derived merely from irreducibility. It is supplied by the exclusion postulate and the further principle that what “exists most” excludes competitors. IIT’s own formulations acknowledge that many overlapping candidate systems may have positive integrated information; maximality determines which one is counted as the complex.

APIT had arrived at:

[
|\mathcal W_i^\ast|=1
]

by a different route, but the structural role was identical:

[
\boxed{
\text{phenomenal privacy demands one bearer;}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{the physical mathematics permits competitors;}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{an exclusion rule is introduced to remove them.}
}
]

Calling it a “uniqueness conjecture” rather than an “exclusion postulate” does not change its theoretical role.

So the previous answer understated this. APIT had not merely reached a frontier resembling IIT’s. It had recreated IIT’s most controversial architectural move.

For a continuously varying, nested physical system, APIT appears unable to have all three of the following without further physical structure.

## Exact uniqueness

[
|\mathfrak S(\mathcal E)|=1.

Exactly one aperture qualifies.

## Perturbational stability

The theory contains no privileged:

* weights;
* thresholds;
* timescale;
* partition grammar;

* spatial grain;
* temporal anchors.

These requirements conflict whenever two overlapping candidates exchange priority continuously.

Let:

[

[

Suppose:

[
q_A(\lambda_1)>q_B(\lambda_1),

At that point the theory must:

1. permit two apertures, sacrificing uniqueness;
2. select one by an additional rule, introducing a selector;
3. allow a discontinuous switch, weakening stability;
4. identify the candidates as descriptions of one deeper physical mode.

The fourth option is now the only genuinely new route available.

# 5. Stop identifying the aperture with a set of components

The persistent mistake may be the assumption that a subject must correspond to one sharply selected subset:

[
W_i^\ast
\subset
\mathcal E.
]

Biological processes do not generally preserve identity by preserving exact material membership. Their:

* molecules change;
* causal participation varies;
* boundaries fluctuate;
* peripheral processes couple and decouple;
* characteristic timescales overlap.

A subject might therefore be better identified with a **persistent dynamical mode**, not a set of elements.

Let the physical event structure support a response or transfer operator:

[
\mathcal L.
]

A coherent mode (v_i) satisfies approximately:

[
\mathcal L v_i
==============

\lambda_i v_i,
]

with:

* persistence across time;
* recurrent causal influence;
* stable control of constitutive variables;
* resistance to perturbation;
* relative separation from competing modes.

The aperture would be:

[
\boxed{
\mathscr A_i
============

\text{one robust metastable causal mode}
}
]

rather than:

[
\boxed{
\mathscr A_i
============

\text{one exact set of neurons, cells, or events}.
}
]

Its material support could be graded:

[
\operatorname{supp}(v_i):
\mathcal E\rightarrow[0,1].
]

The subject could still be definite as a mode even when no exact binary material boundary exists.

This is analogous to the distinction between:

* a vortex;
* the exact molecules currently participating in the vortex.

The vortex is not an additional substance behind the molecules. It is a dynamically invariant pattern whose material realization changes.

But this only advances APIT if the mode is physically privileged.

The theory now needs a selector satisfying all of the following.

## Covariance

It cannot depend upon coordinate choice:

[
\mathfrak S(g\mathcal E)
========================

g\mathfrak S(\mathcal E).
]

## Endogeneity

Its boundaries and timescale must be generated by the system’s dynamics, not chosen by the investigator.

## Stability

Small perturbations must not arbitrarily replace the subject:

[
\mathfrak S(\mathcal E+\delta\mathcal E)
\approx
\mathfrak S(\mathcal E).
]

## Persistence

The process must remain identifiable across changing material realization.

## Nondecomposability

Splitting the mode must destroy some constitutive reciprocal organization.

## Empirical reconstructibility

The proposed mode must be recoverable from interventions and measurements, at least approximately.

## Derived uniqueness

If exactly one mode corresponds to one subject, that fact must arise from the dynamics:

[
|\mathfrak S(\mathcal E)|=1
]

must be an output, not an axiom.

No current APIT formulation supplies such a selector.

That is the honest status.

# 9. The three available futures

APIT now faces a genuine trilemma.

## Option A: primitive exclusion

Postulate:

[
|\mathfrak S(\mathcal E)|=1.
]

This is the IIT route.

It preserves exact privacy but leaves physical uniqueness unexplained.

## Option B: dynamical derivation

Hypothesize that living conscious systems possess one robust metastable causal mode with an endogenous timescale and stable support.

Then:

[
|\mathfrak S(\mathcal E)|=1
]

becomes empirically discoverable.

This is the only route that preserves APIT as a distinctive scientific theory of individuation.

## Option C: plurality or ontic vagueness

Allow:

[
|\mathfrak S(\mathcal E)|>1
]

or allow aperture membership to be graded.

Then APIT must accept:

* overlapping subjects;
* nested subjects;
* indeterminate boundaries;
* or phenomenal identity that is itself vague.

This may be metaphysically possible, but it conflicts with the original motivation that one private field should have one exact physical bearer.

APIT cannot indefinitely postpone this choice.
