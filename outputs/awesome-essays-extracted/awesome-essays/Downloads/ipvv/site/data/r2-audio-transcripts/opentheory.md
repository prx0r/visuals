# Research map: the ten papers most important for Ochema

I surveyed the recent literature from **2024 through August 2026** across:

* active inference and consciousness;
* valence, affect and allostasis;
* multiscale agency;
* developmental bioelectricity;
* system identification and self-representation;
* thermodynamic embodiment;
* persistence, memory and subject individuation.

I have included one older paper—*Deeply Felt Affect*—because nearly all recent active-inference work on valence builds upon it.

The central result is strong:

> **The emerging literature converges on organisms as materially embodied, multiscale, memory-dependent control processes whose affect tracks their changing capacity to maintain themselves and successfully act.**

That does not prove Ochema’s intrinsic-phenomenal identity hypothesis. But it provides an increasingly coherent scientific architecture for the **external organization whose intrinsic aspect Ochema proposes to identify with a subject and its valence**.

## 1. Whyte et al. — *On the Minimal Theory of Consciousness Implicit in Active Inference*

**Christopher Whyte, Andrew Corcoran, Jonathan Robinson, Ryan Smith, Rosalyn Moran, Thomas Parr, Karl Friston, Anil Seth and Jakob Hohwy**

Published online in 2025; journal issue in 2026.

**Full links**

[http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06633](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06633)

[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2025.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2025.11.002)

[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41289944/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41289944/)

### Central idea

The authors ask what minimal theory of consciousness is already implicit in active-inference models. Rather than claiming that free-energy minimization automatically produces consciousness, they identify recurring commitments across models of perceptual awareness, conscious access, selfhood and global conscious state. ([PubMed][1])

The important move is to distinguish:

[
\text{active inference as a general description of agency}
]

from:

[
\text{specific active-inference architectures associated with consciousness}.
]

The relevant architectures involve temporally deep generative models, counterfactual policy evaluation, precision control, integration across domains and hierarchical access to inferred states.

### Why it matters for Ochema

This is probably the **single most important recent paper** for Ochema because it addresses the exact danger Ochema faces:

> If cells, organs, organisms and groups can all be described as active-inference systems, what distinguishes a conscious bearer from a merely self-organizing system?

Ochema should use this paper to divide three questions:

1. **Agency:** does the system regulate states through action?
2. **Sentience:** does it possess affectively weighted, integrated world-directed control?
3. **Consciousness:** does the process constitute one phenomenal field?

Active inference may formalize the first and partially constrain the second. It does not independently settle the third.

### Ochema upgrade

Instead of defining a candidate bearer merely as an organizationally closed system, require:

[
\mathcal A_i =
\langle
\text{temporal depth},
\text{counterfactual policies},
\text{precision control},
\text{cross-modal integration},
\text{persistent self-world model}
\rangle.
]

This helps prevent Ochema from attributing subjecthood to every Markov blanket or homeostatic loop.

### Main caution

The paper develops a **minimal theory**, not a demonstrated sufficient condition for consciousness. Its components must still be tested against rival theories and dissociation cases.

## 2. Hesp et al. — *Deeply Felt Affect: The Emergence of Valence in Deep Active Inference*

**Casper Hesp, Ryan Smith, Micah Allen, Karl Friston and colleagues**

Published in 2021.

**Full links**

[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2020.11.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2020.11.010)

[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33253028/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33253028/)

[https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8594962/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8594962/)

### Central idea

This is the essential formal origin of the active-inference account of valence.

The authors propose that valence is inferred from the expected precision of the agent’s action model—its confidence that its available policies will produce expected or preferred outcomes. They call this estimate **subjective fitness**. They also introduce **affective charge**, an update signal reflecting changes in that estimate. ([PubMed][2])

In simplified terms:

[
\text{positive valence}
\approx
\text{increasing confidence that my policies work},
]

[
\text{negative valence}
\approx
\text{decreasing confidence that my policies work}.
]

More formally:

[
v_t \sim
\Delta
\mathbb E[
\text{policy-model precision}
].
]

### Why it matters for Ochema

This is extraordinarily close to the direction your valence research has reached.

It suggests that pleasure and distress are not simply:

* external reward;
* objective fitness;
* prediction error;
* homeostatic deviation;
* or raw physiological activation.

They concern the process’s **higher-order estimate of its own ability to navigate toward preferred states**.

That connects directly with Ochema’s proposed relation among:

* valence;
* agency;
* self-efficacy;
* temporal continuity;
* endogenous norms;
* and coherent control.

### Ochema upgrade

Ochema can strengthen the theory by distinguishing:

[
F_i(t)
======

\text{objective biological condition},
]

[
\hat F_i(t)
===========

\text{the system’s inferred capacity to regulate that condition},
]

[
V_i(t)
======

\text{phenomenal valence}.
]

A defensible bridge hypothesis is:

[
V_i(t)
\leftrightarrow
\Delta\hat F_i(t),
]

not simply:

[
V_i(t)=F_i(t).
]

This explains why organisms can feel bad while objectively safe, feel good while moving toward harm, or retain positive affect during severe physical limitation. Valence tracks modelled control prospects, not direct evolutionary fitness.

### Main caution

Expected policy precision is a computational variable. Identifying it directly with felt valence would be premature. Ochema should treat it as one strong candidate correlate or constitutive dimension.

## 3. Caria and Pezzulo — *Emotion and Allostatic Control: An Active Inference Account of Emotion Regulation*

Published in 2026.

**Full links**

[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2026.106639](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2026.106639)

[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41825796/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41825796/)

### Central idea

This recent paper integrates emotion, emotion regulation and allostasis under active inference. Emotions are characterized not as decorative outputs layered onto cognition, but as regulatory inferential strategies that coordinate bodily, environmental and behavioural responses in the service of organismal integrity. ([PubMed][3])

Allostasis means anticipatory regulation:

[
\text{do not merely correct current error;}
]

[
\text{predict future needs and regulate in advance}.
]

Emotion becomes a multimodal control mode integrating:

* interoception;
* exteroception;
* proprioception;
* expected threats;
* available policies;
* bodily energetic demands;
* longer-term viability.

### Why it matters for Ochema

This supports a crucial correction to simplistic valence theories.

Valence is unlikely to correspond to one scalar physiological error. A situation can contain:

* short-term discomfort;
* long-term improvement;
* energetic cost;
* increased controllability;
* social threat;
* epistemic gain.

An emotion organizes these into a temporally extended control strategy.

Therefore Ochema should distinguish:

[
\text{momentary affective tone}
]

from:

[
\text{emotion as coordinated regulatory policy}
]

and:

[
\text{mood as a longer-timescale expectation about control and viability}.
]

### Ochema upgrade

Define an allostatic horizon:

[
T_i = {t,t+1,\ldots,t+n}.
]

Then valence depends not only on present error but on expected trajectories:

[
V_i(t)
======

\Phi\left(
\mathbb E[
\text{future viability and controllability}
\mid
\pi_i
]
\right).
]

This aligns strongly with your process ontology: valence belongs to the **trajectory**, not merely to an instantaneous state.

### Main caution

This remains a theoretical synthesis. It provides an excellent organizing model but not one experimentally verified universal formula for emotion.

## 4. Fields, Goldstein and Sandved-Smith — *Making the Thermodynamic Cost of Active Inference Explicit*

Published in 2024.

**Full links**

[https://doi.org/10.3390/e26080622](https://doi.org/10.3390/e26080622)

[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39202092/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39202092/)

[https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11353633/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11353633/)

### Central idea

The paper insists that inference, perception, memory and action are physical processes with thermodynamic costs. Active inference cannot remain an abstract Bayesian description detached from the material resources required to implement it. ([PubMed][4])

Every act of:

* sensing;
* storing;
* discriminating;
* updating;
* selecting;
* acting;

requires physical resources and dissipates energy.

This imposes a fundamental trade-off:

[
\text{better inference}
\quad\text{requires}\quad
\text{energetic expenditure}.
]

An organism therefore cannot indiscriminately process everything. It must allocate limited inferential resources according to expected relevance.

### Why it matters for Ochema

This strongly supports the **Material Enactment Principle**.

The bearer cannot be merely:

* a mathematical generative model;
* a transition table;
* an abstract algorithm;
* or an information pattern independent of implementation.

The actual process depends upon:

* metabolic state;
* membrane and ionic conditions;
* transmission costs;
* memory substrates;
* available energy;
* the physical timing of control loops.

### Valence implication

Valence may partly encode the changing ratio between expected regulatory benefit and expected cost:

[
\eta_i(\pi)
===========

\frac{
\text{expected improvement under policy }\pi
}{
\text{energetic and computational cost of }\pi
}.
]

A system with many theoretically available actions may still experience low control if none are metabolically affordable.

That suggests:

[
V_i
\sim
\Delta
\text{effective control capacity},
]

where “effective” means physically affordable, not merely logically possible.

### Ochema upgrade

Include a resource state (R_i) in the bearer model:

[
\mathcal A_i =
\langle
B_i,M_i,\Pi_i,H_i,G_i,R_i
\rangle.
]

Policy accessibility should be defined as:

[
\Pi_i^{\mathrm{effective}}
==========================

{
\pi:
\operatorname{Cost}(\pi)\leq R_i
}.
]

This may help formalize fatigue, stress, effort, helplessness and exhaustion within one architecture.

## 5. Fields and Levin — *Thoughts and Thinkers: On the Complementarity Between Objects and Processes*

Published in 2025.

**Full links**

[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2025.01.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2025.01.008)

[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39874620/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39874620/)

### Central idea

Fields and Levin reject a sharp ontology of objects versus processes. Objects and processes are complementary descriptions of persistence through time. Memory is essential because recognising something as “the same thing” requires interpretation across changing interactions. They further argue that memory and time are mutually defined. ([PubMed][5])

An object is not simply given as a static item. It is something an observer can:

* re-identify;
* interact with repeatedly;
* interpret as persisting;
* and distinguish from its changing states.

### Why it matters for Ochema

This directly supports REPM’s claim that the subject is not a static substance behind experience.

The subject is a **persistent trajectory of interpretation, regulation and memory**.

It also clarifies the difference between:

[
\text{material replacement}
]

and:

[
\text{loss of process identity}.
]

A biological subject may persist despite cellular and molecular turnover if relevant organizational, historical and interpretive invariants continue.

### Valence implication

Valence requires temporal comparison.

To register:

* improvement;
* deterioration;
* relief;
* frustration;
* fulfilled expectation;
* loss of control;

a system must relate its current condition to a remembered or anticipated trajectory.

Thus:

[
V_i(t)
======

f[
H_i(t-\Delta:t),
\hat H_i(t:t+\Delta)
].
]

Valence is fundamentally historical and prospective.

### Ochema upgrade

Replace the idea of a bearer as a bounded object with a process-equivalence class:

[
[\mathcal A_i]_H
================

{
\mathcal A:
\mathcal A
\text{ preserves the relevant historical-control invariants}
}.
]

This is a better candidate for subject continuity than one fixed material region.

### Main caution

Persistence for an observer and intrinsic phenomenal persistence are not automatically identical. Fields and Levin clarify object identification; Ochema must still establish the psychophysical connection.

## 6. Levin — *The Multiscale Wisdom of the Body: Collective Intelligence as a Tractable Interface for Next-Generation Biomedicine*

Published in 2025.

**Full links**

[https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.202400196](https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.202400196)

[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39623868/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39623868/)

[https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11848127/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11848127/)

### Central idea

Levin argues that biological systems exhibit problem-solving competencies at multiple organizational scales. Cells, tissues, organs and organisms participate in nested control systems, each navigating relevant problem spaces. Biomedical intervention can therefore target goals, memories and competencies rather than only micromanaging molecular hardware. ([PubMed][6])

The core architecture is:

[
\text{competent parts}
\rightarrow
\text{coordination}
\rightarrow
\text{larger-scale goal-directed system}.
]

Bioelectric networks are important because they coordinate cellular activity across distance and help stabilize large-scale morphogenetic targets.

### Why it matters for Ochema

This gives Ochema its strongest biological basis for **nested candidate agency**.

But it simultaneously creates the bearer-selection problem:

* Is the cell a subject?
* Is the tissue?
* Is the whole organism?
* Can several levels be subjects simultaneously?
* What makes a higher-level agent genuinely unified?

Levin’s work establishes competencies and goal-directedness. It does not establish phenomenal unity.

### Ochema’s distinctive role

Ochema can be positioned as the missing extension:

> Multiscale competency architecture identifies candidate agents; bearer recovery tests which candidate process best corresponds to one phenomenal field.

That requires combining:

* causal coordination;
* shared memory;
* common goal structure;
* perturbational unity;
* valence integration;
* phenomenal unity and privacy.

### Valence implication

The most important possibility is that valence acts as a **coordination currency**.

Subsystems possess locally preferred states, but an organism must align them around higher-order goals:

[
G_{\text{cell}}
,;
G_{\text{tissue}}
,;
G_{\text{organ}}
,;
G_{\text{organism}}.
]

Valence may reflect the degree to which these nested norms are mutually compatible:

[
V_i
\sim
-\operatorname{Conflict}
(
G_1,G_2,\ldots,G_n
).
]

This creates a route from biological alignment to phenomenal coherence, although it remains hypothetical.

## 7. Fields, Glazebrook and Levin — *Principled Limitations on Self-Representation for Generic Physical Systems*

Published in 2024.

**Full links**

[https://doi.org/10.3390/e26030194](https://doi.org/10.3390/e26030194)

[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38539706/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38539706/)

[https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10969210/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10969210/)

### Central idea

This paper examines the limitations that any physical system faces when attempting to observe, model and control itself. A physical system cannot construct a completely exhaustive internal representation of itself without encountering resource, recursion and accessibility constraints. ([PubMed][7])

Self-representation is therefore necessarily:

* partial;
* selective;
* purpose-relative;
* compressed;
* temporally delayed;
* dependent upon available interactions.

### Why it matters for Ochema

This provides a powerful formal analogue for **contraction**.

A finite subject cannot fully represent:

* all its internal states;
* all external causal structure;
* all possible actions;
* all timescales;
* or its own total modelling process.

Ochema can interpret its finite constraint set (L_i) as the unavoidable limitation on self-and-world representation:

[
L_i =
\langle
\text{limited access},
\text{limited resolution},
\text{limited memory},
\text{limited control},
\text{limited horizon}
\rangle.
]

This maps surprisingly well onto the kañcukas, provided it is presented as a structural comparison rather than a historical or scientific derivation of Śaiva doctrine.

### Valence implication

Because self-representation is partial, valence cannot require complete knowledge of organismal fitness.

It must operate as a compressed summary:

[
V_i
===

\operatorname{Summary}
\left[
\text{estimated trajectory of self-maintenance}
\right].
]

This helps explain why affect can serve as a global “how am I doing?” signal without representing every relevant biological variable explicitly.

### Main caution

A formal limitation on self-modelling does not prove phenomenal selfhood. It establishes constraints any self-regulating physical system must face.

## 8. Pezzulo, Parr and Friston — *Active Inference as a Theory of Sentient Behavior*

Published in 2024.

**Full links**

[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2023.108741](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2023.108741)

[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38182015/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38182015/)

### Central idea

This paper presents active inference as a framework for **sentient behaviour**, emphasizing action, perception and preference as one integrated process. Organisms act to gather information and bring about preferred outcomes rather than simply reacting to stimuli or maximizing externally specified rewards. ([PubMed][8])

Expected free energy is commonly decomposed into contributions related to:

* pragmatic value: expected preferred outcomes;
* epistemic value: information gain and uncertainty reduction.

Thus an agent sometimes chooses uncertainty-reducing actions even when they do not provide immediate reward.

### Why it matters for Ochema

This supports your claim that cognition is not passive representation.

A world is disclosed through action possibilities:

[
W_i
===

\text{the structured field of states the organism can identify, value and act within}.
]

This is compatible with enactivism and with Ochema’s subject-world co-determination:

[
E_i:\Omega\rightarrow(P_i,W_i).
]

The organism does not first receive a complete neutral world and then attach value. Its generative model, action possibilities and preferences jointly determine what counts as relevant.

### Valence implication

Epistemic value matters.

Positive valence cannot always mean immediate homeostatic improvement. Exploration can be effortful or temporarily unpleasant but still increase future control by reducing uncertainty.

Therefore Ochema should distinguish:

[
V_i^{\text{pragmatic}}
]

from:

[
V_i^{\text{epistemic}}.
]

Curiosity, insight, suspense and aesthetic tension may involve costly uncertainty that is nevertheless positively valued because it promises model improvement.

### Main caution

The word “sentient” can be used more broadly than “phenomenally conscious.” Ochema should define its use explicitly rather than treating the title as evidence that all active-inference systems feel.

## 9. Lobo, Levin and colleagues — *What Does Evolution Make? Learning in Living Lineages and Machines*

Published in 2025.

**Full links**

[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tig.2025.04.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tig.2025.04.002)

[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40500652/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40500652/)

### Central idea

This paper treats development as a learned, interpretive process connecting genotype and phenotype. The genome does not specify an organism in a simple one-to-one blueprint. Physiological and developmental systems possess plastic, problem-solving capacities that interpret inherited constraints in context. The authors highlight deep similarities between evolution and learning, with genomes functioning in part like generative models. ([PubMed][9])

The mapping is not:

[
\text{gene}\rightarrow\text{fixed trait},
]

but more like:

[
\text{genomic constraints}
+
\text{developmental competencies}
+
\text{environment}
\rightarrow
\text{constructed phenotype}.
]

### Why it matters for Ochema

This strengthens the rejection of static biological identity.

The organism is continually reconstructed through processes that:

* interpret signals;
* resolve novelty;
* preserve target states;
* coordinate local agents;
* generalize from evolutionary history.

Ochema can therefore characterize biological identity as an enacted model rather than a material inventory.

### Valence implication

Preferences are not arbitrary subjective additions. They arise from a long history of selected developmental and physiological norms.

However, Ochema must distinguish:

[
\text{evolutionary viability}
]

from:

[
\text{organism-level preference}
]

and:

[
\text{felt valence}.
]

Evolution shapes the machinery that produces preferences, but felt goodness is not necessarily identical to reproductive fitness.

### Ochema upgrade

Add three nested normative histories:

[
N_i =
\langle
N_i^{\text{evolution}},
N_i^{\text{development}},
N_i^{\text{learning}}
\rangle.
]

Current affect is generated by a process whose norms have been formed across all three timescales.

## 10. Mariano, Devoto and Zapparoli — *Feeling in Control When Things Go Well*

Published online in 2025.

**Full links**

[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106443](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106443)

[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41173350/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41173350/)

### Central idea

This meta-analysis investigates how outcome valence influences implicit sense of agency, especially intentional binding—the perceived temporal compression between voluntary actions and their outcomes. It finds that agency-related effects are influenced by action conditions and motivational context rather than being reducible to neutral prediction alone. ([PubMed][10])

One important point is that:

[
\text{control shapes valence}
]

and:

[
\text{valence shapes experienced agency}.
]

The relation is bidirectional.

### Why it matters for Ochema

Ochema has increasingly converged on valence and agency as inseparable. This empirical literature supports treating them as a coupled system.

Positive outcomes can strengthen the experienced relation:

[
\text{I acted}
\rightarrow
\text{this happened}.
]

But free choice, predictability and actual control also influence the effect. This suggests that felt agency is not simply inferred from sensory timing. It includes normative assessment of the action-outcome relation.

### Ochema implication

Model agency and valence recursively:

[
A_i(t)
\rightarrow
V_i(t+1)
\rightarrow
\hat C_i(t+1)
\rightarrow
A_i(t+2),
]

where (\hat C_i) is inferred controllability.

Successful action produces positive affect; positive affect raises expected policy precision; raised expected precision changes future action selection.

This creates self-reinforcing attractors:

[
\text{success}
\rightarrow
\text{control confidence}
\rightarrow
\text{greater exploration}
\rightarrow
\text{further success},
]

or:

[
\text{failure}
\rightarrow
\text{low control confidence}
\rightarrow
\text{withdrawal}
\rightarrow
\text{reduced corrective evidence}.
]

That is directly relevant to mood, learned helplessness, trauma and depression.

# The general scientific picture

## 1. Agency is becoming substrate-general

The emerging view is that agency should not be defined by:

* having a human brain;
* explicit reasoning;
* language;
* neuronal tissue;
* or externally visible complex behaviour.

A system displays increasingly strong agency insofar as it can:

* maintain preferred states;
* select among policies;
* learn from error;
* generalize to novelty;
* integrate information across time;
* modify its environment;
* preserve goals despite component turnover.

Active inference formalizes much of this as generative-model-guided regulation. Levin’s work extends it into developmental and regenerative biology. ([arXiv][14])

But **agency is graded**, and it does not automatically imply consciousness.

---

## 2. A living system is not merely minimizing instantaneous error

The old caricature of the Free Energy Principle is:

[
\text{organisms minimize prediction error}.
]

That is inadequate.

A successful agent must sometimes:

* tolerate current uncertainty;
* explore;
* seek informative novelty;
* accept temporary physiological cost;
* violate short-term expectations;
* restructure its own model.

The better description is:

[
\text{the organism selects trajectories expected to preserve its viable and preferred forms while resolving relevant uncertainty}.
]

This is inherently temporal and counterfactual.

---

## 3. Valence is converging on estimated control, not reward

The most important alignment for Ochema is among several research programmes:

### Active-inference affect

Valence tracks expected policy precision or subjective fitness.

### Allostatic theory

Emotion coordinates anticipatory regulation of future bodily needs.

### Sense-of-agency research

Valence and control attribution influence one another.

### Multiscale biology

Local and global goals must be aligned across nested agents.

### Thermodynamic active inference

Control depends on available physical and energetic resources.

Together they suggest:

[
\boxed{
\text{Valence tracks change in a system’s expected capacity to maintain coherent,
affordable control over its normatively relevant trajectory.}
}
]

That is not yet a consensus formula, but it is a strong synthesis.

# Deep dive: vasocomputation, valence, and Ochema

I checked the current public `prx0r/Ochema` repository for **`ochemamath`**, variations of that name, files containing “math,” and branches containing “math.” Nothing matching is present on the currently pushed `main` branch, and no matching branch is visible. So your newer mathematical work appears to be local or unpushed.

The OpenTheory article is highly relevant, but it contains two very different layers:

1. a strong conceptual synthesis connecting control, bodily tension, uncertainty and suffering;
2. a highly speculative claim that vascular smooth muscle is the principal computational mechanism implementing predictions and craving.

The first layer can substantially deepen Ochema. The second should become a sharply testable hypothesis, not part of Ochema’s established foundation.

---

## 1. What vasocomputation claims

Michael Edward Johnson’s proposal begins from a phenomenological observation: unpleasantness often includes a subtle attempt to resist, constrain, prolong or alter experience. He initially calls this *taṇhā*, although the article’s 2025 revision correctly distinguishes impulsive craving—*taṇhā*—from clinging or appropriation—*upādāna*. ([opentheory.net][1])

The central computational claim is:

> The nervous system generates desired sensory predictions and holds them in place until action makes them true.

Johnson interprets dysfunctional craving as **unskillful active inference**:

* too many predictions are simultaneously maintained;
* predictions concern things outside the agent’s control;
* the world model changes while the predictions remain latched to an obsolete basis;
* insufficient metabolic resources prevent flexible updating. ([opentheory.net][1])

He then proposes three vascular mechanisms:

### Compressive Vasomotion Hypothesis

Vascular contractions allegedly compress ambiguous neural dynamics into a more definite configuration.

### Vascular Clamp Hypothesis

Local vascular contraction allegedly stabilizes a neural pattern long enough to function as an active prediction or medium-term memory.

### Latched Hyperprior Hypothesis

Longer-lasting smooth-muscle latch states allegedly preserve rigid high-level priors, reducing the local neural system’s dynamical repertoire and resistance to updating. ([opentheory.net][1])

The complete architecture is approximately:

[
\text{uncertainty}
\rightarrow
\text{compression pressure}
\rightarrow
\text{vascular contraction}
\rightarrow
\text{restricted neural dynamics}
\rightarrow
\text{committed prediction}.
]

Release would reverse the sequence:

[
\text{prediction resolved}
\rightarrow
\text{vascular release}
\rightarrow
\text{increased dynamical freedom}
\rightarrow
\text{model updating}.
]

This is an extremely elegant hypothesis. It is not currently established.

# 2. What modern neuroscience genuinely supports

## The vasculature is not merely passive plumbing

The strongest legitimate foundation is the **hemo-neural hypothesis** developed by Christopher Moore and Rosa Cao. They argued that cerebral blood flow may actively influence information processing through mechanical, chemical, thermal and astrocyte-mediated effects on neural activity. They predicted that hemodynamics could alter circuit gain, detection and sensory discrimination rather than merely satisfy metabolic demand. ([Physiology Journals][2])

This is directly relevant to Ochema because it undermines a purely neuron-centric bearer model.

The physically enacted cognitive process may include:

[
\text{neurons}
+
\text{glia}
+
\text{vascular dynamics}
+
\text{metabolism}
+
\text{extracellular fields}.
]

That supports Ochema’s **Material Enactment Principle** much more strongly than it supports vasocomputation specifically.

---

## Direct neuron-to-vascular smooth-muscle signalling exists

A major 2024 *Nature Neuroscience* study identified synaptic-like junctions between glutamatergic axons and arteriolar smooth-muscle cells. Individual axons could innervate both neuronal dendrites and arteriolar smooth muscle. Disrupting NMDA-receptor signalling specifically in the smooth-muscle cells reduced stimulus-evoked functional hyperaemia. ([Nature][3])

This is a striking partial confirmation of Johnson’s intuition that vascular smooth muscle is more closely integrated with neural signalling than standard “support plumbing” language suggests.

However, the experiment demonstrated:

[
\text{neuronal activity}
\rightarrow
\text{smooth-muscle signalling}
\rightarrow
\text{blood-flow regulation}.
]

It did **not** demonstrate:

[
\text{smooth-muscle state}
==========================

\text{stored prediction},
]

or:

[
\text{vascular contraction}
===========================

\text{phenomenal clinging}.
]

It establishes anatomical and functional access, not the proposed computational semantics.

---

## Vasomotion forms structured infra-slow dynamics

Cerebral vasomotion occurs across several infra-slow frequency bands commonly described as endothelial, neurogenic and myogenic components. Simultaneous EEG, haemodynamic and metabolic measurements reveal directed interactions among electrical, vascular and metabolic processes rather than one simple neural-to-blood causal chain. ([Nature][4])

Recent neurovascular-coupling reviews likewise describe spontaneous neuronal and haemodynamic dynamics as complex, bidirectionally constrained processes whose relationship varies across brain state, frequency and spatial scale. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][5])

This suggests that the correct unit is not:

[
\text{neural computation}
+
\text{vascular response},
]

but something closer to:

[
\mathcal N_i
============

\text{coupled neuro-glio-vascular-metabolic dynamics}.
]

That is extremely Ochema-compatible.

# 3. Where OpenTheory goes beyond the evidence

Several claims in the article should remain classified as **speculative hypothesis generators**.

## Predictions stored as vascular tension

There is presently no clear evidence that particular semantic predictions are represented by specific patterns of vascular smooth-muscle contraction.

The vascular state certainly influences:

* blood flow;
* oxygen and glucose availability;
* local pressure;
* mechanical conditions;
* thermal conditions;
* extracellular chemistry.

But showing that it modulates a neural computation is different from showing that it carries the **content** of the prediction.

Ochema should distinguish:

[
\text{causal modulation}
\neq
\text{representational encoding}.
]

---

## Vascular latches as medium-term memory

Smooth muscle has latch-like contractile mechanisms that allow sustained force with relatively low energy consumption. That gives Johnson a plausible physical analogy for low-cost persistent constraint.

But the leap is:

[
\text{persistent physical state}
\rightarrow
\text{memory state}.
]

Persistence is necessary for memory, but not sufficient. A memory must also:

* be selectively written;
* preserve discriminable information;
* influence later processing in content-sensitive ways;
* be selectively released or overwritten;
* survive appropriate perturbations;
* predict behaviour better than competing mechanisms.

The vascular-latch hypothesis becomes scientific only when those mappings are demonstrated.

## Clinging equals vascular contraction

The article’s most ambitious identity is:

[
\text{upādāna}
\approx
\text{vascular clenching}.
]

This currently lacks direct evidence. Buddhist *taṇhā* and *upādāna* are phenomenological, motivational and cognitive categories. They may correlate with muscular, autonomic and vascular changes, but no known result shows a one-to-one relation.

The strongest defensible version is:

> Craving and clinging may recruit distributed contractive control processes, including vascular, visceral, skeletal-muscular, fascial and neural precision-regulation mechanisms.

That is broader and much more plausible.

---

## Clinical claims

The article makes strong claims connecting persistent smooth-muscle latching with migraines, psychiatric conditions, bodily suffering, diet and psychedelic effects. Those claims are substantially stronger than the evidence presented and should not be imported into Ochema as facts. ([opentheory.net][1])
