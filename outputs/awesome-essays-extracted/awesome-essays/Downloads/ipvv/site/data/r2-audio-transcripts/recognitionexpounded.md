## The crucial correction from Timalsina

Timalsina’s work confirms that *vimarśa* is not merely the redundant statement that consciousness is self-aware.

His account gives it at least four functions:

1. consciousness mirrors itself;
2. consciousness also mirrors its object;
3. it synthesizes different modes into one conscious event;
4. it reconciles an allegedly non-sequential emergence of consciousness with temporally structured experience.

His abstract explicitly says that recognition is inscribed within reflexivity, that consciousness mirrors both self and other, and that synthesis belongs to its intrinsic nature. ([ResearchGate][1])

That means the stronger structure is:

[
\boxed{
\text{vimarśa}
==============

\text{self-manifestation capable of synthesizing subject, object and temporal modes
within one act}.
}
]

This is much more useful than “consciousness reflects on itself.”

It says a conscious event is not assembled from three independently existing pieces:

[
\text{subject}+\text{object}+\text{relation}.
]

Rather, the conscious act differentiates these poles while retaining them within one synthetic presentation:

[
\operatorname{Vim}
:
\Omega
\rightarrow
(P_i,O_i,R_{P_iO_i}).
]

That is very close to what your exclusion operation was trying to describe.

# The exact Pratyabhijñā architecture

## 1. Prakāśa: manifestation

At minimum:

[
\operatorname{Prak}(x)
======================

x\text{ is present}.
]

This is not physical light. It means that something appears or is disclosed.

In Ochema:

[
D_i
===

\text{intrinsic manifestation of the bearer’s state}.
]

But bare manifestation cannot yet explain:

* why the appearance is for one subject;
* how it is related to other appearances;
* how it persists;
* why it influences action.

## 2. Vimarśa: synthetic self-availability

The stronger definition is:

[
\operatorname{Vim}_i(x_t)
=========================

\text{the occurrence of }x_t
\text{ is available within the same process as}
]

[
\text{an appearance of something, to this subject, bearing significance for its trajectory}.
]

It therefore includes four relations:

[
\operatorname{Vim}_i(x_t)
=========================

\langle
\operatorname{Manifest}(x_t),
\operatorname{For}(x_t,i),
\operatorname{Of}(x_t,o),
\operatorname{Matter}(x_t,G_i)
\rangle.
]

Where:

* `Manifest`: the state appears;
* `For`: it appears for a subject-index;
* `Of`: it presents something as object;
* `Matter`: it is related to norms, goals or possible action.

That is the bridge from neutral appearance to a structured conscious episode.

## 3. Smṛti: retention and synthesis through time

Recognition requires more than instantaneous self-presence.

There must be some persistence across:

[
x_{t-1},x_t,x_{t+1}.
]

Otherwise the system cannot say:

[
\text{“this present one is the same as that previously encountered one.”}
]

So memory is not an optional faculty added after consciousness. In Pratyabhijñā argumentation, it helps expose the unity that synthesizes temporally distinct cognitions.

Ochema currently places this in the register:

> the accumulated, history-carrying configuration that determines what can be selected.

The mathematical version needs refinement because a present state does not necessarily encode its entire history. But the conceptual function is right:

[
H_i(t)
======

\text{retained organization enabling cross-temporal ownership and comparison}.
]

## 4. Svātantrya: self-determining efficacy

Ratié emphasizes that Utpaladeva’s project is intended to uncover absolute freedom in ordinary consciousness, using experience and reasoning rather than relying solely on scripture. The finite subject’s consciousness is presented as the restricted expression of the freedom attributed to Śiva. ([OUP Academic][2])

The argument does not stop at:

[
\text{experience knows itself}.
]

It adds:

[
\text{consciousness determines, combines, remembers, imagines and acts}.
]

The key idea is not unrestricted behavioural freedom. It is that consciousness is not merely a passive screen governed entirely by something external.

Ochema can operationalize a finite form:

[
\operatorname{Freedom}_i
========================

\text{capacity to generate and select among counterfactual trajectories
according to endogenous weighting}.
]

That is:

[
\Pi_i={\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_n},
]

with:

[
\pi_i^*
=======

\operatorname{Select}(\Pi_i\mid\omega_i,G_i).
]

This is finite agency—not yet Śiva’s absolute freedom.

## 5. Saṃkoca: contraction

The finite subject does not lack manifestation, reflexivity or agency absolutely. These capacities are restricted.

Ochema already has an excellent structure for this:

[
L_i=
\langle
\text{limited knowledge},
\text{limited action},
\text{selective valuation},
\text{finite temporal horizon},
\text{restricted causal position}
\rangle.
]

Contraction therefore becomes:

[
A_i
===

\operatorname{Restrict}(A_\infty,L_i).
]

But this must initially remain a two-level formulation:

### Empirically defensible

A biological agent has limited:

* observations;
* policies;
* memory;
* temporal horizon;
* resources;
* causal reach.

### Śaiva metaphysical claim

These limitations are contractions of an unrestricted universal consciousness.

The first does not prove the second.

# What recognition actually recognizes

The word “recognition” implies an identity relation:

[
\boxed{x_{\text{now}}=x_{\text{previously known}}.}
]

In the spiritual case, the two descriptions are:

[
I_{\text{finite}}
]

and:

[
I_{\text{universal}}.
]

The Pratyabhijñā claim is not:

[
I_{\text{finite}}\rightarrow I_{\text{universal}}
]

as though the individual is manufactured into God.

It is:

[
\boxed{
I_{\text{finite}}
=================

\operatorname{Loc}*{L_i}(I*{\text{universal}}).
}
]

Recognition changes the apprehended identity relation, not the underlying identity.

Utpaladeva’s system explicitly aims to demonstrate the identity of the individual self with Śiva through independent philosophical reasoning. ([ORA][3])

## But “beneath what is added” is slightly misleading

The universal subject is not necessarily imagined as a hidden object underneath the body and mind.

A better formulation is:

> The universal subjectivity is the manifesting and synthesizing activity already occurring as the limited experience.

The local subject is not a false layer covering a separate entity. It is the same proposed power under restrictions.

Thus:

[
\text{universal consciousness}
\notin
\text{a hidden location behind experience};
]

[
\text{universal consciousness}
==============================

\text{the unrestricted character attributed to the activity occurring here}.
]

# The exact recognition condition

Here is the strongest current version.

Recognition occurs when the bearer apprehends all four propositions together:

## R1. Contents are not the subject

Every identifiable content can vary:

[
x_t\in
{
\text{body-state, sensation, thought, emotion, identity narrative}
}.
]

Therefore no one content exhausts the first-person condition under which contents appear.

[
I_i\neq x_t.
]

## R2. The local subject pole is itself a determination

The ordinary “me” is not pure subjectivity. It consists of:

* body boundary;
* autobiographical memory;
* action model;
* social identity;
* affective dispositions;
* perspectival location.

These can themselves become manifest.

Therefore:

[
P_i
\subset
D_i,
]

in the phenomenological sense that the local self-model is one appearing structure within the broader field of appearance.

## R3. Subjectivity is not attributed exclusively to the local model

Before recognition:

[
\operatorname{SourceOfManifestation}
====================================

P_i.
]

After recognition:

[
\operatorname{SourceOfManifestation}
\neq
P_i\text{ exclusively}.
]

The local pole remains the locus of finite access and action, but it ceases to be interpreted as the ultimate producer or boundary of manifestation.

## R4. The boundary is reclassified as restriction rather than absolute separation

Before:

[
B_i
===

\text{boundary of subjectivity itself}.
]

After:

[
B_i
===

\text{boundary of this subjectivity’s finite access, memory and action}.
]

This is the decisive transformation.

[
\boxed{
T_{\mathrm{rec}}:
B_i^{\mathrm{ontological}}
\mapsto
B_i^{\mathrm{functional}}.
}
]

The body–mind remains functionally local. Recognition denies that its functional boundary proves an absolutely separate consciousness-substance.

# A canonical recognition operator

Let the bearer be:

[
\mathfrak B_i
=============

(M_i,\omega_i,H_i,G_i,N_i,\iota_i)
]

where:

* (M_i): total enacted process;
* (\omega_i): present register;
* (H_i): historical organization;
* (G_i): endogenous significance structure;
* (N_i): local access/action substructure;
* (\iota_i): first-person index.

The ordinary identification map is:

[
\operatorname{Id}_{\omega_i}(\iota_i)
=====================================

N_i.
]

Meaning:

> I am exclusively this local model and its boundary.

Recognition is:

[
T_{\mathrm{rec}}
:
\operatorname{Id}_{\omega_i}(\iota_i)=N_i
]

[
\longmapsto
]

[
\operatorname{Id}_{\omega_i'}(\iota_i)
======================================

\operatorname{LocalExpression}(M,N_i).
]

Meaning:

> This local process is how subjectivity is finitely expressed here; it is not an independently existing source cut off from the whole.

Crucially:

[
N_i'=N_i
]

may remain physically unchanged.

What changes is:

[
\operatorname{OntologicalRole}*{\omega_i'}(N_i)
\neq
\operatorname{OntologicalRole}*{\omega_i}(N_i).
]

This explains how recognition can be instantaneous.

# Instant recognition

Yes, recognition can occur suddenly because it is a **re-identification**, not the gradual construction of a new capacity.

The analogy is recognizing someone:

[
\text{unknown stranger}
\rightarrow
\text{“that is the person I knew.”}
]

The perceptual contents may remain nearly identical. The identity relation flips.

Similarly:

[
\text{“awareness belongs to this organism-self”}
]

can become:

[
\text{“this organism-self is one localized structure appearing within awareness.”}
]

That re-indexing can happen rapidly.

But distinguish:

[
\boxed{
\text{recognition event}
\neq
\text{stabilization}
\neq
\text{embodiment}.
}
]

### Recognition event

The identity/boundary relation changes.

### Stabilization

The new relation becomes repeatedly available and resistant to reversal.

### Embodiment

Attention, affect, action and relations operate consistently from it.

The *Tantrāloka*’s practices can later be classified according to which stage they target.

# The naturalistic rival

Ochema must keep two interpretations alive.

## Śaiva interpretation

[
T_{\mathrm{rec}}
================

\text{veridical recognition of pre-existing universal identity}.
]

## Biological interpretation

[
T_{\mathrm{rec}}
================

\text{decentring or reconfiguration of the organism’s self-model}.
]

Both predict:

* reduced exclusive identification with autobiographical self;
* altered ownership;
* altered boundary experience;
* changes in valence and action.

Only the first makes the ontological claim:

[
\text{all local subjects are expressions of one consciousness}.
]

The phenomenology alone may not discriminate between them.

The project can turn the tradition into a **graded explanatory architecture**:

That becomes the basis for reading the *Tantrāloka* as a catalogue of:

* practices/interventions;
* expected transformations;
* rival explanations;
* evidence grades.
