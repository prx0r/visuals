# 1. Upgrade the participant: informational individuality

The participant cannot simply be assumed to be:

* the brain;
* the organism;
* a Markov blanket;
* the highest causal scale.

Information-theoretic individuality defines candidate individuals by whether their own past predicts their future better than their parts do, and whether adding external variables weakens rather than improves this self-predictability. This allows individuals to be discovered rather than stipulated and permits nested or overlapping individuality.

Define:

[
\operatorname{Ind}(X)
=====================

\text{degree to which }X_{\text{past}}
\text{ uniquely predicts }X_{\text{future}}.
]

This is stronger than a boundary alone.

A participant must have:

[
\boxed{
\text{informational continuity}
+
\text{causal autonomy}
+
\text{counterfactual persistence}.
}
]

But:

[
\operatorname{Ind}(X)
\not\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Conscious}(X).
]

An ecosystem or collective may have high informational individuality without thereby forming one phenomenal field.

# 2. Upgrade integration: synergy rather than connectivity

“Integration” is far too vague.

Integrated Information Decomposition, or (\Phi)ID, shows that apparently integrated information includes several distinct modes:

* redundant information;
* unique information;
* transferred information;
* synergistic information available only from the whole;
* information that changes category across time.

Therefore two systems may have equal connectivity or mutual information while possessing radically different whole-system causal organizations.

The relevant condition is not:

[
\text{the parts communicate}.
]

It is:

[
\boxed{
\text{the whole carries counterfactual information}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{that no partition of its parts carries independently}.
}
]

Define:

[
\operatorname{Syn}(X)>0
]

when some system-level information is irreducibly available only through the joint organization.

This should replace generic “task irreducibility” inside CT-PARTITION.

But still:

[
\operatorname{Syn}(X)
\not\Rightarrow
\text{phenomenal unity}.
]

Synergy occurs in many complex non-conscious systems.

# 3. Upgrade the dynamics: criticality and metastability

The conscious aperture is unlikely to be a static causal complex.

It appears to require a dynamical regime balancing:

* stability and flexibility;
* integration and segregation;
* persistence and rapid reconfiguration;
* sensitivity and resistance to noise.

Recent anesthesia work found that propofol- and xenon-associated unconsciousness moved neural dynamics away from criticality, whereas unresponsive but experientially vivid ketamine states preserved near-critical dynamics. Criticality measures also predicted perturbational complexity.

Separate work found conscious wakefulness and sleep–wake transitions associated with a dynamic integration–segregation balance and metastability rather than maximum integration alone.

So the candidate condition is:

[
\boxed{
\text{metastable integration},
}
]

not:

[
\boxed{
\text{permanent global synchronization}.
}
]

The system must support a large repertoire of differentiated states while retaining enough global constraint for them to affect one another.

Define:

[
\operatorname{Meta}(X,\Delta)
=============================

\text{capacity to move among many coordinated transient states}.
]

This may explain why consciousness disappears when the system becomes:

* too locally segregated;
* too globally stereotyped;
* too stable;
* too chaotic.

Criticality is nevertheless probably a **necessary operating regime**, not the identity of consciousness.

# 8. Nondual and altered states are boundary experiments

Meditation, psychedelics, dreams and dissociative states matter because they perturb different components of subjectivity independently.

They show that:

[
\text{field presence}
\neq
\text{ordinary ego}
\neq
\text{network segregation}
\neq
\text{behavioral responsiveness}.
]

Psilocybin can substantially desynchronize and desegregate brain networks while preserving or intensifying experience and weakening ordinary self, space and time organization.

A recent study of advanced meditators associated reported nondual experience with reduced differentiation between neural timescales during internally and externally directed attention. This is interesting but early and does not prove metaphysical nonduality.

These states allow separate investigation of:

* (D_i): field presence;
* (O_i): ownership;
* (B_i): self–world boundary;
* (\alpha_i): attention;
* (\mathcal G_i): phenomenal geometry.

They therefore test recognition more effectively than ordinary waking perception.

The crucial rule is:

[
\boxed{
\text{phenomenological boundary dissolution}
}
]

does not establish:

[
\boxed{
\text{numerical identity with universal consciousness}.
}
]

It establishes that the ordinary boundary model is plastic.

# 11. Electromagnetic fields are a legitimate secondary hypothesis

Neural field theories deserve more attention than the framework currently gives them.

Their strongest claim is not that “electromagnetism is mystical.”

It is that an electromagnetic field supplies a physically unified spatial medium in which distributed neural differences may be simultaneously instantiated, potentially addressing binding more directly than spike-based communication alone.

This gives a candidate physical realization of:

[
\Lambda_B.
]

But it does not solve:

[
\Lambda_M.
]

Even if a field physically binds neural information, the question remains:

[
\text{Why is that field manifest?}
]

The decisive test would require interventions that alter the candidate field while controlling ordinary synaptic computation and then predict systematic alterations in phenomenal unity.

At present, this remains a serious but low-confidence branch.

# 13. The disciplined noetic edge

## Core-worthy noetic research

These are scientifically valuable now:

* calibrated introspection;
* meditation and nondual phenomenology;
* dreaming and lucid dreaming;
* psychedelically altered self-boundaries;
* contemplative changes in ownership, time and valence;
* end-of-life cognition studied prospectively.

## Anomalies worth monitoring

Near-death experiences and unexpected lucidity are reported phenomena that deserve prospective measurement, especially because they may expose hidden neural capacities or unusual temporal relationships between brain activity and reported memory. Current reviews emphasize heterogeneous methods and unresolved mechanisms rather than evidence for consciousness operating independently of the brain.

## Quarantined hypotheses

Remote viewing, extrasensory perception and postmortem information fields should remain outside the core framework.

Recent reviews find heterogeneous findings, weak replication and no unified neural signature. Preregistered parapsychology studies also produce substantially fewer significant results than non-preregistered studies.

The correct status is:

[
\boxed{
\text{open empirical anomaly class, not an ontological premise}.
}
]
