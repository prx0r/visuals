# ABHINAVA'S ORIGINAL EXTENSIONS: WHAT THE IPVV ADDS BEYOND THE IPK
## The epistemology of action, the sphurattā, the parā-vāk, and the māheśvarya — the four regions only the commentary develops

*2026-08-11. A companion to the Abhinava-vs-Utpaladeva essay. Where that essay isolated the
identifications (vimarśa = parā-vāk = sphurattā = māheśvarya), this essay expounds the four *regions*
of Abhinava's original contribution — the bodies of doctrine that the IPK leaves undeveloped and that
the IPVV builds into a complete metaphysics. These are the territory the essay-3 razor calls "where the
IPVV goes further than both IPK and Ratié."*

---

## 1. The epistemology of action (the kriyādhikāra, V3A–I)

The IPK states the powers; it does not develop a theory of action. Abhinava devotes a whole adhikāra to
it. This is Abhinava's largest original region.

### 1.1 Action as the will (V3A)

The kriyādhikāra opens the parallel: having established the power of knowledge, now the power of action.
And it confronts the paradox at the heart of the system: the Lord is beyond time and sequence, yet
manifests a world shot through with sequence. The answer is the mirror — a mirror bears countless
reflections without ceasing to be one; so the timeless manifests order precisely *because* it is one.

> "He bows to Śiva, who, though free of sequence, manifests manifold sequences." (V3A)

The action-support (kriyāśraya): the one that acts is one, and it is *because* it is one and undivided
that it can show sequence at all.

### 1.2 The action-cognitions as bhedābheda (V3B)

The action-cognitions (of action, relation, universal, substance, direction, time) are both one and
many — "this is the very same body" (V3B). The distinction-and-non-distinction vimarśa is Abhinava's
answer to how the one appears as many without losing its unity.

### 1.3 Causality as the conscious knower's agency (V3G)

The deepest claim of the kriyādhikāra:

> "The essence of cause and effect is nothing but the relation of agent and object of action.
> Causality, in the end, is the agency of a conscious knower." (V3G)

The inert cannot be a cause — the seed cannot endow the sprout with being (V3G). The real cause is
consciousness, the free will that shapes the effect. This is a full metaphysics of causation, which the
IPK does not develop.

### 1.4 Inference across knowers (V3H)

The Vijñānavādin's objection — how can you infer the fire from the smoke if cause and effect are only
relations between private appearances? — is answered: the Lord creates the appearances for all the
knowers at once, as one, so the pervasion holds across streams (V3H).

### 1.5 The will as cause, agent, and action (V3I)

The crown of the kriyādhikāra:

> "The cause does not cause, the agent does not act, the action does not move — all three are the one
> will, by which the Lord, wishing to stand, becomes the world." (V3I)

And against the Advaita's "un-explainable ignorance," Abhinava argues the difference is real: "even
Brahman with ignorance is [admitted by some], so this is not an absolute monism either" (V3I).

**What this region contributes.** A complete epistemology and metaphysics of action — action = the
will, causality = the knower's agency, inference across knowers by the one creation, and the
refutation of the monist's "ignorance." None of this is in the IPK as a developed theory. It is
Abhinava's.

## 2. The sphurattā: the throbbing as the felt essence (V2I)

The IPK states the reflexivity-claim; Abhinava gives it a dynamic face — the throbbing.

> "The throbbing (sphurattā) — the very pulsing of the manifestation — is the great being
> (mahāsattā), undivided by place and time, the heart of the param-Lord." (kārikā 14, V2I)

The sphurattā is the same as the spanda, the wave (ūrmi), the eternal being. The self-aware
manifestation is not static; it is a *pulsing*, an ongoing act of self-articulation.

**Why this is original.** Utpaladeva's reflexivity-claim is static (the essence of light is reflective
awareness); Abhinava's sphurattā makes it dynamic, temporal, felt. This is where the recognition-thesis
and the felt meet: the pulse of self-awareness is the felt beat of being, "the heart of the
param-Lord."

**The systematic gain.** The sphurattā connects the Pratyabhijñā to the Spanda school (the pulsation),
and it gives the felt register (camatkāra) its home: the throbbing is the felt self-presence, the beat
of the recognition.

---

## 3. The parā-vāk: the Word as the self-grasp (V2H/V2I)

The second identification: the reflexivity-claim is the supreme Word.

> "This self-knowing is the parā-vāk, the supreme speech, from which all the lower levels of speech
> (the madhyamā, the vaikharī) descend." (V2H)

**Why this is original.** Utpaladeva's reflexivity-claim is about light; Abhinava identifies it with
the Word. Language is not an instrument consciousness uses; it is the very form the self-grasp takes.
The word-joined "I" is not a construction; it is the self's own articulation (V2L).

**The systematic gain.** With the parā-vāk, Abhinava absorbs the entire philosophy of language — the
sphoṭa, the grammarian's paśyantī, the vācaka-vācya structure — into the recognition-thesis. Speech is
not a second reality; it is consciousness's own self-grasp. The grammarian's separate supreme principle
is dissolved (V2I): speech and the mind are only the ego's operation, quickened by the Lord's power.

---

## 4. The māheśvarya: the Lordship as the self-awareness (V2R/S)

The crowning identification, closing the jñānādhikāra:

> "The great-Lordship is nothing but the reflexive-awareness — the one who is aware of himself,
> fixedly, is the Lord; and that reflexive-awareness is the God's own pure knowledge-and-action. The
> great-Lordship is not an added power; it is the self-awareness itself." (kārikā 11, V2S)

**Why this is original.** The IPK states the three powers; Abhinava shows they reduce to the
self-awareness, and that the Lordship is that self-awareness. The māheśvarya is not a fifth power; it is
what the four are. The Lord is not a being who has powers; he is the self-awareness the powers express.

**The systematic gain.** This makes the recognition-thesis a metaphysics: the Lord = the self-awareness
= the felt reflexivity of light. Recognition is the re-cognition of the self as this self-aware light.
The jñānādhikāra closes on this: the powers reduce to the self-awareness, and the self-awareness is the
Lord.

## 5. The tattva-ontology and the soteriology (V3J–P)

The final regions are the ontology and the path.

### 5.1 The Śivatattva as the body of every category (V3J)

The āgamādhikāra supplies what reason cannot — the categories of the world, from the Lord's teaching,
because "perception is not an omniscient eye; inference lacks the pervasion; only the āgama measures
the whole" (V3J). And its pivot:

> "At the heart of it all is the Śivatattva, which is the body of every category... knowledge and
> action — manifestation and reflexive-awareness — are un-joined, inseparable; and this inseparable
> pair is the supreme form of all beings." (V3J)

### 5.2 The kañcukas and the malas (V3K/L)

The coverings (niyati, kāla, rāga, vidyā, kalā) hide the pure self in the bound state; the three stains
(āṇava, māyīya, kārma) bind the knowers; the paśu (the bound one) and the pati (the Lord) define the
relation.

### 5.3 The jīvanmukta and the guru-paramparā (V3N–P)

The closing sum: the recognition as the path, the while-living-liberated (jīvanmukta), the transmission
from guru, śāstra, and self.

> "Since liberation is from the recognition alone, all the elaborate effort-yogas are useless here;
> this is the clear means, effort-less, un-undertaken, yet bearing the great fruit." (V3P)

And the lover-image closes the whole: "the world-Lord, always present, always near, is not experienced
in his own splendor until the recognition arises" (V3P).

## 6. The map of Abhinava's original regions

| Region | Locus | What it adds beyond the IPK |
|--------|-------|------------------------------|
| The epistemology of action | V3A–I | action = the will; causality = the knower's agency; inference across knowers; the anti-monist realism |
| The sphurattā (the throbbing) | V2I | the reflexivity-claim made dynamic and felt |
| The parā-vāk (the Word) | V2H/I | the reflexivity-claim identified with speech; language absorbed into recognition |
| The māheśvarya (the Lordship) | V2R/S | the powers reduce to the self-awareness; the Lord = the self-awareness |
| The tattva-ontology | V3J–M | the Śivatattva, the kañcukas, the malas, the categories |
| The soteriology | V3N–P | the jīvanmukta, the guru-paramparā, the recognition-path |

**The thesis.** Abhinava's original contribution is not a single new claim but the *completion* of the
system: the epistemology of action, the dynamic felt (sphurattā), the linguistic absorption (parā-vāk),
the reduction of the powers (māheśvarya), the ontology, and the soteriology. Utpaladeva states the seed;
Abhinava grows it into a complete metaphysics. These are the regions the recognition-thesis could not
have without him, and they are the "goes further" territory of the authoritative source.
