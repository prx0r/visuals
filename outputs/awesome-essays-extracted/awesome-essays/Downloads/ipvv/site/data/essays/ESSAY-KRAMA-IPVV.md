# THE SEQUENCE AND THE RECOGNITION: A COMPARATIVE SYNTHESIS OF THE KRAMA SCHOOL AND THE IPVV
## The Kālīs of cognition and the three powers — time, the akrama vimarśa, and the order-less support

*A magnum-opus essay. 2026-08-11. The Krama school of Kashmiri Śaivism — the "sequence" — makes time
and its goddess-energies (the Kālīs) central: consciousness is understood as a sequence of cognitive
moments, and the goddesses are the divinized powers of the cognition-process. The IPVV is the
recognition-philosophy's deepest commentary. This essay is the complete comparative synthesis of the
two. It shows that the Krama's *sequence* (krama) and the recognition's *order-less* (akrama) are two
faces of one claim: the order-less consciousness manifests as the ordered sequence, and the Kālīs of
cognition are the powers of the one consciousness. The comparison is grounded in the Krama material
(Kramasadbhāva, the Mahānayaprakāśa, the c1 studies) and the IPVV's L2 layer, and it is now possible in
full because the IPVV is translated.*

---

## PART I — THE TWO SCHOOLS

### 1.1 The Krama school

The Krama ("sequence," "succession") is one of the three great currents of nondual Kashmiri Śaivism,
beside the Spanda and the recognition. Its central doctrine is that consciousness is understood through
its *sequence* — the ordered process of cognition, emanation, and withdrawal. And its central image is
the Kālīs — the goddess-energies who are the divinized powers of the cognition-process.

The Krama's key texts include:
- **The Kramasadbhāva** ("The Arising of the Sequence") — a Krama scripture whose maṅgala (the project
  has published the auditable translation of 1.8) invokes Mahākālī.
- **The Mahānayaprakāśa** ("The Illumination of the Great Way") — Rājānaka Śitikaṇṭhācārya's
  philosophical synthesis of the Krama, with the daśākhaṇḍa (the ten divisions of cognition's
  illumination) and the four-egg cosmology.
- **The twelve Kālīs** (a Kālīkrama doctrine) — the divinized sense-organs and the powers of cognition.

The Krama's central insight is that time is not an obstacle but the very texture of consciousness.
The goddesses are not separate deities; they are the powers of the cognition-process — the sequence by
which consciousness knows, remembers, and withdraws. As Wenta's analysis (in the Torella volume) shows,
"the Twelve Kālīs constitute the analytical model of the cognitive process, with the sense-organs and
thoughts accompanying sensory perception, pointing to the ultimate oneness of reality."

### 1.2 The recognition school

The recognition school (Pratyabhijñā) holds that liberation is recognition — the felt re-cognition of
the self as the Lord. Its deepest text is the IPVV, now fully translated.

The recognition school's central claim: the essence of consciousness is vimarśa, the felt
reflexive-awareness — and recognition is the return by which the self re-cognizes itself as the Lord.
Its account of time is precise: the order-less (akrama) consciousness manifests the ordered sequence,
and the "one who knows the order without being caught in it" is the order-less support (V2O).

### 1.3 The question of the comparison

The two schools are related — both belong to the nondual Trika, both come from Kashmir, and both were
reconciled by Kṣemarāja. But they give different faces to consciousness and time:

- **The Krama:** consciousness is the *sequence* — the ordered process of cognition, the Kālīs, the
  daśākhaṇḍa.
- **The recognition:** consciousness is the *order-less* — the akrama vimarśa, the felt self-grasp that
  "knows the order without being caught in it."

**The thesis of this essay.** The two are not opposed; they are two faces of one claim. The Krama's
*sequence* is the manifest face of the recognition's *order-less*; and the Kālīs of cognition are the
powers of the one consciousness. The order-less consciousness manifests as the ordered sequence — and
the Krama studies the sequence while the recognition studies the order-less that it manifests. The
sequence and the recognition are one: the order-less is the source of the order, and the order is the
self-articulation of the order-less.

## PART II — THE CONVERGENCE: THE AKRAMA AND THE KRAMA

### 2.1 The shared claim: the source of time is not temporal

Both schools agree on a claim that is striking in its precision: **the source of time is not itself
temporal.**

**The Krama (and the TĀ's Krama passage).** The project's krama-time session drew out the Krama's
account of time from the TĀ's Krama passage:

> "The sequence (krama) of emanation, persistence, and withdrawal are understood as three processes or
> cycles (cakra) of a single process or cycle, which is the eternal activity of consciousness. **The
> source of time, this activity is not definable in temporal terms as either a process, its absence,
> both or neither.** Inexplicable in these, or any finite terms, the one reality is called the
> Inexplicable." (the TĀ's Krama passage)

The tetralemma — "a process, its absence, both, or neither" — is the logical signature of a ground
prior to its own categories. Time is not illusory (not "absence"), not substantial (not "process"), not
a mixture — it is the self-shaping of the non-temporal activity into succession.

**The recognition (the IPVV).** The IPVV gives the same account, in the transcendental argument of the
order-less support (V2O):

> "The pratibhā is the flashing — the light that runs through the ordered word-objects... It is
> seasoned with their order, touched by it. But the flashing itself is not ordered. Its support — that
> in which the flashing flashes, the one who knows the order without being caught in it — is the
> order-less, infinite-consciousness-form knower."

**The convergence.** Both hold that the source of time is not temporal. The Krama says the source is
"not definable in temporal terms as either a process, its absence, both or neither"; the IPVV says the
flashing is "seasoned with their order, touched by it, but is not the order." The order-less is the
source of the order — in both schools.

### 2.2 The Present as the crack in time

Both schools privilege the Present as the locus of practice — because it is the point where the
order-less ground is accessible.

**The Krama (and the TĀ's Krama passage):**
> "The practice is to cultivate a stable state (sthiti) of awareness of the Present in the centre
> between the past moment that has ceased and the future moment that does not yet exist... The yogi
> absorbs time into eternal consciousness even as he experiences how all things stream forth from the
> immediacy of the Eternal Now and return to it."

The session's penetration: "the Present is the crack in time through which the non-temporal ground is
met." The past has ceased; the future does not yet exist; only the Present is the actual moment of
consciousness, the pivot where eternity "descends" into time.

**The recognition (the IPVV).** The IPVV gives the same point in the time-work: the un-brokenness of
the manifestation is what makes time-dealing possible (V2A). The "one who says 'I' across all of this"
holds the past, present, and future together — the one self who saw, remembers, and will see. The
Present is where the order-less self-grasp is met, because it is the point not yet absorbed into the
past or not-yet-real as the future.

**The convergence.** The Present is the seat of the recognition: it is the crack in time through which
the akrama vimarśa is met. The Krama's practice (settle into the Present) and the recognition's return
(re-cognize the self in the Present) are the same movement.

### 2.3 The Kālīs and the powers

The deepest convergence is structural: the Krama's Kālīs and the recognition's powers are the same
cognition-process, divinized or philosophized.

**The Krama's Kālīs.** The twelve Kālīs (and the daśākhaṇḍa) are the divinized powers of the
cognition-process — the sense-organs, the thoughts, the moments of knowing. Wenta: "the Twelve Kālīs
constitute the analytical model of the cognitive process." The goddesses are not separate deities; they
are the cognition-sequence made divine — each Kālī a moment or power of consciousness.

**The recognition's powers.** The IPVV's powers (knowledge, memory, exclusion — jñāna, smṛti, apohana)
are the same cognition-process, philosophized: the powers of the one consciousness by which it knows,
remembers, and excludes. They unfold in a necessary order — manifestation → persistence → self-knowledge
→ self-expression.

**The convergence.** The Kālīs and the powers are two maps of the same reality — the cognition-process
of the one consciousness. The Krama divinizes it (the Kālīs as goddesses); the recognition philosophizes
it (the powers as operations). Both are the self-articulation of consciousness into its moments and
powers. The Kālīs of cognition ARE the powers of the one consciousness, in the Krama's mythological
register.

### 2.4 The daśākhaṇḍa and the sequence of the powers

The Mahānayaprakāśa's daśākhaṇḍa — the ten divisions of cognition's illumination — is the Krama's
structured account of the cognition-process. And it corresponds to the IPVV's account of the powers'
unfolding.

The daśākhaṇḍa maps the moments of cognition: the arising, the persistence, the withdrawal, and the
goddess-energies that govern them. The IPVV's vimarśa-arc (from the pushing-ipvv session) maps the same
process: the powers unfold in a necessary order — manifestation (jñāna) → persistence (smṛti) →
self-knowledge (apohana) → self-expression (kriyā) — and the whole converges on the one self-awareness.

**The convergence.** The daśākhaṇḍa (the ten divisions) and the vimarśa-arc (the powers in order) are
two structures of the same cognition-process. The Krama sequences it into ten divisions; the IPVV
sequences it into the powers. Both show consciousness as an ordered self-articulation, and both converge
on the one that sequences it.

## PART III — THE DIFFERENCES

The convergence is real, but the two schools differ in register and emphasis.

### 3.1 The register: mythological vs. philosophical

**The Krama is mythological.** Its account of consciousness is expressed as goddesses — the Kālīs, the
daśākhaṇḍa's divinities, the twelve. The cognition-process is *divinized*.

**The recognition is philosophical.** Its account is expressed as powers and operations — knowledge,
memory, exclusion. The cognition-process is *philosophized*.

**The difference is one of register, not substance.** The Krama's Kālīs and the recognition's powers
are the same reality, in two vocabularies. Wenta's point — the senses are "differentiated wonder"
(ciccamatkāra) — is the recognition's point that the powers are the self-awareness, expressed as
goddesses.

### 3.2 The soteriology: the sequence as the path vs. the recognition

**The Krama's soteriology is the sequence.** The Krama path works *through* the sequence — it does not
escape time but works with it. The yogin cultivates the Present, absorbs time into eternal
consciousness, and so passes through the Kālīs. The sequence is the path.

**The recognition's soteriology is the recognition.** The IPVV offers the recognition — the re-cognition
of the self as the Lord, the "effort-less" means (V3P). The recognition cuts through the sequence rather
than working through it.

**The difference.** The Krama works *through* the sequence; the recognition works *by* the recognition
of the order-less. But they are not opposed: the Krama's sequence is the manifest face of the order-less,
and the recognition of the order-less is the consummation of the Krama's path. The two soteriologies are
two phases: the Krama traverses the sequence; the recognition re-cognizes the order-less it manifests.

### 3.3 Time as divine vs. time as the mode of manifestation

**The Krama divinizes time.** Time is not an obstacle but the goddess-energies — the Kālīs who are the
cognition-process. Time is sacred.

**The recognition makes time the mode of manifestation.** The IPVV: time is the un-brokenness of the
manifestation (V2A), the appearance-order of the one light. The order is the mode by which the order-less
manifests.

**The difference.** The Krama makes time divine (the Kālīs); the recognition makes time the mode of
manifestation (the order-less's self-articulation). But again, they are one: the divinized time (the
Kālīs) IS the self-articulation of the order-less. The goddesses are the moments by which the order-less
manifests.

## PART V — THE RECONCILIATION AND THE COMMITMENT

### 5.1 The reconciliation: two schools, one consciousness

The comparative synthesis yields a reconciliation. The Krama and the recognition are two schools of one
consciousness:

- **The Krama** studies the *sequence* — the ordered cognition-process, the Kālīs, the daśākhaṇḍa.
- **The recognition** studies the *order-less* — the akrama vimarśa, the felt self-grasp that knows the
  order without being caught in it.
- **The one claim:** the order-less manifests as the order. The Krama studies the manifest sequence;
  the recognition studies the order-less source.

The two schools are not rivals; they are two registers of the same nondual consciousness — the sequence
and its order-less source. And the IPVV itself acknowledges this: the transcendental argument (V2O) that
derives the order-less support from the ordered experience is the philosophical form of the Krama's
insight that the source of time is not temporal.

### 5.2 The historical lineage

The relationship is historical as well as doctrinal. The Krama and the recognition both belong to the
Trika, and both were reconciled by Kṣemarāja (who wrote on the Spanda and the Pratyabhijñā). The
Mahānayaprakāśa explicitly links the Krama to the Śivadṛṣṭi and the Pratyabhijñā (from the project's
dossier: "the explicit yoking of the Krama-path to the Śivadṛṣṭi and the Pratyabhijñā"). The two schools
were not strangers; they were converging currents of the one nondual synthesis.

And the IPVV draws on the Krama material: the vimarśa-arc (manifestation → persistence → self-knowledge
→ self-expression) is the philosophical form of the Krama's sequence, and the sphurattā (the throbbing,
V2I) is the Krama's dynamic pulse. The recognition-philosophy is, in part, the Krama's sequence brought
to the level of the order-less.

### 5.3 The shared commitment

And the two schools share the one commitment. Both assert the universal:
- The Krama: the one cognition-process, the one consciousness through the sequence.
- The recognition: the one Lord — all order-less supports are one (the universalization, A4).

Both *make* this commitment, and neither *forces* it. The sequence of one consciousness and the sequence
of another are asserted to be one sequence; the support of one field and the support of another are
committed to be one support. **The felt→ground step — from the per-field sequence/support to the
universal — is the commitment both schools share.** This is the same wall the IPVV hits against the TĀ,
the Spanda, and every rival: the universalization is the Śaiva wager.

## PART VI — THE SCHOLARLY CONCLUSION

The comparative synthesis of the Krama school and the IPVV yields five results:

1. **The two schools are two faces of one consciousness.** The Krama studies the sequence; the
   recognition studies the order-less; the order-less manifests as the order.
2. **The convergence is precise.** Both hold that the source of time is not temporal (the Krama's
   tetralemma; the IPVV's order-less support).
3. **The Kālīs and the powers are the same cognition-process.** The Krama divinizes it; the recognition
   philosophizes it.
4. **The Present is the seat of the recognition.** Both privilege it as the crack in time where the
   order-less is met.
5. **Both share the one commitment.** The universalization is the wager both schools make and neither
   forces.

**The significance of the completed IPVV.** The comparison is grounded in the Krama material (the
Kramasadbhāva's auditable translation, the Mahānayaprakāśa's daśākhaṇḍa, the krama-time session) and the
IPVV's L2 layer (the order-less support, the vimarśa-arc, the sphurattā). The IPVV, now fully translated,
reveals the recognition-philosophy as the order-less face of the Krama's sequence — and it shows that
the two schools, the sequence and the recognition, are one. The sequence is the manifest order; the
recognition is the order-less source; and the Kālīs of cognition are the powers of the one consciousness.

*This is the magnum-opus comparative synthesis of the Krama school and the IPVV. The two schools are
two faces of one consciousness: the Krama studies the sequence, the recognition studies the order-less,
and the order-less manifests as the order. The convergence is precise (both hold the source of time is
not temporal); the Kālīs and the powers are the same cognition-process; the Present is the seat of the
recognition. Both share the one commitment (the universalization).*
