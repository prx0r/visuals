# Recognition as the Felt Re-Cognition of the Self
## The Authoritative Statement from the Īśvarapratyabhijñāvivṛtivimarśinī
### With its Expansion of Utpaladeva's Kārikās and its Correction and Completion of Ratié's Reconstruction

*This is the authoritative recognition source. It states the Pratyabhijñā thesis — recognition
(*pratyabhijñā*) is the felt re-cognition of the self — from the deepest source that survives: the
Īśvarapratyabhijñāvivṛtivimarśinī (IPVV) of Abhinavagupta, in a complete new translation. It shows,
systematically and in full, how the IPVV *expands* the root kārikās of Utpaladeva (IPK) and how it
goes *further than* Isabelle Ratié's reconstruction in *Le Soi et l'Autre*. The IPVV is taken as the
authority: every claim is anchored to a passage of the commentary, and the essay keeps the discipline
that the thesis demands — that no conclusion is inherited from a shared structure alone, and that the
felt is distinguished from the ground.*

# PART ONE — THE THESIS AND ITS SOURCES

## 1. The thesis, stated with its full support

The thesis of the whole Pratyabhijñā — and of this source — is one sentence:

> **Recognition is the felt re-cognition of the self: the self, already established, re-cognizes
> itself as the Lord — the removal of a false self-positioning, and nothing added.**

Every element of this sentence is load-bearing, and each will be argued in what follows:

- **The self, already established** (*siddha*). Recognition presupposes a self that is already there.
  It does not *bring* the self into being; it re-cognizes what never was absent. This is the
  "I was never not" of the IPVV (V1D): the self is un-cut by all three times.
- **Re-cognizes** (*pratyabhijñā*, *pratyavamarśa*). The self knows again what it always knew but
  had forgotten — not a new fact, but a return. Ratié's finding that recognition "produces no new
  knowledge" (Ch. Conclusion) is the same point.
- **Itself as the Lord** (*ātmā īśvara*). The self that is re-cognized is not a mere finite ego but
  the Lord — the one whose essence is self-aware manifestation. This is the maximal claim, and it is
  the one the IPVV argues most.
- **The removal of a false self-positioning** (*moha-nivṛtti*). Recognition subtracts. It removes the
  mis-taking of the self as a thing among things, an instrument lost in the world. This is the
  "removal" (*apohana*) that gives the third power its name.
- **Nothing added** (*apūrva-nivṛtti*). Because the self was never absent, recognition adds no
  content. What changes is not *what* is known but *where the self stands* — the positioning (*σ*),
  not the content. The IPVV states this at three registers (§IV).
- **The felt** (*camatkāra*, *rasa*, *viśrānti*). The re-cognition is not a cold formal operation; it
  is experienced as the turning of un-known nearness into the joy of union (V3P), the savouring that
  is the essence of light (IPK 1.5.11). The felt is not a side-effect; it is the register of the
  recognition.

**The three-source spine.** The thesis stands on three sources, and their relation is the structure of
this essay:

```
THE ROOT      Utpaladeva's IPK (Torella's ed.)      — the thesis in seed
   ↕
THE AUTHORITY Abhinavagupta's IPVV (our translation) — the thesis argued, expanded, completed
   ↕
THE COMPASS   Ratié's Le Soi et l'Autre             — the modern reconstruction, checked against the IPVV
```

The **root** states the seed (IPK 1.5.11: vimarśa is the essence of light; IPK 1.3.7: the three
powers). The **authority** unfolds the seed into a complete system: the powers, the vimarśas, the
opponents, the soteriology, and the liberation. The **compass** reconstructs the system from the
fragments; this essay checks that reconstruction against the authority, and shows where the authority
goes further than the compass could.

**The methodological reversal.** This essay reverses the usual order of authority. The secondary
literature (Ratié) reconstructs the Pratyabhijñā from what survives; this essay holds the IPVV — which
survives *whole* — as the measure, and checks the reconstruction against it. The finding is not that
Ratié is wrong. It is that the IPVV goes further than she could, in four directions:

1. **Proof, not report.** Ratié reports the arguments; the IPVV *runs* them (the sārūpya proof, the
   two-mirror, the anupalabdhi, the avayavin/paramāṇu refutations, the other-minds inference, the
   piśāca, the omniscient).
2. **System, not theme.** Ratié works by theme; the IPVV is organized as a system — the vimarśas, the
   powers' necessary order, the three adhikāras.
3. **Soteriology, not just epistemology.** Ratié notes recognition as "removing a mis-knowledge"; the
   IPVV *ends* in the jīvanmukta, the guru-paramparā, the dīkṣā/bhāvanā path.
4. **The whole opponent.** The IPVV gives the full pūrvapakṣa (momentariness, apoha, Vijñānavāda, the
   other-stream inference, the determination), which Ratié assembles from fragments.

---

## 2. The honesty rule

This source holds itself to the discipline that the recognition-thesis demands of its own proponents.
The Pratyabhijñā is a metaphysics; but its *method* is anti-dogmatic — it argues, in the *nanu* (objection)
→ *āha* (reply) rhythm, against every rival. The essay follows suit:

- **No tradition may inherit another's conclusion merely because they share a structure.** The felt is
  shared with naturalism; only the *ground* is the Pratyabhijñā's addition. This distinction is kept
  throughout, and it is the source of the essay's final honesty (§VIII).
- **The felt is distinguished from the ground.** The felt of one cognition proves the felt exists; it
  does not, by itself, prove one universal consciousness. The IPVV argues the ground more strongly than
  the secondary literature allows — but the final universalization remains the commitment, and this
  essay marks it as such.

# PART TWO — THE ROOT (THE IPK)

## 3. What the IPK is

The Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā ("The Stances on the Recognition of the Lord") of Utpaladeva (fl. c.
925–975 CE) is a short text of some fifty-five kārikās in four adhikāras (books):

- **Āgamādhikāra** (Book I) — the root of the recognition philosophy.
- **Jñānādhikāra** (Book II) — cognition.
- **Kriyādhikāra** (Book III) — action.
- **Tattvasaṅgrahādhikāra** (Book IV) — the collection of the thirty-six categories.

The IPK is the root text. Everything else — the IPVV, the essay, the whole Pratyabhijñā school — is a
commentary on, or reconstruction of, this root. And a crucial fact established by the Torella volume is
that **Utpaladeva, not Abhinavagupta, is the real innovator** — the Vivṛti (Utpaladeva's lost
auto-commentary) is "the real centre of gravity of the system," and most of Abhinavagupta's ideas are
the development of what Utpaladeva had already expounded. This matters for citation practice: the
thesis originates in the IPK; the IPVV is its deepest unfolding.

## 4. The root kārikās that carry the thesis

### 4.1 IPK 1.5.11 — vimarśa is the essence of light (the reflexivity claim)

The single most important kārikā for this essay. It states the reflexivity claim — that the essence
of consciousness is not a passive showing but a felt self-awareness:

> "The essential nature of light is reflective awareness (vimarśa); otherwise light, though 'coloured'
> by objects, would be similar to an insentient reality, such as crystal and so on."

The Vṛtti adds the decisive gloss: reflective awareness (*pratyavamarśa*) is the primary essence
(*mukhya ātman*) of light, "since there is no savouring (*camatkṛteḥ*)." The point:

- A light that merely *showed* the world but did not *know* that it showed it would be no different
  from a crystal — it would reflect the blue, the yellow, the jar, but not be aware of reflecting.
- What makes the light *conscious* — what makes it not a thing — is that it is *present to itself* in
  the act of manifesting.
- And that self-presence is a *savouring*, a *camatkṛti* — a felt, not merely formal, reflexivity.

This is the seed of the whole thesis: consciousness is not a showing but a *felt* showing. The felt
re-cognition is not imported into the system from outside; it is here in the root, as the very essence
of light.

### 4.2 IPK 1.3.7 — the three powers (the structure of the subject)

The self that is thus self-aware is not featureless. It is a single reality with three constitutive
powers:

> "There would be one reality whose nature is consciousness, possessing the powers of cognition,
> memory, and exclusion" (*jñāna-smṛti-apohana-śaktimān*).

Three points:

1. **Constitutive, not accidental.** The powers are not things the self *has*; they are the very
   structure of the self's operation. The self just is the act of knowing, remembering, and excluding.
2. **Apohana is the Śaiva transformation of the Buddhist apoha.** For the Buddhists, apoha is a
   negative operation of language ("cow" = "not-non-cow"); for Utpaladeva, it is a *positive* power of
   determination — the delimiting by which the self knows what it is not. The power of exclusion is
   what makes conceptual thought possible; it is not secondary but one of the three fundamental powers.
3. **The triad is jñāna-smṛti-apoha, not icchā-jñāna-kriyā.** This distinction is not pedantic. The
   IPK's constitutive triad is the *epistemic* one — the structural account of the subject. The
   volitional triad (will-knowledge-action) is a later, prefatory gloss. The IPVV's own anatomy
   confirms this (§VI): the jñānādhikāra is organized around exactly the jñāna-smṛti-apoha triad, in
   that order.

### 4.3 IPK 1.2.1–6 — the Buddhist pūrvapakṣa (the debate's textual source)

The IPK opens with the hardest rival — the Buddhist no-self argument. Ratié's Ch. 1 reconstructs it:
the perception-of-Self argument (IPK 1.2.1–2) and the inference-of-Self / memory argument (IPK
1.2.3–6). The debate the whole recognition-thesis engages is here in the root. The IPVV will run this
pūrvapakṣa in full (§V).

### 4.4 Camatkāra is Utpaladeva's concept

It is sometimes supposed that the felt register of the doctrine — the savouring, the wonder, the bliss
— is an Abhinavagupta addition. The Torella volume establishes the contrary:

> "Precisely to Utpaladeva we do owe the entrance of aesthetics into philosophical-religious
> speculation. His concept of camatkāra (wondrous enjoyment) marks a higher level of experience, which
> leaves the reality and beauty of the manifested world intact, but at the same time projects it into a
> totality whose centre is Supreme Consciousness."

The felt re-cognition is thus in the root. It is Utpaladeva's own concept, not a later addition.

## 5. What the root leaves open

The root states the thesis in seed. But it leaves open the questions this essay answers from the IPVV:

- **Does the reflexivity claim have an argument?** The IPK asserts that vimarśa is the essence; the
  IPVV *argues* it (§VI).
- **Is the "I" a construction?** The IPK says the self has the powers; the IPVV *proves* the "I" is not
  a construction (§VI).
- **What is the ground?** The IPK implies the one; the IPVV argues it via the order-less support (§VIII).
- **How does one attain it?** The IPK gestures; the IPVV gives the soteriology (§VII).

The root is the seed; the IPVV is the full tree. This essay's task is to show how the tree grows from
the seed — and, where Ratié reconstructed the tree from fallen leaves, to measure the reconstruction
against the living whole.

# PART THREE — THE COMPASS (RATIÉ'S RECONSTRUCTION)

## 6. What Ratié's Le Soi et l'Autre does

Isabelle Ratié's *Le Soi et l'Autre: Identité, différence et altérité dans la philosophie de la
Pratyabhijñā* (Brill, 2011) is the most rigorous modern reconstruction of the Pratyabhijñā. Working
from the IPK, its Vṛtti, and the fragments of the lost Vivṛti, Ratié reconstructs the doctrine with
philological care and philosophical precision. Her book is organized around the paradox of
self-recognition: we recognize our face, our past, our thought — but what is it to recognize *oneself*?

Her findings converge exactly on the root thesis, and they are worth stating before the IPVV is brought
forward, so that the measure of the IPVV's further reach is clear.

## 7. Ratié's findings, chapter by chapter

### 7.1 Ch. 1 — The Buddhist critique of the Self

Ratié reconstructs the Buddhist no-self (anātmavāda) position that the rest of the book refutes — the
IPK's Buddhist pūrvapakṣa. The hardest rival denies the Self; but self-luminosity (svaprakāśa) is
*shared* — the debate is whether it implies a Self. Recognition is an ordinary felt cognition.

> "la conscience ne s'y vise donc pas objectivement… mais s'y sait être sur un mode parfaitement
> immédiat ou 'non positionnel'." (*Consciousness knows itself in a non-positional mode.*)

### 7.2 Ch. 2 — Cognitive synthesis

The Buddhist has shown no Self; Ratié shows the Pratyabhijñā's answer: the Self is needed for the
synthesis of cognitions. Without a persisting subject, memory and recognition across moments are
impossible. Memory is re-appropriation (never truly lost); vimarśa is latent in every perception; the
synthesizing self is non-objective — "blind, deaf, mute" cognitions cannot unify themselves.

> "cette 'synthèse des formes antérieure et postérieure du Je'… par laquelle le sujet percevant se
> saisit à la fois comme la conscience d'où émerge l'objet et comme la conscience où il se résorbe."
> (*The synthesis of the anterior and posterior forms of the I.*)

### 7.3 Ch. 3 — The absolute subject (IPK 1.3.7) ⚠️ corrected

The core positive claim. Ratié centers this on IPK 1.3.7 — the "heart of the treatise." And here this
project has made a correction, confirmed by the IPVV's own structure: 1.3.7 is the **jñāna–smṛti–apoha**
triad (knowledge, memory, exclusion) — "possessing the powers of knowledge, memory and exclusion" —
**NOT** the icchā/jñāna/kriyā triad. The volitional triad is the prefatory/structural reading, not the
text of 1.3.7. The IPVV's jñānādhikāra is organized around exactly the jñāna-smṛti-apoha triad, in
that order (§VI).

### 7.4 Ch. 4 — The Self, the cognitions, their relation

The "I" is a cognition of recognition (pratyabhijñāpratyaya); recognition is *experience, not
construction*; identity = dynamism; the never-lost identity.

> "une reconnaissance (pratyabhijñā) qui… n'est pas une pure et simple construction abstraite, mais
> une expérience." (*Recognition is an experience, not an abstract construction.*)

### 7.5 Ch. 5 — Common idealism

Śaiva and Buddhist idealism share consciousness-fundamental. The shared ākāra thesis *implies* a
permanent subject; the object is re-cognized as one's own luminous self (tādātmya).

> "Il y a donc une identité (tādātmya) foncière du reflet et du miroir… hors du miroir, le reflet
> n'est rien." (*The fundamental identity of reflection and mirror.*)

### 7.6 Ch. 6 — The mirror, the dreamer, and the yogin (IPK 1.5.1–9)

The famous chapter. Consciousness *creates* (yogin) not *reveals* (lamp/mirror); freedom is established
by self-awareness, not hypothesis. The key image: the yogin — for the Śaiva, consciousness does not
merely reveal (like a lamp) but creates (like a yogin projecting).

> "cidātmaiva hi devo 'ntaḥsthitam icchāvaśād bahiḥ / yogīva nirupādānam arthajātam prakāśayet."
> (*Like a yogin, consciousness manifests its objects by its free will, without material cause.*)

### 7.7 Ch. 7 — Freedom (svātantrya) ⭐ the load-bearing chapter

Freedom is the essence; vimarśa = freedom + felicity + wonder; identity = freedom to become Other;
alienation = misrecognition of freedom.

> "camatkāra… c'est la divine surprise… dans laquelle on s'étonne moins de l'objet lui-même que de la
> **satisfaction qu'on se découvre éprouver** à son égard." (*The satisfaction one discovers oneself
> feeling.*)

### 7.8 Ch. 8 — The other: solipsism or intersubjectivity?

The other is *recognized* (not inferred) as my own subjectivity outside myself; resists objectivation;
monism founds compassion.

> "être conscient de l'existence d'autrui, c'est reconnaître hors de soi sa propre subjectivité."
> (*Being conscious of the other's existence is recognizing one's own subjectivity outside oneself.*)

### 7.9 Ch. 9 — The ontological status of difference

Difference is real yet non-dual — the deployment of the Self's dynamic essence, an upāya; the
"infinitesimal gap from oneself."

### 7.10 Conclusion — Identity, or the freedom to become other

One solution (freedom); identity = freedom-to-become-other; recognition = the return that shows (not
demonstrates) the already-present Self.

> "on ne produit aucune connaissance nouvelle chez cet individu." (*One produces no new knowledge.*)
> "Le traité… ne démontre pas ; il montre." (*The treatise does not demonstrate; it shows.*)

## 8. The three load-bearing findings

Three of Ratié's findings converge on this essay's thesis:

1. **Recognition is experience, not construction** (Ch. 4) — the felt, experiential nature of
   recognition.
2. **Recognition produces no new knowledge** (Ch. Conclusion) — the removal thesis: recognition
   subtracts a mis-knowledge, it does not add a fact.
3. **The essence is freedom** (Ch. 7) — svātantrya as the being of consciousness, of which recognition
   is the return.

Together these state the thesis of this essay: recognition is the felt re-cognition of the
already-present self, a change in positioning and not in content. **The compass points the same way as
the root.**

## 9. Where Ratié stops

But Ratié works from fragments. And this is the crucial point for the essay's claim of the IPVV's
authority:

- Ratié can report that recognition *removes a mis-knowledge*; she cannot, from the surviving
  fragments, show the *argument* by which the IPVV establishes the non-constructed "I" that the removal
  presupposes.
- Ratié can report the yogin image; she cannot give the *full refutation* of externalism (the
  avayavin/paramāṇu arguments) that the IPVV runs.
- Ratié can report the three powers; she cannot show the *vimarśa structure* — the necessary order of
  the powers and the pivot of the apohana — that organizes the IPVV's second volume.
- Ratié can report that recognition is the return; she cannot give the *soteriology* — the dīkṣā,
  the jīvanmukta, the guru-paramparā — that closes the IPVV.

**These survive — in the IPVV, whole.** It is to the IPVV that the essay now turns, and from it that
the thesis will be *argued* rather than merely reported.

# PART FOUR — THE AUTHORITY (THE IPVV)

## 10. What the IPVV is

The Īśvarapratyabhijñāvivṛtivimarśinī of Abhinavagupta is his enormous commentary on Utpaladeva's
(mostly lost) Vivṛti — some thirty-four thousand lines across three volumes. It is organized as a
running commentary on the IPK, glossing each kārikā and then arguing, in the *nanu* (objection) → *āha*
(reply) rhythm that gives the text its dialectical texture.

**The structure (the IPVV's own anatomy):**

```
Vol 1 (M00020)  jñānādhikāra  — the upoddhāta, the self-as-Lord, memory, the reflexion core
Vol 2 (M00021)  jñānādhikāra  — the caturtho → aṣṭamo vimarśas: memory/aham, the jñānaśakti
                                (manifestation), the smṛti/apohana, the one-support
Vol 3 (M00022)  kriyādhikāra  — the action-section (ending the kāryakāraṇabhāvavimarśa)
                āgamādhikāra  — the teaching-section (the śivatattva, the padārthavarga)
                tattvādhikāra — the categories-section (the closing sum, the ||samāptā||)
```

**The nanu→āha skeleton.** The IPVV's whole texture is dialectical — every passage is an objection
(nanu) met by a reply (āha), usually against the Buddhist, the Vaiśeṣika/Kāṇāda, the Advaitin, or the
Sāṃkhya. This is the IPVV's own internal rhythm, and it is the first thing that distinguishes the IPVV
from the IPK: where the IPK states, the IPVV argues.

**Why it is the authority.** The IPVV is the deepest primary source for the doctrine, and it survives
*whole*. Where Ratié reconstructs, the IPVV states and argues. This essay therefore treats the IPVV as
authoritative and checks the reconstruction against it.

## 11. The three registers of the recognition-thesis

The IPVV states the recognition-thesis not once but at three distinct registers. That it does so is
itself evidence that the thesis is the heart of the system: the same move, at three levels, is not an
occasional claim but the structure of the whole. And — decisively — all three registers are the *same
move*: **content unchanged, positioning changed.**

### 11.1 Register 1 — Foregrounding (V1E)

The first register is spatial. Against the Buddhist's worry that recognition must *establish*
something, the IPVV insists it merely foregrounds what is already there:

> "The pramāṇa here is not establishing something new. It is *foregrounding* what is already present
> but hidden. The recognition does not bring the Lord into being; it brings the Lord, who was always
> near and always present, into the foreground of our own awareness."

The recognition changes where the self stands in the field of awareness — from an instrument lost among
the things of the body and the world to the primary thing seen. Nothing is added; the same content is
re-arranged. **This is the removal thesis in its simplest form**: the false self-positioning (the self
as a thing among things) is removed, and the self stands where it always was.

### 11.2 Register 2 — The "I was never not" (V1D)

The second register is temporal, and it grounds the removal in the eternity of the self:

> "The manifestation of the self is not unprecedented. It does not begin to shine at some moment; it
> was always shining… The self is un-cut by all three times: past, present, and future. It did not
> come into being, and it will not pass away."

Because the self was never absent, there is nothing for recognition to add — nothing could be added to
an eternal self. The "unprecedented manifestation," the appearance of something new, is only apparent;
it rests on the eternal, always-manifested knower. **Here the removal thesis is grounded**: recognition
cannot be an attainment, because the self never was not.

### 11.3 Register 3 — The lover-union (V3P)

The third register is aesthetic and relational, and it ties the thesis to the felt:

> "The lover stands near, sought by the slender woman; but as long as he is not recognized, he cannot
> be enjoyed. So the world-Lord, always present, always near, is not experienced in his own splendor
> until the recognition arises… The single act of recognition turns the un-known nearness into the joy
> of union."

The lover is near — the content is present, never absent. What changes with recognition is the
relation: from un-known nearness to the joy of union. And this change is *felt* — it is the joy, the
savouring, the *camatkāra*. The recognition does not bring the lover; it turns the near into the
enjoyed. **This is the removal thesis in its fullest form**: the felt re-cognition.

### 11.4 The three are one

Foregrounding (spatial), "I was never not" (temporal-eternal), lover-union (relational-aesthetic): all
three are *content unchanged, positioning changed*. This is the thesis of this essay, stated in the
authority itself at three registers. The recognition is not the addition of a content but the
re-positioning of the self — and, in the third register, that re-positioning is experienced as a felt
joy. **The felt re-cognition is not a metaphor imposed on the system; it is the system's own statement,
in its deepest source.**

# PART FIVE — THE EXPANSION OF THE IPK (WHAT THE IPVV ADDS TO THE ROOT)

The IPVV is not merely a commentary; it is an *expansion*. Where the IPK states, the IPVV argues;
where the IPK asserts, the IPVV proves. This part shows, systematically, how the IPVV expands the root
kārikās.

## 12. The expansion of the reflexivity claim (IPK 1.5.11 → the vimarśa as the heart)

The IPK states that vimarśa is the essence of light. The IPVV expands this into the heart of the whole
doctrine (V2H):

> "The manifestation (prakāśa) is not a passive, inert light. Its very nature is the *reflexive-
> awareness* (vimarśa) — the light's own grasp of itself. Without the vimarśa, the manifestation would
> be like crystal: it would show the world, but it would not know that it showed it; it would be inert.
> What makes the light *light* — what makes it consciousness and not a thing — is that it is aware of
> itself in the very act of manifesting."

**What the IPVV adds:** the proof. The IPK asserts the reflexivity claim; the IPVV proves it by the
swift-action argument — in quick reading, rapid motion, hurried speech, we abandon and seize without
pausing to reflect, yet these acts are preceded by a re-reflection (parāmarśa), and this shows the
knower and the known are not two separate things but one act of the self-aware consciousness (V2H).
The swift act is the empirical proof that reflexivity is intrinsic, not an added second act.

**And the identification with the supreme Word.** The IPVV identifies the reflexive-awareness with the
parā-vāk, the supreme speech:

> "This self-knowing is the parā-vāk, the supreme speech, from which all the lower levels of speech
> (the madhyamā, the vaikharī) descend. It is the Lord's freedom (svātantrya) and his lordship
> (aiśvarya)." (V2H)

The IPK's reflexivity claim becomes, in the IPVV, the identification of consciousness with the Word and
with freedom — a systematic identification the root only implies.

## 13. The expansion of the three powers (IPK 1.3.7 → the vimarśa structure)

The IPK states the three powers. The IPVV is *organized* by them: the jñānādhikāra runs through the
vimarśas of exactly the jñāna-smṛti-apoha triad, in that order. And it shows the powers have a
*necessary order* — not a random list but the self's own unfolding:

> "The powers are not a random list; they form a necessary ORDER — manifestation (jñāna) → persistence
> (smṛti) → self-knowledge (apohana) → self-expression (kriyā). Each presupposes the previous. The
> order of the vimarśas IS the order of the self's unfolding." (the internal arc)

**What the IPVV adds:** the *anatomy*. The IPK lists the powers; the IPVV shows their order, their
interdependence, and their root. And it shows the pivot — the apohana, the power of exclusion, turns
the sequence from the powers to the *self*:

> "The apohana is the pivot of the jñānādhikāra because it turns the sequence from the POWERS
> (jñāna/smṛti) to the SELF (the 'I' that underlies them). Its deepest result is that the 'I' is not a
> construction (V2L) and the inner appearance stands firm across all cognition (V2N)."

## 14. The expansion of the Buddhist pūrvapakṣa (IPK 1.2 → the full refutation)

The IPK states the Buddhist no-self argument. The IPVV runs it — and answers it — in full:

- **V1L** (memory-error, the self-luminous dṛk): the yogin's knowledge of another is a *perception*
  (yogipratyakṣa), not an inference — in the union the two are one.
- **V1M** (the determination cannot reach outside): the Buddhist's last resort — the determination
  (adhyavasāya) by which the cognition is supposed to establish an external — is dismantled. The
  "external" is only the strength of the practice-impression; the outwardness is a manner of seeing, not
  a thing established; the determination is error-form that cannot establish on either the inert or the
  non-inert side.
- **V2C** (the cognition is never the object of another): the two-mirror argument, the sārūpya proof,
  the ajaḍa pramātṛ.
- **V2F** (the other-minds inference refuted): the candana/agni example; the "being-gone-to-the-other"
  as un-inferable; only the aimed action is the wish's effect.
- **V2G** (the external refuted, the yogin-creation): the avayavin and paramāṇu refutations; the
  dream-inversion.

**What the IPVV adds:** the *opponent in full*. The IPK gestures at the Buddhist; the IPVV gives the
entire pūrvapakṣa — and answers it with arguments, not assertions. This is the first and clearest sense
in which the IPVV expands the IPK: it turns the seed's implied debate into a complete dialectic.

## 15. The expansion of recognition-not-attainment (the precise mechanism)

Ratié's Ch. Conclusion finds that recognition produces no new knowledge. The IPVV gives the *precise
mechanism* (V1E):

> "The pramāṇa here is not establishing something new. It is *foregrounding* what is already present
> but hidden… the 'see, see' — turning the attention upon what was always before us."

**What the IPVV adds:** the *mechanism*. Ratié reports that recognition produces no new knowledge; the
IPVV states *how*: foregrounding — "not an instrument lost among the things, but the primary thing we
see." This is the master-key's "inward turn" made concrete: the self-apprehension that was turned
outward into the world (the instrument) is re-turned to the self (the primary). The lover-image (V3P)
is the same mechanism in aesthetic form.

## 16. The expansion of the yogin-creation (IPK 1.5.7 → the full refutation of externalism)

Ratié's Ch. 6 reports the yogin image. The IPVV gives the full refutation of externalism (V2G):

> "The whole (avayavin) cannot stand: a jar has shadow on one side and sun on the other, its middle
> covered and its sides uncovered — so the one whole that was to bear all these contradictory states is
> impossible. And the atom (paramāṇu) fares no better: the middle atom, being part-less, cannot have
> two sides, one sun-lit and one shaded; the two-part atom collapses back into the whole it was meant
> to replace; and the parts run on to infinity."

**What the IPVV adds:** the *proof*. The IPK asserts consciousness creates like a yogin; the IPVV refutes
externalism (the avayavin and the paramāṇu) and *proves* the creation-power by the dream:

> "The dream proves that the consciousness alone can create an object-mass, by its will, without a
> material cause." (V2G)

And it extends the mirror-image into the kriyādhikāra (V3A): the timeless Lord "manifests manifold
sequences," and the mirror shows sequence without division precisely because it is one.

## 17. The expansion of freedom (IPK 1.5.10–15 → the māheśvarya)

Ratié's Ch. 7 finds freedom is the essence. The IPVV *closes* the freedom into the māheśvarya, the
great-Lordship (V2S):

> "The great-Lordship is nothing but the reflexive-awareness — the one who is aware of himself,
> fixedly, is the Lord; and that reflexive-awareness is the God's own pure knowledge-and-action. The
> great-Lordship is not an added power; it is the self-awareness itself." (V2S)

**What the IPVV adds:** the *systematic identity*. The IPK implies freedom is the essence; the IPVV
states it as the conclusion of the whole jñānādhikāra: the Lordship = self-awareness = the knower. This
is the internal arc — the powers reduce to the self-awareness, and the self-awareness is the Lord.

# PART SIX — THE ARGUMENT (THE IPVV'S PROOF OF THE INTRINSIC "I")

This is the heart of the essay's claim that the IPVV goes *further than* Ratié. Ratié reports that
recognition removes a mis-knowledge; the IPVV *argues* the "I" that the removal presupposes.

## 18. The objection

The reflexive-awareness, the IPVV has said, is the manifestation's own nature. But the reflexive-
awareness is *word-joined* — it takes the form of the word "I." And the word-joined, it might seem, is
a construction (*vikalpa*). If the Lord's own self-awareness were a construction, it would be of the
*māyā*-form, the saṃsāric — which cannot be. The objection is precise, and it threatens the whole
doctrine: **does the felt "I" of recognition reduce to a mental construction?**

## 19. The answer: the "I" is not a construction

The IPVV answers by defining construction and showing the "I" is none of it (V2L):

> "The construction is of three kinds: it joins what is distinct (as one joins molasses, curd, and
> pepper); it constructs the one in many ways; or it splits off a doubted, other form. All three are
> operations of division and joining, of this-and-that. But the 'I'-recollection is none of these. It
> does not join a distinct 'this' to a distinct 'I'; it is the single self-grasp, resting in itself. It
> is the pure self-awareness that underlies every construction, the very thing that makes construction
> possible, without itself being one."

**The force of this is precise.** Construction, whatever else it is, is an operation on parts — it
joins distinct things, or multiplies a one, or splits off a doubted other. The "I"-grasp is none of
these:

1. Construction joins distinct things → the "I" does not (it is not "this I" built from a distinct
   "this" and "I").
2. Construction constructs the one into many → the "I" does not (it *is* the one).
3. Construction splits off a doubted other → the "I" does not (it is the un-doubted foundation).

Therefore the "I"-grasp is *not a construction*. And because every higher-order operation — every model,
every self-attribution, every re-description of experience — is a joining or a splitting, the "I"-grasp
cannot be a higher-order operation at all. **It is the self-presence that higher-order operations
presuppose, the root that makes them possible without itself being one.**

## 20. The meeting of the structural rival

Here the IPVV meets its deepest rival, and it does so not by refutation but by absorption. The rival's
position — in its ancient Buddhist and its modern form — is that reflexivity is *structural*: experience
can include its own occurrence, can fold back and represent itself, and this folding is all the self
that exists. This is the position that Ñāṇavīra, in the modern Pāli tradition, states with great force,
and it carries a warning directed precisely at the Pratyabhijñā: that Abhinavagupta "converted the
reflexive self-identity of a conditioned structure into an ultimately existing universal Self."

**The IPVV's answer is not to deny that the folding occurs. It is to deny that the folding is the
root.**

- The *structural* reflexivity — experience folding back and including itself — is a *construction-
  capacity*: it is the higher-order operation, the joining and splitting that the self-model theories
  describe.
- The IPVV grants that this capacity exists. What it denies is that it is the root.
- The "I"-grasp is not one more instance of the folding; it is the self-presence that the folding
  presupposes — the "pure self-awareness that underlies every construction, the very thing that makes
  construction possible, without itself being one."

**Structural reflexivity is the folding; intrinsic self-manifestation is the self-grasp that the
folding serves.** In the terms of the modern debate: a self-model explains the *content* of the "I" —
its self-attributions, its self-representation. It cannot explain the self-grasp that is aware of the
model as its own. The IPVV's argument is exactly this: the model is a construction; the "I"-recollection
is what is *not* a construction; therefore the model cannot be the root of the "I." The rival describes
the capacity; the IPVV points to what the capacity presupposes.

## 21. The confirmation in the IPVV's own anatomy

That this is not a reading imposed on the text is confirmed by the IPVV's own structure. The
jñānādhikāra is organized around exactly the triad the IPK states — jñāna-smṛti-apoha, in that order.
And the powers unfold in a necessary order: manifestation → persistence → self-knowledge →
self-expression. The *apohana* is the pivot: it is the power by which the self excludes what is not
itself, and its deepest result is the argument just given — that the "I" is not a construction. The
IPVV is not a loose collection of arguments; it is one arc, the powers unfolding in order, the
self-awareness their root, and the whole converging on the one knower. The non-constructed "I" is not a
single proof; it is the pivot of the entire second volume.

# PART SEVEN — WHERE THE IPVV GOES FURTHER THAN BOTH IPK AND RATIÉ

The IPVV is not just deeper on the root and the reconstruction; it adds *whole regions* that neither the
IPK nor Ratié's book (focused on the recognition/subject/other) develops. These are the essay's claim
to originality: the IPVV's epistemology of action, its tattva-ontology, and its soteriology.

## 22. The kriyādhikāra: the epistemology and metaphysics of action (V3A–I)

Ratié's book is focused on the jñāna side — the subject, the other, identity. It treats action only in
passing. The IPVV devotes a whole adhikāra to it:

- **V3A** — Action, time, sequence: the timeless that manifests order. The Lord is beyond sequence, yet
  manifests manifold sequences; the mirror shows order without division.
- **V3B** — The action-cognitions as bhedābheda: "this is the very same body," the distinction-and-
  non-distinction vimarśa.
- **V3C–F** — The pramāṇavimarśa: the valid means operates on the appearance; error is a matter of the
  reflexive-awareness being un-contradicted; the knower is the ground of everything.
- **V3G–I** — The kāryakāraṇatāvimarśa: **causality is the conscious knower's agency**. The inert
  cannot be a cause; inference holds across knowers because the Lord creates the appearances as one;
  the will is cause, agent, and action; the difference of things is real, not mere ignorance.

**The crown of the kriyādhikāra (V3-I).** The deepest objection — if all is one, how is action
possible? — is answered by the will-to-do (the parāmarśa that joins agent and object), and the
Advaita's "un-explainable ignorance" is refuted:

> "The one-form Brahman cannot have an ignorance that is the mere absence of something else. Nor can
> the separate souls exist… If the ignorance is called 'un-explainable' (anirvācya), then we still do
> not know whose it is; 'it shines and yet is un-explainable' is the speech of a yakṣa… Even if we
> appeal to the revealed teaching, that teaching too would be ignorance."

**This is a full epistemology and metaphysics of action that neither the IPK nor Ratié develops.** It
is the essay's first "goes further" region.

## 23. The āgamādhikāra: the tattva-ontology (V3J–M)

The IPVV devotes an adhikāra to the categories of the world, expounded from the Lord's teaching:

- **V3J** — The Śivatattva as "the body of every category"; the inseparable jñāna-kriyā pair.
- **V3K** — The kañcukas (niyati, kāla, rāga, vidyā, kalā) as the coverings of the pure self; the
  twenty-three categories of the known.
- **V3L** — The knowers and the three malas (āṇava, māyīya, kārma); the paśu and the pati.
- **V3M** — The states (pralayākala, suṣupta, svapna, jāgrat) and the way to the fourth.

**This is the thirty-six-tattva ontology, systematically expounded.** Neither the IPK nor Ratié's book
develops it.

## 24. The tattvasaṅgrahādhikāra: the collection and the soteriology (V3N–P)

The IPVV closes with the collection and the liberation:

- **V3N** — "The own-self alone of all beings is the one Lord, the maheśvara"; how the many arise from
  the one by freedom.
- **V3O** — The bond and the liberation: the paśu's asādhāraṇī (un-common) creation, sustained by the
  Lord's sādhāraṇā (common) creation; the abandonment of construction as the path.
- **V3P** — The closing sum: the recognition-path, the guru-paramparā, the seventeen ślokas of the
  vivṛtikāra, the jīvanmukta.

**This is the soteriology — the dīkṣā, the jīvanmukta, the guru-paramparā — that neither the IPK nor
Ratié supplies.** It is the essay's final "goes further" region, and it completes the recognition-thesis

# PART EIGHT — THE DEEP CROSS-EXAMINATIONS (THE IPVV AGAINST THE RIVALS)

The pushing-ipvv sessions have hounded the IPVV against its rivals, forcing its own reasoning and
finding where it resolves, deepens, or hits the same wall. These cross-examinations are the essay's
adversarial depth.

## 26. The IPVV vs. the Tantrāloka's cruxes

Probing the IPVV with the Tantrāloka's tough cruxes reveals that the IPVV is the sharper, more
dialectical work:

| Crux | Tantrāloka | IPVV | Verdict |
|------|-----------|------|---------|
| 1. Universality gap | asserted | **argued** (V2C sārūpya; V3C the blue-to-me-not-Caitra; V2G from-within) | **RESOLVED better** |
| 2. Memory→identity | asserted | **answered** (V2L the non-constructed "I") | **RESOLVED better** |
| 3. Karma vs. freedom | freedom-contracted | **deepened** (V3B the un-thwarted; true-by-stability-use) | resolved + sharpened |
| 4. Two-levels | binary | **threefold** (satya/saṃvṛtisatya/asatya) | **made more precise** |
| 5. Recognition | reversal of direction | **foregrounding** (V1E); the lover (V3P) | **made concrete** |
| 6. Felt→ground | asserted | **asserted** (the one light) | **SAME WALL** |

**The universality gap, resolved.** The Tantrāloka asserted the step from "manifest to me" to "the
universal perceiver." The IPVV *argues* it (V3C): the blue appears "to me and not to Caitra" — and this
very fact, which the Tantrāloka treated as the index of finitude, is inverted into the proof of
consciousness as the ground. If the blue stood by its own power, it would shine for everyone; it shines
for me, so it stands by the power of consciousness. And "the one light rises, born of means, object and
knower" — the knower, means, and known are one act.

**The memory→identity step, answered.** The Tantrāloka asserted a persisting subject. The IPVV answers
the mechanistic objection (V2L): source-attribution is a construction, but the "I" that the construction
serves is the non-constructed self-grasp. The mechanism explains the *content* of memory; it cannot
explain the non-constructed "I" that holds it together.

**The two-levels, made precise.** The Tantrāloka's binary (ground/contraction) becomes, in the IPVV, a
threefold: satya (true), saṃvṛtisatya (conventional-true, "coordinate with the real"), asatya (untrue).
The conventional is not a fiction; it is real-but-not-substantial. This answers the reality-vs-illusion
objection more precisely than the Tantrāloka.

**Recognition, made concrete.** The Tantrāloka said recognition is the reversal of direction; the IPVV
says it is *foregrounding* — "not an instrument lost among the things, but the primary thing we see."
The lover-image is the master-key in aesthetic form.

## 27. The IPVV's co-opting of the Buddhist

Against the Buddhist, Abhinavagupta does not refute; he *co-opts*. The Buddhist's own tools are
accepted and turned to the Śaiva conclusion:

1. **The inference-theory (V2E):** the Vijñānavādin uses the reflection to infer an external ground;
   Abhinavagupta keeps the inference-structure but changes its conclusion — "even the error, being a
   relation, is a pramā." The determination of the external is valid *by its bond to the thing*.
2. **The other-minds inference (V2F):** the Buddhist hopes to infer another's mind; Abhinavagupta uses
   the Buddhist's *own* inference-rules (the candana/agni fragrance; the "being-gone-to-the-other" as
   un-inferable) to show the inference fails. Then: "the appearances alone suffice."
3. **The dream (V2G) — the deepest co-opting:** the Buddhist uses the dream to prove the world is
   unreal; Abhinavagupta agrees the dream is uncaused but re-reads it — the dream proves that
   consciousness *creates* an object-mass by its will. The Buddhist's own example of illusion becomes
   the proof of the creation-power.
4. **Self-awareness (V2C):** the Buddhist's own svasamvedana is adopted and deepened — cognition is
   self-luminous and never the object of another.

**The meta-gem:** the co-opting reveals that the Buddhist and the Śaiva are *two readings of the same
starting point* — appearances (ābhāsa) as all we have. The Buddhist reads them negatively (unreal,
no-self); the Śaiva reads them positively (the real self-articulation of consciousness). **The debate
is not a clash of worldviews but a dispute over the valence of the same appearances.** The Buddhist is
not the enemy of the recognition-thesis but its co-owner of the starting point.

## 28. The recognition-formulations and the σ-flip

The IPVV's three registers of recognition (foregrounding, "I was never not," the lover-union) are the
same move at three registers — *content unchanged, positioning changed*. This is exactly the formal
σ-flip (AM0): the tuple content identical, σ changes. The IPVV states our formalization at three
registers, and the third (the lover) ties it to the felt/rasa.

And the IPVV supports the "internally manifested forms, not the filter" account (V2D: "to know a thing
is to be the light"; V2G: "the Lord manifests the world from within himself"; V3C: "the one light rises,
born of means, object and knower"). **Our formulation is not imposed on the IPVV; it IS the IPVV's own
account.**

# PART NINE — THE HONEST LIMIT (THE COMMITMENT)

## 29. The crux: the felt does not, by itself, entail the ground

An authoritative source owes its reader its limit. The crux is the one raised at the outset: the felt
is shared with naturalism; the universal-ground interpretation is the Pratyabhijñā's *addition*, not a
consequence. The felt of one cognition proves the felt exists; it does not, by itself, prove that there
is *one universal* consciousness whose felt it is.

The IPVV argues the ground more strongly than the secondary literature allows — but it does not force
the final step.

## 30. The IPVV's strongest argument for the ground

The IPVV's best argument for the felt→ground step is NOT the assertion of the "one light" (V3C) but the
**transcendental argument of the order-less support** (V2O) plus the **causal proof of the one knower**
(V2P):

> "The *pratibhā* is the flashing — the light that runs through the ordered word-objects, through all
> the varied things we name and know. It is seasoned with their order, touched by it. But the flashing
> itself is not ordered. Its support — that in which the flashing flashes, the one who knows the order
> without being caught in it — is the order-less, infinite-consciousness-form knower. And that knower is
> the great Lord." (V2O)

**This is a genuine transcendental argument.** Ordered experience is given; but order requires an
order-less support — the one who knows the order without being caught in it. You cannot have ordered
appearances without the order-less light in which they flash. The one support is not posited arbitrarily;
it is required by the very structure of ordered experience. And it is confirmed by the causal proof of
the one knower (V2P): memory requires the one who remembers — "the valid means is the self-consciousness
of the one knower." The anvaya-vyatireka (presence-absence): the dealing of the world is fit *with* the
one support, not without it.

**This DERIVES the need for an order-less, self-conscious support from the structure of ordered,
memory-bearing experience.** It is a real argument, not a bare assertion of the "one light."

## 31. The precise limit

But here is the limit, and this source states it plainly:

- **What the IPVV's argument ESTABLISHES:** that any ordered, memory-bearing cognition REQUIRES an
  order-less self-conscious support — a knower who is not caught in the order. This is a strong argument
  for a *transcendental* one-support-per-experience (the order-less knower of THIS ordered field).
- **What it does NOT establish:** that the order-less knower of one field is *numerically identical*
  with the order-less knower of another field. The argument proves each ordered field requires AN
  order-less support; it does not prove ONE order-less support for all fields.

**The move from "each field needs an order-less support" to "all fields share one support" requires the
further step** — the argument that the "I" has no counter-correlate (V2C: it is never the object of
another, so there is no internal boundary by which one "I"-grasp could be distinct from another). And
that step, the IPVV argues but does not force. **It is the Śaiva commitment** — the point at which the
recognition-thesis passes from consequence to wager.

The IPVV narrows the gap more than Ratié's reconstruction allows: the order-less support is *derived*
from ordered experience, not merely asserted. But the final universality — the numerical identity of all
supports — is the commitment. This is true of the IPK, the IPVV, and Ratié alike; and it is the point at
which the essay's own discipline ("no tradition may inherit another's conclusion merely because they
share a structure") requires the argument to stop and mark its boundary. **The felt re-cognition of the
self is established as the removal of a false self-positioning, nothing added; the universalization of
that self is the wager the tradition makes and this source holds open.**
