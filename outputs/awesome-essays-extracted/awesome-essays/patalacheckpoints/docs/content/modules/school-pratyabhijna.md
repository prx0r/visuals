# Pratyabhijñā — the School of Recognition

> **School module** · `tradition: pratyabhijna` · foundation

## QUICK (the school in one idea)

Pratyabhijñā ("Recognition") is the philosophical current of Somānanda → Utpaladeva →
Abhinavagupta (9th–11th c. Kashmir) that turns an ordinary mental event — *recognising
something as what it already is* — into the whole structure of liberation.

Its claim: **you are not working toward a new self; you are recognising the self you already
are.** Just as you might fail to recognise a friend who stands before you in disguise, the
finite subject fails to recognise that it is unrestricted consciousness. Every perception,
memory, and moment of knowing is actually this consciousness showing itself — but we take the
show for a separate self looking out at a separate world.

It is called the *semantic control corpus* of the whole project because it gives unusually
**precise, philosophical definitions** of the vocabulary (prakāśa, vimarśa, svātantrya,
recognition) that the other schools — Trika, Spanda, Krama — all draw on. It is the lens
through which the entire tradition can be read cleanly.

## DEEP (the full introduction)

### 1. The lineage: three generations of one argument

The school's history is short and concentrated:

- **Somānanda** (c. 900–950) — the *Śivadṛṣṭi* ("Vision of Śiva") opens the enquiry: is the
  world real and is it a manifestation of consciousness? He defends the thesis that everything
  is Śiva's own activity.
- **Utpaladeva** (c. 925–975) — the *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā* (IPK) with its own Vṛtti gives
  the argument its rigorous, aphoristic form. This is the founding text of the school.
- **Abhinavagupta** (c. 975–1025) — the *Īśvarapratyabhijñāvivṛti* (IPVV) writes the
  massive extended commentary and integrates the whole into his Trika/Tantrāloka synthesis.

Each generation refines the same claim: consciousness is intrinsically reflexive, and its
apparent limitation is a forgetting that recognition undoes.

### 2. The founding text: the IPK

The *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā* ("Verses on the Recognition of the Lord") is the school's
scripture — short, dense, and rigorous (Torella's is the critical edition + translation). Its
kārikās run the whole system: what perception is, what memory is, how error arises, why the
subject appears limited, and how recognition liberates.

Key moves in the text:
- **Perception presupposes a knower** who is present to itself before any object.
- **Memory and imagination show creative freedom** (*svātantrya*) — the mind can hold and
  combine forms; this same freedom, unconstrained, is how consciousness manifests a world.
- **Error is not ignorance of a fact but misrecognition** — a failure to identify what is
  already present.
- **Recognition completes perception** — the world is re-known as the self's own expression.

### 3. The philosophical precision (why this school matters)

The Pratyabhijñā is the *control corpus* because it defines terms the whole tradition uses
loosely:

- **prakāśa** — the appearing, the luminosity of awareness. Nothing is known without it.
- **vimarśa** — the reflexive knowing of that appearing. A light that did not know its own
  shining would not be awareness. *prakāśa* and *vimarśa* are inseparable poles of one
  consciousness.
- **svātantrya** — the creative freedom by which consciousness manifests and knows; the power
  underlying both perception and liberation.
- **recognition** — the synthesis of a present perception with a remembered form, yielding
  reidentification, veridicality, and stability (see `recognition.md`).

These are not ornaments; they are the load-bearing definitions the other schools inherit.

### 4. Recognition = the A/B/C stratification

Liberation as recognition is not a single leap. It stratifies:

- **A — reflexive presence:** consciousness is always present to itself. Secured.
- **B — ownership:** this presence is *mine*, a first-person awareness. Plausible.
- **C — universalization:** this very consciousness is the Lord's — the "I" is not a private
  subject but the self of all. The live frontier.

The school's honesty lives here: it does not pretend all three levels are equally easy to
secure. The module's teaching keeps them distinct so the difficulty is visible.

The key point of the whole stratification is captured by Torella in the edition's
introduction: recognition is not the acquisition of something absent, but the noticing of
what was always already manifest —

> "Recognition... concerns an entity which, invested from the outset with a valid means of
> knowledge, is already manifest."
> — Torella, *IPK* (critical ed.), introduction

This is why the school can treat liberation as *recognition* rather than *attainment*: the
entity to be recognised never ceased to be present and known.

### 5. The key distinction: a control corpus, not a sect

The Pratyabhijñā is best modeled as a **philosophical/exegetical current that intersects the
Trika**, not another directional Kaula āmnāya. It does not have a separate ritual, mantra, or
initiation lineage of its own in the same way; its contribution is conceptual. This is why it
functions as the *semantic control* for everything else — it supplies the precise meanings
that the ritual traditions assume.
