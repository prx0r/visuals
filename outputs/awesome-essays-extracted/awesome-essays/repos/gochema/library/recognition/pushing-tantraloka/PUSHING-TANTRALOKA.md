# PUSHING-TANTRALOKA — hounding the text with the Logicvid method

*The working scaffold. Method: take the Tantrāloka's asserted claims, turn each into a
"why?" question, then hound the TEXT ITSELF (the Dyczkowski translations + Jayaratha's
Viveka + the research objects) for its OWN reasoning — NOT our frameworks. Our frameworks
only supply the questioning; the answers must come from the Tantrāloka. Keeps asking "why"
until the text's deepest reasoning is exposed.*

**Rule:** the extracted passages are the source of truth. Each question stores the full
quoted passage (more than needed) so the text's own argument can be re-read and re-pushed.
Our frameworks (aperture, Solms/valence, checkpointcharlie, pure-thesis) enter only AFTER
the text has been allowed to speak.

# Q1 — WHY must prakāśa be accompanied by vimarśa?

*(The reflexivity question. Why isn't mere manifestation enough? Why must manifestation be
self-apprehending?)*

## The stored passage
`pushing-tantraloka/Q1-reflexivity-extracted.txt` — TĀ 1/52–55 with Jayaratha's Viveka and
Dyczkowski's notes (lines 13624–14098 of Vol 1).

## The text's own reasoning (the chain the Tantrāloka gives)

1. **The supreme reality of the object is Śiva-as-Light** (TĀ 1/52): "The supreme reality of
   the object of perception is Śiva Who is the Light, for that which is not Light can neither
   be illumined nor even have any existence (vastutā)."
2. **Non-light cannot be made manifest.** A building that is not white does not become white
   (the Nareśvaraviveka analogy). Manifestation cannot be added to what is unmanifest by
   nature — existence and manifestation are one and the same.
3. **The objector:** perhaps the object is manifested by *association* with the Light
   (Sāṁkhya's view). **The reply:** then manifestation would be impossible — if what is
   unmanifest is independent of the Light, it could not exist, because "existence and
   manifestation are one and the same."
4. **Even non-existence is in the field of wonder (camatkāra)** (TĀ 1/53): the *notion*
   "this is non-existent" is itself conscious — it differs from an inert wall. So the Light
   covers both existence and non-existence.
5. **The Light is proved by its own shining** (TĀ 1/54): it shines at all times, cannot be
   denied, so no contrived means of knowledge is needed. A means of knowledge has as its
   object something *not yet known*; but the Light is ādisiddha (proved from the start).
6. **The means of knowledge themselves live in it** (TĀ 1/55): the means give life to
   things; their supreme living essence is the Lord Himself.
7. **The reflexivity step (Jayaratha, ad 1/52):** the difference between blue and not-blue
   "arises as being essentially perceived through an act of reflective awareness (vimarśa)….
   Its arising ultimately culminates in a state of oneness (tādātmya)." The variety of the
   world is "the sprouts and flowers of the tree of the reflective awareness of one's own
   Self."

## The "why" that remains (the text's frontier)
The text asserts vimarśa is *needed* (otherwise the crystal is insentient, and mere
manifestation can't differentiate). But the deepest "why" — why self-apprehension is
*intrinsic* rather than added — is carried by the regress argument (a second cognition
would require a third). The text states this compactly; the IPVV is where Abhinava *argues*
it at length.

# Q2 — WHY will, knowledge, action (icchā, jñāna, kriyā)?

*(Why are there three powers? Why is action not a separate thing?)*

## The stored passage
`pushing-tantraloka/Q2-will-knowledge-action-extracted.txt` — TĀ 1/151–153 with the
Gamatantra quotation, the ĪPVV preface, and Dyczkowski's notes (lines 24050–24300 of Vol 1).

## The text's own reasoning

1. **Action does not differ from knowledge** (TĀ 1/151, quoting the Gamatantra): "Yoga does
   not differ from action and action does not differ from Yoga." The insight that quells
   latent impressions is *called* action.
2. **The latent impressions are the products of the three impurities** (TĀ 1/152): āṇava,
   māyīya, karma. The insight that quells them is consciousness itself.
3. **Insight, resting on the principles, is itself action and Yoga** (TĀ 1/153): the same
   insight that quells vāsanā and deploys the tattvas is action; it is Yoga in the merging of
   the principles into consciousness.
4. **The unity argument (Abhinava's ĪPVV preface):** knowledge is not an *aid* to action;
   rather action causes knowledge to become *firm* — worship without knowledge does not
   become firm, knowledge without action does not become firm. They are two faces of one
   dynamism.
5. **The dynamism chain (ad 1/232):** the will (icchā) in the Divine Means → knowledge
   (jñāna) in the Empowered Means → action (kriyā) in the Individual Means. "The inscrutable
   dynamism of consciousness — the supreme form of action — which, by the freedom of its own
   will, has formed itself first into perception ('knowledge'), assumes the form of gross
   outer action." So the three powers are one freedom descending into succession (krama).

## The "why" that remains
Why *three* (not two, not four)? The text grounds the triad in the structure of freedom
descending (will → knowledge → action), and ties it to the kañcukas and the means. But the
deeper "why these three and no others" is a live question worth pushing against the IPVV.

# Q3 — WHY three upāyas (āṇava, śākta, śāmbhava)?

*(Why are there three means to realization? Why does one need all three?)*

## The text's own reasoning

1. **The three means (ad 1/230, MM quotation):** "the Individual Means consists of impure
   thought constructs centred on the cyclic pulse of the breath; the Empowered One consists
   of pure thought constructs effected by the mind alone; the Divine One is the complete
   dissolving away of thought constructs."
2. **They are degrees of one Śakti:** "the manifestation of Śakti threads through it
   everywhere in association with the hierarchy of gross, subtle and supreme." The
   excellence of the Empowered state is due solely to the developing strength of the power
   of grace.
3. **The hierarchy of succession (krama):** the Divine Means (śāmbhava) is centred on the
   immediacy of self-awareness, progresses spontaneously in a moment, is non-successive
   (akrama). The Empowered and Individual Means progress successively.
4. **All ritual is included in knowledge** (TĀ 1/230): "the entire extent of ritual action
   will be included in the last form of knowledge." Ritual is not a separate means.
5. **Action = knowledge as means** (TĀ 1/231): "Action is not something different from
   knowledge; rather it is knowledge itself which, in order to serve as a means, has assumed
   the nature of a succession."
6. **Śāmbhava is the supreme, direct means** (ad 1/213): the power of the will (Parā), the
   "Direct Means" (sākṣādupāya) operating within unconditioned Being. The other two operate
   in the domain of Māyā (māyopāya).
7. **Three penetrations, three grades of reflection:** the same reflective awareness unfolds
   through the oneness of the perceiver (śāmbhava), the plurality of the object (āṇava), and
   the unity-in-difference of the perception connecting them (śākta).

## The "why" that remains
Why *three* depths? The text ties them to the three powers (will/knowledge/action) and the
three grades of subject–object relation. The graded-removal structure (see the audit:
stream-enterer sees, arahant removes) is our *later* register — the text itself gives the
threefold as degrees of one Śakti's intensity.

# Q4 — WHY is recognition the goal, not attainment?

*(Why is liberation recognition of what already is, not the acquisition of something new?)*

## The stored passage
`pushing-tantraloka/Q4-recognition-extracted.txt` — the recognition-not-attainment passages
(lines 5770–5809 + 8889–9010 of Vol 1).

## The text's own reasoning

1. **Recognition is that the Lord is one's own Self** (ad 1/10–11): the practitioners of
   "sound discriminating reasoning (tarka) which is intent on bringing about the recognition
   that the Lord is one's own Self that is self-luminous and the supreme goal, should be
   saluted above all."
2. **Not engaged to attain a unique status** (ad 1/…): "I am not engaged in this work to
   attain some unique status." The work is not about becoming something new.
3. **The object's supreme reality IS the Light already** (TĀ 1/52): Śiva-as-Light is already
   the reality of the object; recognition is seeing this, not producing it.
4. **The Light is ādisiddha** (TĀ 1/54): proved from the start, shining at all times — so
   nothing is *added* by any means of knowledge. Recognition returns what was always present.
5. **Wonder (camatkāra) is the mark of consciousness** (ad 1/53): the non-existence of a jar
   is itself "in the field of wonder" — sentience, distinguishing consciousness from the
   insentient. Recognition is the *felt* re-cognition, not a new fact.

## The "why" that remains
The text states recognition-not-attainment compactly (nothing is added; the Light was always
shining). The full argument — that recognition "confers nothing but the removal of a
mis-knowledge" — is Ratié's reading of the IPK/IPVV. The Tantrāloka Vol 1 gives the claim;
the IPVV is where it is argued.

## The method recap (how each "why" is being hounded)

1. **State the assertion** the Tantrāloka makes.
2. **Turn it into a "why?"** (why must it be reflexive? why three powers? why three means? why
   recognition not attainment?).
3. **Search the primary text** (Vol 1 clean + Vols 2–11 + GRETIL + the ROs) for the text's
   OWN answer.
4. **Extract the full quoted passage** (more than needed) and store it with the question.
5. **Read the text's chain** (its premises, its objections-and-replies, its analogies).
6. **Mark the frontier** — the "why" the text asserts but does not fully argue (usually
   carried forward to the IPVV).
7. **ONLY THEN** bring in our frameworks, to test whether they independently rediscover the
   same structure (the logicvid3 convergence rule).
