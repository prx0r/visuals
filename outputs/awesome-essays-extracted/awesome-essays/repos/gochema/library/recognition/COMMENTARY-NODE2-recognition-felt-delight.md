# COMMENTARY III — NODE 2: RECOGNITION AS THE FELT RE-COGNITION (the delight of re-cognition)
*Academic commentary on the second convergence node: the recognition-event as a felt
delight. Ratié's camatkāra + IPK's pratyabhijñāpratyaya ↔ Solms' pleasure-at-decreasing-
uncertainty. This is the paper's thesis proper.*

---

## 1. THE PASSAGE [THE "I" IS A COGNITION OF RECOGNITION]

**Location:** Ratié Ch4, lines 12272–12281 + 12315–12351.
**Verbatim (French):**
> "cette cognition du Je est une 'cognition qui est une reconnaissance' (pratyabhijñāpratyaya)…
> c'est le fait que je me reconnais comme le sujet qui a perçu dans le passé… ('c'est moi
> qui ai perçu et qui me souviens à présent de X')."
> "il y a dans le souvenir une reconnaissance de soi qui n'est pas le fruit d'une
> construction conceptuelle… mais d'une saisie intuitive de soi… 'moi qui ai perçu ceci
> [autrefois], je perçois cela [à présent]'."
**Translation:**
> "this cognition of the I is a 'cognition that is a recognition' (pratyabhijñāpratyaya)…
> it is the fact that I recognize myself as the subject who perceived in the past…
> ('it is I who perceived and who now remembers X')."
> "there is in memory a self-recognition that is not the fruit of conceptual construction…
> but of an intuitive self-grasping… 'I who perceived this [before], I perceive this [now]'."

**Analysis — Interpretation:**
- The most basic self-cognition — the "I" — is *already a recognition*. It is not an
  abstract inference but an *intuitive self-grasping* across time: the felt continuity of
  the "I who perceived" and the "I who now perceives."
- **This is the felt re-cognition at its most elementary.** Recognition is not an added,
  higher achievement; it is the very form of self-awareness ("I who perceived, I
  perceive").

**Analysis — Context:**
- Ratié Ch4 is where the Pratyabhijñā responds to the Buddhist/Mīmāṃsā debate over the
  cognition of the I. The point: the self-recognition is a *felt given*, not a
  construction. This refutes the Buddhist claim that recognition is "a perfectly abstract
  construction."

**Analysis — Cross-references:**
- **IPK 1.2 [exact to the thesis]:** the "I" cognition (ahaṃpratīti) is treated in the
  Buddhist pūrvapakṣa; Ratié elevates it into the pratyabhijñāpratyaya.
- **Ratié Ch2 (Q6) [exact to Ch4]:** the "synthesis of the anterior and posterior forms of
  the I" (aham iti pūrvāpararūpānusandhāna) — the felt spanning of moments that IS
  recognition.
- **Solms Ch2 (the conscious id) [R]:** the id's "world of perception of its own" detects
  changes and "these changes become conscious as feelings" — the felt self-relation, the
  precursor of self-recognition.
- **Solms Ch9 [C]:** "I feel like this about that" — the felt has a dual aspect
  (self + about-an-object); parallels the recognition's "I who perceived, I perceive."
- **Ratié Conclusion (the possessed man) [exact to Ch4]:** recognition "produces no new
  knowledge" — it uncovers the already-present self; the felt is always already there.

**Analysis — Grade:**
- [T] the pratyabhijñāpratyaya (the "I" is a recognition, intuitive, felt).
- [R] Solms' felt self-relation (the conscious id; "I feel like this about that").
- **Limits:** This establishes recognition is *felt and elementary* within the Śaiva
  system. It does NOT, by itself, establish the stronger claim that ALL consciousness is
  recognition (the [C] universalization is held open).

**Analysis — Argument in the thesis:**
- **[Anchor]** Recognition is the felt re-cognition at the most elementary level (the "I").
- **[Convergence]** Solms' felt self-relation ("I feel like this about that") resonates
  with the pratyabhijñāpratyaya.
- **[Payoff]** Since the "I" is a felt recognition, the recognition-event (the paper's
  subject) is not a rare attainment but the basic structure of self-awareness.

## 2. THE PASSAGE [THE DELIGHT OF RECOGNITION = DECREASING UNCERTAINTY]

**Location:** Ratié Conclusion (recognition as the return, lines 33441–33455) ↔ Solms Ch9 +
report7 (pleasure at decreasing uncertainty).
**Verbatim — Ratié (Conclusion):**
> "On éveille les gens asservis… qui, à cause d'une méconnaissance (moha), ont la conviction
> telle que 'je ne suis pas le Seigneur'… en lui répétant encore et encore : 'vois (paśya) !
> Ce que tu décris est en toi-même !' – et on ne produit aucune connaissance nouvelle."
> "One awakens the enslaved people… who, because of a misknowledge (moha), hold the
> conviction 'I am not the Lord'… by repeating over and over: 'see (paśya)! What you
> describe is in yourself!' — and one produces no new knowledge."

**Verbatim — Solms Ch9 (lines 6354–6363):**
> "All of it is just felt uncertainty… The feelings tell you how well or badly you are
> doing."

**Verbatim — report7 (Solms 2019, the felt-certainty mapping):**
> "decreases in expected uncertainty feel like pleasure, and increases feel like
> unpleasure… the core event of knowing is inherently pleasant: Solms says returning to
> certainty *feels good*, while Rasa texts say self-recognition *feels like bliss*."

**Analysis — Interpretation:**
- **Ratié:** recognition is the awakening of the already-present self; it "produces no new
  knowledge" — it is a *re-*cognition, an unveiling (the felt realization "this is me").
- **Solms:** the felt registers the reduction of uncertainty; returning to certainty
  *feels good*.
- **The bridge [C]:** the delight of recognition (Ratié's bliss at self-realization) is
  structurally the felt register of a reduction of uncertainty (Solms). At the moment of
  recognition, uncertainty collapses and the felt registers it as *pleasure/delight*.

**Analysis — The tension (from Commentary I):**
- Ratié's camatkāra is explicitly *not* need-satisfaction (an overflowing, not a filling).
- But recognition (the awakening) is precisely the *resolution of a misknowledge* (moha) —
  and here it IS a reduction (of the erroneous "I am not the Lord"). So recognition, in
  particular, fits Solms' pleasure-at-decreasing-uncertainty, even though the *aesthetic*
  camatkāra (of the beautiful) does not.
- **The paper's precise claim:** it is *recognition* (the re-cognition of the already-
  present self) that pairs with Solms' pleasure-at-certainty — because recognition
  resolves the uncertainty of misknowledge. The overflowing camatkāra is recognition's
  *felt height*; Solms gives its *mechanism of salience*.

**Analysis — Grade:**
- [T] recognition is the felt unveiling of the already-present self (Ratié, Conclusion).
- [C] the specific "recognition's delight = decreasing uncertainty" (report7's mapping) —
  a candidate convergence, held open.
- **Limit:** report7's mapping is our own research brief (not peer-reviewed as such); it
  draws on Solms 2019 + Ratié. The [C] status is honest.

## 3. THE ARGUMENT IN THE THESIS (NODE 2)
The second convergence node establishes **recognition is the felt re-cognition**:
1. **[Anchor]** The "I" is already a felt recognition (pratyabhijñāpratyaya); recognition
   is elementary, not constructed.
2. **[Convergence]** Recognition is the felt unveiling of the already-present self (Ratié)
   — and the felt registers that unveiling as pleasure-at-decreasing-uncertainty (Solms).
3. **[Tension surfaced]** The aesthetic camatkāra is not need-satisfaction, but
   *recognition* (the resolution of misknowledge) IS a reduction — so the Solms pairing
   is precise to recognition, not to aesthetic pleasure in general.
4. **[Payoff]** Recognition is the felt event: Solms gives the mechanism (uncertainty-
   salience), Ratié the phenomenology (self-relishing unveiling).
