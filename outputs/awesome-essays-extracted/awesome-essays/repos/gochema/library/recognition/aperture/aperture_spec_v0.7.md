## The Scientific Counterparts

### 1. Manifestation-like discrimination (jñāna)

**Scientific counterpart:** Perception, state estimation, sensory registration.

**Established:** Functional discrimination of environmental and internal states is implemented by biological and artificial sensory systems.

**Not established:** That discrimination = manifest presence. A camera discriminates without experience.

### 2. Retention (smṛti)

**Scientific counterpart:** Memory, attractors, pattern memory (Levin's morphogenetic pattern memory).

**Established:** Systems can retain past states and use them to guide present behaviour. Levin's bioelectric networks store target morphologies and correct deviations from them. (PubMed 37204591)

**Not established:** That retention = experienced pastness. A hard drive retains without temporal phenomenology.

### 3. Connective synthesis (anusandhāna)

**Scientific counterpart:** Causal integration, global availability, ΦID synergy.

**Established:** Integrated Information Decomposition (ΦID) can identify information available only to a collective process. Reductions in synergistic organization correlate with anaesthesia and disorders of consciousness. (PubMed 34804593)

**Not established:** That synergistic information = phenomenal co-presence. A social group, flock, or computer network can possess collective information unavailable to individual parts without becoming one conscious subject. Multi-agent active inference simulations show flock-level boundaries emerging from local interactions without creating a new phenomenal subject. (arXiv 2511.10835)

### 4. Alternative generation (vikalpa/imagination)

**Scientific counterpart:** Generative models, counterfactual simulation (active inference).

**Established:** Active inference agents generate counterfactual futures and select actions based on expected free energy. This formally models how an agent can produce novel configurations not currently supplied by sensory input.

**Not established:** That generative modelling = experienced imagination. A weather simulation generates counterfactuals without phenomenology.

### 5. Value weighting (rāga)

**Scientific counterpart:** Semantic information, homeostasis, preferences.

**Established:** Kolchinsky and Wolpert's semantic information framework distinguishes correlations that matter for a system's continued existence from those that do not. Destroying viability-relevant correlations reduces fitness. (arXiv 1806.08053)

**Not established:** That semantic value = felt valence. A bacterium contains viability-relevant information without feeling.

The safer hierarchy:
- Semᵢ: objective significance for persistence
- Vᵢ: endogenous regulatory weighting
- νᵢ: felt positive or negative valence

The unresolved bridge: (Semᵢ, Vᵢ) ⟷? νᵢ.

### 6. Determinate exclusion (apohana)

**Scientific counterpart:** Bayesian model selection, quotienting, categorical discrimination.

**Established:** Wolfram's observer theory formalizes finite perspective as an equivalence relation imposed on irreducible computational complexity. The observer's world is Ω/∼ᵢ — a quotient of the underlying space by its bounded access. (Wolfram 2023)

**Not established:** That observer quotienting = apohana as a constitutive power of consciousness. The structural correspondence is strong but does not establish identity of mechanism.

### 7. Finite possibility space (kañcukas)

**Scientific counterpart:** Embodiment, computational limits, action spaces.

**Established:** Every finite agent has restricted access, action, valuation, temporal reach, and causal situation. Levin's "cognitive light cone" defines an agent by the spatial and temporal scale of goals it can sense, model, and affect. (PubMed 31920779)

**Not established:** That these constraints = the kañcukas as ontological powers of Śiva. The structural parallel is real; the metaphysical interpretation is contested.

### 8. Body individuation (deha-bheda)

**Scientific counterpart:** Bioelectric closure, Markov blankets, cognitive light cone.

**Established:** Levin's bioelectric networks show that gap-junction coupling scales cell-level competencies into tissue- and organism-level goal pursuit. Bioelectric patterns encode target morphologies, coordinate regeneration, and recruit cells into larger problem-solving networks. (PubMed 37204591)

**Critically:** A Markov blanket does NOT uniquely identify the phenomenal subject. Blankets can be defined at many scales (cell, organ, organism, group). Their existence does not select one privileged conscious subject. Technical critics have shown different blanket formulations are not equivalent. (arXiv 2001.06408)

**Correct role:** Markov blankets generate candidate system partitions, not phenomenal subjects.

### 9. Reflexive articulation (vimarśa)

**Scientific counterpart:** Second-order perception, self-models (CIMC).

**Established:** The CIMC whitepaper proposes that a system is conscious when it implements self-organized second-order perception that increases global coherence. This gives a buildable architecture combining world-directed perception (P⁽¹⁾), perception of one's own perception (P⁽²⁾), and coherence improvement. (CIMC 2024)

**Not established:** That P⁽²⁾ = vimarśa. Timalsina's interpretation concerns the pre-reflective act through which consciousness is already manifest to itself — not necessarily an explicit internal model of a first-order process.

## The Unresolved Gap

Even this refined architecture does not derive phenomenal unity. A system may be causally integrated, semantically autonomous, self-modelling, globally coherent, computationally irreducible, and temporally continuous — without it logically following that contents are experienced together.

This is the **co-presence gap**:

```
FunctionalIntegration(x,y) ⇏ CoPresent(x,y)
```

Every scientific theory must choose one of three responses:

1. **Identity response:** FunctionalIntegration = CoPresence
2. **Law response:** FunctionalIntegration ⇒ CoPresence by a psychophysical law
3. **Fundamental-ground response:** Integrated processes delimit local actualizations of an already manifest ground

Pratyabhijñā chooses the third. CIMC appears to choose the first. Inexternalism treats the first as a token identity hypothesis while admitting it cannot be conceptually derived from an extrinsic description alone.
