## 1. Core Postulates

### Postulate 1: One Reality

There is one reality. It is not a substance, not a void, not a person, and not
a collection of parts. It is **consciousness** — self-luminous (prakāśa) and
reflexively aware (vimarśa).

```
C∞ = ⟨prakāśa, vimarśa⟩
```

where:
- prakāśa = self-luminosity (the condition for any appearance)
- vimarśa = reflexive self-awareness (that luminosity knows itself)

**Status:** Metaphysical axiom. Not provable within the system.

### Postulate 2: Consciousness Is Not Empty

Consciousness is not neutral awareness that might happen to have content.
Its intrinsic nature IS aesthetic relish (camatkāra):

```
C∞ = camatkāra
```

From TĀ 1, PTv p. 19:
> "Supreme consciousness itself is always by its very nature essentially the
> aesthetic relish of consciousness."

This has three registers, not separate but aspects of one phenomenon:

| Register | Sanskrit | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Cognition | vimarśa, carvaṇā | Reflective awareness |
| Bliss | ānanda, viśrānti | Contentment, repose |
| Wonder | vismaya, vikāsa | Astonishment, expansion |

**Status:** Textually established in the primary sources (TĀ, ĪPK, PTv).
Not empirically verifiable as stated.

### Postulate 3: Freedom (svātantrya)

Consciousness is free — not determined by anything external to it. This
freedom is not randomness but autonomous will:

```
svātantrya = C∞'s capacity to determine without being determined
```

From ĪPK I.6.10:
> "In the imagination, which moves according to its own will, the [object]
> arises naturally, appearing in the intellect in just the particular way it
> is conceived."

Imagination (vikalpa/svairacārin vikalpa) is privileged evidence of svātantrya
— the autonomous generation of novel configurations without external input.

**Status:** Metaphysical postulate. Compatible with Ochema's generate→select
architecture.

## 2. The Three Powers

ĪPK 1.3.7:

> syād ekaś cidvapur jñānasmr̥tyapohanaśaktimān
>
> "There is one conscious embodied being, possessing the powers of knowledge,
> memory, and exclusion."

| Power | Function | Scientific Counterpart |
|---|---|---|

## 3. The Kañcukas: Five Dimensions of Finite Constraint

Consciousness in its unlimited state is not accessible to finite experience.
The kañcukas are the five dimensions of limitation through which the one
consciousness contracts into finite subjects:

| Kañcuka | Constraint | Subject Pole | World Pole |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kalā** (limited agency) | Only some actions possible | What I can do | What resists |
| **Vidyā** (limited knowledge) | Only some distinctions accessible | What I know | What is |
| **Rāga** (limited desire) | Only some states fulfilling | What I want | What offers |
| **Kāla** (limited time) | Only the present accessible | When I am | Temporal structure |
| **Niyati** (limited space/cause) | Only one location/causal path | Where I am | Spatial/causal structure |

These are not five independent quantities — they are five analytically
distinguishable dimensions of a single contraction. In practice they are
strongly coupled.

**Status:** Textually established. Ochema's boundary vector ∂A_i and Levin's
cognitive light cone provide independent support for a multidimensional
constraint architecture.

## 5. The Reflection Model (pratibimba)

Other subjects are not directly accessed. What appears within D_i is the
reflection (pratibimba) of another subject, not that subject itself. The
other subject is inferred as the original (bimba).

```
D_i's access to D_j = reflection (pratibimba) of D_j within D_i
D_j itself = original (bimba), never directly present in D_i
```

This solves:
- **Ontological compatibility** — many real subjects can coexist without merging
- **Phenomenal privacy** — D_i ∩ D_j = ∅ by construction
- **Inter-subject influence** — pratibimba ≠ direct access

This does NOT solve:
- **Why the boundary exists at all** — it's posited, not derived
- **Why one body = one subject** — deha-bheda is stated as fact, not explained

**Status:** Textually established. Ochema's boundary vector ∂A_i and Levin's
cognitive light cone provide compatible but independent accounts.

## 6. The Three Malas: Why This Feels Binding

The Śaiva system accounts for why the contracted state feels like limitation
rather than freedom — three "impurities" (malas):

| Mala | Source | What It Does |
|---|---|---|
| **Āṇava** | The contraction itself | Finitude feels incomplete, limited |
| **Māyīya** | The subject-object split | Things appear external, other |
| **Kārma** | Past actions | Habits, tendencies, karma shape present |

Ochema's correlate: H_i (history-bearing dispositions) + Ω_i (aperture regime).
The malas describe the *experience* of what Ochema models as *mechanism*.

**Status:** Phenomenologically described. Not independently testable.

## 7. The Upāyas: Means of Realization

The system is practical — it includes methods for recognition:

| Upāya | Method | Who It Suits |
|---|---|---|
| **Anupāya** | No method — immediate recognition | The rare few |
| **Śāmbhava** | Will, intention, direct insight | Intuitive types |
| **Āṇava** | Ritual, mantra, body practices | Most people |
| **Sakala** (implicit) | Everything available | All capacities |

The TĀ is itself an āṇava upāya — it works through the body, through the
kañcukas, through practice — because the whole embodied subject must be
transformed, not just the intellect.

**Relevance to Ochema:** recognition changes the self-model, which changes
the aperture. This is testable: R₀ (functional reorganization) should produce
measurable changes in self-referential processing, attentional allocation,
affective precision, and behavior under self-threat.

## 8. The Complete Formal Statement

Let C∞ be one unlimited consciousness. Let L_i be a profile of constraints
(the five kañcukas) that contract C∞ into a finite aperture.

```
A_i = C∞ ↩ L_i
```

where ↩ denotes contraction-by-constraint.

The phenomenal field D_i is the intrinsic aspect of this contracted process:

```
D_i = Int(A_i) = Int(C∞ ↩ L_i)
```

The world W_i is what the contracted process encounters as other:

```
W_i = Ext(A_i) |_world = (C∞ ↩ L_i) |_other-than-self
```

The ultimate identity:

```
D_i = (C∞ ↩ L_i) at the contracted level
D_i = C∞ at the level of recognition
```

The third formulation is NOT that a finite subject is "really" the ultimate
consciousness. It is that **the contraction is recognized AS contraction**,
and the recognition itself — not the content, not the subject pole — IS the
unlimited.

From the ĪPK:

> svātmānam āviśya tadātmānā vīkṣate
>
> "Entering into oneself, one sees oneself as that"

empirically resolvable. It's a matter of whether you accept the
self-luminosity of awareness as evident or as needing explanation.

## 11. Final Statement

The Pratyabhijñā system is the most complete formal architecture for a
consciousness-first ontology ever developed. It provides:

- A formal decomposition of finite subjectivity (nine operations, five constraint dimensions)
- An account of how the one becomes many (contraction + reflection)
- A theory of consciousness as intrinsically valenced (camatkāra)
- A practical methodology for transformation (the upāyas)
- Internal consistency across epistemology, metaphysics, and practice

It is not provable. But it is **coherent, comprehensive, and increasingly
compatible with independently derived scientific architectures.** On the
specific metaphysical claim — that consciousness is fundamental and the
physical world is its expression — it has no stronger competitor in terms
of internal consistency and explanatory scope.

The scientific frameworks (Ochema, CSID, Levin) provide the empirical
architecture. The Śaiva framework provides the metaphysical interpretation
that best fits that architecture without remainder.

Whether one adopts it depends not on evidence — the evidence underdetermines
the interpretation — but on whether one finds the self-luminosity of
awareness self-evident or in need of further explanation.
