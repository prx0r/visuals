## Against rebirth / Buddhism generally
1. **The materialist's case** — Ajita Kesakambalī's own words (DN 2):
   "Generosity is taught by idiots… with the breakup of the body, the wise
   and the foolish alike are annihilated." (Already in Truth of Rebirth ch.2 —
   the tradition's own record of its best opponent.)
2. **Rigley's critique of Trumbull** (IAI comment, Mar 2026): memory is
   biologically dependent; lesions change recall; "reconstruction under
   constraint," not free-floating in time. Quote him against our Bergson beat.
3. **Mashour et al. 2024** (Consciousness and the Dying Brain): dying-brain
   gamma surges exist, BUT "no evidence to correlate the neurophysiologic
   changes with any conscious experience." The neuroscientist's no.
4. **Secular Buddhist objection**: rebirth is optional scaffolding; momentary
   rebirth suffices; karma works this-life only. (The exact position V1's
   ending half-adopts — name the tension.)
5. **Pāyāsi's experiment** (DN 23): the ancient empiricist — oven, sealed
   jar, scale. "Maybe we'll see his soul escaping. But we don't."
6. **Hyde 2021**: Bergsonism is "an extreme dualism fundamentally
   mistaken" — if we lean Bergson in v2-science, quote the critic.
7. **The no-self contradiction charge**: if there's no self, what is
   reborn? (The charge; the Buddha's answer = not-a-valid-question — but
   SAY the charge first.)

## FAQ list (one line answers, sources in QUOTES.md)
- If no self, who is reborn? → process, not person (SN 23:2 satta).
- Doesn't this contradict not-self? → the Buddha refused both horns (SN 44:10).
- Can science test it? → not yet; science can't test free will either
  (Truth of Rebirth ch.8) — plus the honest "we don't know" from Parnia.
- Why does it matter if I can't verify? → the wager (MN 60 double-throw).
- Is this the Buddha or the commentaries? → early suttas (MN 38, SN 44.9,
  DN 15) vs Visuddhimagga elaborations — name the layers.
- What about NDEs/children remembering lives? → v2-science lane, not here.
- Is rebirth eternalism? → the Buddha's third position: neither (SN 12:35).
