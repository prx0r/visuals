# EssayViz Creation Process — Fundamental Structure

## 1. Source type

The supplied material is not primarily:

- a conventional essay;
- a poem;
- a historical survey;
- an empirical scientific explanation.

It is a constructive formal treatise.

Each section introduces a representation, exposes a failure, adds a new
distinction, and recursively rebuilds the structure.

The selected genre is therefore:

```text
constructive-formal-treatise
```

## 2. Why ordinary argument diagrams fail

A normal premise/conclusion diagram would flatten the argument because the same
objects are repeatedly reinterpreted at different orders.

The viewer must see that:

```text
symbol
→ difference
→ operation
→ tetrad
→ group of interchanges
→ recursive structure
→ temporal hierarchy
```

is one continuous construction.

## 3. Persistent alphabet

The visual language uses only five primitive identities.

| Identity | Visual form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `o` | hollow blue circle | this, current priority, present member |
| `x` | red cross | that, alternate or displaced member |
| `T` | gold exchange arrow | transformation operation |
| frame | graphite square | invariant whole |
| weight | node size / line thickness | relative intensity |

Complexity is generated by operations on these identities, not by introducing
unrelated imagery.

## 4. Static composition

### One thing

A single `o` appears without a coordinate grid. The point is the thing, not its
position.

### Repetition fails

Several spatially separated circles appear. A caption states that repetition
still gives one symbol-type. Only `x` introduces another represented thing.

### Priority

`o` and `x` are labelled this and that. The connecting relation remains
undirected until priority is established.

### Difference as operation

Two opposing arrows show that difference is the transformation required to
pass between the two members. `T` is presented as a higher-order thing.

### Pairing

`oo` and `ox` appear as distinct pair-types. This is the first secure
one/two distinction.

### Tetrad

The four symbols form a 2×2 representation. Spatial arrangement is explicitly
treated as a convenience for the transformation structure.

### Three interchanges

Column, row, and both-together operations are distinguished by three independent
arrow geometries.

### Superposition

The zero operation and three non-zero interchanges become four members of one
thing.

### Recursive reflexion

The tetrad becomes a tetrad of tetrads. Larger member counts are shown as
recursive growth rather than individually drawing every 256-member state.

### Invariance

An outer frame remains fixed while a diagonal member permutation occurs.

### Structure of structure

Nested frames preserve the same form at immediate and reflexive levels.

### Infinite hierarchy

The frame stack extends toward both particularity and generality.

## 5. Dynamic composition

### Duration

A fixed lamp mark and repeated clock marks coexist. Neither alone represents
duration.

### Moments

The tetrad alternates by column interchange while its invariant frame remains.

### Finite-rate rejection

Two gears with different speeds show why slow change plus fast change is not
absolute unchange plus change.

### Accelerating infinity

Successive intervals halve in width:

```text
1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + …
```

The infinite series visibly reaches a finite boundary.

### Three levels

The renderer separates:

- invariant frame;
- regular moments;
- accelerating sub-moments.

### No outside

Concentric temporal levels show why no universal external present is available.

### Replacement

`o → x → new o` is drawn as replacement, not reversible swapping.

### Intensity

Areas encode:

```text
2/3, 1/3, 1/6, 1/12, 1/24 …
```

with a displayed total of unity.

### Generality/time switch

Nested squares are intentionally ambiguous between different sizes in one plane
and equal squares receding through depth.

### Past, present, future

Three simultaneous cards encode:

- past: over-determined;
- present: determined;
- future: under-determined.

### Weighted interchanges

One large `this` and three weighted `that` determinations preserve total
intensity.

### Negative definition

Chair, table, bed, and wardrobe show identity through variable relative
determinations.

### Repeated squaring

Three bar distributions display:

```text
3:2:1
→ 9:4:1
→ 81:16:1
```

The dominant interchange emerges asymptotically.

### Unfinished frontier

The film stops with intention, action, and choice rather than inventing the
missing continuation.

## 6. Epistemic restraint

The visuals present the supplied construction.

They do not independently claim that:

- the formal derivation is mathematically established;
- the system refutes modern physics;
- precognition is empirically verified;
- all time literally has this structure;
- the unfinished selection argument is complete.

Source claims and visual reconstruction remain distinct.

## 7. Audio design

Suggested score:

- `o`: low pure tone;
- `x`: higher contrasting tone;
- transformation: brief interval exchange;
- tetrad: four-note cell;
- 16-member reflexion: nested four-note cells;
- regular moments: constant pulse;
- acceleration: geometrically shortening pulse spacing;
- replacement: timbral substitution rather than reverse playback;
- repeated squaring: progressive dominance of one voice.

Narration should lead formal introductions. Motion should complete slightly
after the relevant clause.

## 8. Review requirements

Render five checkpoints per scene.

Reject a scene when:

- page position appears to define identity;
- the tetrad looks like an arbitrary table;
- row and column operations are indistinguishable;
- recursive levels introduce new symbols;
- acceleration looks like ordinary constant-speed movement;
- replacement looks reversible;
- node area no longer matches stated weight;
- temporal depth loses the generality/depth ambiguity;
- repeated squaring does not visibly concentrate weight;
- the unfinished argument receives a fabricated conclusion.
