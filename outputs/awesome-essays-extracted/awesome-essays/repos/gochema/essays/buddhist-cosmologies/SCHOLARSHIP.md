## THE DIRECT ANSWER (scholarly consensus, with cites)

**Same address, different counts, three maps in Tibet.**

1. **Same Abhidharma backbone.** Encyclopedia of Buddhism (fetched):
   "The elaborate cosmological systems detailed in these two Abhidharmas
   [Theravāda + Sarvāstivāda], are, however, substantially the same,
   differing only occasionally on minor points of detail." Tibetan
   Buddhism's default map IS the Abhidharmakośa (Vasubandhu) — so the Thai
   forest 31-plane map and the Tibetan Abhidharma map describe the SAME
   Meru, SAME four continents, SAME three planes. Theravāda and Tibetan
   are two renderings of one Indian address.

2. **But the count differs: 31 vs 32/33.** Encyclopedia of Buddhism
   (fetched): "The total number of sub-divisions is between 31 and 33
   sub-realms, depending upon the tradition. The Theravada tradition
   counts 31 sub-realms in total, and the Sanskrit traditions count 32 or
   33." The discrepancies are things like whether unconscious beings
   (asañññatta) and asuras get counted as their own line. (Note: the
   "33" the owner may also be thinking of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three
   — ONE kāma-deva plane, Tāvatiṃsa, not the total.)

3. **Tibet runs THREE cosmologies at once.** Springer, *The Offering of
   Mount Meru* (2020, fetched): "The three predominant cosmologies in
   Tibet were the Abhidharma, Kālacakra, and Dzokchen (rdzogs chen)." And
   Tibet itself needed a repair manual: Khedrup Norsang Gyatso
   (1423–1513) wrote a commentary chapter titled "Resolving
   Contradictions Between Kālacakra and Abhidharma Cosmology."

4. **Kālacakra's universe is geometrically NOT Abhidharma's.** Berzin /
   StudyBuddhism (fetched, side-by-side tables):
   - Meru shape: Abhidharma = **square** cross-section, 160,000 yojanas,
     silver/blue/red/gold by direction · Kālacakra = **round**, 100,000
     yojanas, black in the east (colors shifted one position)
   - Rings: Abhidharma = **seven** golden ranges, progressively smaller ·
     Kālacakra = **six** rings, all equal
   - Form-realm heavens: Abhidharma = **seventeen** · Kālacakra =
     **sixteen**
   - A Kālacakra mile is **twice** an Abhidharma mile (~9 modern miles)
   - Hells: Abhidharma = below Bodh Gaya/Tibet · Kālacakra = inside the
     elemental mandalas
   - Sun/moon motion differs (Encyclopedia: Kālacakra "differs from
     Abhidharma texts on the shape of Mount Meru as well as on the motion
     of the celestial bodies").

5. **The scholar's answer to "how can there be two?"** — Berzin,
   *Introduction to the Kalachakra Initiation* (via Shambhala, fetched):
   "It is significant that Buddhism offers two descriptions of the
   universe. Each is valid for a different purpose, and there is no
   contradiction in having multiple portraits… Nothing exists with
   inherent characteristics on its own side rendering only one correct
   way to conventionally perceive, apprehend or describe it." Purpose-
   relative cosmology: Abhidharma's map trains discriminating awareness
   (set theory with variables); Kālacakra's is a unified field theory
   mirroring cosmos = body = mandala. "Neither was formulated for the
   sake of navigation" (Berzin, StudyBuddhism).

6. **Asura: the five-vs-six fight is the counting fight.** Samye
   Institute (fetched): "Some early Indian Buddhist schools counted only
   five classes as they counted the asuras as members of the god realm.
   In the Tibetan tradition, over time it has become more common to list
   six classes." And: "The status of asuras is not consistent across all
   texts" — some texts put asuras in the lower realms instead. The
   Theravāda 31-plane count has asuras NOT as a separate plane; the
   Tibetan bhavacakra makes them one of six. (Wikipedia claims the
   six-world asura split is "Tsongkhapa's authority" — NEEDS A BETTER
   SOURCE before banking as fact.)

7. **The "mental" reading is stated by the traditions themselves.**
   Samye Institute (fetched): "In the Mahayana teachings, the hell realms
   (like all the other realms) are understood to be mindstates that are
   characterized by a particular type of suffering, rather than actual
   physical locations." — tradition-side psychologization, quotable.

## THE FRONTIER ARGUMENTS — cosmology's live fights, per tradition (websearched 2026-10-21)
> Provenance: verbatim excerpts from result pages (URLs implied by titles
> below); re-check at draft before READ-aloud. These are the CURRENT
> scholarly/live debates — CH.9's scholarly mirror + CH.3 Beat D's
> "modern live fight" rows in one place.

### Theravāda — Buddhadasa vs Bhikkhu Bodhi (the headline fight)
- **McMahan's frame** (via Seeger/Suan Mokkh, fetched): Buddhist modernism
  = "detraditionalization, demythologization and psychologization" —
  Buddhadasa is its Thai flagship; King Mongkut (Rama IV) preceded him
  (skeptic on heaven/hell/future states — also in Analayo fn.4, in-house).
- **Buddhadasa's own careful position** (fetched, Seeger quoting): "the
  cycle of rebirth that consists of dying, being placed in a coffin, being
  reborn again… is something I am not concerned with; **I do not touch
  this kind of rebirth but I do not deny that it exists.**" — decline, not
  denial.
- **Bodhi's counter** (fetched): removing rebirth "would virtually reduce
  the Dhamma to tatters"; the suttas "never hint that these terms are
  meant symbolically" — they say rebirth occurs "with the breakup of the
  body, after death," which "clearly implies they intend the idea of
  rebirth to be taken quite literally."
- SCHOLARLY VERDICT SHAPE: both sides claim the Pali; the fight is over
  what "with the breakup of the body" is doing (index of truth vs. figure
  of speech).

### Tibetan — the Dalai Lama's two-step (the frontier's sharpest moment)
- **Step one (fetched, Dalai Lama 2005:80 via JHI Blog/Sheehy):** "it is
  hard to take the Abhidharma cosmology literally… even without recourse
  to modern science, there is a sufficient range of contradictory models
  for cosmology within Buddhist thought for one to question the literal
  truth of any particular version. **My own view is that Buddhism must
  abandon many aspects of the Abhidharma cosmology.**"
- **The credibility dictum (fetched, same JHI/Sheehy excerpt, 2005:80):**
  "There is a dictum in Buddhist philosophy that to uphold a tenet that
  contradicts reason is to undermine one's credibility; to contradict
  empirical evidence is a still greater fallacy." — the SOLUTION sentence
  that precedes "abandon many aspects" (CH.9b Beat C).
- **Step two (fetched, Sheehy 2020 fn.):** elsewhere he "does not concede
  that the Buddha was wrong about cosmology but rather shifts to question
  **whether Vasubandhu actually believed in the Buddhist cosmology that
  he presented**" (Dalai Lama 2005, 80) — did the systematizer mean it?
- **Internal context (fetched, Sheehy):** three cosmologies coexist
  (Abhidharma/Kālacakra/Dzogchen); Kālacakra = "syncretism of Vaibhāṣika
  atomic theory, Sāṃkya… and Jaina and Purāṇic cosmographies"; the Meru-
  question "was not raised in Tibet until the twentieth century"
  (Gendun Chopel, via Lopez); Tibetan astronomers already knew spherical
  planets from Kālacakra.
- FRONTIER SHAPE: the tradition's own hierarchy publicly reweights its own
  map while not conceding the source was wrong — CH.3 Beat D's "triple +
  repair" row has a living voice.

### Madhyamaka — the map is conventional truth; and samsāra = nirvāṇa
- **Two truths** (fetched, SEP): Nāgārjuna — "only the transactional truth
  is capable of being articulated in language"; conventional truth is
  "a kind of screen… putative truths of quotidian life are actually
  delusions that, if believed, prevent one from attaining… nirvana."
  Cosmology is *usable furniture at the conventional level*, not a thesis
  about reality.
- **The radical edge** (fetched, Britannica): "If this world of constant
  change is not real, neither can the serial transmigration be real, nor
  its opposite, nirvana. Transmigration and nirvana being equally unreal,
  they are one and the same." — the strongest frontier claim: no need to
  choose between map-real and map-unreal, because the distinction
  dissolves at the ultimate.
- **The live technical fight** (fetched, SEP/IEP): Svātantrika vs
  Prāsangika — is there a "true convention" (tathya-saṃvṛti) or does
  sorting conventions collapse into "anything goes"? (The debate that
  decides whether cosmology-talk can be *better or worse* even while
  merely conventional.)

### Yogācāra — mind-only: how idealist, exactly?
- (fetched, SEP Yogācāra 2024) cittamātra/vijñaptimātra is "the hotly
  debated doctrine" — "quite difficult to determine whether the doctrine
  of 'mere consciousness' holds true at the ultimate level"; standardly
  read as ontological OR epistemological idealism.
- **The school's self-position** (fetched): the middle way between "the
  'nihilistic' interpretation of emptiness attributed to Madhyamaka and
  the Abhidharmic reification of phenomena" (Saṃdhinirmocana via SEP).
- **Allies or rivals** (fetched, Garfield & Westerhoff 2015, OUP): Indian
  and Tibetan doxographies treat Madhyamaka/Yogācāra as rivals; modern
  scholarship keeps asking if they can be synthesized — CH.9's mind-made
  reading vs deconstructive reading, institutionalized as a 21st-c.
  research question.

### Pure Land — other-direction vs mind-only (the mode/destination fight, internal)
- (fetched, Wikipedia Sukhāvatī) Chinese Buddhism holds TWO views, "most
  often combined": **other-direction Pure Land** (a realm far away from
  ours) vs **mind-only Pure Land** ("non-dual with our world which only
  appears impure due to our minds") — CH.7's whole fight, inside CH.8's
  tradition.
- Also contested: Sukhāvatī as sambhogakāya land (Shandao) vs nirmāṇakāya
  (some Tibetans) (fetched).
- **The text itself evolved** (fetched, Harrison 1998, J. Indian Phil.):
  across recensions of the Larger Sutra, the "no women" stance moves from
  "hard-line" to "ambiguous… with nymphs" — the geography is visibly
  redacted over centuries (historical-critical frontier line).
- Practice-side frontier: self-power vs other-power (Hōnen/Shinran;
  "faith alone"; nembutsu as gratitude not transaction) (fetched, Britannica
  + Buddhistdoor review).

- The historical verdict: "Rebirth is an integral component of the

## THE TIBETAN-SYNTHESIS SOURCES (TRADITIONS dossier — fetched 2026-10-21)

**[S22] Kongtrul / Kālacakra / Dzogchen — the tradition's own four-system map:**
- **Kongtrul, *Myriad Worlds* (via Enc. of Buddhism + Shambhala +
  namsebangdzo, all fetched):** "describes four major cosmological
  systems found in the Tibetan tradition—those associated with the
  Hinayana, Mahayana, Kalachakra, and Dzogchen teachings. **Each of these
  cosmologies shows how the world arises from mind**, whether through the
  accumulated results of past actions or from the constant striving of
  awareness to know itself." + "the dazzling non-cosmology of the
  Dzogchen system, which dispenses with the dualistic perspectives
  revealing the creative principle to be awareness alone."
- **Kālacakra cosmogony excerpt (Shambhala, fetched):** "In the formation
  of a world-system, the mind is the [productive] agent, the environment
  and inhabitants are the objects [created], and creation is accomplished
  by evolutionary actions… energy-winds gather the subtle particles [that
  remain in space following the destruction of the previous world-system]."
- **Dzogchen ground (Wikipedia/Ground + Rigpa Wiki, fetched):** the ground
  "before realization produced buddhas and nonrealization produced
  sentient beings"; "Since the basis transcends time, any temporal
  language used to describe it… is purely conventional"; Longchenpa's
  "groundless ground"; the seven assertions (only #7 primordial purity
  correct — then even that incomplete; union of essence/nature/compassion)
  — an internal Dzogchen debate over what the ground IS.
- **The cosmos=person identity (Namkhai Norbu, fetched):** "the
