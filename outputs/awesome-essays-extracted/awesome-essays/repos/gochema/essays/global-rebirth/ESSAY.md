## CH.1 — FRAME: THE RETURN (0:00–1:30)

last time we compressed every buddhist theory of rebirth into one video and
landed on a cliff: birth is happening now. the last school said nobody
travels. but buddhism is one room in a very old building, and today we walk
the whole building. egypt was weighing hearts before the buddha was born.
persia was holding a three-night tribunal. the greeks had a judge. the
gnostics had a toll booth. china tried to cancel death by appointment. and
somewhere in the 1970s a trance-medium dissolved the timeline just as
thoroughly as any monk. same question every stop: what is rebirth, what
happens at the exact moment you die, and why does your tradition say it's
the true one. observer here. let's walk.

## CH.2 — HINDU: THE GARMENT (1:30–4:00)

the oldest answer in the room, and still the most intuitive: the ātman —
a real self — is the one wearing. bodies are garments. [READ — Bhagavad
Gītā 2.22, Gambirananda]: "As after rejecting wornout clothes a man takes
up other new ones, likewise after rejecting wornout bodies the embodied one
unites with other new ones."

what's going on: karma steers the dresser. two paths after death in the
chāndogya: the bright path to the moon and back as a man, the smoke path
down to the plants (verify final wording at draft). and the moment of death
in their own ritual: the breath is offered upward — the katha's chariot with
the self as passenger: the body is the chariot, you are not the horse.

the argument is the image itself: nobody has a better everyday picture —
you don't grieve a discarded shirt, so why grieve the body. says who: the
gītā is scripture, not notes.

and the counter writes itself from our last film: [CALLBACK v1] — what if
the passenger is a designation and not a passenger? the buddhists spent
thirty minutes dissolving exactly this. hinduism answers with the only
rebuttal older than the objection: no, he changes garments — a dresser who
persists is the whole doctrine.

## CH.3 — ZOROASTRIAN: THE TRIBUNAL (4:00–6:45)

now the hinge. zoroastrianism is the hinge — babylon moves it into judaism,
judes move it into christianity. two layers: the individual trial and the
universal resurrection.

the moment of death, their exact map: three nights your soul sits at the
head of your own corpse. [READ — Encyclopaedia Iranica, Shaked]: "The soul
of a righteous person sits during the first three nights after death at the
head of the corpse and chants the uštavaiti Gāthās with joy… After three
nights… a beautiful and radiant maiden of fifteen years of age appears…
identifying herself as the daēnā, i.e., his own religious soul."

your deeds wrote her. [READ, ibid.]: "The good thoughts, speak and deeds of
the person while alive caused the daēnā to become more and more beautiful."
for the wicked she's an ugly naked woman — meet yourself. then the Činwad
bridge: wide for you if you're fine, knife-edge if you're not.

and at the end of time the whole species gets its bodies back: [READ —
Timuş 2022]: bodily resurrection "attested in the Young Avesta, dated
before the Christian era." resurrection was persian before it was christian —
that's the primacy claim, and it's checkable.

says who: the split-self problem — fravashi and daēnā and the soul, three
things at the head of the corpse. the chair is getting crowded.

> DIR: maiden + resizing bridge animation. Avestan dualism cards.

## CH.4 — EGYPTIAN: THE AUDIT (6:45–9:15)

egypt runs the oldest afterlife industry on record — and almost no conveyor
belt. ka, ba, akh: your vital double, your mobile soul-bird, your justified
effective spirit. the ba commutes: out to the fields of reeds by day, back
to the tomb to you. no migration — iteration.

the moment of death is a ceremony on your body: the opening of the mouth —
they restore your senses, because the dead needs eyes and a mouth to keep
living. then the weighing: heart against ma'at's feather, thoth takes
notes. [READ — Papyrus of Ani, Spell 125, Negative Confession, verify tr.]:
"I have not stolen. I have not killed. I have not caused suffering…"
forty denials — the first life-review in history.

and their most honest line, refusing the whole event-name: [READ — Pyramid
Texts, verify cite]: "O king, you have not departed, you have gone."

the argument: the industry itself — humanity has always needed the transfer
managed. the objection: egypt barely believes in your continuity at all —
becoming-osiris is assimilation, not survival. the self doesn't continue;
it's absorbed.

## CH.5 — JEWISH: THE TWO WALLS (9:15–11:45)

judiasm holds both walls at once. mainstream: one life, bodily resurrection
at the end — [READ — Maimonides, Mishneh Torah, Laws of Resurrection 8:1,
verify exact]: the body that rises is the same body. the soul in between:
judgment, a limited term — same skeleton as the christian west, because it
built it.

and the esoteric wall: kabbalistic gilgul — souls recycled through families,
owing tikkun: repair debts paid across lineages. the famous moment-image:
the soul pulled from the body [READ — talmud bava metzia 83b, verify exact]:
like a hair being drawn from dough. and the deathbed image the whole living
tradition performs: the shema at the window, the last breath unifying.

says who: if gilgul is esoteric-only, what's the layman's film showing? same
structural position as secular buddhism's momentary rebirth — believed by
some, creedal for none.

## CH.10 — JAIN: THE DUST (0:00–2:45)

part two starts with the heretics. jainism says: no soul — but karma is
literally matter. fine particles, pudgala, stick to the jiva like dust on a
mirror, and the load weighs you through six destinies. every act is a
deposition. at death the gross body goes; the karmic sheath — a shadow you
can't outrun — continues into the next embodiment, and mokṣa is releasing
particle by particle, austerity until the mirror is clean.

the argument: intention alone can't travel across lives — only something
physical can carry consequences. verify the exact tattvārtha sūtra line at
draft. says who: the only karma on earth with actual weight.

the counter: the buddhist critic points at your dust and says — you reified
the mechanism back into a substance. and the deeper one: why does fine

## CH.11 — DAOIST: THE APPOINTMENT (2:45–5:15)

china holds two answers that refuse each other. philosophical daoism:
death is hua — transformation, the same process as autumn. zhuangzi's
butterfly runs to the end, and his best joke: if your arm becomes a rooster
after death, use it to tell the time. [READ — Philosophy Now, verify trim]:
"Life, in general, goes on, though we may not."

religious daoism wants the other thing: to keep the garment. the xian are
refined off the lot — no dying needed, dragon, noon, witnessed. and the
middle trick: shijie, corpse-deliverance. [READ — Ying Zhou, Penn 2019]:
"seemingly succumbed to death, but what appeared to be his corpse was later
replaced by either a bamboo staff, sword or sandals."

the moment of death on this map is optional — either skipped by appointment
or staged with props. says who: the only system trying to cancel death
entirely AND declaring it nothing, in one tradition.

the counter: the immortal-body track is craving-for-continuation wearing
cultivation robes; the zhuangzi freedom-track leaves unclear whether
butterfly-you is anyone. same satta-problem, chrysalis dress.
