## OBJECTION 1 — "jhana is actually perpetually being ruined by you" is the load-bearing claim, and the banked sources say the opposite about the mechanism.

**The claim:** Each moment you are actively taking yourself out of a state of jhana; just stop doing that.

**The answer from the banked sources:** The banked HH material never frames jhana as a default state you are "in" and then leave. HH 38 (`quotes-hh-38-natural-state.txt`, `quotes-hh-38-samadhi-jhana.txt`) frames the failure mode as *direction of attention* — "If your concentration is concerned with focusing on the particular… you're basically preying upon something… hunting down the moment… like an owl waiting for a mouse." That is a wrong *method*, not a state you're falling out of. HH 205 (`quotes-hh-205-jhana-lifestyle.txt`) is even more hostile to the "stop doing that" framing: "Jhana is an establishment of mind that results from your own efforts of purifying your lifestyle. It's not a magical experience that will purify your life for you." That is the opposite of "you're already in it, just stop ruining it." MN 27 (`quotes-mn27-four-jhanas.txt`) likewise describes jhana as something you *enter* after abandoning the five hindrances — an attainment verb, not a default condition.

**Resolution:** The owner's line is a rhetorical inversion, not a sourced claim. It can stay as owner-to-camera verbatim (it's his voice, his framing), but it cannot be presented as what Nyanamoli/MN 27 teach. The banked sources support "you're doing something wrong *toward* jhana," not "you're in jhana and leaving it."

**Honest remainder:** The "natural state" passage in `quotes/hh-38-natural-state.txt` is referenced but its actual wording is not quoted in the chapter — the chapter says "SILENT, check…" meaning no READ is banked. So the one source that *might* support the "you're already in it" reading is unverified here.
