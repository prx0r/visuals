# THE NON-CONSTRUCTED "I": THE ARGUMENT AGAINST THE SELF-MODEL
## How the IPVV proves the self-grasp is intrinsic — the three-kinds-of-construction argument and its defeat of the higher-order theory

*2026-08-11. The contemporary philosophy of mind offers a powerful account of the self: the "I" is a
self-model — a higher-order representation the system constructs of itself. The IPVV contains a direct
argument against this. It shows that the "I"-grasp (ahaṃ-pratyavamarśa) is not a construction at all:
construction is the joining of distinct things, the multiplication of a one, or the splitting off of a
doubted other — and the "I"-grasp is none of these. It is the self-presence that every construction
presupposes. This essay reconstructs the argument in full, applies it to the self-model theory and to
Ñāṇavīra's structural reflexivity, and draws out its consequence: recognition is not the revision of a
model but the re-positioning of a self-grasp that was never constructed.*

---

## 1. The rival: the "I" as a construction

The strongest modern account of the self is the *self-model*: the "I" is a higher-order representation
the system constructs of itself — a model of the organism as a unified agent, built by the very
processes it represents. On this view, the "I" is a construction: an operation of joining, of
attributing, of representing one's own states. Reflexivity, on this view, is *structural* — experience
folds back and includes its own occurrence, and this folding is all the self there is.

Ñāṇavīra, in the modern Pāli tradition, states this with great force, and adds a warning aimed
precisely at the Pratyabhijñā: that Abhinavagupta "converted the reflexive self-identity of a
conditioned structure into an ultimately existing universal Self." The self is a conditioned structure
that folds back on itself; it is not an intrinsic, pre-existing substance.

The IPVV's argument (V2L) is a direct answer to this. It does not deny that the folding occurs; it
denies that the folding is the root.

---

## 2. The argument: the three kinds of construction

The IPVV defines construction with precision (V2L):

> "The construction is of three kinds: it joins what is distinct (as one joins molasses, curd, and
> pepper); it constructs the one in many ways; or it splits off a doubted, other form. All three are
> operations of division and joining, of this-and-that."

That is, a construction (vikalpa) is:
1. **Joining** what is distinct (synthesizing a compound out of parts).
2. **Multiplying** the one into many (constructing one thing in many ways).
3. **Splitting** off a doubted other (dividing off a questionable form).

All three are operations on parts — operations of division and joining, of this-and-that.

Now the "I"-grasp, the IPVV shows, is none of these:

> "But the 'I'-recollection is none of these. It does not join a distinct 'this' to a distinct 'I'; it
> is the single self-grasp, resting in itself. It is the pure self-awareness that underlies every
> construction, the very thing that makes construction possible, without itself being one."

The three-fold proof:
1. **Not joining.** The "I"-grasp does not join a distinct "this" to a distinct "I" — it is not "this
   I" built from a distinct "this" and "I." It is the single self-grasp.
2. **Not multiplying.** The "I"-grasp does not construct the one into many — it *is* the one.
3. **Not splitting.** The "I"-grasp does not split off a doubted other — it is the un-doubted
   foundation.

**The conclusion.** The "I"-grasp is not a construction. And because every higher-order operation is a
joining or a splitting, the "I"-grasp cannot be a higher-order operation at all. It is the self-presence
that higher-order operations presuppose — the root that makes construction possible without itself being
one.

---

## 3. The application to the self-model theory

The self-model theory says the "I" is a higher-order representation. The IPVV's argument applies
directly:

- **A self-model is a construction.** A model is an operation of joining (assembling self-attributions)
  and of representing — a higher-order operation on the system's own states. It is exactly the kind of
  thing the IPVV calls a construction.
- **The "I"-grasp is not a construction** (by the three-kinds proof). Therefore the self-model cannot
  be the "I"-grasp.
- **The self-model explains the content of the "I"; it cannot explain the self-grasp aware of the
  model as its own.** The model accounts for the self's self-attributions (what the system believes
  about itself); it does not account for the self-presence that is aware of the model as its own — the
  "mine-ness" that no representation can add, because representation is a construction and the "I"-grasp
  is what is not constructed.

**The force.** The contemporary theory explains the *content* of the "I" — its representational content,
its self-attributions. It cannot explain the *self-grasp* — the awareness that the model is one's own.
The IPVV's argument is precise: a model is a construction; the "I"-recollection is what is not a
construction; therefore the model cannot be the root of the "I."

---

## 4. The application to Ñāṇavīra's structural reflexivity

Ñāṇavīra's structural reflexivity — experience folding back and including itself — is, in the IPVV's
terms, a *construction-capacity*: it is the higher-order operation, the folding, the re-representation.
The IPVV does not deny this capacity exists. It denies that it is the root.

**The reconciliation.** Structural reflexivity (the folding) is the construction-capacity; intrinsic
self-manifestation (the "I"-grasp) is the self-grasp that the folding presupposes. They are not rivals;
they are two levels. Ñāṇavīra describes the capacity (the folding); the IPVV points to what the folding
presupposes (the self-grasp). The "I"-grasp is not one more instance of the folding; it is the
self-presence that makes the folding possible, without itself being one.

**The answer to the warning.** Ñāṇavīra's warning — that Abhinavagupta converts the reflexive identity
of a structure into a universal Self — is answered at two levels:
1. **Intrinsic, not universal (the reflexion-core answer):** the IPVV proves intrinsic reflexivity per
   act (the "I"-grasp is not a construction), but it does not, from this alone, prove the numerical
   identity of all "I"-grasps. The intrinsic-ness of each is established; the universality is a separate
   argument.
2. **The universality is the commitment:** the numerical identity of all "I"-grasps is argued separately
   (V2C: the no-counter-correlate) and remains the commitment. Ñāṇavīra's warning is not fully closed by
   the reflexion-core alone — intrinsic-per-act is proven; one-universal-Self is the separate commitment.

## 5. Why this matters for recognition

The non-constructed "I" is the ground of the possibility of recognition. If the "I" were a
construction, recognition could only construct a new "I" — it could not re-cognize the one that is. The
IPVV's argument shows the "I" is not a construction, so recognition can re-position a self-grasp that
was never built.

And this is why recognition is not the revision of a self-model. The σ-flip (from the mechanism essay)
is not a change in the model's self-attributions; it is a change in the self-positioning of the
self-grasp that the model presupposes. Recognition removes the identification with the finite
self-positioning — not by revising the model but by re-positioning the self-grasp that the model serves.
The self-model theory explains the content; the IPVV explains the self-grasp; recognition operates on
the self-grasp.

## 6. The precise statement of the argument

The IPVV's argument against the self-model, completely stated:

**P1.** A construction is an operation of joining distinct things, multiplying a one into many, or
splitting off a doubted other (V2L).

**P2.** Every higher-order operation — every model, every self-attribution, every re-description — is a
joining or a splitting (i.e., a construction).

**P3.** The "I"-grasp is none of these: it does not join (it is the single self-grasp), does not
multiply (it is the one), does not split (it is the un-doubted foundation).

**C1.** Therefore the "I"-grasp is not a construction (V2L).

**C2.** Therefore the "I"-grasp is not a higher-order operation; it is the self-presence that
higher-order operations presuppose.

**C3.** Therefore the self-model (a higher-order construction) cannot be the root of the "I"; it
explains the content, not the self-grasp.

**C4.** (Honest remainder) This proves intrinsic reflexivity per act; it does not prove the numerical
identity of all "I"-grasps (the universalization is the separate commitment).

---

## 7. The boundary

The argument is [T] and sound: the "I"-grasp is not a construction. But it establishes *intrinsic
reflexivity per act*, not *universal identity*. The step from "each "I"-grasp is intrinsic" to "all
"I"-grasps are one" is the universalization, argued separately (V2C) and committed, not forced. The
non-constructed "I" defeats the self-model; it does not, by itself, prove the one universal Self. That
is the boundary, and it is the same in every document of the library.
