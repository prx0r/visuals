# ABHINAVAGUPTA VS UTPALADEVA: THE EXPANSION OF THE ROOT
## Where the IPVV goes beyond the IPK — vimarśa as the parā-vāk, the sphurattā, and the māheśvarya as Abhinava's systematization

*2026-08-11. The IPK (Utpaladeva) states the recognition-thesis in seed; the IPVV (Abhinavagupta)
unfolds it into a complete system. This essay isolates exactly what Abhinava *adds* — the original
extensions that are his, not Utpaladeva's. The Torella volume has established that Utpaladeva is the
innovator (camatkāra is his concept); this essay asks the reverse question and answers it precisely:
if Utpaladeva is the innovator, what is Abhinavagupta's contribution? The answer is systematic. Where
Utpaladeva states, Abhinava *unifies* — he identifies the reflexivity-claim with the Word, the
throbbing, and the Lordship, and he builds the epistemology of action that turns the recognition-
thesis into a complete metaphysics.*

---

## 0. The question

The modern recovery of the Pratyabhijñā — the work of Torella and Ratié — has reversed an old
judgment. It is now established that **Utpaladeva, not Abhinavagupta, is the real innovator**: most of
Abhinavagupta's ideas are the development of what Utpaladeva had already expounded in the Vivṛti, and
camatkāra — the felt register that looks so distinctively Abhinava — is Utpaladeva's own concept.

This raises a sharper question than the recovery usually asks. If Utpaladeva is the innovator, **what
is Abhinavagupta's contribution?** The answer is not "nothing" — the IPVV is thirty-four thousand
lines, and it is not mere repetition. The precise answer is that Abhinava's contribution is
*systematization*: he takes Utpaladeva's seed and *unifies* it, identifying its scattered claims with
each other and with a single ground. The IPVV is where the recognition-thesis becomes a *system*.

This essay isolates three of Abhinava's original moves — moves that are genuinely his, not
Utpaladeva's — and shows how each turns the seed into a system:

1. **vimarśa = the parā-vāk** (the reflexivity-claim identified with the supreme Word).
2. **vimarśa = the sphurattā** (the reflexivity-claim identified with the throbbing).
3. **vimarśa = the māheśvarya** (the reflexivity-claim identified with the Lordship).

These three identifications are Abhinava's signature. Utpaladeva states the reflexivity-claim; Abhinava
*extends* it by identifying it with the three things — the Word, the throbbing, the Lordship — that
make it systematic.

---

## 1. The root: what Utpaladeva states

The IPK states the reflexivity-claim in one kārikā:

> "The essential nature of light is reflective awareness (vimarśa); otherwise light, though 'coloured'
> by objects, would be similar to an insentient reality, such as crystal." (IPK 1.5.11)

And the Vṛtti adds the felt gloss: reflective awareness is the primary essence of light, "since there
is no savouring (camatkṛteḥ)."

Utpaladeva's contribution, then, is this: **the essence of consciousness is the felt self-awareness,
not the passive showing of objects.** The seed is a single, precise, and original claim.

What Utpaladeva does *not* do in the IPK is tell us how this reflexivity-claim connects to the rest of
the system — to the Word, to the powers, to the Lordship. The IPK states the three powers (1.3.7), the
reflexivity-claim (1.5.11), the yogin-creation (1.5.7), and the felt register (camatkāra). But it does
not *unify* them. The unity is Abhinava's work.

---

## 2. Abhinava's first move: vimarśa = the parā-vāk

Abhinava's most striking identification is the reflexivity-claim with the supreme Word (V2H):

> "The light that shines is the light that knows itself; and this self-knowing is the parā-vāk, the
> supreme speech, from which all the lower levels of speech (the madhyamā, the vaikharī) descend."

**The move.** Utpaladeva said the essence of light is reflective awareness. Abhinava says: that
reflexive awareness *is* the supreme Word. The self-grasp of the light, the moment it is present to
itself, is speech — the supreme speech that articulates the world. The lower levels of language (the
middle, the gross) are its descent.

**Why this is an extension, not a repetition.** Utpaladeva's reflexivity-claim is about *light* (the
manifestation); it does not mention the Word. Abhinava identifies the two. The consequence is
systematic: language is not an instrument the consciousness uses; it is the very form the self-grasp
takes. The word-joined "I" (the ahaṃ-pratyavamarśa) is not a construction imposed on the self; it is
the self's own articulation. This is why the "I" can be word-joined yet not a construction (V2L) — a
move that would be impossible if the Word were external to the self.

**The systematic gain.** With this identification, Abhinava can claim that the whole of language —
the entire vācaka-vācya structure, the sphoṭa, the grammarian's art — is the unfolding of the one
self-aware consciousness. The grammarian's separate supreme principle (the paśyantī as a distinct
entity) is absorbed: speech is not a second reality over against consciousness; it is consciousness's
own self-grasp (V2I). The reflexivity-claim, identified with the Word, swallows the entire philosophy
of language.

---

## 3. Abhinava's second move: vimarśa = the sphurattā

The second identification is the reflexivity-claim with the *throbbing* — the sphurattā, the pulsing
of the manifestation (V2I):

> "The throbbing (sphurattā) — the very pulsing of the manifestation — is the great being
> (mahāsattā), undivided by place and time, the heart of the param-Lord." (kārikā 14)

**The move.** The essence of light is reflective awareness; Abhinava identifies this with the
throbbing — the pulse, the wave, the spanda. The self-aware manifestation is not a static light; it is
a *pulsing*, an ongoing act of self-articulation. The sphurattā is the same as the spanda, the wave
(ūrmi), the eternal being.

**Why this is an extension.** Utpaladeva's reflexivity-claim is static — the essence of light is
reflective awareness. Abhinava makes it *dynamic*: the reflective awareness is the throbbing, the
pulse of self-manifestation. This connects the Pratyabhijñā to the Spanda (the pulsation) school, and
it gives the reflexivity-claim a temporal, dynamic face.

**The systematic gain.** With this identification, the felt register of the reflexivity-claim (the
camatkāra) is given its home: the pulse of self-awareness is the felt beat of being. The sphurattā is
"the heart of the param-Lord" — the felt throbbing at the centre of consciousness. This is where the
recognition-thesis and the felt meet: the reflexivity-claim, identified with the throbbing, is the
felt beat of the self.

---

## 4. Abhinava's third move: vimarśa = the māheśvarya

The third identification is the deepest, and it closes the whole jñānādhikāra (V2S):

> "The great-Lordship is nothing but the reflexive-awareness — the one who is aware of himself,
> fixedly, is the Lord; and that reflexive-awareness is the God's own pure knowledge-and-action. The
> great-Lordship is not an added power; it is the self-awareness itself." (kārikā 11)

**The move.** The reflexivity-claim, identified with the Lordship, yields the conclusion of the whole
jñānādhikāra: the Lord is not a being who *has* powers (knowledge, memory, exclusion) as external
attributes; he *is* the self-awareness that those powers express. The māheśvarya is not a fifth power
added to the four; it is what the four reduce to. "The great-Lordship is not an added power; it is the
self-awareness itself."

**Why this is an extension.** Utpaladeva states the three powers and the reflexivity-claim separately.
Abhinava identifies them: the powers are the self-awareness in its different operations, and the
Lordship is the self-awareness as such. The system closes — the powers reduce to the self-awareness,
and the self-awareness is the Lord.

**The systematic gain.** This is the crowning identification. It makes the recognition-thesis a
*metaphysics*: the Lord = the self-awareness = the felt reflexivity of light. Recognition, then, is
not the acquisition of a new belief about a separate Lord; it is the re-cognition of the self as this
self-aware light — the return by which the felt reflexivity, already the essence of every cognition,
is recognized as the Lord. The three identifications (parā-vāk, sphurattā, māheśvarya) are one:
vimarśa = the Word = the Throb = the Lord.

---

## 5. The epistemology of action: Abhinava's fourth region

Beyond the three identifications, Abhinava adds an entire *region* Utpaladeva's IPK leaves undeveloped:
the epistemology and metaphysics of action (the kriyādhikāra, V3A–I).

Utpaladeva's IPK states the reflexivity-claim and the powers; it does not develop a full theory of
action. Abhinava does, in the kriyādhikāra:

- **Action = the will** (V3A–I): the timeless Lord manifests sequences by his freedom; the will is
  the un-divided, un-ordered source of action.
- **Causality = the conscious knower's agency** (V3G): the essence of cause and effect is the agent-
  object relation; the inert cannot be a cause.
- **The will as cause, agent, and action** (V3I): "the cause does not cause, the agent does not act,
  the action does not move — all three are the one will, by which the Lord, wishing to stand, becomes
  the world."

**Why this is Abhinava's contribution.** This is not in the IPK as a developed theory. The IPK states
the powers; Abhinava builds the metaphysics of action that turns the recognition-thesis from an
epistemology (knowledge is self-aware) into a complete metaphysics (action and causation are the
self-awareness's agency). This is the fourth — and largest — of Abhinava's original moves.

---

## 6. The tattva-ontology and the soteriology: Abhinava's completion

Finally, Abhinava adds the tattva-ontology (the āgamādhikāra, V3J–M) and the soteriology (the
tattvasaṅgraha, V3N–P):

- **The Śivatattva as "the body of every category"** (V3J): the categories of the world are the
  unfolding of the one reality whose essence is the jñāna-kriyā pair.
- **The kañcukas and the malas** (V3K/L): the finite knower's coverings and stains.
- **The jīvanmukta** (V3P): the one liberated while still living, the fruit of recognition.

**Why this is Abhinava's contribution.** The IPK states the thesis; Abhinava supplies the world-
system and the path. The recognition-thesis, in Abhinava's hands, becomes a complete philosophy —
epistemology (knowledge), metaphysics (action, causation), ontology (the categories), and soteriology
(the liberation).

---

## 7. The summary: what Abhinava adds

| | Utpaladeva (IPK) | Abhinavagupta (IPVV) |
|---|---|---|
| The root claim | the essence of light is felt reflexivity (1.5.11) | states and expands it |
| The felt | camatkāra (his concept) | identifies it with the Word, the Throb, the Lordship |
| The powers | the three powers (1.3.7) | the vimarśa-structure, the apohana as pivot, the māheśvarya as their reduction |
| Language | — | vimarśa = the parā-vāk (V2H/I) |
| The dynamic | — | vimarśa = the sphurattā (V2I) |
| The Lord | the reflexivity-claim | the māheśvarya = the self-awareness (V2S) |
| Action | — | the kriyādhikāra: action = the will, causality = agency (V3A–I) |
| Ontology | — | the Śivatattva, the kañcukas, the tattvas (V3J–M) |
| Soteriology | — | the jīvanmukta, the guru-paramparā (V3N–P) |

**The thesis of this essay.** Utpaladeva is the innovator — camatkāra, the reflexivity-claim, and the
three powers are his. But Abhinavagupta's contribution is real and specific: he *systematizes* the
seed. He identifies the reflexivity-claim with the Word, the Throb, and the Lordship; he builds the
epistemology of action; and he completes the whole with the ontology and the soteriology. Where
Utpaladeva states a thesis, Abhinava builds a system. The IPVV is not a repetition of the IPK; it is
the IPK's unfolding into a complete metaphysics — and that unfolding is Abhinavagupta's own
contribution.

---

*This is the Abhinava-vs-Utpaladeva essay. Abhinava's original moves: (1) vimarśa = the parā-vāk, (2)
vimarśa = the sphurattā, (3) vimarśa = the māheśvarya, plus the whole kriyādhikāra (the epistemology
of action) and the tattva-ontology + soteriology. Utpaladeva is the innovator; Abhinava is the
systematizer — and the systematization is his own contribution.*
