# Gods Acting Through Matter

## Iamblichus’s complete theory of theurgy reconstructed

# I. The central problem

Iamblichus’s entire theurgic system begins from one contradiction:

[
\boxed{
\text{the human soul is radically dependent upon the gods}
}
]

but:

[
\boxed{
\text{the embodied human soul cannot reach them through its}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{own intellectual powers alone.}
}
]

Plotinus had proposed that something in the human soul remains permanently established in the intelligible realm. Liberation could therefore occur through an inward intellectual return to a level of the self that never truly descended.

Iamblichus rejects this.

The human soul genuinely descends into embodied and temporal existence. It does not retain an untouched private summit permanently contemplating intelligible reality. It becomes involved in:

* body;
* material conditions;
* temporality;
* passions;
* divided cognition;
* fate;
* changing activity.

This complete descent is the anthropological reason theurgy is required. The whole embodied participant must be elevated; an intellectual act performed by one faculty cannot by itself restore the whole human being.

The fundamental Iamblichean argument is:

[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Human intellectual activity is finite and soul-level};\
&\text{the gods transcend soul and intellect as humans possess them};\
&\text{an inferior cause cannot generate a superior mode of being};\
&\therefore\text{ human intellection cannot produce divine union};\
&\therefore\text{ the superior cause must elevate the inferior}.
\end{aligned}
]

Thus:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{theurgy is not primarily something humans do to gods.}
}
]

It is:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{something gods do in and through properly prepared}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{human beings, materials, words, images, and rites.}
}
]

This is the literal force of:

[
\textit{theourgia}
==================

\text{divine activity or god-work}.
]

---

# II. Iamblichus’s theory is not just in *De Mysteriis*

The theurgic system draws together several projects.

## 1. Platonic metaphysics

Plato supplies:

* the Good beyond ordinary being;
* immutable divine causation;
* the hierarchy of intelligible and sensible reality;
* assimilation to god as the human goal;
* the soul’s need for purification.

## 2. Pythagorean formation

Iamblichus’s ten-book project *On Pythagoreanism* was designed as a unified curriculum: the Pythagorean life, philosophical exhortation, general mathematics, arithmetic, and lost treatments relating number to physics, ethics, theology, geometry, music, and astronomy. Mathematics was not merely technical knowledge; it purified the soul by training it to perceive stable proportion, order, and intelligible structure through sensible forms.

## 3. The Chaldaean Oracles

Iamblichus treated the Chaldaean Oracles as divine revelation and wrote a now-lost extensive commentary on them. The Oracles supplied:

* a revealed metaphysical hierarchy;
* divine fire;
* Father, Power, and Intellect;
* Hecate as a major mediating divinity;
* angels and intermediary powers;
* divine names and symbols;
* ritual ascent;
* purification from Fate;
* a model of the gods actively providing the means of return.

Only roughly two hundred fragments survive, mainly through later Neoplatonists, so exact reconstruction is uncertain. The Oracles nonetheless became integral to Iamblichus’s philosophy in a way they had not been for Plotinus.

## 4. Egyptian and Hermetic priestly theology

Iamblichus presents *De Mysteriis* under the persona of the Egyptian priest Abamon, answering Porphyry’s questions to Anebo. He identifies Hermes–Thoth as patron of priestly wisdom and claims that Pythagoras, Plato, Democritus, and Eudoxus received instruction from ancient sacred traditions. He treats Egyptian, Chaldaean, and Greek wisdom as concordant expressions of one theology.

This should not be read as a transparent report of historical Egyptian religion. In Book VIII, Egyptian gods and Hermetic cosmology are systematically interpreted through Iamblichus’s Neoplatonic hierarchy. Scholars therefore speak of an Egyptian–Neoplatonic theology: Egyptian names and myths are retained, but their relations are expressed in Greek metaphysical terms.

Iamblichus is constructing a universal sacred science:

[
\boxed{
\text{Pythagorean discipline}
+
\text{Platonic metaphysics}
+
\text{Chaldaean revelation}
+
\text{Egyptian priesthood}.
}
]

# III. The complete metaphysical hierarchy

Iamblichus’s exact highest metaphysics is difficult to reconstruct because crucial works are lost and later witnesses are indirect.

But his general structure can be stated.

## 1. The wholly ineffable principle

At the summit appears a principle so radically transcendent that even the term “One” risks saying too much.

Let:

[
\mathbb U_0
]

denote the wholly ineffable principle.

It is:

* beyond being;
* beyond intelligibility;
* beyond relation;
* beyond causal coordination with subsequent reality;
* not one object or entity among others.

Iamblichus appears to introduce this principle to preserve absolute transcendence.

## 2. The causally productive One

Below—or conceptually after—the ineffable is an uncoordinated One:

[
\mathbb U_1.
]

This is more like the productive source familiar from Plotinus:

* prior to the intelligible order;
* source of unity;
* source of the first determinate principles;
* causally related to what proceeds.

The distinction allows Iamblichus to hold both:

[
\boxed{
\text{the ultimate is absolutely beyond everything}
}
]

and:

[
\boxed{
\text{the first cause genuinely produces everything}.
}
]

The reconstruction of these two levels comes largely through Damascius and remains debated, so it should not be treated as securely documented in every detail.

## 3. Limit, Unlimited, and One-Being

The next major structure is an intelligible triad:

[
\boxed{
\langle
\text{Limit},
\text{Unlimited},
\text{One-Being}
\rangle.
}
]

Limit gives:

* determination;
* identity;
* boundary;
* unity;
* intelligibility.

The Unlimited gives:

* fecundity;
* multiplicity;
* dynamism;
* indefinite productive power;
* repeated instantiation.

Their union gives determinate being:

[
\operatorname{OneBeing}
=======================

\operatorname{Mixture}
\left(
\operatorname{Limit},
\operatorname{Unlimited}
\right).
]

This draws on Plato’s *Philebus*, Pythagorean pairs such as Monad–Dyad, and the Chaldaean triad Father–Power–Intellect. Iamblichus’s metaphysics therefore does not derive multiplicity from a static One by inexplicable degeneration. Productive otherness is already represented by an irreducible principle of unlimited power.

## 4. Intelligible and intellective orders

From the first intelligible structure proceed increasingly articulated orders:

[
\text{intelligible}
\rightarrow
\text{intelligible–intellective}
\rightarrow
\text{intellective}.
]

At each level, unity is progressively expressed as:

* being;
* life;
* intelligence;
* forms;
* causal paradigms;
* ordered activity.

Iamblichus’s major innovation is multiplication of mediating levels. The purpose is not decorative complexity.

It addresses the problem:

[
\boxed{
\text{How can an absolutely transcendent cause produce}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{a materially differentiated cosmos without an}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{unexplained ontological jump?}
}
]

The answer is a graded series of causes, each translating the power of higher levels into a form receivable below.

## 5. Soul, nature, and body

Below intellect is soul:

[
\Psi.
]

Below soul are:

[
\text{nature}
\rightarrow
\text{matter}
\rightarrow
\text{embodied generated beings}.
]

But matter is not simply outside divinity.

The gods’ causal powers reach all the way into the material order. Matter is capable of receiving divine symbols because it has already been organized through divine causation.

That principle is indispensable:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{matter can elevate because it first descended from}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{divine formative causes.}
}
]

Theurgy reverses the causal chain through the very materials that embody it.

# IV. Who are the gods?

This is the most important question.

Iamblichus’s gods are not primarily:

* extremely powerful human-like minds;
* invisible organisms;
* mythological characters literally located in the sky;
* abstract concepts invented by communities;
* psychological archetypes;
* impersonal natural forces.

They are **superior causal unities**.

## 1. Each god is completely good

The gods do not intermittently possess goodness.

Their essence, power, and activity are unified in goodness.

A human may:

[
\text{possess goodness as an acquired state}.
]

A god:

[
\boxed{
\text{is good by essence}.
}
]

Therefore gods cannot be:

* jealous;
* bribed;
* emotionally manipulated;
* harmed;
* improved;
* compelled;
* deceived.

Iamblichus repeatedly insists that true gods are immutable and impassive. Apparent divine anger, desire, or need must be assigned either to symbolic speech, lower intermediary beings, or human misunderstanding.

## 2. The gods are unities before beings

There is evidence that Iamblichus conceived the gods in a way functionally similar to Proclus’s later henads.

A god is not simply a member of the category “being.”

It is a unifying cause whose distinctive character organizes an entire causal series.

Let:

[
G_a
]

be one god.

Then:

[
\operatorname{Series}(G_a)
==========================

\left{
g_a^{\mathrm{intelligible}},
g_a^{\mathrm{intellective}},
g_a^{\mathrm{psychic}},
g_a^{\mathrm{natural}},
g_a^{\mathrm{material}}
\right}.
]

The same god is expressed differently at multiple ontological levels.

The explicit fully developed doctrine of henads belongs to later Neoplatonism, especially Proclus. But vocabulary and arguments in *De Mysteriis* suggest that Iamblichus already regarded gods as superessential or unity-like causes rather than ordinary beings.

## 3. Each god remains wholly itself

A god is not divided when participated.

Suppose many participants receive Apollo:

[
P_1,P_2,\ldots,P_n.
]

Apollo does not become:

[
\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{Apollo}
]

inside each.

Rather:

[
\operatorname{Apollo}
]

remains undivided while each participant receives according to its own capacity.

This is the core reception principle:

[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Reception}(G,P)
=============================

G
\text{ as received according to }P.
}
]

The cause is not diminished.

The effect is limited by the receiver.

## 4. Gods do not descend spatially

When a god becomes present in a rite, the god does not leave a celestial location and enter a body like a material object.

The transformation occurs in the participant’s mode of participation:

[
\operatorname{Presence}(G,P)
\neq
\operatorname{SpatialTransfer}(G,P).
]

The god is already causally present throughout its series.

The rite changes:

[
\operatorname{Receptivity}(P,G).
]

Iamblichus can therefore maintain simultaneously:

[
G
\text{ remains immutable}
]

and:

[
P
\text{ receives a new divine illumination}.
]

## 5. Gods are genuinely plural

Iamblichus is not a simple monotheist.

The gods possess real individual causal identities.

Yet their plurality does not fracture the Good because each divine unity is wholly unified with the divine order.

Thus:

[
G_a\neq G_b
]

while:

[
\operatorname{DivineUnity}(G_a,G_b)
]

remains complete.

The gods are plural without being competing, externally related sovereign individuals.

This is polycentric unity:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{many irreducible divine causes, perfectly unified}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{through the Good without becoming one psychological}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{super-person.}
}
]

That sharply distinguishes Iamblichus from a naïve “one cosmic mind” model.

# V. The higher genera

Between gods and human souls Iamblichus recognizes a hierarchy of superior beings:

[
\boxed{
\text{gods}
\rightarrow
\text{archangels}
\rightarrow
\text{angels}
\rightarrow
\text{daimons}
\rightarrow
\text{heroes}
\rightarrow
\text{cosmic archons}
\rightarrow
\text{material archons}
\rightarrow
\text{souls}.
}
]

This is not simply a hierarchy of power.

Each order has a different:

* essence;
* causal source;
* domain;
* activity;
* relation to bodies;
* mode of manifestation.

Iamblichus explicitly rejects defining the orders merely by subjective appearance. Their appearances differ because their realities and functions differ.

## Gods

Gods are:

* uniform;
* immutable;
* completely good;
* causes of order;
* purifying and elevating;
* entirely superior to matter while causally present through it.

Their presence is said to confer:

* order;
* intellectual illumination;
* purification;
* health;
* virtue;
* elevation toward first principles.

## Archangels

Archangels stand nearest to divine causes among the explicitly mediating orders.

They transmit large-scale divine government and illumination but with more division than the gods.

## Angels

Angels carry divine messages and powers into more particular contexts.

Their Chaldaean role is especially anagogic:

[
\boxed{
\text{they lead souls upward}.
}
]

They represent divine activity becoming communicable at a level near soul.

## Daimons

Daimons administer generation, nature, bodies, and particular processes.

Iamblichus describes them as emerging from the generative and demiurgic powers of the gods.

Their functions include:

* completing natural processes;
* overseeing generated individuals;
* binding souls to bodies;
* administering particular regions and activities.

A daimon is therefore not simply an evil spirit.

It is an intermediary causal administrator.

Some daimons are good and ordered; Iamblichus also accepts disordered or evil daemonic powers, a view partly associated with the Chaldaean tradition.

## Heroes

Heroes arise from divine life-producing powers rather than primarily demiurgic powers.

They:

* vivify;
* guide rational souls;
* elevate human beings;
* assist release from generation.

They stand closer to souls than gods do, but are more stable and universal than ordinary human souls.

## Archons

Cosmic archons administer large-scale orders of the generated world.

Material archons preside more directly over material and sublunary conditions.

These orders matter because not every impressive apparition or force is divine.

One of Iamblichus’s principal concerns is **ontological discrimination**.

A practitioner must distinguish:

[
\text{god}
\neq
\text{daimon}
\neq
\text{material spirit}
\neq
\text{human projection}.
]

That is why *De Mysteriis* discusses differences in:

* light;
* order;
* stability;
* bodily effect;
* truthfulness;
* emotional tone;
* magnitude;
* the gifts produced by appearances.

# VI. The soul

Iamblichus’s soul is not an all-powerful hidden god.

It is ontologically intermediate and unstable.

## 1. Soul is capable of both directions

Soul can turn:

[
\text{upward toward intelligible causes}
]

or:

[
\text{downward toward body, division, and generation}.
]

Its distinctive power is versatility.

It can become assimilated to many levels without becoming identical in essence to those levels.

## 2. Soul is intermittent

Gods possess their activity continuously.

Human souls think, remember, perceive, and act intermittently:

[
A(t_1)\neq A(t_2).
]

This intermittence marks their ontological weakness.

## 3. The whole soul descends

The embodied human does not contain an eternally enlightened private intellect untouched by incarnation.

The descent affects the whole soul.

This makes theurgy a whole-participant transformation.

## 4. The soul cannot change species by its own power

A soul may act “angelically” through divine illumination, but it does not become an angel by natural essence.

Iamblichus distinguishes:

[
\operatorname{Essence}(S)
]

from:

[
\operatorname{Activity}(S).
]

A human soul may participate in an activity superior to its normal level:

[
\operatorname{Activity}(S)
\approx
\operatorname{Activity}(\text{angel}),
]

while remaining:

[
\operatorname{Essence}(S)
=========================

\text{soul}.
]

Recent scholarship interprets the highest pure souls as functioning angelically during their highest activity rather than permanently changing ontological species.

## 5. The soul’s vehicle

Iamblichus also inherits the theory of a subtle or ethereal vehicle connecting incorporeal soul with physical body.

The exact reconstruction is difficult, but the vehicle functions as a mediator:

[
\text{soul}
\leftrightarrow
\text{ethereal vehicle}
\leftrightarrow
\text{material body}.
]

It receives accretions during descent and becomes purified during ascent.

This is another instance of his general law:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{nothing radically unlike interacts without a}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{proportionate intermediary.}
}
]

---

# VII. The Chaldaean contribution

The Chaldaean Oracles supply Iamblichus with a revealed version of Platonic metaphysics that is already ritualized.

## 1. Father, Power, Intellect

The Chaldaean structure is often reconstructed as:

[
\boxed{
\text{Father}
\rightarrow
\text{Power}
\rightarrow
\text{Intellect}.
}
]

The Father is the transcendent source.

Power is productive fecundity.

Intellect articulates forms and governs cosmic order.

Iamblichus can map this onto:

[
\text{Limit}
\rightarrow
\text{Unlimited}
\rightarrow
\text{One-Being/Intellect}.
]

The correspondence is not exact in every fragment, but the triadic structure allows revelation, Pythagorean number, and Platonic metaphysics to be read as mutually confirming.

## 2. Hecate

In the Oracles, Hecate is not merely the later popular goddess of crossroads and magic.

She functions cosmologically as:

* Power;
* a life-bearing source;
* a membrane or boundary;
* mediator between intelligible and cosmic fire;
* source or channel of world-soul and life.

She represents a metaphysical interface:

[
\boxed{
\text{transcendent intelligible power}
\rightarrow
\text{cosmically distributable life}.
}
]

This makes Hecate structurally central to theurgy.

The transcendent cannot simply fall into matter.

It must be mediated through powers capable of preserving difference while communicating unity.

## 3. Iynges, Connectors, and Teletarchs

The surviving Chaldaean tradition contains intermediary entities such as:

* Iynges;
* Connectors or Synocheis;
* Teletarchs;
* Eros.

Their details vary across fragments and later interpreters.

Their shared function is to:

* communicate paternal power;
* connect ontological levels;
* sustain cosmic orders;
* transmit life-giving fire;
* provide divine names and ritual means of ascent.

The Cambridge reconstruction describes them as actualizations of the Father’s power to harmonize the universe and as the conditions that make theurgy possible.

## 4. The Oracles convert metaphysics into practice

A Middle Platonist might contemplate the hierarchy.

The Chaldaean theurgist attempts to enter it.

Thus:

[
\text{metaphysical connector}
]

becomes:

[
\text{ritual connector}.
]

The universe contains actual mediating powers through which:

[
\text{divine activity descends}
]

and:

[
\text{the soul ascends}.
]

The same structure is both:

* cosmological;
* epistemological;
* ritual;
* soteriological.

# VIII. Egyptian theology

Iamblichus defends Egyptian symbolism against Porphyry’s suspicion that it is irrational mythology.

## 1. Nature itself is symbolic

The Egyptians, he says, imitate the universe.

Invisible formative principles express themselves through visible forms.

Thus:

[
\text{visible image}
====================

\operatorname{Expression}
\left(
\text{invisible cause}
\right).
]

A lotus, solar boat, mud, serpent, animal form, or hieroglyph is not interpreted merely as a humanly invented analogy.

It may disclose how invisible causes operate through sensible reality.

## 2. Symbols preserve multiple levels simultaneously

A single image may refer to:

* material process;
* nature;
* soul;
* cosmic order;
* intellect;
* divine cause.

This is not ordinary ambiguity.

It is vertical polysemy:

[
s
\mapsto
{
m,n,\psi,\nu,G
}.
]

The symbol is read according to ontological depth.

## 3. Divine names are not arbitrary labels

Porphyry asks why untranslated “barbarian” names should matter if their conceptual meaning can be translated.

Iamblichus answers that sacred names are not established by human convention alone.

They are connected to the nature and divine order of what they name.

Translation may preserve dictionary meaning while destroying:

* sound;
* rhythm;
* phonetic form;
* traditional consecration;
* ritual placement;
* the name’s causal affinity.

Therefore:

[
\operatorname{Meaning}(N)
\neq
\operatorname{Power}(N).
]

The name is not merely a proposition about a god.

It is treated as a divine synthema in acoustic form.

## 4. Silence

At the highest level, even sacred names cease.

Iamblichus’s Egyptian hierarchy includes an indivisible first principle worshipped through silence alone.

This is consistent:

[
\boxed{
\text{the more transcendent the cause, the less adequately}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{it can be captured by differentiated language.}
}
]

Ritual speech does not eliminate apophatic theology.

It guides the soul toward the point where speech is surpassed.

# IX. The axiom of receptivity

The entire system depends on:

[
\textit{epitēdeiotēs}
=====================

\text{fitness, suitability, or receptivity}.
]

A cause gives according to its own abundance.

A participant receives according to its capacity.

Let:

[
G
]

be a divine cause and:

[
P
]

a participant.

Then:

[
\operatorname{Reception}(P,G)
=============================

f
\left(
G,
\operatorname{Capacity}(P)
\right).
]

The limitation lies in:

[
P,
]

not in:

[
G.
]

This explains why the same divine cause can produce different effects in different participants.

[
P_1
\rightarrow
E_1,
]

[
P_2
\rightarrow
E_2.
]

It also explains why preparation matters without making human preparation the principal cause.

Purity, knowledge, virtue, timing, ritual accuracy, and bodily condition change:

[
\operatorname{Capacity}(P,G).
]

They do not create:

[
G.
]

---

# X. Divine symbols

The Greek terms:

[
\textit{symbolon}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\textit{synthema}
]

are often translated “symbol” and “token.”

But modern “symbol” is dangerously weak.

A normal sign operates:

[
s
\overset{\text{interpretation}}{\longrightarrow}
m.
]

An Iamblichean divine token operates:

[
G
\overset{\text{causal expression}}{\longrightarrow}
\Sigma_G.
]

Its efficacy does not depend entirely upon human understanding.

Iamblichus claims that the gods recognize their own signs and that the signs accomplish their own work. Human thought is not the primary unifying cause.

The complete structure is:

[
\boxed{
G_a
\rightarrow
\Sigma_a
\rightarrow
R_a
\rightarrow
P_i.
}
]

Where:

* (G_a): god;
* (\Sigma_a): divine signature;
* (R_a): correct ritual activation;
* (P_i): suitable participant.

But the arrow:

[
\Sigma_a\rightarrow G_a
]

must not be interpreted as coercion.

The sign works because:

[
G_a
]

is already present as its cause.

---

# XI. Why matter can be divine

This is Iamblichus’s most radical correction to anti-material Platonism.

If matter were absolutely alien to divinity, ritual objects could not mediate divine action.

But matter has been formed within the divine cosmos.

Thus:

[
m
=

\operatorname{TerminalExpression}
\left(
G,D,N
\right),
]

where:

* (G): divine cause;
* (D): demiurgic formative power;
* (N): natural organization;
* (m): material expression.

A plant, stone, animal, sound, fragrance, or constructed image can bear a divine signature because the entire causal sequence remains active within its material result.

Matter is not divine in the same mode as a god.

It is divine participatively:

[
\operatorname{Divine}*{\mathrm{material}}
\neq
\operatorname{Divine}*{\mathrm{superessential}}.
]

This is precisely why embodied ritual can return upward.

The material thing is not a ladder placed against an unrelated transcendence.

It is the last visible articulation of a causal chain whose summit is divine.

Recent scholarship continues to identify Iamblichus’s positive treatment of matter as one of the distinguishing features of his theurgy.

# XII. Theurgy as reverse participation

Cosmological procession is:

[
G
\rightarrow
I
\rightarrow
\Psi
\rightarrow
N
\rightarrow
m.
]

Theurgy activates the reverse orientation:

[
m
\rightarrow
N
\rightarrow
\Psi
\rightarrow
I
\rightarrow
G.
]

But this is not literally a reversal of causal production.

The material effect does not become the cause of the god.

Rather:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{the participant becomes progressively transparent}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{to the higher causes already sustaining it.}
}
]

Theurgy is therefore:

[
\boxed{
\operatorname{ActualizeDependence}(P,G).
}
]

The participant ceases functioning as though it were self-grounding and becomes an appropriate vehicle of its higher cause.

# XIII. Prayer

Iamblichus gives prayer a three-stage structure.

## 1. Contact

Prayer initially turns the soul toward the god:

[
P
\rightarrow
\operatorname{Orientation}_G(P).
]

This creates conscious relation and prepares receptivity.

## 2. Communion

The soul becomes increasingly conformed to divine activity:

[
P
\rightarrow
\operatorname{Likeness}_G(P).
]

It receives:

* illumination;
* ordering;
* divine gifts;
* participation in divine activity.

## 3. Union

At the highest stage, the soul is established in the gods:

[
P
\rightarrow
\operatorname{EstablishedIn}(G).
]

This does not mean that the human soul becomes numerically identical to the god.

It means that its activity is no longer experienced or exercised as merely private human activity.

Iamblichus describes prayer as progressing from contact, through communion, to ineffable union and plenitude of divine fire.

Prayer is therefore not primarily asking for objects.

It is a progressive change in ontological participation.

---

# XIV. Sacrifice

Iamblichus’s account of sacrifice is complex because he rejects several simple explanations.

Sacrifice is not fundamentally:

* feeding hungry gods;
* bribing them;
* paying a debt;
* moving them emotionally;
* manipulating them through vapours;
* exploiting merely physical sympathy.

Gods are self-sufficient and impassive.

## Material sacrifice

Embodied souls require embodied ritual.

Material offerings correspond to participants still deeply involved in generation.

The sacrifice operates through:

* natural sympathy;
* divine signatures;
* proper timing;
* appropriate materials;
* ritual order;
* the participant’s level.

## Immaterial sacrifice

Highly purified souls can offer:

* contemplation;
* intellect;
* silence;
* pure prayer;
* immaterial gifts.

Iamblichus explicitly distinguishes material rites suited to embodied souls from simpler and more immaterial worship suited to rare purified souls.

## The highest cause

The offering is efficacious because the material entity remains internally related to the divine formative cause.

The causal chain is:

[
G_a
\rightarrow
D_a
\rightarrow
N_a
\rightarrow
m_a.
]

The rite places:

[
m_a
]

within a configuration that actualizes its causal kinship:

[
\operatorname{Kinship}(m_a,G_a).
]

The god does not need the offering.

The participant needs the offering because the participant cannot yet ascend without sensible mediation.

---

# XV. Divination

Book III of *De Mysteriis* distinguishes authentic divine divination from ordinary forms of inference and intuition.

A physician predicting illness from symptoms is not performing divine prophecy.

An animal anticipating weather may possess natural sensitivity.

True divination, for Iamblichus, originates in divine power.

Formally:

## Natural inference

[
e_1,e_2,\ldots
\rightarrow
\operatorname{Infer}(e_{n+1}).
]

## Divine divination

[
G
\rightarrow
\operatorname{Knowledge}_G
\rightarrow
P.
]

The information is not assembled from the participant’s ordinary cognitive resources.

It is received through participation in a superior knower.

Iamblichus discusses:

* dreams;
* oracles;
* divine possession;
* inspired speech;
* visions;
* luminous epiphany;
* ritual photagogy or “leading in light.”

He claims that authentic divine manifestations can be distinguished by:

* their order;
* luminosity;
* stability;
* truth;
* transformative gifts;
* superiority to the medium’s normal powers.

The critical problem is obvious:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{these criteria are reported from within the same}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{ritual worldview they are meant to verify.}
}
]

They are not independent modern controls.

---

# XVI. Divine possession

Possession has several modes.

Iamblichus says that:

* the god may possess the participant;
* the participant may surrender entirely;
* god and participant may engage in common activity;
* soul alone may participate;
* body and soul together may participate;
* the entire living being may become involved.

The resulting phenomena may include:

* altered voice;
* unusual movement;
* stillness;
* insensitivity;
* prophetic speech;
* light;
* unusual bodily capacities;
* loss or transformation of ordinary agency attribution.

Iamblichus means actual divine presence, not merely a metaphor for psychological absorption.

The formal relation is:

[
\operatorname{Agency}(P)
\rightarrow
\operatorname{CoActivity}(P,G).
]

At the highest level:

[
\operatorname{Activity}(P)
\subset
\operatorname{Activity}(G).
]

Yet:

[
P\neq G
]

by essence.

The person becomes a vehicle of superior activity without becoming the god’s total being.

---

# XVII. Divine light and epiphany

Iamblichus places enormous importance on luminous manifestation.

Different orders display different forms of light.

Divine light is described as:

* uniform;
* stable;
* overwhelming;
* purifying;
* ordered;
* independent of ordinary material illumination.

Inferior appearances are increasingly:

* fragmented;
* unstable;
* turbulent;
* mixed with vapours or matter;
* emotionally disruptive.

The light is not merely evidence that the god is present.

It is the mode by which immaterial divine power becomes perceptible to an embodied recipient.

Thus:

[
\operatorname{DivineCause}
\rightarrow
\operatorname{LuminousMediation}
\rightarrow
\operatorname{EmbodiedAppearance}.
]

Clarke’s interpretation is essential here: the supernatural or miraculous operation is not dispensable ornament in *De Mysteriis*. Animal sacrifice, oracles, possession, and visible epiphany are part of the theory’s actual content. Any interpretation reducing them entirely to psychological symbolism has changed the theory rather than explained it.

---

# XVIII. Sacred names

A sacred name has at least four levels.

## Semantic

What the name means conceptually.

## Phonetic

Its sound, rhythm, and articulation.

## Traditional

Its reception through a sacred lineage or revealed language.

## Ontological

Its direct relation to the divine power expressed through it.

Thus:

[
N_G
===

\langle
m,
p,
t,
o
\rangle.
]

Translation may preserve:

[
m
]

while changing:

[
p,t,o.
]

This is why Iamblichus defends untranslated Egyptian and Assyrian names.

The strongest claim is not merely that sound affects psychology.

It is:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{certain names belong objectively to divine causal}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{orders and therefore operate even beyond conceptual}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{understanding.}
}
]

That claim is central and currently unverified.

---

# XIX. Statues and images

The broader Iamblichean and later Proclean tradition treats statues as potential receptacles of divine presence.

Materials, shapes, names, plants, stones, fragrances, and ritual actions can be combined into a suitable receptacle:

[
R_G
===

\operatorname{Configure}
\left(
\Sigma_G^1,
\Sigma_G^2,\ldots,\Sigma_G^n
\right).
]

The image is not thought to imprison the god.

Nor is the god numerically identical with the material statue.

The statue becomes:

[
\operatorname{SeatOfParticipation}(G).
]

Thus:

[
\boxed{
\text{presence without confinement}.
}
]

The full later theory of statue animation is more explicit in Proclean and Hermetic materials, but *De Mysteriis* supplies the metaphysics that makes it possible. Theurgy historically included practices extending from healing and divination to statue animation and soul ascent.

---

# XX. The personal daimon

Iamblichus rejects a narrowly astrological account in which the personal daimon is determined from one isolated natal factor.

The guardian daimon is connected to the whole order through which the soul enters embodied life.

It:

* exists as a paradigm before embodiment;
* oversees the soul’s vital completion;
* guides the embodied life;
* supplies principles of thought and action;
* eventually yields to a superior divine guide through theurgy.

The sequence is:

[
G
\rightarrow
D_i
\rightarrow
P_i.
]

Where:

* (D_i): personal daimon;
* (P_i): embodied person.

The personal daimon mediates between the person’s local life and more universal cosmic causes.

This is not identical to a modern “higher self.”

It is an ontologically distinct intermediary being assigned to the soul’s life.

---

# XXI. Fate

Iamblichus does not deny Fate.

He limits its jurisdiction.

Fate governs:

* bodies;
* natural processes;
* generated conditions;
* planetary and cosmic relations;
* the ordinary embodied sequence.

But the gods transcend Fate.

The soul, through divine participation, may become connected to causes above Fate.

Thus:

[
\operatorname{UnderFate}(P)
]

can be transformed into:

[
\operatorname{ParticipatingAboveFate}(P,G).
]

Theurgy does not necessarily cancel every physical condition.

It changes the principal cause under which the soul acts.

This is liberation as causal reassignment:

[
\boxed{
\text{from being governed principally by generated causes}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{to acting through participation in divine causes.}
}
]

Iamblichus attributes to Egyptian priestly theurgy an ascent to causes established above Fate rather than a denial of the physical and astrological cosmos.

---

# XXII. Evil

Iamblichus must explain how evil is possible in a cosmos governed by perfectly good gods.

His broad answer is that evil has no independent divine source.

It arises through:

* partiality;
* material limitation;
* disorder in reception;
* conflict among lower processes;
* mistaking a local effect for the whole;
* daemonic and material disturbance.

Let:

[
G
]

give an ordered causal power.

A limited participant receives:

[
E_P
===

\operatorname{PartialReceive}(G,P).
]

When the local effect is isolated from the whole order:

[
\operatorname{LocalHarm}
]

may occur despite:

[
\operatorname{WholeOrder}.
]

This resembles privation theory but is complicated by Iamblichus’s acceptance of evil daimons.

The unresolved problem is:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{If every level depends upon perfectly good gods,}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{why can a lower causal agent become sufficiently}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{disordered to oppose the good?}
}
]

Calling it deficient reception describes the location of failure more readily than its ultimate origin.

---

# XXIII. Purification and virtue

Ritual accuracy alone is insufficient.

The participant must become suitable.

Iamblichus’s virtue hierarchy probably includes:

[
\text{natural}
\rightarrow
\text{ethical}
\rightarrow
\text{political}
\rightarrow
\text{purificatory}
\rightarrow
\text{contemplative}
\rightarrow
\text{paradigmatic}
\rightarrow
\text{theurgic}.
]

Theurgic or hieratic virtue is highest because it does not arise from merely human activity.

It is activity exercised through divine participation.

Purification involves:

* ethical order;
* detachment from disordered passions;
* ritual cleanliness;
* intellectual preparation;
* correct relation to material life;
* alignment of the soul’s faculties;
* freedom from false agency claims.

Its function is:

[
\operatorname{Noise}(P)\downarrow
]

and:

[
\operatorname{Receptivity}(P,G)\uparrow.
]

But “noise reduction” is only a modern analogy.

For Iamblichus, purification is ontological conformation to a superior order.

---

# XXIV. The complete causal model of theurgy

We can now formalize the system without replacing its supernatural claims.

Let:

[
\mathcal I
==========

\left\langle
\mathbb U,
\mathbb G,
\mathbb M,
\mathbb P,
\mathbb R
\right\rangle.
]

Where:

* (\mathbb U): first principles;
* (\mathbb G): divine and intermediary orders;
* (\mathbb M): natural and material cosmos;
* (\mathbb P): embodied participants;
* (\mathbb R): revealed ritual relations.

For god (G_a), define its causal series:

[
\mathscr S_a
============

\left{
\Sigma_a^{\mathrm{intelligible}},
\Sigma_a^{\mathrm{psychic}},
\Sigma_a^{\mathrm{natural}},
\Sigma_a^{\mathrm{material}},
\Sigma_a^{\mathrm{linguistic}}
\right}.
]

A rite selects and configures members of this series:

[
R_a
===

\operatorname{Configure}
\left(
\mathscr S_a
\right).
]

A participant has a receptivity state:

[
E_i^a
=====

\operatorname{Receptivity}
\left(
P_i,G_a
\right).
]

The rite does not create the divine cause:

[
R_a\nrightarrow G_a.
]

It creates a suitable participatory condition:

[
(P_i,R_a)
\rightarrow
P_i^{\mathrm{fit}}.
]

The god acts through its own causal series:

[
G_a
\rightarrow
\mathscr S_a
\rightarrow
R_a
\rightarrow
P_i^{\mathrm{fit}}.
]

The theurgic result is:

[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_a:
P_i^{-}
\rightarrow
P_i^{G_a}.
}
]

Where (P_i^{G_a}) may exhibit:

* purification;
* illumination;
* divination;
* possession;
* divine coactivity;
* assimilation;
* liberation;
* union.

The principal cause is:

[
G_a.
]

The rite, symbols, priest, and participant are:

[
\text{instrumental and receptive causes}.
]

---

# XXV. Why the god remains unchanged

A potential contradiction remains:

[
\text{How can an immutable god perform a new action}
]

[
\text{in response to a temporal ritual?}
]

Iamblichus’s answer is that the divine activity is eternally complete.

The temporal novelty occurs in the receiver.

Let divine activity be:

[
A_G
===

\text{continuous}.
]

Before ritual:

[
\operatorname{Receive}(P,A_G)=r_0.
]

After ritual:

[
\operatorname{Receive}(P',A_G)=r_1.
]

Thus:

[
A_G
\text{ does not change},
]

but:

[
P\rightarrow P'.
]

This is analogous to illumination:

[
\text{sun remains luminous;}
]

[
\text{window opens}.
]

The analogy is not a proof, but it displays the intended logic:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{divine initiative is permanent; ritual changes}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{the local conditions of its manifestation.}
}
]

---

# XXVI. Is theurgy coercive?

## Gods cannot be coerced

True gods are:

* immutable;
* impassive;
* complete;
* superior to the rite.

Therefore:

[
R\not\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Compel}(G).
]

## Lower powers may be commanded

The priest has a double position.

As a human:

[
P<G.
]

As a bearer of divine symbols and authority:

[
P[\Sigma_G]

>

D_{\mathrm{lower}}.
]

Thus a priest may supplicate gods while commanding subordinate daemonic powers.

Threatening formulas cannot properly alter a god, but may address irrational or lower powers.

This nuance prevents the crude equation:

[
\text{all theurgy}=\text{non-commanding prayer}.
]

Theurgy includes hierarchical authority, but that authority derives from superior divine orders rather than the private ego of the practitioner.

---

# XXVII. Theurgic union

What is the final union?

It is not:

[
P=G
]

as numerical identity.

Nor is it merely:

[
P
\text{ believes in }G.
]

It is:

[
\operatorname{Activity}(P)
\rightarrow
\operatorname{ParticipationInActivity}(G).
]

The participant becomes:

* godlike;
* illuminated;
* causally ordered from above;
* free from lower determination;
* established in the divine.

The final books of *De Mysteriis* describe the goal as purification, liberation, salvation, and union with the gods; the modern standard edition likewise characterizes union as the true good and theurgy as its required route.

A compact expression is:

[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Henosis}(P,G)
===========================

\operatorname{PerfectParticipation}(P,G).
}
]

Difference remains at the level of essence.

Separation is overcome at the level of activity and participation.

---

# XXVIII. Theurgy and recognition

Iamblichean theurgy is not Pratyabhijñā recognition.

The Śaiva claim is:

[
P_i
===

\operatorname{Mode}_i(\mathbb S).
]

The Iamblichean claim is:

[
P_i
\neq
G_a
]

but:

[
P_i
\text{ can participate in }G_a.
]

Recognition in Iamblichus is therefore:

[
\boxed{
\text{“I am not an independently self-grounding agent;}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{my existence and highest activity derive from}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{superior divine causes.”}
}
]

It is not:

[
\boxed{
\text{“I am numerically the God who creates the universe.”}
}
]

The transformation is:

[
SM_i^{-}
========

\text{independent human agent}
]

into:

[
SM_i^{+}
========

\text{embodied participant capable of divine coactivity}.
]

This is **participatory recognition**, not identity recognition.

---

# XXIX. Iamblichus and Proclus

Proclus systematizes much that Iamblichus initiated.

Iamblichus gives:

* complete soul descent;
* necessity of theurgy;
* divine symbols;
* hierarchical mediation;
* gods as unity-like causes;
* Pythagoreanized Platonism;
* Chaldaean revelation;
* positive ritual use of matter.

Proclus gives a more architectonic formalization through:

* henads;
* causal series;
* remaining–procession–return;
* participated and unparticipated terms;
* a highly explicit divine hierarchy.

Thus:

[
\boxed{
\text{Iamblichus supplies the soteriological necessity;}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{Proclus supplies the mature causal geometry.}
}
]

But Proclus should not be retrojected into every detail of Iamblichus.

Terms like “henadic series” are useful reconstructions, not always explicit Iamblichean formulations.

---

# XXX. Iamblichus and process inexternalism

Process inexternalism can explain part of what ritual unquestionably does.

Let:

[
O_i
]

be the complete embodied participant-process and:

[
D_i
\equiv_{\mathrm{inext}}
O_i.
]

A rite changes:

[
O_i^{-}
\rightarrow
O_i^{R}.
]

Therefore:

[
D_i^{-}
\rightarrow
D_i^{R}.
]

This explains why theurgy must include:

* body;
* sound;
* material;
* movement;
* environment;
* memory;
* authority;
* expectation;
* social relation.

They are not external decorations around a private mind.

They are constituents of the process whose intrinsic presentation is experience.

But CTPI alone gives only:

[
R\rightarrow\Delta O_i.
]

Iamblichus claims:

[
G_a
\rightarrow
R
\rightarrow
\Delta O_i.
]

The divine cause remains an extra commitment.

---

# XXXI. Constructor-theoretic reconstruction

The formal physical vehicle can be represented without pretending that constructor theory proves gods.

Let:

* (P_i): participant substrate;
* (R_a): ritual environment;
* (K_a): ritual tradition as distributed constructor;
* (H_i): historical record;
* (\Sigma_i): self-attribution variable;
* (Q_i): affective state;
* (\Pi_i): policy state;
* (C_\delta): timer.

The ritual transformation is:

[
\mathcal T_R^a
==============

\left{
(P_i^{-},R_a,H_i,\Sigma_i^{-},Q_i^{-},\Pi_i^{-},c_s)
\rightarrow
(P_i^{a},R_a',H_i',\Sigma_i^{a},Q_i^{a},\Pi_i^{a},c_h)
\right}.
]

A historical ritual system is an approximate constructor when:

[
K_a
\Vdash_{\epsilon,\rho}
\mathcal T_R^a.
]

This formalizes reproducible participant transformation.

Iamblichean theurgy adds:

[
\operatorname{PrincipalCause}
\left(
\mathcal T_R^a
\right)
=======

G_a.
]

Constructor theory neither proves nor disproves that attribution.

---

# XXXII. QRI and Iamblichus

QRI could potentially model changes in:

[
\partial_i,
\quad
U_i,
\quad
\tau_i,
\quad
\mathcal G_i,
\quad
\nu_i.
]

Ritual might alter phenomenal geometry through:

* rhythmic entrainment;
* sensory symmetry;
* harmonic sound;
* breath;
* spatial enclosure;
* luminous stimulation;
* attention;
* social synchrony.

This may help explain why ritual form matters phenomenally.

It does not explain:

* why one sound is a divine name;
* why a symbol belongs to Apollo rather than another god;
* why prophecy contains unavailable information;
* why a god rather than neural dynamics caused the experience;
* why union liberates the soul from Fate.

QRI can model the field transformation.

It cannot yet identify the divine agent.

---

# XXXIII. What Iamblichus gets exactly right

## 1. Human transformation is embodied

A human being is not rescued by changing propositions alone.

Body, perception, emotion, environment, and action must be transformed together.

## 2. A transcendent cause requires mediation

Radically different ontological levels cannot interact through unexplained direct leaps.

## 3. Materiality need not oppose spirituality

If matter belongs to a meaningful causal order, it can mediate rather than merely obstruct.

## 4. Participation is not coercion

The lower does not produce or control the higher.

## 5. Divine plurality need not imply divine conflict

Multiple irreducible unities can remain perfectly integrated.

## 6. Phenomenal intensity is not sufficient evidence of divinity

Iamblichus at least attempts to distinguish gods, daimons, heroes, souls, and deceptive appearances.

## 7. Philosophy has performative limits

An accurate concept of transformation is not the transformation itself.

## 8. Salvation is causal reorganization

Liberation is not merely believing a doctrine. It is acting from a different order of causes.

---

# XXXIV. Where the system does not hold up

## 1. The divine-token identification problem

How can one independently establish:

[
\operatorname{TokenOf}(\Sigma_a,G_a)?
]

Iamblichus appeals to:

* revelation;
* priestly tradition;
* ritual success;
* divine signs.

But each is interpreted through the same theological framework.

The theory lacks an external criterion for distinguishing:

* divine token;
* cultural symbol;
* conditioned stimulus;
* ineffective material.

## 2. The circular verification problem

A rite produces an experience.

The tradition interprets that experience as divine.

The divine interpretation confirms the rite.

Formally:

[
R
\rightarrow
E,
]

[
T
\rightarrow
\operatorname{Interpret}(E,G),
]

[
\operatorname{Interpret}(E,G)
\rightarrow
\operatorname{Confirm}(T).
]

Without independent information or controlled prediction, this can become circular.

## 3. The hierarchy-access problem

The exact taxonomy of:

* gods;
* archangels;
* angels;
* daimons;
* heroes;
* archons

is asserted through revelation and metaphysical systematization.

It is not deductively established from ordinary experience.

## 4. The immutability–response problem

The receptivity answer is elegant:

[
G
\text{ does not change;}
\quad
P
\text{ changes}.
]

But Iamblichus also describes gods as initiating specific temporal interventions.

The theory must explain why one particular rite at one time receives one particular divine result without introducing a new decision in the immutable god.

## 5. The information problem

True divination should yield information unavailable through ordinary means.

That claim is empirically testable in principle.

The surviving philosophical theory does not supply modern-quality controlled evidence.

## 6. The evil-daimon problem

If every causal order ultimately depends on perfect divine goodness, the existence of genuinely deceptive or evil intermediary agents requires more explanation than deficient reception.

## 7. The universal-tradition problem

Iamblichus harmonizes:

* Egypt;
* Chaldaea;
* Pythagoras;
* Plato.

But historical traditions contain real differences.

Concordance may reflect Iamblichus’s interpretive programme more than an objectively identical ancient revelation.

## 8. The ritual-specificity problem

Even where ritual transformation is reliable, it remains difficult to separate:

* sensory form;
* expectation;
* authority;
* conditioning;
* group influence;
* divine correspondence.

---

# XXXV. Four possible readings

## 1. Psychological reduction

[
G_a
===

\text{projection or archetype}.
]

Ritual reorganizes the human mind.

This explains some effects but fails to represent Iamblichus’s actual claim.

## 2. Enactive naturalism

[
R_a
\rightarrow
\Delta O_i.
]

Theurgy is an embodied cultural technology that transforms perception, identity, physiology, and action.

This is scientifically credible but is not strict Iamblichean theurgy.

## 3. Realist participatory metaphysics

[
G_a
===

\text{real transpersonal causal unity}.
]

Ritual aligns a local process with a real pattern extending beyond it.

This comes closest to naturalizing Iamblichus without eliminating the gods.

## 4. Full Iamblichean supernaturalism

[
G_a
]

is an ontologically superior divine being that:

* exists independently of human culture;
* places or recognizes divine symbols;
* manifests light;
* grants prophecy;
* possesses mediums;
* purifies souls;
* liberates from Fate;
* elevates participants into divine union.

That is the historical theory in its full strength.

---

# XXXVI. The strongest reconstruction of the gods

Using our present framework, the most rigorous interpretation is:

[
\boxed{
G_a
===

\text{an irreducible, transpersonal, superordinate causal unity}
}
]

whose effects extend across multiple levels:

[
\text{intelligible}
\rightarrow
\text{psychic}
\rightarrow
\text{natural}
\rightarrow
\text{material}
\rightarrow
\text{phenomenal}.
]

A god is not identical with:

* one image;
* one myth;
* one planet;
* one psychological state;
* one natural force.

These are participant-relative expressions:

[
\pi_k(G_a).
]

Thus:

[
\text{Sun}
\neq
\text{Apollo},
]

but the Sun may participate an Apollonian causal order.

A hymn is not Apollo.

But its harmonic and semantic structure may be an Apollonian token.

A lucid state is not Apollo.

But it may participate Apollo’s intelligible clarity.

The god is the unity across the series:

[
G_a
===

\operatorname{Unity}
\left(
\pi_1(G_a),
\pi_2(G_a),\ldots
\right).
]

This is the strongest logically coherent extraction from Iamblichus.

What remains unproven is whether such transpersonal unities possess:

* awareness;
* agency;
* knowledge;
* intention

in the literal divine manner Iamblichus attributes to them.

---

# XXXVII. The deepest meaning of theurgy

The ordinary modern picture is:

[
\text{isolated human}
\rightarrow
\text{uses symbol}
\rightarrow
\text{attempts to contact distant god}.
]

Iamblichus reverses every term.

The human is not isolated.

The symbol is not merely humanly invented.

The god is not distant in space.

The rite does not create the relation.

The real structure is:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{the gods are already the causal ground of the}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{participant, the symbol, and the cosmos.}
}
]

The embodied soul has forgotten how its own existence is causally suspended from higher orders.

Theurgy reconstructs that relation at every level:

* material;
* bodily;
* acoustic;
* imaginative;
* intellectual;
* ethical;
* cosmic.

Thus:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{theurgy is matter becoming transparent to the}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{divine causes already active within it.}
}
]

The practitioner does not climb out of the world.

The world is reorganized as a vertical manifestation of its causes.

---

# Final synthesis

Iamblichus’s complete system can be compressed into twelve propositions.

[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
1.;&\text{The ultimate principle radically transcends being and thought.}\
2.;&\text{Reality proceeds through graded mediating orders.}\
3.;&\text{The gods are immutable, perfectly good causal unities.}\
4.;&\text{Each god extends through a multilevel causal series.}\
5.;&\text{Matter is a terminal participation in divine causation.}\
6.;&\text{Divine symbols belong objectively to these causal series.}\
7.;&\text{The human soul has completely entered embodied generation.}\
8.;&\text{Human thought cannot elevate itself beyond its ontological rank.}\
9.;&\text{The gods provide revealed rites that activate receptivity.}\
10.;&\text{The gods act through their own symbols without being coerced.}\
11.;&\text{The whole embodied participant is purified and reorganized.}\
12.;&\text{Salvation is perfect participation in divine activity.}
\end{aligned}
}
]

The definitive theurgic equation is:

[
\boxed{
G_a
\rightarrow
\mathscr S_a
\rightarrow
R_a
\rightarrow
P_i^{\mathrm{fit}}
\rightarrow
P_i^{G_a}.
}
]

Where:

* the god remains immutable;
* its causal series is already present;
* the rite configures its signs;
* the participant becomes receptive;
* divine activity transforms the participant.

Iamblichus’s most profound contribution is not “ritual magic.”

It is the claim:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{a finite being cannot overcome finitude merely by}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{forming a better concept of infinity.}
