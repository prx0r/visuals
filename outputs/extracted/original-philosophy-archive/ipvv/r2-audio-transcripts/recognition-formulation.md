## Verdict

Your formulation captures the **direction of dependence** in Pratyabhijñā correctly:

[
\boxed{
\text{finite consciousness is not generated by a sufficiently complex nonconscious mechanism}
}
]

Rather, finite subjects, bodies, cognition, and worlds are determinate manifestations of an already self-manifest and active consciousness.

But this expression is too mechanistic:

[
M_\infty\xrightarrow{L_i}M_i
]

It makes (L_i) look like an independently existing filter applied to an otherwise featureless universal consciousness. That is not quite Utpaladeva’s or Abhinavagupta’s position.

The more faithful formulation is:

[
\boxed{
C_\infty
\xrightarrow{\text{its own differentiating freedom}}
\langle S_i,W_i,R_i\rangle
}
]

where:

* (C_\infty) is universal consciousness as **prakāśa–vimarśa–svātantrya**;
* (S_i) is a finite knower;
* (W_i) is its determinate field of objects;
* (R_i) is the restricted relation of knowledge, action, value, time, and causation connecting them.

The finite subject is not merely universal phenomenality passed through external constraints. **The subject, constraints, body, objects, and their relations are all internally manifested forms of the same conscious power.**

That is what Torella, Ratié, and Biernacki’s reconstructions support. Your attached framework is therefore structurally right, but its ontology needs tightening.

# 1. What Torella’s work actually supports

Torella presents Utpaladeva’s project as an attempt to establish that three ordinary capacities—

[
\text{cognition}
+
\text{memory}
+
\text{exclusion or determination}
]

—cannot be adequately explained as merely mechanical sequences of momentary events. Their analysis is meant to show the coincidence of the individual subject with universal consciousness. ([Academia][1])

The argument is not:

[
\text{a universal field exists}
\rightarrow
\text{it is filtered into individuals}.
]

It begins from concrete acts of consciousness.

## Cognition

A cognition is not just a state containing information. It manifests an object while being non-objectively present itself.

## Determination

A cognition does not merely contain a phenomenal form. It determines:

[
\text{“this is this”}
]

through exclusion of alternatives.

## Memory

Memory does not merely reproduce information. It presents:

[
\text{“I previously experienced this.”}
]

Torella emphasizes Utpaladeva’s contention that a causal trace may explain why memory has content, but not why the content appears as something previously experienced by the same subject. ([Academia][2])

From these capacities, Utpaladeva argues toward a subject that:

* manifests contents;
* retains them through transformations;
* connects temporally separated acts;
* determines objects;
* remains one while assuming different cognitive forms.

The universal subject is therefore not introduced primarily as a reservoir of phenomenality. It is introduced as the only reality allegedly capable of being:

[
\boxed{
\text{one across transformations while freely taking multiple forms}
}
]

So your (M_\infty) is too thin. A better variable is:

[
C_\infty=
\langle
M,U,D,A
\rangle
]

where:

* (M): manifestation;
* (U): unity across cognitive transformations;
* (D): determinate self-articulation;
* (A): freedom or agency.

Utpaladeva’s ultimate is not merely **universal manifest capacity**. It is **universal self-manifest agency**.

# 2. What Ratié confirms

Ratié is explicit about the school’s metaphysical claim:

> All phenomena, including empirical subjects, are limited aspects of Śiva understood as one consciousness encompassing the universe.

Liberation is recognition that the apparently spatially and temporally limited subject is actually the universal consciousness called the Lord. ([Academia][3])

This supports:

[
\boxed{
S_i=\operatorname{LimitedMode}*i(C*\infty)
}
]

But Ratié also reveals why the word **restriction** needs qualification.

The finite subject is not created by subtracting quantities from an independently completed cosmic subject. Consciousness freely manifests itself as:

* one or many;
* subject or object;
* unity or differentiated form.

Its defining freedom is precisely the ability to take determinate forms without losing its underlying unity. ([Academia][3])

Therefore the correct operator is not a passive filter:

[
L_i(C_\infty)
]

but an active self-determination:

[
\delta_i(C_\infty)
]

where:

[
\delta_i=
\text{a mode in which consciousness determines itself as}
]

[
\text{this knower, with this range of powers, confronting this field}.
]

A more faithful equation is:

[
\boxed{
\delta_i(C_\infty)
==================

\langle
S_i,B_i,K_i,A_i,V_i,T_i,C_i,W_i
\rangle
}
]

The body and limitations are not independent variables added afterward. They belong to the same determinate manifestation as the finite subject and its world.

# 3. Why (M_i) is misleading

Your notation treats:

[
M_1,M_2,M_3
]

as local phenomenal fields produced by restricting:

[
M_\infty.
]

This is useful for comparison with consciousness science, but it risks imposing a contemporary field-individuation model on Pratyabhijñā.

For the Śaivas, the finite subject is not a detachable piece of universal consciousness:

[
M_i\not\subset M_\infty
]

in the sense of a spatial or substantial part.

Nor is it a second consciousness causally generated by the first:

[
M_\infty\not\rightarrow M_i
]

in an ordinary temporal causal sense.

It is the universal consciousness **appearing and acting under a restricted form of self-apprehension**:

[
\boxed{
M_i=
C_\infty
\text{ as apprehending itself through standpoint }i
}
]

The identity is modal:

[
M_i\equiv_i C_\infty
]

meaning:

> the same reality, operating under a particular mode of knowledge and agency.

This is why Ratié describes recognition not as acquisition of new information but as removal of a distraction or false superimposition. The self’s manifestation has never ceased; what changes is the manner in which it identifies and understands itself. ([Academia][3])

Recognition is therefore:

[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_i:
C_\infty\text{ apprehended as only }S_i
\rightarrow
C_\infty\text{ apprehended through }S_i
}
]

The finite standpoint remains. Its exclusivity is removed.

# 4. The world is not generated after the subject

This formula is too sequential:

[
M_i+B_i+G_i+SM_i\rightarrow W_i.
]

It resembles a contemporary predictive-processing pipeline:

```text
phenomenal subject
+ body
+ model
→ experienced world
```

Pratyabhijñā’s relation is more reciprocal and more radical.

The finite subject and its objective world arise together:

[
\boxed{
S_i\leftrightarrow W_i
}
]

A limited knower is only determinate relative to:

* what it can know;
* what it can do;
* what can affect it;
* what appears as external;
* what it excludes from itself.

Likewise, an object is determinate only as something manifest to a subject under particular cognitive conditions.

The better expression is:

[
\boxed{
\delta_i(C_\infty)
==================

\langle
S_i,W_i,\Gamma_i
\rangle
}
]

where:

[
\Gamma_i=
\text{the structured relation between finite knower and finite world}.
]

This is compatible with your five-dimensional constraint set:

[
\Gamma_i=
\langle
K_i,A_i,V_i,T_i,C_i
\rangle
]

but these are not merely constraints imposed on a pre-existing subject. They jointly constitute the finite subject–world structure.

# 5. The role of exclusion

Your model needs a distinct variable for **exclusion**, because this is central to Utpaladeva and is not reducible to informational limitation.

Let:

[
X_i=
\text{the exclusions constituting standpoint }i.
]

A finite subject does not merely fail to access some information. It determines itself as:

[
\text{this and not that}.
]

Its world similarly contains objects determined through exclusion:

[
x=
\text{not-}y,\text{ not-}z,\ldots
]

Prueitt’s reconstruction of the Śaiva transformation of Buddhist *apoha* theory is important here. In Pratyabhijñā, the subject–object division is itself tied to conceptual exclusion and to consciousness’s inherent will. The error is not simply an impoverished sensory sample; it is the presentation of internally manifested distinctions as independently separated subject and object. ([Academia][4])

Therefore contraction has at least two components:

[
L_i=
\text{restricted capacity}
]

and:

[
X_i=
\text{exclusive self-determination}.
]

These are not identical.

A liberated embodied subject may retain:

[
L_i
]

because it still has finite senses, actions, and location, while loosening:

[
X_i
]

the conviction that its finite standpoint exhausts what it is.

Thus:

[
\boxed{
\text{bondage}\neq\text{mere finite architecture}
}
]

More precisely:

[
\boxed{
\text{bondage}
==============

L_i
+
\operatorname{AbsoluteIdentification}(S_i)
+
\operatorname{Externalization}(W_i)
}
]

That distinction is essential.

# 6. Biernacki’s correction: the agent is not unitary even locally

Your formulation says:

[
\text{an agent is the restriction of universal consciousness}
]

as though each organism were one cleanly bounded agent.

Biernacki complicates this.

Her treatment of the subtle body stresses that human volition contains a multiplicity of agencies. The human is “not master in its own house”; embodiment is an ecology of partially autonomous forces rather than one sovereign Cartesian controller. ([OUP Academic][5])

That aligns more closely with:

* Levin’s multiscale agency;
* nested Markov blankets;
* distributed control;
* predictive hierarchies;
* microbial, endocrine, cellular, and neural contributions to behaviour.

The local subject should therefore be represented not as one simple system:

[
S_i
]

but as a hierarchically integrated collection:

[
\mathbb S_i=
{s_i^1,s_i^2,\ldots,s_i^n}
]

with partial alignment under an organism-level organization.

Then:

[
\delta_i(C_\infty)
==================

\langle
\mathbb S_i,W_i,\Gamma_i
\rangle.
]

This is much closer to both Biernacki and Levin:

[
\text{finite embodiment}
========================

\text{a nested ecology of powers temporarily acting as one life}.
]

The organism-level self is real, but not absolutely elementary.

# 7. What *The Matter of Wonder* adds

Biernacki would support your rejection of this picture:

[
\text{dead matter}
\rightarrow
\text{complex organization}
\rightarrow
\text{consciousness}.
]

Her project places foundational subjectivity within materiality rather than treating consciousness as a late addition to an intrinsically non-conscious world. She explicitly presents Abhinavagupta’s panentheism as a way of rethinking the boundaries between matter, life, and consciousness. ([OUP Academic][6])

But her formulation is not simply:

[
M_\infty\rightarrow S_i.
]

It is closer to:

[
\boxed{
\text{matter is one mode of conscious power’s internal differentiation}
}
]

The world is not an output displayed to consciousness. Materiality is part of how consciousness obtains:

* persistence;
* resistance;
* opacity;
* causal differentiation;
* multiple agencies;
* stable forms;
* relations between embodied standpoints.

Her “It from Cit” move is therefore stronger than phenomenal idealism. It concerns information and meaning.

Information is never simply bare difference:

[
\text{difference}\neq\text{meaning}
]

Meaning requires a system for which the difference possesses significance. Biernacki uses Abhinavagupta to challenge an ontology in which meaning is supposedly assembled from fundamentally meaningless bits. Her alternative places intrinsic subjectivity or “cit” prior to meaningful information. ([OUP Academic][7])

So the scientifically relevant contrast is not merely:

[
S_i\rightarrow M_i
\quad\text{versus}\quad
M_\infty\rightarrow S_i.
]

It is:

### Naturalistic construction

[
\text{non-normative dynamics}
\rightarrow
\text{information}
\rightarrow
\text{meaning}
\rightarrow
\text{agency}
\rightarrow
\text{experience}
]

### Biernacki–Abhinavagupta reconstruction

[
\text{intrinsic self-articulating subjectivity}
\rightarrow
\text{meaningful differentiation}
\rightarrow
\text{materially stabilized agents and worlds}.
]

That is a deeper opposition.

# 8. Bäumer’s emphasis

Bäumer’s work is less an attempt to formalize a causal cosmology than to preserve recognition as an experiential and transformative disclosure. She describes the *Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam* as providing an experiential key to nondual Śaivism. ([Utpaladeva][8])

Her emphasis warns against reducing recognition to:

[
\text{a new proposition in the self-model}.
]

The doctrine claims that recognition reorganizes the mode in which:

* perception occurs;
* the body is inhabited;
* action is attributed;
* multiplicity is experienced;
* the world appears as other.

A merely representational account would be:

[
SM_i\rightarrow SM_i'.
]

A more faithful account is:

[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_i:
\Gamma_i\rightarrow\Gamma_i'
}
]

The whole subject–world relation changes.

The finite organism continues to operate, but objects are no longer apprehended as ontologically independent of manifest consciousness, and finite action is no longer attributed to an absolutely separate doer.

Thus recognition is not merely:

> “I now believe I am universal consciousness.”

It is:

[
\boxed{
\text{a transformation in the enacted relation between self, world, agency, and manifestation}.
}
]

# 11. Does Ratié think the argument succeeds?

Ratié reconstructs the arguments with great precision, but does not simply endorse every inference.

Her work repeatedly identifies places where the Śaiva conclusion depends upon controversial steps. In her examination of the Śaiva transformation of *satkāryavāda*, she notes that Abhinavagupta treats causes and effects as ways universal consciousness appears to itself, but also observes that he does not explicitly justify the crucial transition at the point where it is most needed. ([Academia][9])

That matters enormously.

The school can show:

[
\text{objects as known are forms appearing in cognition}.
]

It can argue:

[
\text{cognition is self-manifest and cannot be reduced to an inert object}.
]

It can expose difficulties in explaining:

* memory;
* determination;
* agency;
* first-person unity;
* other minds

through momentary mechanical sequences.

But the jump remains:

[
\text{all objects are manifest through consciousness}
]

therefore:

[
\text{all reality is the self-manifestation of one universal consciousness}.
]

Ratié’s scholarship makes that leap clearer; it does not make it disappear.

# 12. The strongest justification for the universal step

The best Pratyabhijñā justification is not simply that every individual is conscious.

It is this chain:

### 1. Consciousness can manifest different contents without losing itself.

[
C(x)\rightarrow C(y)
]

while the subject recognizes continuity.

### 2. Its nature is therefore not exhausted by any one determinate content.

[
C\neq x,\quad C\neq y
]

### 3. Consciousness can assume the form of objects while remaining the unitary basis in which they appear.

### 4. Any proposed external object is known only through a manifestation whose form already belongs to consciousness.

Ratié reconstructs the Śaiva criticism of attempts to infer something existing beyond consciousness: the school argues that the inference cannot establish a reality categorically outside manifestation. ([Academia][10])

### 5. Subject–object separation is constituted through determination and exclusion within consciousness.

### 6. Therefore apparent externality need not indicate ontological independence.

### 7. The most parsimonious ontology is one universal, form-assuming consciousness rather than a consciousness plus unknowable external objects.

This gives:

[
\boxed{
\text{universal consciousness as an explanatory unification}
}
]

It does not give an incontrovertible deduction.

A realist can still answer:

> The fact that external reality is accessible only through consciousness does not imply that its existence consists in consciousness.

That epistemic–ontological gap remains.

[

]

with:

[
\boxed{
C_\infty\xrightarrow{\delta_i}
\langle
\mathbb S_i,W_i,\Gamma_i

}
]

Replace:

> “An agent is the restriction of manifestation.”

with:

> **A finite agent–world system is a self-determination of universal manifest agency under restricted knowledge, action, value, temporality, causal situation, and exclusive identification.**

Replace:

[
M_i+B_i+G_i+SM_i\rightarrow W_i
]

with:

[
\boxed{
\langle
\mathbb S_i,W_i,\Gamma_i
\rangle
=======

And distinguish:

[
\text{limitation}
]

from:

[
\text{bondage}.
]

Finite limitation makes a perspective possible.

Bondage is treating that perspective as an independent and exhaustive self.

# Final formulation

[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
C_\infty
&=
\text{universal self-manifest, self-determining agency},\
\delta_i
&=
\text{one restricted mode of its knowledge, action, value, time,}\

That is the cleanest formulation I can defend as simultaneously:

* faithful to Torella’s reconstruction of cognition, memory, and exclusion;
* faithful to Ratié’s analysis of universal consciousness, action, otherness, and recognition;
* compatible with Biernacki’s panentheistic and multiscale account of matter and agency;

* and precise enough to compare with active inference, predictive processing, and multiscale agency.

Its unresolved claim remains exactly one:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{Why must the common ground of finite manifestation be one self-determining subject, rather than neutral reality producing multiple local subjects?}

The scholars confirm that this is what Pratyabhijñā asserts and tries to demonstrate. They do not eliminate the contested inference by which the system reaches it.
