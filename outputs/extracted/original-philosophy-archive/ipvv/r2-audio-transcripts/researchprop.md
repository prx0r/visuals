This proposal treats the **law of perspectival partition** as the shared explanandum. It does not presuppose physicalism, idealism, Buddhism, or Pratyabhijñā, and it separates textual reconstruction from scientific testing. The attached Checkpoint 10 supplies the immediate conceptual basis. 

# THE PERSPECTIVAL PARTITION PROJECT

## A comparative research programme in consciousness science and Sanskrit philosophy

## 1. Project title

**The Perspectival Partition Project: Manifestation, Subject Individuation, and the Formation of Phenomenal Domains**

## 2. Central problem

Consciousness does not appear as an undifferentiated totality.

Experience occurs in apparently bounded domains:

* these perceptions, thoughts and feelings are jointly present;
* another person’s simultaneous experiences are absent;
* contents remain integrated across some temporal intervals;
* integration may weaken, divide, overlap or reorganize;
* the resulting domain is ordinarily experienced from one embodied standpoint.

The central problem is therefore:

> **What establishes the domains within which experiences are jointly manifest, and outside which other experiences are absent?**

Call this the **perspectival partition problem**.

A satisfactory theory must explain both:

### Phenomenal inclusion

Why two experiences occur together as parts of one manifest field.

### Phenomenal exclusion

Why another experience is absent from that field.

The project’s guiding thesis is methodological:

> The hard problem of consciousness contains an individuation problem that cannot be reduced to asking why processing is conscious.

Even if a theory explained why some process was phenomenally manifest, it would still need to explain:

* why manifestation has this boundary;
* why it occurs at this scale;
* why its contents are unified;
* why other contents are excluded;
* why the domain persists or divides through time.

Contemporary philosophy distinguishes phenomenal unity from access and representational unity, while split-brain cases leave open one-stream, two-stream and partially unified models. This means that behavioral and informational integration do not straightforwardly determine phenomenal domain structure.

---

# 3. Research objective

The project will identify and compare the strongest available accounts of phenomenal domain formation across:

1. Sanskrit philosophical traditions;
2. contemporary philosophy of mind;
3. neuroscience and cognitive science;
4. biological theories of multiscale agency;
5. process, structural and neutral-monist metaphysics.

The aim is not to prove that one ancient system anticipated modern science.

The aim is to determine:

> **Which explanatory work requires a subject, which requires only organization or process, and which remains unexplained by every current theory?**

---

# 4. Source-neutral framework

The framework begins with no commitment regarding whether consciousness is:

* generated by physical organization;
* identical with organization;
* an intrinsic aspect of neutral reality;
* locally fundamental;
* cosmically fundamental;
* or the self-articulation of universal consciousness.

## 4.1 Minimal explananda

Every candidate theory must account for the following.

### Manifestness

Why the relevant states are experienced rather than merely causally operative.

### Reflexivity

Whether conscious occurrence is intrinsically self-present or becomes available only through an additional process.

### Co-presence

Why some experiences are jointly present.

### Exclusion

Why other experiences are absent.

### Temporality

Why experiences form one temporally extended stream rather than isolated moments.

### Ownership

Why a state is presented as mine, anonymous, or belonging to some other subject.

### Boundary

What distinguishes one phenomenal domain from its subagents, environment and other domains.

### Scale

Why subjecthood occurs at the neuronal, hemispheric, organismic or some other scale.

### Agency

Whether the phenomenal domain acts, controls, selects or merely accompanies causal organization.

### Transformation

Whether subjects can split, merge, overlap, expand or contract.

# 5. Propositions already established

The project will treat the following as provisional constraints rather than reopen them in every analysis.

## Proposition 1 — Cognition is not passive copying

Perception is an active, temporally extended organism–environment achievement.

## Proposition 2 — Subjects and cognitions need not be atomic

A subject or cognitive event may be a real higher-level pattern without possessing one exact microscopic boundary.

## Proposition 3 — Finite agency requires restriction

A finite agent necessarily possesses bounded:

* information;
* action;
* valuation;
* temporal reach;
* causal situation.

This is compatible with every major metaphysical position and therefore does not favor Pratyabhijñā by itself.

## Proposition 4 — Limitation is not bondage

Finite architecture is required for determinate embodied perspective.

Bondage, in the Pratyabhijñā reconstruction, additionally involves treating the finite standpoint as independent and exhaustive.

## Proposition 5 — Information continuity is not ownership

A causal trace may explain retention without explaining why a past event is presented as previously experienced by the present subject.

## Proposition 6 — Reflexivity does not establish universal identity

Even if every experience has an intrinsic first-personal character, it does not follow that every experience belongs to one numerically identical subject.

## Proposition 7 — Organization does not yet determine phenomenal boundaries

Agent boundaries, Markov blankets, recurrent processing and global access may identify functional systems without uniquely identifying phenomenal domains.

---

# 6. The central theoretical object

Let a **phenomenal domain** mean a collection of experiential elements that are jointly manifest.

The project does not initially assume that such domains form clean, mutually exclusive sets.

They may instead be:

* nested;
* overlapping;
* graded;
* temporally variable;
* only partially unified.

This is important because contemporary split-brain debate includes partial-unity models in which two experiences may each be unified with a third without being unified with one another. Such models challenge the assumption that phenomenal unity is necessarily transitive.

The project therefore asks whether phenomenal structure is best represented as:

* a partition;
* a graph;
* a hypergraph;
* a hierarchy;
* a sheaf of local domains;
* a temporally evolving topology.

The mathematical representation is secondary. It will be adopted only if it generates testable distinctions or exposes invalid inferences.

# 7. Principal research questions

## RQ1 — Manifestation

What distinguishes a manifest state from a structurally equivalent non-conscious process?

## RQ2 — Reflexivity

Does every conscious occurrence possess prereflective self-presence?

Phenomenological reflexivism maintains that experience is first-personally given before reflection or narrative self-representation. This remains contested, particularly concerning whether such “for-me-ness” is universal or partly constructed.

## RQ3 — Phenomenal inclusion

What makes two experiences co-conscious rather than merely simultaneous, causally connected or jointly accessible?

## RQ4 — Phenomenal exclusion

What makes another experience absent from the current domain?

## RQ5 — Diachronic unity

What makes experiences at different times parts of one life?

Candidate answers include:

* one persisting substance;
* prereflective continuity;
* causal continuity;
* bodily continuity;
* memory reconstruction;
* dynamically maintained organization.

## RQ6 — Subject scale

Why is one subject associated with an organism rather than:

* each cell;
* each neural subsystem;
* each hemisphere;
* the organism–environment system;
* or a larger collective?

## RQ7 — Universalization

What would justify moving from locally reflexive domains to one universal ground or subject?

## RQ8 — Decombination

If consciousness is universal, what makes genuinely private local domains possible?

Cosmopsychism faces an analogous decombination problem: a conscious universe does not automatically explain the existence of local conscious subjects.

## RQ9 — Freedom

Does manifest determination involve merely disclosure, minimal selection, genuine action or unrestricted creative power?

## RQ10 — Recognition

What changes in liberating recognition:

* conceptual belief;
* self-attribution;
* phenomenal ownership;
* perceived subject–world structure;
* or the actual ontology of the subject?

# 8. Rival model classes

Each model will be reconstructed in its strongest form.

## 8.1 Emergent naturalism

Non-conscious processes generate bounded organisms, cognition and eventually consciousness.

### Burden

Explain why the relevant organization is intrinsically manifest and why it forms one phenomenal domain.

## 8.2 Organizational identity

Consciousness is identical with an appropriate organization rather than produced by it.

Examples may include versions of integrated-information, global-workspace or recurrent-processing theories.

### Burden

Specify why this organization, grain and boundary constitute one subject rather than merely correlate with conscious reports.

The 2025 adversarial collaboration between IIT and GNWT tested preregistered predictions and challenged central predictions of both programmes without decisively validating either as a complete theory.

## 8.3 Buddhist process theories

Cognition consists in causally related, possibly reflexive events without a permanent owner.

### Burden

Explain co-presence, diachronic ownership and domain exclusion without reifying an integrative function into a subject.

## 8.4 Nyāya plural-substance realism

Many enduring selves bear changing cognitive qualities within one independently real world.

### Burden

Explain why qualities inhering in one self become jointly manifest and how a self that is not intrinsically consciousness becomes a phenomenal subject.

## 8.5 Phenomenological reflexivism

Experience includes prereflective first-personal givenness but need not imply a substantial or cosmic self.

### Burden

Explain subject individuation and diachronic unity without simply assuming one local owner.

## 8.6 Neutral or Russellian monism

Physical structure and phenomenality are aspects of a more fundamental reality.

### Burden

Explain the mapping from underlying structure to particular phenomenal domains.

## 8.7 Cosmopsychism

Local subjects are grounded in a conscious cosmos.

### Burden

Solve decombination: why and how a cosmic subject grounds many private local subjects.

## 8.8 Pratyabhijñā

Finite subjects and worlds are restricted articulations of universal self-manifest, self-determining consciousness.

### Burdens

1. Explain phenomenal partition.
2. Establish why local reflexivity has a universal ground.
3. Distinguish universal ground from one all-containing cosmic perspective.
4. Derive freedom rather than merely asserting it.
5. Explain why recognition changes identity without eliminating embodiment.

# 9. Sanskrit research track

## 9.1 Methodological principle

The Sanskrit track will not ask:

> What does this tradition say about consciousness?

It will ask:

> What exact explanandum is the passage addressing, what argument does it offer, and what cheaper rival could explain the same phenomenon?

Each textual object will contain:

1. Sanskrit passage;
2. source and edition;
3. literal translation;
4. philosophical translation;
5. opponent;
6. explicit premises;
7. hidden premises;
8. conclusion asserted;
9. conclusion validly established;
10. strongest countermodel;
11. unresolved residual.

## 9.2 Claim statuses

* **T — Textual**
* **L — Linguistically supported**
* **R — Reconstructed**
* **V — Valid inference**
* **D — Defeasible**
* **C — Structural correspondence**
* **E — Empirical**
* **H — Hypothesis**
* **U — Unresolved**
* **X — Rejected**

No textual assertion will be upgraded to a valid conclusion merely because a commentator endorses it.

# 11. Scientific research track

Study:

* partial unity;

* disorders of ownership and agency.

### Target

Determine whether phenomenal unity is:

## 11.2 Consciousness theories

Compare:

* IIT;
* GNWT;
* recurrent processing;
* higher-order theories;
* predictive processing;
* active-inference approaches.

### Required questions

For every theory:

1. What selects the subject boundary?
2. What selects the relevant scale?
3. What excludes overlapping candidate systems?
4. Does it explain phenomenal inclusion or only information access?
5. What would count as evidence of division or fusion?
6. Does it explain manifestness or assume it?

## 11.3 Multiscale biological agency

Examine:

* cellular goal-directedness;
* bioelectric coordination;
* morphogenetic memory;
* top-down constraint;
* nested control systems.

### Target

Determine whether larger biological agency supplies:

* a functional boundary;
* a normative boundary;
* a cognitive boundary;
* or a phenomenal boundary.

These must not be conflated.

## 11.4 Neurophenomenology

Study whether first-personality remains identifiable across:

* radically changing perceptual contents;
* reduced narrative self-processing;
* minimal-content states;
* dreaming;
* contemplative absorption;
* disturbances of body ownership.

### Target

Test the limited claim that prereflective first-personality may be content-invariant.

Even positive evidence would not establish universal consciousness.

# 13. Proof obligations

A theory succeeds only if it meets the proof obligations appropriate to its claims.

## Naturalist proof obligation

Show why a nonphenomenally described organization is identical with, rather than merely correlated with, one phenomenal domain.

## Buddhist proof obligation

Show why momentary or processual events form one co-conscious stream without an additional owner.

## Nyāya proof obligation

Show why cognitive qualities inhering in one substance are jointly manifest to that substance.

## Phenomenological proof obligation

Show whether intrinsic for-me-ness entails a local subject or merely perspectival givenness.

## Cosmopsychist proof obligation

Show how a cosmic subject grounds many private local domains.

## Pratyabhijñā proof obligation

Show why unrestricted manifestation possesses an intrinsic law of local exclusion without becoming divided into independent substances.

# 14. Falsification and model elimination

The project does not assign metaphysical probabilities by intuition.

A model loses standing when it:

* cannot explain the target phenomenon;
* changes the meaning of a term during an inference;
* places its conclusion inside a premise;
* confuses epistemic dependence with ontological constitution;
* confuses shared structure with numerical identity;
* merely renames the explanandum;
* cannot specify a subject boundary;
* cannot distinguish functional and phenomenal unity;
* protects itself from all counterevidence by definition.

Possible outcomes include:

* **Established**
* **Best current explanation**
* **Plausible but underdetermined**
* **Invalid inference**
* **Metaphysical posit without discriminator**
* **Empirically unsupported**
* **Textually asserted but philosophically unproved**
* **Open**

An unresolved verdict counts as progress when it precisely locates the missing premise.

# 17. Original contribution

The project’s central proposed contribution is not a claim that Sanskrit philosophy solved consciousness.

It is the discovery of a shared missing object:

> **A theory of consciousness must explain not only manifestation, but the formation, persistence and exclusion structure of phenomenal domains.**

This reframes several debates.

## Naturalism

The task is not merely:

> Why does processing become conscious?

It is:

> Why does this organization constitute this domain of co-presence?

## Buddhism

The task is not merely:

> Can continuity exist without a self?

It is:

> What binds reflexive moments into one domain and excludes others?

## Nyāya

The task is not merely:

> Does an enduring self exist?

It is:

> Why does inherence in one self create phenomenal co-presence?

## Cosmopsychism

The task is not merely:

> Is the cosmos conscious?

It is:

> How does cosmic consciousness decompose into private perspectives?

## Pratyabhijñā

The task is not merely:

> Are finite subjects Śiva?

It is:

> What power of universal manifestation establishes genuine local exclusion without producing independent substances?

# 18. Working hypothesis

The source-neutral working hypothesis is:

> A finite subject is a dynamically maintained phenomenal domain characterized by co-presence, temporal continuity, affective concern, coordinated agency and an exclusion profile.

This does not yet state what produces or grounds the domain.

The competing hypotheses are:

### Naturalist

The domain is constituted by a specific organization.

### Buddhist-process

The domain is a conventionally individuated stream of reflexive and causally connected events.

### Nyāya

The domain consists of cognitions inhering in one enduring self.

### Neutral-monist

The domain is the intrinsic aspect of one underlying physical–phenomenal structure.

### Pratyabhijñā

The domain is a locally exclusive articulation of universal self-manifest power.

The project tests these against the same proof obligations.

# 19. Final research proposition

The proposal begins from the following proposition:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{A complete theory of consciousness must specify a law}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{that determines phenomenal inclusion, exclusion, scale}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{and temporal continuity.}
}
]

Call the unknown law:

[
\Lambda=\text{the law of perspectival partition}.
]

The project does not assume that (\Lambda) is literally one equation or mechanism.

It may prove to be:

* a physical identity;
* a family of organizational constraints;
* a metaphysical relation;
* a process of self-articulation;
* or evidence that the initial concept of a sharply bounded subject is mistaken.

The project succeeds if it does any of the following:

1. derives a defensible partition principle;
2. identifies the strongest current candidate;
3. eliminates major rival accounts;
4. proves that phenomenal domains are not clean partitions;
5. precisely locates the premises no current theory can justify.

The governing question is:

> **Why does reality manifest here, together, through this history and this body, while excluding what is manifest elsewhere?**

The next useful move is to turn **Phase 1 and the first research cycle** into a fixed agent protocol and source packet; the proposal itself is now sufficiently neutral to govern both the Sanskrit and scientific workstreams.
