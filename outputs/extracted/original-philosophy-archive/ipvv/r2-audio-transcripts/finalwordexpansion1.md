# 2. QRI: the strongest immediate lead

Its Symmetry Theory of Valence proposes that the valence of a conscious state is determined by symmetry in the mathematical object describing that state. QRI separates three problems:

[
\mathbf D
\overset{\mathcal C}{\longrightarrow}
\mathbf S
\overset{\mathcal I}{\longrightarrow}
\text{phenomenological properties},
]

where:

* (\mathbf D): neurophysiological data;
* (\mathbf S): proposed consciousness-state space;
* (\mathcal C): mapping from data to formal conscious-state structure;
* (\mathcal I): interpretation of that structure. ([Qri][1])

This is almost exactly our frontier graph:

[
\Sigma_i
\overset{\mathcal C}{\longrightarrow}
\mathfrak S_i
\overset{\Lambda_\Phi}{\longrightarrow}

## What QRI adds

QRI takes seriously the possibility that phenomenology has:

* geometric structure;
* symmetries;
* topological invariants;
* harmonic modes;
* coupling relations;
* transformations across altered states.

It also develops psychophysical tools intended to quantify complex visual textures and altered-state phenomenology rather than reducing everything to coarse report categories. ([Qualia Research Institute][2])

This is valuable because most consciousness science measures:

* whether something was detected;
* whether it was reportable;
* how confident the subject was.

QRI asks about the internal shape of the experience.

# 3. Tymoczko: the best methodological lead

Tymoczko’s geometry of music is not a theory of consciousness. Its relevance is methodological and potentially enormous.

A chord can be represented as a point in a geometric space. Musical equivalences—such as octave equivalence, permutation of voices, and in some cases transposition—identify points that differ in one representation but count as the same musical object. Quotienting by these symmetries produces orbifolds. For example, Tymoczko describes two-note chord spaces with Möbius-strip-like structure and three-note chord types using cone-like quotient spaces. Voice leadings become paths through these spaces. ([Princeton University][4])

The fundamental construction is:

[
X
\longrightarrow
X/G,
]

where:

* (X): a high-dimensional labelled space;
* (G): a group of transformations treated as equivalences;
* (X/G): the quotient space of meaningful objects.

## Why this matters

The experienced world may be constructed similarly.

The nervous system receives a huge field of variation:

[
X_{\text{sensory}}.
]

But it treats many transformations as preserving one object:

* translation;
* rotation;
* scale change;
* illumination change;
* movement;
* partial occlusion;
* changing viewpoint.

An object is therefore not one raw state.

It is something like an equivalence class:

[
[x]
===

{g(x):g\in G_i}.
]

The bottle remains the bottle across many sensorimotor transformations.

This gives a precise mathematical interpretation of Ñāṇavīra’s:

[
\boxed{
\text{identity through transformation}.
}
]

## The deeper transfer

Tymoczko does not begin by assuming that “a chord” is a primitive object.

He derives chord space from:

1. primitive dimensions;
2. equivalence relations;
3. quotient operations;
4. distance metrics;
5. permissible paths.

We should do the same for experience.

Instead of beginning with:

[
\text{phenomenal contents}
]

as already individuated objects, ask:

1. What are the primitive phenomenal variations?
2. Which transformations preserve experienced identity?
3. Which distinctions are ignored?
4. Which transformations create a new experience?
5. What are the singularities of phenomenal space?
6. Which experiential transitions are short, difficult or impossible?

## The phenomenal-orbifold hypothesis

Let:

[
X_P
]

be a high-dimensional pre-objectual state space.

Let:

[
G_i
]

be the transformations participant (i) treats as preserving identity.

Then:

[
W_i
===

X_P/G_i
]

is the participant’s structured world.

Different organisms could inhabit different quotient spaces because:

[
G_i\neq G_j.
]

A bat, human and octopus do not merely receive different data.

They may quotient possibility space through different invariances.

This offers a stronger account of perspective:

[
\boxed{
\text{a perspective is partly a choice of invariances}.
}
]

## Connection to the partition problem

Participant individuation may involve not only a boundary but a specific quotient operation:

[
\Pi_i
=====

(G_i,d_i,\mathcal P_i),
]

where:

* (G_i): transformations treated as equivalent;
* (d_i): experienced distance metric;
* (\mathcal P_i): permitted transition paths.

Then:

[
D_i
===

\Lambda_\Phi
\left(
X_P/G_i,
\Sigma_i
\right).
]

This does not solve manifestness, but it makes perspective mathematically concrete.

## The highest-value question generated by Tymoczko

[
\boxed{
\textbf{Are phenomenal subjects distinguished by different}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\textbf{quotient geometries over a shared possibility space?}
}
]

That is a real frontier programme.

# 8. Channeling may be more important than the channeled cosmologies

The existence of Seth or Selig’s Guides as independent beings has not been established.

But channeling itself is a remarkable phenomenon requiring explanation.

A person can produce:

* extended coherent speech;
* a stable alternate voice;
* a distinct claimed identity;
* unfamiliar-seeming content;
* reduced sense of authorship;
* rapid verbal production;
* long-term stylistic consistency.

Studies of channeling and mediumship exist, but the evidence base is methodologically mixed. Reviews emphasize the need for stronger blinding, source controls and statistical procedures. Controlled protocols are being developed, but they do not currently establish discarnate or nonphysical authorship. ([ResearchGate][8])

The Jane Roberts case has been examined under multiple hypotheses, including fraud, cryptomnesia, creative dissociation, subconscious cognition, extrasensory access and independent agency; published discussion does not decisively establish Seth’s claimed discarnate identity. ([Rivier University][9])

## Why channeling matters to our frontier

It directly attacks:

[
P_{06}
\rightarrow
P_{07}
\rightarrow
P_{08}.
]

Specifically:

* How is authorship assigned?
* How can coherent agency arise without ordinary ownership?
* Can one organism instantiate multiple stable agent models?
* What makes speech feel received rather than produced?
* Can cognition access information unavailable to the ordinary self-model?
* What distinguishes dissociated construction from external agency?

Channeling may reveal that:

[
\boxed{
\text{cognitive authorship}
\neq
\text{phenomenal ownership}.
}
]

A process can generate language while the ordinary ego experiences itself as not generating it.

That is a direct experimental route into the construction of:

[
\text{“I am the thinker.”}
]

## Proper test programme

A serious channeling study should compare:

1. ordinary speech;
2. improvisational performance;
3. automatic speech;
4. claimed channeling;
5. deliberately simulated channeling.

Measure:

* linguistic fingerprints;
* semantic novelty;
* error patterns;
* source monitoring;
* agency attribution;
* neural dynamics;
* physiological state;
* information accuracy under blinded conditions;
* longitudinal identity consistency.

The extraordinary claim should be tested separately:

[
\text{Does the material contain independently verified information}
]

[
\text{unavailable through ordinary means?}
]

Without that, channeling is evidence for unusual cognition, not external entities.
