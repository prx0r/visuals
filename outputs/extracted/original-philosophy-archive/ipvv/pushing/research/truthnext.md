Go deeper into **specific Nyāya and Buddhist arguments first**, not Navya-Nyāya as a whole and not the whole tantric corpus.

The immediate bottleneck is extremely precise:

[
\text{local synthesis}
;\not\Rightarrow;
\text{one universal subject}.
]

You need texts that either:

1. supply Utpaladeva’s missing premise;
2. explain the same phenomena with plural selves;
3. explain them with no persisting self;
4. replace the subject with relation, process, or holistic meaning.

## 1. Best text sequence

### Phase A — finish Utpaladeva’s proof

#### 1. Selected *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā* with *Vṛtti*

This is the highest-value source because GRETIL’s text is revised against Torella’s edition and preserves stable section numbering.

Translate argument dossiers rather than the whole work initially:

* **1.3.6–7:** whether mutually isolated cognitions can know or connect with one another;
* **1.4:** memory and past ownership;
* **1.5.11–19:** *prakāśa*, *vimarśa*, determination, direct perception and freedom;
* **1.7:** the synthesizing subject;
* **1.8:** absence, objectivity and manifestation;
* **2.3–2.4:** relation, causal order and synthesis;
* passages concerning other minds and inference from action.

The task is to identify every transition in this ladder:

```text
self-manifest cognition
→ first-personality
→ diachronic unity
→ persisting knower
→ unrestricted knower
→ universal Lord
```

Each arrow receives a separate verdict.

#### 2. *Sambandhasiddhi*

This should come next, because “relation” is currently carrying almost every unexplained step:

* relation between cognition and object;
* past and present;
* finite and universal;
* self and other;
* unity and difference.

The decisive question is:

> Can two terms be genuinely different without requiring an independently existing relation between them?

Do not use it merely as supplementary doctrine. Use it to define whether Utpaladeva has an actual account of modal identity or only asserts that the finite and universal are “the same in different modes.”

#### 3. *Īśvarasiddhi*

This is where the transition from cognition to Lordship must be audited.

Search specifically for arguments connecting:

* self-establishment to independence;
* independence to agency;
* agency to unrestricted agency;
* unrestricted agency to world-manifestation.

The likely weakness is:

[
\text{epistemic self-presence}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{causal sovereignty}.
]

The text may supply another premise; until it does, *svātantrya* is not derived.

#### 4. Abhinavagupta’s *Vimarśinī* and *Vivṛtivimarśinī*

Use these after the root arguments are mapped. Otherwise Abhinavagupta’s elaboration may conceal where Utpaladeva’s original inference was compressed.

Ask only:

* Does Abhinavagupta identify the missing premise?
* Does he change a deduction into a transcendental or abductive argument?
* Does he introduce scriptural premises?
* Does he answer plural-self and Buddhist countermodels?

---

### Phase B — strongest external controls

#### 5. Udayana’s *Ātmatattvaviveka*

This is the best next major project—not general Navya-Nyāya.

It is a sustained Nyāya defence of enduring selves against Buddhist momentariness and idealism. Sanskrit editions with English translations and extensive analysis exist, including Dravid’s large study and other annotated translations.

It supplies the cleanest rival:

```text
memory + recognition + agency
→ enduring individual selves
→ real external objects
→ other minds by inference
```

No universal consciousness required.

Translate thematically:

1. critique of momentariness;
2. recognitive perception—“this is the same object”;
3. memory and the self;
4. causal persistence;
5. other minds;
6. external-world realism.

Udayana’s argument that a cause may persist while waiting for auxiliary conditions directly attacks the Buddhist claim that causal efficacy requires momentariness.

This is the essential discriminating test:

> Does plural realism explain everything Utpaladeva explains, while avoiding the numerical-unity leap?

#### 6. Foundational Nyāya before Navya-Nyāya

Use your Jīvānanda collection in this order:

1. *Tarkasaṅgraha* with commentary;
2. *Bhāṣāpariccheda* and *Muktāvalī*;
3. relevant *Nyāyasūtra* and commentarial passages;
4. *Ātmatattvaviveka*;
5. only then Navya-Nyāya analytical texts.

You need stable concepts first:

* self;
* cognition;
* inherence;
* memory;
* recognition;
* inference;
* relation;
* causation.

Navya-Nyāya can later give you a much sharper technical language for qualifiers, relations, absences and cognition-objects. Beginning there now would create enormous formal overhead before the main metaphysical alternatives are stable.

### Verdict on Navya-Nyāya

[
\boxed{
\text{Use Navya-Nyāya later as an analytical language, not now as the primary rival.}
}
]

---

### Phase C — Buddhist countermodels

Build one controlled Buddhist packet, not “Buddhism” generally:

* Dignāga on reflexive awareness and memory;
* Dharmakīrti on momentariness, causal continuity and *svalakṣaṇa*;
* Ratnakīrti where directly relevant;
* Madhyamaka critiques of intrinsic reflexivity;
* Buddhist *apoha* sources targeted by Utpaladeva.

The interrogation is:

```text
Can causal continuity explain:
- content retention?
- remembered ownership?
- recognition?
- agency?
- stable reference?
without a persisting knower?
```

This gives you the cheapest no-self model.

---

### Phase D — internal Śaiva alternatives

#### 7. *Spandakārikā* with multiple commentaries

Do not translate only Kṣemarāja. Mature Kṣemarāja already reads Spanda through the completed Pratyabhijñā synthesis.

Compare:

* Kallaṭa;
* Rāmakaṇṭha;
* Bhagavadutpala;
* Kṣemarāja.

Dyczkowski’s edition/translation is valuable because it presents the root stanzas with four major commentaries.

Question:

> Is the invariant a persisting subject, or the self-renewing dynamism of the process itself?

#### 8. Krama and *Mahānayaprakāśa*

This is the best original translation frontier once the core dispute is stabilized.

Question:

> Can emergence, persistence, dissolution and the “fourth” explain experiential unity without a static witness?

Do not assume Krama solves the problem. The fourth phase may simply reintroduce the universal ground under process language.

#### 9. Somānanda’s *Śivadṛṣṭi*

Use this genealogically:

> Which commitments were inherited before Utpaladeva constructed his arguments?

This helps distinguish:

* conclusion first, justification later;
* actual philosophical discovery;
* later retrofit.

The textual situation is less secure than for the ĪPK, so it should not be the first automated corpus.

---

### Phase E — Bhartṛhari

GRETIL has a complete searchable *Vākyapadīya* and a second text based on Rau’s edition for major sections.

Target:

* unitary sentence meaning;
* sequence versus nonsequence;
* *pratibhā*;
* relation;
* time;
* action;
* whole and parts.

The question is:

> Can unitary meaning perform the explanatory work attributed to a persisting subject?

This may be a cheaper account of synthesis:

```text
sequential components
→ holistic intelligibility
```

without:

```text
sequential components
→ one metaphysical owner
```

Deliverable:

## Sprint 4 — process alternatives

Corpus:

* early Spanda commentaries;
* *Mahānayaprakāśa*;

> Whether process or holistic meaning replaces numerical subject identity.

Only then move heavily into Navya-Nyāya or the large tantric manuscript corpus.

# 3. Semi-automated pipeline

Do **not** build one autonomous “translator agent.”

Build a deterministic workflow with small model calls and hard gates:

```text
source acquisition
→ segmentation
→ philological analysis
→ translation candidates
→ argument extraction
→ adversarial countermodels
→ audit
→ human approval
→ graph ingestion
```

## Stage 1 — Source registry

Store exact source metadata:

```json
{
  "text_id": "utpaladeva_ipk_vrtti",
  "work": "Isvarapratyabhijnakarika with Vrtti",
  "author": "Utpaladeva",
  "edition": "Torella-revised GRETIL transcription",
  "source_url": "...",
  "license_note": "...",
  "reference_scheme": "Ipk_1,5.11",
  "text_layers": ["karika", "vrtti"]
}
```

GRETIL provides machine-readable plain text, pada-marked and TEI-style representations for many texts, including the ĪPK and *Vṛtti*.  Muktabodha provides more than 3,000 preserved texts and over 570 searchable e-texts, heavily concentrated in Śaiva and tantric literature.

Never scrape silently without checking terms. Record where each segment came from.

## Stage 2 — Segment objects

Split by the smallest meaningful unit:

* verse;
* prose gloss;
* commentary paragraph;
* opponent statement;
* reply.

```json
{
  "segment_id": "utajp_17_root",
  "parent": "utajp_17",
  "layer": "mula",
  "sanskrit": "...",
  "preceding_context": ["utajp_16"],
  "following_context": ["utajp_18"]
}
```

Preserve root text and commentary separately.

## Stage 3 — Philology pass

The model outputs strict JSON:

```json
{
  "padaccheda": [],
  "tokens": [
    {
      "surface": "",
      "lemma": "",
      "morphology": "",
      "syntactic_role": "",
      "confidence": 0.0
    }
  ],
  "compounds": [],
  "syntax_options": [],
  "technical_terms": [],
  "uncertainties": []
}
```

Use reference tools or local Sanskrit analyzers where possible. The model proposes analyses; it does not certify them.

A recent Sanskrit digital-reader project reports strong results from a hybrid pipeline combining rule-based splitting, lexicon lookups, LLM-assisted analysis, confidence bands and a persistent human-correction loop. That architecture is more appropriate than pure end-to-end LLM translation.

## Stage 4 — Three translations

Separate model calls should produce:

1. **literal syntactic translation**;
2. **contextual philosophical translation**;
3. **commentarial/doctrinal interpretation**.

Store all three. Never let the doctrinal interpretation overwrite the literal one.

```json
{
  "literal": "",
  "philosophical": "",
  "doctrinal": "",
  "translation_dependencies": [],
  "confidence": {
    "syntax": 0.78,
    "technical_terms": 0.62
  }
}
```

## Stage 5 — Argument extraction

```json
{
  "explanandum": "diachronic ownership",
  "opponent": "vijnanasantana theorist",
  "premises_explicit": [],
  "premises_hidden": [],
  "conclusion_textual": "",
  "conclusion_validated": "",
  "inference_type": "abduction",
  "non_entailments": [],
  "status": "underdetermined"
}
```

The fields `conclusion_textual` and `conclusion_validated` must remain separate.

Example:

```text
Textual conclusion:
one universal subject

Validated conclusion:
a local cognitive stream requires some form of synthesis
```

That single distinction prevents most hallucinated “proofs.”

## Stage 6 — Countermodel dispatch

Run independent calls:

* Buddhist stream model;
* Nyāya plural-self model;
* process/dynamical model;
* reflexivist-local-subject model;
* Śaiva defence.

Each must answer the **same JSON schema**:

```json
{
  "theory": "Nyaya",
  "accepts_premises": [],
  "rejects_premises": [],
  "alternative_explanation": "",
  "added_commitments": [],
  "unexplained_residuals": [],
  "decisive_objection": ""
}
```

## Stage 7 — Logic audit

A final reasoning model checks:

* conclusion already hidden in a premise;
* epistemic-to-ontological slide;
* local-to-universal slide;
* structural sameness versus numerical identity;
* equivocation in Sanskrit terms;
* root text/commentary conflation;
* invalid use of analogy.

It should output only:

```json
{
  "valid_steps": [],
  "invalid_steps": [],
  "disputed_steps": [],
  "missing_premises": [],
  "verdict": ""
}
```

## Stage 8 — Human gate

Require manual approval for:

* Sanskrit segmentation below a confidence threshold;
* every root translation;
* hidden premises;
* universalization claims;
* cross-tradition correspondences;
* final verdicts.

Your corrections become durable overrides:

```json
{
  "segment_id": "utajp_17",
  "field": "translation.philosophical",
  "old_value": "...",
  "new_value": "...",
  "reviewer": "tom",
  "reason": "anusaṃdhāna means synthesis here, not memory alone"
}
```

The regeneration process always reapplies approved overrides.

# 4. OpenAI API architecture

Use the **Responses API** for individual stages, with strict JSON schemas. OpenAI’s current API supports Structured Outputs through JSON Schema, which is preferable to loose JSON mode for reliable pipeline objects.

Use the **Agents SDK** only for orchestration and handoffs, not to create one unconstrained research swarm. The official quickstart supports typed agents and handoffs in Python and TypeScript.

Use the **Batch API** for large, independent operations such as:

* morphology proposals for 2,000 segments;
* literal translation candidates;
* opponent classification;
* term extraction.

Batch supports JSONL requests to `/v1/responses`, up to 50,000 requests and 200 MB per input file, with a 24-hour completion window and discounted processing.

Do not batch:

* synthesis;
* source-critical decisions;
* cross-segment argument reconstruction;
* final verdicts.

Those require context and iterative scrutiny.

# 5. Minimal implementation

```text
corpus/
  registry.json
  raw/
  normalized/
  segments/

runs/
  philology/
  translations/
  arguments/
  countermodels/
  audits/

reviews/
  overrides.jsonl
  approved_segments.jsonl

graph/
  claims.jsonl
  arguments.jsonl
  relations.jsonl
  contentions.jsonl
```

Core Python workflow:

```python
for segment in pending_segments:
    philology = run_structured("philologist", segment)
    translations = run_translation_passes(segment, philology)

    if min_confidence(philology, translations) < 0.75:
        queue_human_review(segment)
        continue

    argument = run_structured(
        "argument_extractor",
        {
            "segment": segment,
            "philology": philology,
            "translations": translations,
            "context": load_context(segment),
        },
    )

    countermodels = [
        run_structured(role, argument)
        for role in [
            "buddhist_prosecutor",
            "nyaya_prosecutor",
            "process_prosecutor",
            "phenomenology_prosecutor",
            "saiva_defender",
        ]
    ]

    audit = run_structured(
        "logic_auditor",
        {
            "argument": argument,
            "countermodels": countermodels,
        },
    )

    save_research_object(
        segment,
        philology,
        translations,
        argument,
        countermodels,
        audit,
    )
```

The orchestrator should choose **which passage to analyze next** from unresolved contentions, not simply process pages sequentially.

Priority function:

```text
priority =
argument_centrality
× uncertainty
× discriminating_power
× source_quality
÷ processing_cost
```

This makes the system drill into the load-bearing transitions.

# 6. Do not use embeddings as the truth engine

Use embeddings only to retrieve candidates:

* all uses of *anusaṃdhāna*;
* passages mentioning *aham*;
* objections involving momentariness;
* related arguments across commentators.

Then verify lexically and contextually.

Embedding similarity cannot determine:

* whether two technical terms are equivalent;
* whether one passage supports another;
* whether the same inferential function is present;
* whether two authors agree.

# Recommendation

Build **one focused Pratyabhijñā proof extractor first**.

Then translate *Ātmatattvaviveka* as the principal adversarial corpus.

After that:

[
\text{Spanda}
\rightarrow
\text{Krama}
\rightarrow
\text{Bhartṛhari}
\rightarrow
\text{Navya-Nyāya}.
]

Navya-Nyāya becomes extremely valuable once you have stable contention objects. It can then sharpen relations, absences, qualifier structures and inferential conditions. Before that, it will mostly multiply terminology.
