# The 7-Fold Comparative Model in Kashmir Śaivism  

Each school of non-dual Kashmir Śaivism embodies a distinct stage of this seven-fold “anatomy.” Below we run through each school in turn, answering the seven questions (Being, Power, Manifestation, Limitation, Cognition, Value, Liberation) from that school’s perspective. The foregrounded stage is noted, and the others appear in the background.

## Pratyabhijñā (Recognition)  

*Foregrounds Stage 1 (Being) and Stage 5 (Cognition).* Pratyabhijñā holds that the fundamental reality is **one universal consciousness** (Śiva) that is self-aware.  Śiva is the innermost Self of all, *“embodi[ed]…as all things, full of unbroken awareness”*.  Every stage of experience is the Self knowing itself.  Thus:  

- **Being (Stage 1):** Pure consciousness itself is fundamental.  As Kshemarāja says, *“Awareness, free and independent, is the cause of the performance of everything.”*  The one Divine is the ground of all existence.  
- **Power (Stage 2):** Śiva’s **will** (svātantrya) is creative power.  The world is animated by Śiva’s own Śakti; *“She unfolds the universe through Her own will and on Her own canvas.”*.  
- **Manifestation (Stage 3):** The cosmos is a self-manifestation of Śiva.  It appears as an **ābhāsa** (appearance) or reflection in the mirror of consciousness.  The universe is real, not illusory, as Shiva projects it by pure will.  
- **Limitation (Stage 4):** Apparent limitation comes from ignorance (māyā) and the bonds (pāśa) that veil our true nature.  In bondage the soul (paśu) forgets its identity with Śiva.  Pratyabhijñā teaches that these fetters are ultimately self-imposed.  
- **Cognition (Stage 5):** True cognition is *“recognition”* of one’s own divine nature.  The individual consciousness realizes *“the inner Self of all creatures”*.  In Pratyabhijñā, knowing is simply the Self knowing itself; consciousness does not use an external instrument but recognizes its own identity.  
- **Value (Stage 6):** The highest value is **śraddhā** (faith) or bliss in recognizing the Self.  Aesthetic delights or moral values are secondary to the joy of realizing one’s unity with Śiva.  In effect, the supreme “rasa” (savor) is the bliss of recognition itself.  
- **Liberation (Stage 7):** Liberation is immediate union with Śiva – *“the slave becomes the master”*.  The soul is freed by recognizing its identity with the divine Self and abides in the undivided, infinite awareness.  

## Spanda (The Divine Pulse)  

*Foregrounds Stage 2 (Power).* Spanda teaches that the fundamental reality is **dynamic vibration** – the divine pulse.  Consciousness is not static but a constant pulsation (spanda) of Śakti.  The stages unfold as:  

- **Being (Stage 1):** Consciousness is *intrinsically active*.  The very being of Śiva is spanda – an all-pervading creative trembling.  As the Spanda Kārikā states, consciousness is *“active and dynamic, described as the spontaneous vibration… of universal consciousness”*.  
- **Power (Stage 2):** This pulsation **is power itself**.  The divine power (śakti) is embodied in spanda. Śiva’s freedom (svātantrya) expresses through this pulse.  (Hence Stage 2 – Power – is Spanda’s focus.)  
- **Manifestation (Stage 3):** The world manifests through the pulse.  Spanda affirms that phenomena are real transformations (parinama) of consciousness.  Creation is Śiva vibrating; the cosmos is the dance of that foundational pulse.  
- **Limitation (Stage 4):** Limitation is identifying with the inert witness.  If one clings to the “separate” mind that ignores the pulse, the vibrant reality is hidden.  Sleep and ignorance veil the spanda; awakening dissolves that limitation.  
- **Cognition (Stage 5):** The pulse *knows itself*.  In Spanda philosophy, the luminous vibration (prakāśa) is self-cognizing (vimarśa). Awareness and its vibration are one: the same pulse is both knower and known.  
- **Value (Stage 6):** Valued is the inner joy of the pulse. Every thrill or emotion is a manifestation of Śakti; sensing Spanda is delight. In practice, feeling the tremor of consciousness is itself the highest taste (rasa) for the adept.  
- **Liberation (Stage 7):** Liberation is *never falling out of the pulse’s awareness*.  The sage maintains constant attention to spanda, realizing there is no separate self apart from the divine pulsation.  (As texts say, the liberated yogin *“never deviates”* from the pulse.)  

## Kubjikā (Phonemic Tantra)  

*Foregrounds Stage 3 (Manifestation).* Kubjikā is often called the “phonemic” school, seeing reality as sound.  Śakti manifests as Śabda (primeval Speech), and the world is articulated by the Sanskrit phonemes.  

- **Being (Stage 1):** The fundamental is **sound**. The primordial Goddess Kubjikā is identified with Speech. In the Kaula corpus (to which Kubjikā belongs), the 50 Sanskrit phonemes are treated as seed-mantras that encompass all aspects of consciousness and energy.  The cosmos is the Song of Śiva.  
- **Power (Stage 2):** Creative power is vibration. The divine Energy is Śabda: every emanation is uttered, every form resonant. The active potency of consciousness is expressed in sound.  
- **Manifestation (Stage 3):** The world appears as **mantra and phoneme**. Every object corresponds to a sound.  In fact, “the 50 phonemes… form a complete description of reality, from the lowest (‘earth’) to the highest (Śiva-consciousness) level”.  To Kubjikā, creation is literally sung into existence by Śiva-Śakti’s sacred sounds.  
- **Limitation (Stage 4):** Limitation arises when one is deaf to the primal sound. The contracting mind (hrasva) fails to hear the eternal resonance. The bound state is forgetting Śabda; liberation is remembering it.  
- **Cognition (Stage 5):** Knowledge is attuning to sound. To “know” is to meditate on the śabda-śakti behind all names. Every mantra is a pointer; realizing their meaning reveals the mind itself. The guru often transmits mantra by breath (śabdābhiṣeka), making cognition itself a sound-transmission.  
- **Value (Stage 6):** The highest value is **rasa of mantra** – savoring the taste of sacred sound. The beauty and harmony of phonemes is revered. Art, poetry and music are seen as direct encounters with the divine Word.  
- **Liberation (Stage 7):** Liberation comes through dīkṣā (initiation into mantra). The guru-vākya (initiation formula) plants the seed of Śabda in the disciple. When the initiate realizes the identity of Śabda and consciousness, limitation falls away. (In one text it is said the guru’s word itself brings instant emancipation.)  

## Krama (Sequence)  

*Foregrounds Stage 4 (Limitation).* The Krama school centers on Śakti as Kālī cycling through a **sequence** of stages (kramās, or limbs of Kālī). Limitation is built into this sequence.  

- **Being (Stage 1):** The primal essence is Śakti-as-Kālī. Krama holds the Goddess Kālī (in her highest form, Kalasankarṣinī) as the ultimate ground. The Absolute is the unconditioned Kali, the flame above time.  
- **Power (Stage 2):** Power is Śakti in ordered movement. The divine energy unfolds through a fixed sequence of twelve (or more) Kālī forms. Each step is a measured outflow of power in time.  
- **Manifestation (Stage 3):** Reality manifests as cyclical processes. Time is ruled by the Kalī cycle; creation, preservation and dissolution occur in strict sequence. The entire world is seen as the play of these goddess-stages.  
- **Limitation (Stage 4):** **Limitation is literal and structural.** The Krama school’s doctrine is that spiritual development moves through a rigid sequence of binders (paśas) and releasers. Each Goddess in the cycle binds and then frees consciousness. As Abhinavagupta notes, he drew heavily on a *“Kali-based Krama tradition”* of Kaula. In practice, the aspirant moves through sequential initiations in different manifestations of Kālī (the Kālikās), each stage imposing and then removing specific limitations.  
- **Cognition (Stage 5):** Knowledge proceeds stage by stage. At each step of the sequence, the yogin gains a new insight corresponding to that limb of Śakti. Cognition is calibrated by the order: one sees reality as it unfolds in the prescribed sequence of goddesses.  
- **Value (Stage 6):** Each stage of release is valued as progress. The pleasure of the soul is in advancement through the Kali cycle. The spiritual taste is the confidence that each sequential step brings one closer to the divine total.  
- **Liberation (Stage 7):** Liberation is identifying with Kālī’s infinite form beyond sequence. Ultimately one attains Kalasankarṣinī – *“the highest form of Kālī…consuming time within herself”* – transcending all limits. In Sarvamnāya systems (which integrate Kramā) she is the final Pītha.  

## Rasa (Aesthetic Delight)  

*Foregrounds Stage 6 (Value).* The Rasa school teaches that consciousness is **self-relishing**. Beauty and emotional delight are not distractions but direct encounters with Shiva.  

- **Being (Stage 1):** The fundamental being is aesthetic consciousness. Beauty is sacred – *“in beauty, you meet the Absolute”*. In Rasa thought, the core of reality is Ānanda (bliss) experienced through art and nature.  
- **Power (Stage 2):** Power is the delight of creation. Śakti’s energy is felt in the joy of expression. Every creative act (art, music, dance) is an exertion of divine power, celebrating form.  
- **Manifestation (Stage 3):** The world appears as a grand play (līlā) of sensory forms. Every object and event is a “canvas for Shiva’s līlā”.  *“Every painting, poem, dance, or moment of heartfelt emotion becomes a spanda of Consciousness”*. Thus all phenomena are imbued with rasa (flavor).  
- **Limitation (Stage 4):** Limitation is turning away from the divine in the world. In other traditions emotions are denigrated, but Rasa sees that as a false constriction. For Rasa, true “bondage” is missing the sacred in sensation; liberation means celebrating the divine in every experience.  
- **Cognition (Stage 5):** Knowledge comes through feeling. To “know Shiva” is to taste Śrīṅgāra (erotic rasa), Śānta (peace), etc. The connoisseur of rasa literally cognizes the divine by immersing in aesthetic moods. As one text says, devotion through emotion is the path to seeing all as Shiva.  
- **Value (Stage 6):** This is Rasa’s heart: **aesthetic delight is supreme value**. The savor of Śrīṅgāra (love), Hāsya (humor), Karuṇā (compassion), etc., is divine savor. Rasa teaches that *“aesthetic delight… is not only to be acknowledged but celebrated as a powerful manifestation of the divine”*. Every emotion and work of art is a doorway to the sacred.  
- **Liberation (Stage 7):** Liberated life is one of constant blissful appreciation. In practice, Rasa uses the yearning of lover and beloved as a metaphor: union with Shiva is like the consummation of love.  Great artists-saints like Abhinavagupta express that spiritual liberation is the rapture of union (symbolized by the erotic rasa). Thus the devotee is liberated by savoring the sacred in every form.  

## Kaula (Kula)  

*Foregrounds Stage 7 (Liberation).* The Kula system (the Śaiva Kaula) emphasizes *dīkṣā* (initiation) and the guru. Spiritual power is freedom (svātantrya) itself.  

- **Being (Stage 1):** The ground is Śiva-Śakti unity, often represented by the “heart” (āham) as microcosm. The individual soul (kula = “family”) is seen as constituted of eight elements unified in consciousness. The primal reality is this integrated heart of awareness.  
- **Power (Stage 2):** Śakti’s svātantrya (freedom) is operative everywhere. Kula doctrine asserts that consciousness freely plays as the cosmos – Śiva creates the universe out of his own volition as lila. Creative potency is revered as limitless freedom.  
- **Manifestation (Stage 3):** The world is not separate; every practice is part of the whole. In Kula, the physical body and material life are themselves sacred manifestations. The divine appears in mundane reality – e.g. Kundalini energy, chakras, mantra, all are real.  
- **Limitation (Stage 4):** Limitation is any imposed dogma or egoistic barrier. Kula prescribes means to break taboos: what is “forbidden” can be used as “nectar”. Thus the only true bondage is ignorance; once one truly surrenders, even the gravest “limit” can become liberating.  
- **Cognition (Stage 5):** Knowledge is transmitted through the guru-paramparā. Kula is famed for mantra transmission and guru-yoga. Consciousness is cognized by the practices of the Kaula lineage – mantra, nyāsa, bindu visualization, etc. The implicit message is that authentic cognition is a lineage-gift.  
- **Value (Stage 6):** The supreme value is living in freedom. Kula values practical realization (siddhi) over theory. Whatever in life is helpful (food, fashion, sex) can be used in yoga – if it yields clarity, it is worthy. The emphasis is on immediate experience of divine bliss in life.  
- **Liberation (Stage 7):** **Initiation is liberation.** Kula literally means “family,” and initiation brings one into the guru’s family. *“Kaula functions as a form of guru-yoga, where the disciple’s only essential practice is to surrender himself to his guru”*. The guru is seen as sharing one consciousness with the disciple – *“He leads the disciples to the discovery of their own Atman with his own consciousness”*. Thus by śakti-dīkṣā (power transmission) and surrender, the initiate instantly “enters into the freedom of Śiva”. In Kaula, the very moment of guru initiation is liberation.  

## Trika (Calibration)  

*Synthesizes All Stages (calibration of the threefold reality).* The Trika tradition (Śaiva-Advaita) underpins the others. It teaches that all seven questions answer consistently once one recognizes the threefold unity (trika) of Śiva, Śakti, and the individual soul (aṇu).  

- **Being (Stage 1):** At its core, Trika is *advaita*: one infinite divine consciousness. *“All that exists, throughout all time and beyond, is one infinite divine Consciousness…which projects a vast multiplicity of subjects and objects…”*. Śiva and Śakti are non-dual; every being is that same Śiva.  
- **Power (Stage 2):** Trika explicitly names the three powers (Icchā, Jñāna, Kriyā). These are the manifestations of Śiva’s svātantrya. Śiva’s will (icchā) initiates action, knowledge (jñāna) understands it, and action (kriyā) completes it. Every power in the cosmos is a facet of these three.  
- **Manifestation (Stage 3):** The world is seen in triadic terms. Reality is the play of triads – Śiva-Śakti-Anu, Shiva-Shakti-Kala, or other trinities. Trika’s famous triads (e.g. Śāmbhavopāya, Śāktopāya, Āṇavopāya) describe how the one consciousness appears manifold. In this way, the cosmos is the inseparable dance of the three forces.  
- **Limitation (Stage 4):** Any limitation is illusory division. In Trika, the bound soul (pāśu) is actually Śiva who has forgotten his nature. Thus bondage and ignorance are themselves the play of the Divine (theśakti masking herself). The emphasis is on dissolving the sense of limitation through recognition.  
- **Cognition (Stage 5):** All cognition is the self-cognition of consciousness. As Kshemarāja puts it, *“Awareness… unfolds the universe through Her own will… The individual conscious being…embodies the entire universe in a microcosmic form.”*.  In other words, every knowing is Shiva recognizing himself. The mind is not separate from Śiva’s awareness.  
- **Value (Stage 6):** The interplay of Śiva, Śakti and Śiva’s reflection is itself supremely valued. Pure bliss (ānanda) is the essence of this triadic vibration. Trika holds that the *sāmarasya* (homogeneous bliss) of union is the ultimate rasa. Feelings, art, devotion – all are honored insofar as they reveal the inner oneness.  
- **Liberation (Stage 7):** Liberation is realization of the single Self behind all triads. As Christopher Wallis notes, once one “recognizes that one’s real identity is that of the highest Divinity,” that awareness expands *“into perfect wholeness.”*. The liberated state is simply being that nonconceptual ground of experience. In practice, Trika yoga integrates all methods (Śāmbhavopāya, etc.) so that the yogin attains immediate Siddha-avasthā (perfect freedom).  

## Sarvāmnāya (All-Encompassing Synthesis)  

*Synthesizes **all** seven stages (meta-view).* Sarvāmnāya refers to the universal path. Abhinavagupta’s **Tantrāloka** is its classic expression – an all-comprehensive exposition of Śaiva Tantra.  

- **Being (Stage 1):** In the Sarvāmnāya, the Being question is answered universally as Śiva alone. Tantrāloka treats Śiva as pure consciousness underlying everything. It assumes Stage 1 for all systems: the One is the basis of all pluralities.  
- **Power (Stage 2):** Sarvāmnāya collects all powers from every school. Every method (mantra, bhakti, yoga) is seen as Śiva’s power. Abhinavagupta brings together Spanda’s pulsation, Kubjikā’s Śabda, Rasa’s delight, etc., into one system. He notes that everything *“has its place”* in this grand scheme.  
- **Manifestation (Stage 3):** The Sarvāmnāya vision affirms all appearances. It includes the gods and goddesses of every lineage in one mandala. Diversity is celebrated: one may approach the divine via Śakti as sound, form, sequence, flavor, etc., and all are valid śaktis of Śiva.  
- **Limitation (Stage 4):** Any limitations are seen as path-specific only. The Sarvāmnāya Krama merges all kramās of the various Āmnāyas. Each school’s “bindings” become steps on a larger spiral. There is no absolute limitation in Sarvāmnāya – every “paśa” is matched by a “niṣpatti” (release) in the integrated scheme.  
- **Cognition (Stage 5):** Abhinavagupta systematically shows how each school’s practices lead to knowledge of Śiva. He synthesizes the cognition of all seven paths. The Tantrāloka covers every upāya and practice and shows that each one can reveal the same reality. Christopher Wallis notes Tantrāloka is *“encyclopedic in scope… bringing [practices] into a coherent framework in which… everything makes sense in relation to the whole.”*  
- **Value (Stage 6):** The Sarvāmnāya holds that every mode of devotion or yoga has value. Beauty, sound, devotion, asceticism – all are Śiva’s expressions. Abhinavagupta quotes freely from all schools, implicitly teaching that aesthetic, intellectual, and bhakti values alike are sacred. In effect, *“the group (kula) of … phonemes form a complete description of reality”* (so sound, sight, etc., all matter).  
- **Liberation (Stage 7):** Final liberation is realizing this unity of everything. Tantrāloka’s vision is that at the end *“everything has its place and everything makes sense in relation to the whole.”*. In other words, mastery of any one stage or path suffices, because all are faces of the one Self. Abhinavagupta shows that recognizing the oneness behind all dualities is the highest mokṣa.  

**Sources:** This mapping follows traditional accounts of Kashmir Śaivism. For example, Kṣemarāja says Pratyabhijñā is the philosophy of recognizing one’s true Self, Spanda texts describe consciousness as *“spontaneous vibration… an expression of… power”*, and Abhinavagupta’s Tantrāloka is noted to be *“encyclopedic in scope”* synthesizing all paths. We have cited these and other sources above to show how each school answers the seven questions.
