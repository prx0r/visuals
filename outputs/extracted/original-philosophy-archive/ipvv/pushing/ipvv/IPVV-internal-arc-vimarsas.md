# LOGICVID SESSION — the IPVV's own internal structure: the vimarśa-arc (jñāna→smṛti→apohana→kriyā) and the unity it builds to

*Focusing on the IPVV's INTERNAL structure, not our frameworks. The text's own anatomy: how the
vimarśas build on each other, how the powers unfold, and where the whole thing is going. Method: read
the arc as the text presents it — the sequence of reflexive-examinations and the unity they converge
on. No frameworks imposed; the commentary's own logic.*

---

## ROUND 0 — the arc as the text presents it

The IPVV's jñānādhikāra runs through a sequence of vimarśas, each establishing a power and each
building on the last:
1. **The self-as-Lord** (V1A-F): the upoddhāta establishes the self, against the Buddhist no-self.
2. **Memory** (V1D, V2A): memory as the "un-broken manifestation" — how the "I" holds time together.
3. **The jñānaśakti** (V2D-G): manifestation as the form of objects.
4. **The smṛti/apohana** (V2L-N): the "I"-recollection is not a construction; the inner appearance
   stands firm.
5. **The one-support** (V2O): the powers require a support — the maheśvara.
6. **The great-Lordship** (V2R-S): the freedom in the appearance-group; the unity.
7. **The kriyādhikāra** (V3A-I): action = the will; the kāryakāraṇatāvimarśa.
8. **The āgamādhikāra** (V3J-M): the teaching-section.
9. **The tattvādhikāra** (V3N-P): the closing sum.

---

## ROUND 1 — the internal logic of the powers: jñāna→smṛti→apohana→kriyā

**The text's own ordering (from the maṅgalas and vimarśa-openings):** The IPVV explicitly orders the
powers: first knowledge (jñāna), then memory (smṛti), then removal/exclusion (apohana), then action
(kriyā). And each vimarśa-opening says it is "taking up" the next power after establishing the
previous.

**Why this order (the internal reason, from the text):**
- **Jñāna first:** manifestation must be established before anything else — the objects are the form
  of the light (V2D).
- **Smṛti second:** memory is the first thing that shows the "I" holds time together (V2A) — it
  establishes the persistent knower.
- **Apohana third:** the removal/exclusion is the power by which the "I" knows itself as not-a-thing
  (V2L: the "I" is not a construction; it excludes what is not itself). This depends on jñāna and
  smṛti being established.
- **Kriyā last (V3):** action presupposes the whole knowledge-side — you can only show the
  action-power after the knowledge-power and its knower are established. The kriyādhikāra opens by
  saying "having established the power of knowledge, now the power of action."

**The deep internal logic:** The powers are not a random list; they form a necessary ORDER —
manifestation → persistence (memory) → self-knowledge (exclusion of the not-self) → self-expression
(action). Each presupposes the previous. This is the IPVV's own architecture: the powers are the
self's own unfolding, and the order of the vimarśas IS the order of the unfolding.

> **PENETRATION 1 (the internal logic):** The IPVV's powers have a NECESSARY order — manifestation
> (jñāna) → persistence (smṛti) → self-knowledge (apohana) → self-expression (kriyā). Each vimarśa
> explicitly "takes up" the next power after establishing the last. This is the IPVV's own
> architecture: the order of the vimarśas IS the order of the self's unfolding. The powers are not a
> list; they are a sequence, each presupposing the previous.

## ROUND 2 — the apohana as the pivot: the "I" is not a construction (V2L), and the inner appearance stands firm (V2N)

**The internal turning-point:** The apohana (removal) is the pivot of the jñānādhikāra. It is the
power by which the self excludes what is not itself. And its deepest result is:
- **V2L:** the "I"-recollection is NOT a construction — it is the pure self-grasp that underlies all
  construction.
- **V2N:** the inner appearance "stands firm in memory, construction, and cognition alike" — it is
  innate across all three.

**The internal role:** The apohana does not CUT anything off (that would be the māyā-form, a
construction). It is "the self's own luminous grasp." So the removal that the apohana names is NOT the
removal of a thing; it is the self's self-knowledge — the exclusion by which the self knows itself as
the non-constructed foundation. This is the pivot because it turns the sequence from the powers
(jñāna/smṛti) to the self (the "I" that underlies them).

> **PENETRATION 2 (the pivot):** The apohana is the pivot of the jñānādhikāra because it turns the
> sequence from the POWERS (jñāna/smṛti) to the SELF (the "I" that underlies them). Its deepest result
> is that the "I" is not a construction (V2L) and the inner appearance stands firm across all
> cognition (V2N). **The removal is not the cutting off of a thing; it is the self's own luminous
> grasp — the exclusion by which the self knows itself as the non-constructed foundation.**

## ROUND 3 — the great-Lordship is NOT an added power; it IS the self-awareness (V2S)

**The internal climax of the jñānādhikāra (V2S):**
> "The great-Lordship is nothing but the reflexive-awareness — the one who is aware of himself,
> fixedly, is the Lord; and that reflexive-awareness is the God's own pure knowledge-and-action. The
> great-Lordship is not an added power; it is the self-awareness itself."

**The internal structure:** This is the completion of the jñānādhikāra's arc. The powers (jñāna,
smṛti, apohana) were established; the one-support was shown (V2O); now the great-Lordship is revealed
as NOT a fifth power added to the four, but the self-awareness that IS the knower. The Lordship is not
something the self HAS; it is what the self IS (self-awareness). This is the text's own internal
climax: the powers reduce to the self-awareness, and the self-awareness is the Lord.

> **PENETRATION 3 (the climax):** The jñānādhikāra climaxes with the great-Lordship revealed as NOT an
> added power but "the self-awareness itself" (V2S). The four powers (jñāna, smṛti, apohana) were
> established; the one-support was shown; now the Lordship = self-awareness = the knower. **This is
> the internal arc: the powers reduce to the self-awareness, and the self-awareness is the Lord. The
> Lordship is not something the self HAS; it is what the self IS.**

## ROUND 4 — the unity (V2S) and the Sāṃkhya's puruṣa as the un-recognized own-lordship

**The internal unity-argument (V2S):**
> "The whole dealing of the world — the path by which consciousnesses know, remember, and act
> together — would be impossible if the consciousnesses were a scattered crowd of mutually opaque
> streams. But the dealing is; so the consciousnesses must be one... The one light that shines in all
> the consciousnesses is the one knower."

**And the puruṣa:** "The Sāṃkhya's puruṣa — the separate self it posits — is not another at all. It
is the un-recognized own-lordship, the contracted consciousness."

**The internal move:** The IPVV's unity-argument is NOT that all consciousnesses are numerically
identical (which would need the felt→ground step). It is that the DEALING (the world-path of
knowing/remembering/acting together) would be impossible without the one manifestation. And the
Sāṃkhya's separate puruṣa is reinterpreted: it is not another self; it is the one self, unrecognized,
contracted. **This is the text's own way of handling the many: they are the one, contracted, and the
difference is the māyā's making.**

> **PENETRATION 4 (the unity):** The IPVV's unity-argument is that the DEALING would be impossible if
> the consciousnesses were "a scattered crowd of mutually opaque streams" (V2S). And the Sāṃkhya's
> puruṣa is "the un-recognized own-lordship, the contracted consciousness" — not another self, but the
> one, unrecognized. **The many are the one, contracted; the difference is the māyā's making. This is
> the text's own internal handling of the one-and-many — without needing the felt→ground step.**

## ROUND 5 — the kriyādhikāra: action as the will, and the creation-for-all (V3A-H)

**The internal turn to action (V3A):** "Having established the power of knowledge, now the power of
action." The kriyādhikāra runs in parallel: the same one that knows must also act.

**The internal gems:**
- **V3A:** the timeless Lord "manifests manifold sequences" (the mirror); time is the Lord's freedom.
- **V3D:** the synthesis (anusandhāna) makes many appearances one thing — "the lamp-lights gathered
  in one house do one thing (make it bright); the streams, falling into the ocean, do one thing."
  The doer of one object-action must be one.
- **V3H:** inference across knowers, because "the Lord creates the appearances for all the knowers at
  once, as one." This is the internal basis for shared inference.

**The internal arc of the whole:** knowledge → memory → removal → action — and each is the same
self-awareness, shown in a different power. The jñānādhikāra climaxed with the great-Lordship =
self-awareness; the kriyādhikāra shows that the same self-awareness, as action, is the will.

> **PENETRATION 5 (the full arc):** The kriyādhikāra runs in parallel to the jñānādhikāra: the same
> one that knows also acts. And its internal gems: the synthesis makes many appearances one (V3D, the
> lamp-house, the streams-ocean), and inference across knowers holds because "the Lord creates the
> appearances for all the knowers at once, as one" (V3H). **The full arc: knowledge → memory →
> removal → action, each the same self-awareness in a different power — and the whole converges on
> the one knower, the Lord.**

## THE LOGICVID LESSON

Reading the IPVV by its own internal structure (not our frameworks) reveals a clear architecture:

1. **The powers have a NECESSARY order** (manifestation → persistence → self-knowledge →
   self-expression); the order of the vimarśas IS the order of the self's unfolding.

2. **The apohana is the pivot** — it turns the sequence from the powers to the self (the "I" is not
   a construction; the inner appearance stands firm).

3. **The jñānādhikāra climaxes with the great-Lordship = self-awareness, NOT an added power** (V2S).

4. **The unity is argued via the DEALING** (the world-path would be impossible without one
   manifestation), and the Sāṃkhya's puruṣa is "the un-recognized own-lordship" — the one, contracted.

5. **The kriyādhikāra runs in parallel** (the same one that knows also acts), with the synthesis
   making many appearances one, and inference-across-knowers grounded in "the Lord creates the
   appearances for all the knowers at once."

**The deep internal finding:** The IPVV is not a collection of arguments; it is ONE arc — the powers
unfold in order, the self-awareness is their root, and the whole converges on the one knower, the
Lord. The great-Lordship is not added; it IS the self-awareness. This is the text's own internal
structure, and it is the clearest when read as an arc rather than as separate vimarśas.

*This is the internal-structure session. The IPVV's own arc: powers in necessary order, the apohana
as pivot, the great-Lordship = self-awareness, the unity via the dealing, and the parallel
kriyādhikāra. The whole is one unfolding of the one self-awareness into its powers — converging on
the one knower, the Lord.*
