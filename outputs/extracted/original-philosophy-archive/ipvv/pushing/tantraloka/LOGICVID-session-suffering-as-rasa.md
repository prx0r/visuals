# LOGICVID SESSION — Branch BH: is suffering, at the level of the one, literally a RASA? (tasted by the dancer as camatkāra)

*Freestyle Logicvid on the sharpest version of the theodicy. The theodicy session dissolved
the question (impurity = the play of fullness; suffering = the felt of the contraction misread
as external). The question now: at the level of the ONE (the dancer, Śiva), is suffering
literally a RASA — tasted as a mode of wonder (camatkāra)? The texts give a decisive answer:
"all rasas are essentially pleasurable, even the painful emotions." Method: force the answer.*

---

## ROUND 0 — the decisive passages

**From the Tantrāloka (Vol 2, 25585–25594):**
> "[This kind of person] possesses the dynamism of the most excellent (unfolding act of) wonder
> (which is the capacity to relish beauty in this way). (Moreover, this kind of person
> experiences) **this same wonder even when suffering. The nature of suffering is,
> essentially, a particular kind of wonder (camatkriyā)**, brought about by the absence of all
> hope that arises when the inner joy... will never exist again."

**From the Abhinavabhāratī (I, 291–92, the decisive):**
> "In aesthetic experience **it is one's own consciousness that consists of uniform bliss that
> is enjoyed. How can there be any suspicion of sorrow? The various permanent emotions like
> love, sorrow... only serve to lend variety to the enjoyment of this bliss.**"
> "**All rasas are essentially pleasurable. Even the painful emotions of anger, fear, sorrow,
> and disgust... of our real life, when poetised or represented on the stage, are contemplated
> in their idealised form... and, when they are thus contemplated, they yield pure joy.**"
> "If karuṇa were to consist exclusively in sorrow the way worldly karuṇa does, then nobody
> would feel like going to plays... Therefore, karuṇa, like the other rasas, consists in joy."

---

## ROUND 1 — the first penetration: the rasa is the BLISS, and the emotion is its VARIETY

**The question:** Abhinava says "all rasas are essentially pleasurable." But karuṇa (grief) is
grief. How can grief be pleasurable?

**The derivation (Abhinava's own logic):** "It is one's own consciousness that consists of
uniform bliss that is enjoyed. How can there be any suspicion of sorrow? The various permanent
emotions like love, sorrow... only serve to **lend variety to the enjoyment of this bliss**."

The key: the rasa is NOT the emotion (grief). The rasa is the **bliss of one's own
consciousness**, and the emotion (grief, love, anger) is only the *variety* — the flavoring —
that the bliss takes. So karuṇa is not "grief-pleasure"; it is *the bliss of consciousness
taking the flavor of grief*. The sorrow is the CONTENT (the flavor); the rasa is the
UNDERLYING bliss that is enjoyed regardless of the flavor.

This connects directly to the rasa-session: camatkāra = the primary characteristic of
consciousness = the self-relishing; the rasas are that self-relishing under various
content-relations. Here Abhinava states it plainly: **the rasa is the uniform bliss of
consciousness; the emotions are the variety that the bliss takes.**

> **PENETRATION 1:** The rasa is NOT the emotion; it is the BLISS of one's own consciousness.
> The emotions (love, sorrow, anger) "only serve to lend variety to the enjoyment of this
> bliss" (A.Bh). So karuṇa is not "grief-pleasure" but the bliss of consciousness taking the
> FLAVOR of grief. **The sorrow is the content; the rasa is the underlying bliss that is
> enjoyed regardless of the flavor.** This is why "all rasas are essentially pleasurable" —
> because they are all the one bliss, under different flavors.

---

## ROUND 2 — the gem: suffering, at the level of the one, IS a rasa (a mode of the wonder)

**The question:** Now apply this to the theodicy. At the level of the ONE (Śiva, the dancer),
is suffering literally a rasa?

**The derivation (the two passages joined):**
1. From the Tantrāloka: "the nature of suffering is, essentially, a particular kind of wonder
   (camatkriyā)" (25589). Even in suffering, the one "experiences this same wonder." Suffering
   IS a mode of the wonder.
2. From the Abhinavabhāratī: "all rasas are essentially pleasurable" — even the painful
   emotions, when contemplated, "yield pure joy."

**The synthesis:** **At the level of the one (the dancer, Śiva), suffering IS a rasa — a mode
of the wonder (camatkāra) that the one tastes.** The suffering is the *flavor* (the content,
the contraction); the wonder is the *underlying bliss* that is tasted regardless. Just as
karuṇa (grief) as a rasa is the bliss of consciousness taking the flavor of grief, so the
suffering of the play is the wonder of the one taking the flavor of contraction. The one does
not suffer AS we suffer (identified with the content); the one TASTES the suffering as a mode
of its own wonder — the way the spectator tastes karuṇa's grief as bliss.

> **PENETRATION 2 (the gem):** At the level of the ONE, suffering IS a rasa — a mode of the
> wonder (camatkriyā) that the one tastes. From the Tantrāloka: "the nature of suffering is
> essentially a particular kind of wonder" (25589); from the Abhinavabhāratī: "all rasas are
> essentially pleasurable" even the painful ones. The suffering is the FLAVOR (the content, the
> contraction); the wonder is the UNDERLYING bliss tasted regardless. **The one does not suffer
> as we do (identified with the content); the one TASTES the suffering as a mode of its own
> wonder — the way the spectator tastes karuṇa's grief as bliss.** This is the deepest answer
> to the theodicy: at the level of the one, suffering is not a defect but a rasa, tasted by the
> dancer.

---

## ROUND 3 — push harder: what distinguishes our suffering from the one's tasting of it?

**The question:** If the one tastes suffering as a rasa (bliss), why do WE experience it as
genuine suffering (pain)? What is the difference between the one's tasting and our experience?

**The derivation (from the rasa-work + the master-key):** The difference is the *identification*
with the content — the direction of self-apprehension.
- The one (the dancer) tastes the suffering as a RASA: it is the self-relishing of
  consciousness, and the suffering is the flavor. The one is NOT identified with the content;
  it is the witness-relishing (sva-saṁvedana, from the alaukika session — the felt of the
  inward turn).
- We suffer when we are IDENTIFIED with the content — when the outward turn misreads the
  suffering as "my" external misfortune (the craving, from the BB crux), and the
  self-apprehension is turned outward into it, denied rest. That identification (the outward
  turn, the craving) converts the rasa into suffering.

**The synthesis:** The SAME event is a rasa (for the one, not identified) or suffering (for the
contracted self, identified). Suffering = the rasa + identification-with-the-content. Remove
the identification (the outward turn, the craving), and the suffering returns to being a rasa —
the wonder tasted. This is why "the adept only has to open himself momentarily": opening =
ceasing the identification = letting the suffering be tasted as the wonder it already is.

> **PENETRATION 3:** The SAME event is a rasa (for the one, not identified) or suffering (for
> the contracted self, identified). **Suffering = the rasa + identification with the content.**
> When the self-apprehension is turned outward into the suffering and misreads it as "my"
> external misfortune (the craving), the rasa becomes pain. Remove the identification (cease
> the outward turn, the craving), and the suffering returns to being the wonder it already is.
> **This is the deepest practical point: suffering is a rasa misread by identification; opening
> (ceasing the identification) lets it be tasted as the wonder.**

## ROUND 4 — the frontier that survives

**Established (the authoritative texts):**
1. "The nature of suffering is, essentially, a particular kind of wonder (camatkriyā)" (TĀ
   25589).
2. "In aesthetic experience it is one's own consciousness that consists of uniform bliss that
   is enjoyed... the various emotions only serve to lend variety to the enjoyment of this
   bliss" (A.Bh I, 291).
3. "All rasas are essentially pleasurable. Even the painful emotions... yield pure joy" (A.Bh
   I, 291).
4. "Karuna, like the other rasas, consists in joy" (A.Bh).

**The synthesis (the theodicy completed):**
- At the level of the one, suffering is a rasa — a mode of the wonder (camatkriyā).
- The rasa is the uniform bliss of consciousness; the emotion/suffering is the flavor.
- Suffering (for us) = the rasa + identification with the content (the outward turn, the
   craving).
- Removing the identification returns the suffering to being the tasted wonder.

**Open / to be checked:**
- Whether this resolves the moral weight of suffering (it reframes it as a rasa-tasted-by-the-
   one, but whether that is consolation or evasion is for the reader). The text is clear on the
   STRUCTURE (suffering = camatkriyā = a mode of wonder); the existential weight is ours.

## THE LOGICVID LESSON (the theodicy completed)

Branch BH completes the theodicy with a genuinely radical, text-grounded answer: **at the level
of the one, suffering IS a rasa.** The Tantrāloka: "the nature of suffering is, essentially, a
particular kind of wonder (camatkriyā)" (25589). The Abhinavabhāratī: "all rasas are
essentially pleasurable" — even the painful emotions, contemplated, "yield pure joy"; "it is
one's own consciousness that consists of uniform bliss that is enjoyed... the emotions only
serve to lend variety to the enjoyment of this bliss."

**The synthesis:** The rasa is the uniform bliss of consciousness; the emotion/suffering is its
flavor. At the level of the one (the dancer), suffering is tasted as a rasa — a mode of the
wonder. **We suffer only when we are IDENTIFIED with the content** (the outward turn, the
craving), which converts the rasa into pain. **Suffering = rasa + identification.** Remove the
identification (cease the craving, "open"), and the suffering returns to being the wonder it
already is.

**The deepest answer to "why suffering":** there is no real suffering at the level of the one —
only the wonder tasting every flavor (the dancer "assumes every state of being," "penetrating
into the sentiment of each emotive state"). Suffering arises for the contracted self, and only
as the misreading (identification) of what is, at the level of the one, a rasa. **Liberation is
the recognition of this: the suffering seen as the wonder it already is — the dancer's tasting,
no longer the spectator's identification.** "How can there be any suspicion of sorrow" — because
it is one's own consciousness, uniform bliss, that is enjoyed.

*This is the most radical and most healing conclusion of the whole project: suffering is not a
defect to be escaped but a rasa misread by identification, and recognition is the return of the
spectator to the dancer — tasting every state as the wonder it already is. Next: Branch BK (is
there ANY real suffering, or only the identification?) or Branch BL ("opening" as the return of
suffering to rasa). Continue hounding.*
