# Sanskritree Research Programme

## Principle

Do not simply translate texts in order of fame. Choose texts that maximize **overlap**, **existing scholarship**, and **evaluation capability**. Each new translation should make the next one easier.

## Progression

```
Vedic Sanskrit
        ↓
Epic/Classical Sanskrit
        ↓
Early Śaiva Tantra
        ↓
Kubjikā
        ↓
Krama
        ↓
Abhinavagupta
        ↓
Manthānabhairava
```

Every stage becomes a training corpus for the next.

---

## STAGE 0 — Build the linguistic engine

Don't begin with Tantra. First master the language.

### Resources

- [The Sanskrit Library](https://sanskritlibrary.org/) — professional digital Sanskrit
  - [Tools](https://sanskritlibrary.org/tools.html)
  - [Morphological Analyzer](https://www.sanskritlibrary.org/morphologicalAnalyzer.html)
  - [Digital Philology](https://www.sanskritlibrary.org/digphil.html)
  - [Bloomfield Papers](https://sanskritlibrary.org/bp2012.html)

### Learn

- sandhi
- compounds
- case endings
- finite verbs
- participles
- absolutives
- quotation syntax

Without these you'll misread almost every Tantra.

### Heritage Parser

[https://sanskrit.inria.fr/](https://sanskrit.inria.fr/)

Learn how Sanskrit can be segmented in multiple ways. One sentence can legitimately produce dozens of parses. Never trust one segmentation.

### Lexicon Resources

- [Cologne Lexicon](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/)
- [Cologne Inflection](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/csl-inflect/web/index.php)
- [Monier-Williams](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MWScan/) — the dictionary brain

## STAGE 1 — Easy calibration texts

Not because they're philosophically important. Because they're translated extremely well.

### Bhagavad Gītā

- Source: [GRETIL](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/)
- Purpose: grammar, verbs, cases, simple compounds, core philosophy

### Yoga Sūtras

- Source: GRETIL, Muktabodha
- Purpose: learn concise aphoristic Sanskrit

### Vijñānabhairava

- Source: GRETIL, Muktabodha
- Purpose: learn Trika vocabulary — *spanda, madhya, prāṇa, śakti, bhairava, vikalpa, dhāraṇā*

---

## STAGE 2 — First Tantric translation

### Devīpañcaśataka

- Status: **No complete modern English translation found**
- ~500 verses, dense, important, Krama
- Learn: Kālī terminology, ritual language, meditation language, goddess epithets

#### Prereading

- Dyczkowski — *The Doctrine of Vibration* ([archive.org](https://archive.org/))
- Krama article — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10781-021-09488-9)

---

## STAGE 3

### Kramasadbhāva

Everything is process. Reads almost like a computational model of consciousness.

Vocabulary: *krama, uccāra, visarga, kālī, grasana, kramaśakti, prakāśa, vimarśa*

Prereading: Tantrāloka chapters 5–6, Spandakārikā

---

## STAGE 4

### Kularatnoddyota

Where Sanskritree becomes unique.

- Source: [Muktabodha](https://muktabodha-digital-library.org/)
- Explains Kubjikā. Lots of overlap. Already edited by Dyczkowski.

Vocabulary: *āmnāya, kubjikā, cakra, śakti, kula, akula, yoginī, mantra, nyāsa, mudrā*

Prereading:
- [Kubjikā articles](https://www.anuttaratrikakula.org/goddess-kubjika/)
- [Anuttara Trika Kula articles](https://www.anuttaratrikakula.org/articles/)

---

## STAGE 5

### Kubjikāmatatantra

- Source: Muktabodha, GRETIL
- Gold standard Kubjikā scripture
- Translation exists only for part (chapters 14–16 already translated) — perfect evaluation corpus

Learn: phonemic cosmology, cakra systems, kula metaphysics, initiation, ritual syntax

Prereading: Schoterman, Heilijgers-Seelen, Dyczkowski

---

## STAGE 6

### Ṣaṭsāhasrasaṃhitā

First five chapters translated. Remaining largely untouched — ideal for blind translation → compare → continue.

Vocabulary: almost identical to Kubjikā.

---

## STAGE 7

### Ciñcinīmatasārasamuccaya

- Source: Muktabodha
- One of the coolest texts in Indian religion
- Connects Kubjikā → Krama → Kālī → Āmnāyas → Kaula

Vocabulary: solar symbolism, Kālīs, emanation, absorption, transmission lineages

Prereading: Dyczkowski's Krama papers

---

## STAGE 8

### Ūrmikaulārṇava

- Source: Muktabodha
- Quoted by Abhinavagupta. Tiny. Huge influence.
- Very Kaula. Many ritual compounds. Need excellent compound parser.

---

## STAGE 9

### Tantrasadbhāva

- Source: GRETIL
- Older Sanskrit. Harder grammar. Non-Pāṇinian forms, older ritual language.

Prereading: Svacchandatantra, Netratantra, Kubjikā.

---

## STAGE 10

### Siddhayogeśvarīmata

- Need critical edition. See [Judit Törzsök](https://ephe.academia.edu/JuditTorzsok)
- Earliest Trika. Yoginīs. Initiation. Magic. Lots of feminine forms.

---

## STAGE 11

### Mālinīvijayottaratantra

- Edition: [EFEO](https://publications.efeo.fr/en/livres/618_the-yoga-of-the-malinivijayottaratantra-chapters-1-4-7-i2-17)
- Translate only missing chapters. This is the backbone of Tantrāloka.

---

## STAGE 12

### Manthānabhairavatantra

Finally. Specifically Yogakhaṇḍa → Siddhakhaṇḍa.

Use Dyczkowski's Kumārikākhaṇḍa plus [NGMCP manuscripts](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/ngmcp/index.html).

## Proposal: Tantric Sanskrit Lexicon

Before translating any large Tantra, build a **Tantric Sanskrit Lexicon** as a standalone project. Start with ~5,000–10,000 technical terms from the Kubjikā, Krama, Trika, and Bhairava corpora, each with:

- lemma and morphology
- attested meanings by text and tradition
- example verses
- parallel usages across related Tantras
- Dyczkowski's preferred translations where available
- competing translations from other scholars

That lexicon becomes the semantic foundation of Sanskritree. Instead of rediscovering the meaning of terms like *vimarśa*, *uccāra*, *kula*, *akula*, *grasana*, or *svātantrya* in every new translation, the system accumulates evidence over time and makes progressively better, more consistent decisions.
