Yes. For Sanskrit parsing, I would add **six metadata fields** to every work: `tradition`, `subschool/śākhā`, `date_range`, `authorship_type`, `attributed_author`, and `linguistic_register`. The last one matters because early Vedic, late Vedic, Epic/Purāṇic, and tantric Sanskrit behave differently; the Niśvāsa editors explicitly flag departures from Pāṇinian norms. ([publications.efeo.fr][1])

Dates below are deliberately **broad working ranges**, not fake exact dates. Purāṇas and epics especially are layered “composition-in-transmission” corpora, so eventually Pāṭala should date passages/strata, not assign one year to an entire work. ([OUP Academic][2])

The Upaniṣadic ordering here should be treated as relative and approximate. Modern scholarship explicitly treats Śvetāśvatara as comparatively late among the classical Upaniṣads, influenced by Sāṃkhya-Yoga and emerging theism; Olivelle's critical corpus contains Sanskrit plus translations for Kaṭha and Śvetāśvatara among the classical group. ([OUP Academic][3]) Atharvaśiras is particularly high signal because its surviving Sanskrit explicitly has the gods ask **Rudra** who he is, followed by an all-encompassing self-identification. ([Gretil][4]) Kaivalya likewise explicitly combines brahman/ātman discourse with `sadāśiva`, `īśa` and `śivarūpa`. ([Gretil][5])

For the later history, the Pāśupata sources are crucial: recent scholarship describes Pāñcārthika Pāśupata textual evidence as reaching approximately the **4th–5th centuries CE**. ([Cambridge University Press][6]) The Śivadharma corpus is assigned broadly to the **6th–9th centuries**, while the early Skandapurāṇa provides securely useful sixth-/seventh-century evidence for early Śaivism and Pāśupata sacred geography. ([French Institute of Pondicherry][7]) The Niśvāsamukha is especially useful because its editors describe it as perhaps seventh century and explicitly say it maps **Pāñcārthika Pāśupata, Lākula, Mantramārga and pre-Mantramārga lay Śaivism** against one another. ([French Institute of Pondicherry][8])

# About the “14 lokas”

This needs one epistemic correction.

There isn't one immutable pan-Hindu cosmological chart called **THE fourteen lokas** sitting unchanged from the Veda onward.

What people commonly call the 14 lokas is a later systematization resembling:

```text
UPPER

Satya / Brahma
Tapa
Jana
Mahar
Svar
Bhuvar
Bhū

──────── earthly plane ────────

Atala
Vitala
Sutala
Talātala
Mahātala
Rasātala
Pātāla

LOWER
```

But texts can vary in names, ordering, interpretation, and whether terminology like `loka`, `tala`, `bhuvana`, etc. is being used in exactly the same ontological sense.

That's fantastic for Pāṭala because rather than hardcoding:

```text
14 LOKAS = FACT
```

we can build:

```text
COSMOLOGICAL MODEL
         │
         ├── source
         ├── date
         ├── tradition
         ├── realm
         ├── parent realm
         ├── inhabitants
         ├── ruler/deity
         ├── vertical position
         ├── measurements
         └── correspondences
```

Then compare:

```text
Mahābhārata model
        ↕
Viṣṇu Purāṇa model
        ↕
Bhāgavata model
        ↕
Vāyu model
        ↕
Śaiva Purāṇic model
        ↕
Tantric Śaiva model
```

The Mahābhārata itself already provides subterranean-world terminology such as Rasātala and Nāgaloka, so we can observe the system **develop**, rather than projecting the later 14-world diagram onto every earlier source. ([Gretil][9])

# Śaiva cosmology is where it gets much more interesting

Once you hit Tantra, cosmology stops being merely:

> Earth → heavens → underworlds.

It increasingly becomes **ontological cosmology**.

Instead of only:

```text
WHERE is this realm?
```

we get:

```text
WHAT level of manifestation is this?

Śiva
↓
Śakti
↓
Sadāśiva
↓
Īśvara
↓
Śuddhavidyā
↓
Māyā
↓
kañcukas
↓
puruṣa
↓
prakṛti
↓
buddhi
↓
ahaṃkāra
↓
manas
↓
tanmātras / indriyas
↓
mahābhūtas
↓
earth
```

And then texts map **worlds, gods, mantras, bodies and initiation** onto that vertical structure.

For this, add these specific Śaiva works to your cosmology packet:

| Text                     | School                     | Approx. layer          | Why cosmologically essential                   |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |

| **Kiraṇatantra**         | Śaiva Siddhānta            | early-medieval         | tattvas, bondage, souls, worlds                |
| **Mataṅgapārameśvara**   | Śaiva Siddhānta            | early-medieval         | ontology/cosmology + liberation                |

The **Mṛgendra** is particularly valuable because the IFP explicitly describes it as giving one of the most detailed surviving accounts of dualist Śaiva philosophy and the emanation of the tattvas **from Śiva down to earth**. ([French Institute of Pondicherry][11]) The Raurava likewise includes tattvas and the sixfold path of liberation. ([French Institute of Pondicherry][12]) And the published Niśvāsa edition explicitly lists **cosmology/cosmography** among its central subjects. ([publications.efeo.fr][1])

There is even modern scholarship specifically studying **the evolution of the Śaiva tattvas**, which is exactly the genealogy we want to capture rather than assuming the familiar 36-tattva list existed fully formed from the beginning. ([French Institute of Pondicherry][13])

```text
A. SPATIAL / MYTHIC COSMOLOGY

Vedic worlds
↓
Epic trailokya / subterranean realms
↓
Purāṇic Meru + dvīpas + oceans + lokas/talas
↓
Nāgaloka / Vāsuki / cosmic beings
↓
Śaiva Purāṇic cosmos


B. ONTOLOGICAL / TANTRIC COSMOLOGY

early categories
↓
Sāṃkhya-like tattva structures
↓
early Śaiva tattvas
↓
Niśvāsa
↓
Siddhānta
↓
Bhairava/Vidyāpīṭha
↓
Trika
↓
36-tattva synthesis
↓
Abhinavagupta
```

**That second genealogy could be one of the best things in the whole historical atlas.**

Instead of the app telling someone “Kashmir Śaivism has 36 tattvas,” it could literally show:

> **Where did tattva #X enter the Śaiva textual record? Which earlier system did it come from? When did new levels above māyā get articulated? Which scriptures disagree about the number/order of worlds? When did bhuvana cosmography get mapped onto initiation?**

That's exactly the sort of question for which your full Sanskrit corpus becomes substantially more powerful than a normal library.
