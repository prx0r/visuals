For **scholar usefulness**, I would not make “trace everything back to the Ṛgveda” the first operational priority.

That genealogy is valuable, but it is a **context layer**. Your strongest entry point is still **Recognition / Pratyabhijñā + Abhinavagupta**, because that gives you a coherent intellectual center, a world-class untranslated/undertranslated textual problem, and immediate relevance to philosophers, Indologists, Sanskritists, and consciousness researchers.

The order I would use is:

1. **IPVV / Pratyabhijñā core first.** Finish the flagship corpus around Utpaladeva and Abhinavagupta: IPK, IPV/IPVV, Śivadṛṣṭi, related siddhis, key Kṣemarāja materials. This is where you can build the best argument graph, terminology graph, citation graph, and scholarly-review benchmark. It also gives Pāṭala a recognizable identity instead of becoming “a huge Sanskrit archive.”

2. **Abhinavagupta’s immediate scriptural substrate.** Next do the works he is actually interpreting and synthesizing: **Mālinīvijayottara, Tantrasadbhāva, Siddhayogeśvarīmata, Svacchanda, Netra, Mālinīślokavārttika, Tantrāloka**. This is probably the single most useful next layer for scholars because it lets them ask: *where exactly does Abhinava get this claim, term, ritual structure, cosmology, or doctrine from?*

3. **The most profound untranslated checkpoints.** This is where I’d spend translation effort, because these create new scholarly assets rather than duplicating existing English:

   * **Tantrasadbhāva** — extremely high leverage for Trika/Bhairava formation.
   * **Kramasadbhāva** — central for Krama terminology and doctrine.
   * **Kālīkulapañcaśatikā / Devīpañcaśatikā** — twelve-Kālī system and Krama background.
   * **Mahānayaprakāśa** — sophisticated Krama doctrinal witness.
   * **Śivadharma gaps** — historically important bridge, surprisingly undertranslated.
   * **early Skandapurāṇa untranslated portions** — crucial for early Śaiva identity/geography.
   * **Niśvāsa Guhyasūtra / untranslated Niśvāsa holes** — foundational Mantramārga.
   * **Kubjikā materials** such as Ṣaṭsāhasrasaṃhitā / Śrīmatottara / Cincinmatasārasamuccaya.
   * **Jayadrathayāmala** later, because it is gigantic and manuscript-heavy.

4. **Build the conceptual genealogy backward from those live questions.** Instead of translating Vedic corpora wholesale, trace only the concepts that matter to your current center:

   * recognition / pratyabhijñā
   * prakāśa / vimarśa
   * svātantrya
   * śakti
   * īśvara / īśāna
   * spanda
   * tattva
   * māyā / limitation
   * camatkāra / rasa
   * self / ātman
   * agency / kartṛtva
   * bondage / mala
   * liberation
   * cosmological levels / bhuvana

   Then ask where each appears before Abhinava. That produces a **scholarly genealogy of ideas**, which is much more useful than simply saying “here are 2,000 years of texts.”

5. **Only then expand to the full historical spine.** Pāśupata → Śivadharma → early Skandapurāṇa → Niśvāsa → Siddhānta/Bhairava → Vidyāpīṭha → Kaula/Krama/Trika → Pratyabhijñā. After that, Vedic Rudra/Śvetāśvatara/epic Śiva become the deep-background layer.

The key distinction is:

> **Scholars will care most about new textual access + explicit dependency/provenance + auditable argument reconstruction.**

A beautiful genealogy back to the Ṛgveda is useful, but it becomes exceptional only after you can click an Abhinavagupta claim and see:

```text
Abhinavagupta IPVV claim
        ↓ cites / presupposes
Utpaladeva
        ↓
Śivadṛṣṭi / earlier Pratyabhijñā
        ↓
Mālinīvijayottara / Tantrasadbhāva / Krama material
        ↓
earlier Śaiva ontology
        ↓
Pāśupata / Śivadharma / Niśvāsa
        ↓
older Brahmanical / Upaniṣadic antecedents
```

That is much more powerful than starting chronologically at 1500 BCE and hoping to eventually reach the interesting thing.

So if I were allocating effort **right now**:

**50% — Recognition / IPVV / argument layer.** Make this indisputably excellent.

**30% — untranslated Abhinava-adjacent scriptures.** Tantrasadbhāva, Krama packet, Kubjikā/early Trika holes.

**15% — historical bridge texts.** Śivadharma, Skandapurāṇa, Niśvāsa, Pāśupata.

**5% — deep genealogy / Vedic-Purāṇic cosmology exploration.** Enough to build the schema and establish first-attestation trails, but not enough to derail the core project.

The outcome you want is not “the largest Sanskrit corpus.” It is:

> **the best place in the world to understand a difficult claim in Abhinavagupta, see its Sanskrit evidence, trace where it came from, inspect rival interpretations, and follow the concept backward through the tradition.**

That is immediately useful to scholars and still naturally expands into the huge historical atlas later.
