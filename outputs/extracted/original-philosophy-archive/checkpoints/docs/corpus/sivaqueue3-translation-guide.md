Yes. I went back through the **pre-tantric historical spine** rather than padding the bibliography with random Śaiva texts. The important finding is that several of these should **not** be machine-translated from zero: good human translations already exist and should become gold/reference layers. The genuine translation frontier becomes much sharper around **Śivadharma, parts of the early Skandapurāṇa, and the still-unedited/untranslated portions of Niśvāsa**.

I have excluded obvious pirate-book mirrors. For public-domain material I give direct scans/e-texts; for modern copyrighted scholarship I give official publisher/institutional pages or legitimate author repositories. “All translations” can never literally be guaranteed because translations also hide in articles, dissertations, and book chapters, but this covers the major versions I could verify.

### What remains?

The 2015 volume is explicitly **Volume 1** and covers Mūla, Uttara and Naya. It does **not** mean “the whole Niśvāsa has now been translated.” ([French Institute of Pondicherry][24])

The remaining textual strata, especially the **Guhyasūtra**, therefore need to be tracked independently rather than giving the ledger the false status `NIŚVĀSA = TRANSLATED`.

I'd encode it as:

```text
NISVASA CORPUS

Niśvāsamukha
    Sanskrit critical edition    YES
    English                      YES — Kafle 2020

Niśvāsa Mūlasūtra
    Sanskrit critical edition    YES
    English                      YES — Goodall et al. 2015

Niśvāsa Uttarasūtra
    Sanskrit critical edition    YES
    English                      YES — Goodall et al. 2015

Niśvāsa Nayasūtra
    Sanskrit critical edition    YES
    English                      YES — Goodall et al. 2015

Niśvāsa Guhyasūtra
    KEEP AS SEPARATE FRONTIER
```

**Pāṭala:** `PARTLY INGEST-GOLD + PARTLY TRANSLATION FRONTIER`.

# So the new historical acquisition queue is much clearer

After checking the actual availability, I would **not** send an agent through these texts indiscriminately.

| Corpus                        | Sanskrit                     | Good English                                            | What Pāṭala should do                 |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Ṛgveda                        | excellent                    | complete + modern                                       | **INGEST**                            |
| Yajurveda Rudra               | excellent                    | complete old translations                               | **INGEST + align recensions**         |
| Atharvaveda                   | excellent                    | complete Śaunaka; Paippalāda evolving                   | **INGEST**                            |
| Brāhmaṇas                     | good but distributed         | substantial/complete for major texts                    | **INGEST + extract Rudra dossier**    |
| Śvetāśvatara                  | excellent                    | many complete translations                              | **INGEST GOLD**                       |
| Mahābhārata                   | critical e-text              | complete translations                                   | **INGEST + extract Śiva strata**      |
| Pāśupatasūtra/Pañcārthabhāṣya | **excellent**                | complete translation exists, rights awkward             | **TRANSLATE / acquire licensed gold** |
| Śivadharma                    | **substantial Sanskrit**     | **fragmentary by chapter**                              | **TRANSLATE**                         |
| early Skandapurāṇa            | **critical electronic text** | **mostly scholarly synopses, not complete translation** | **TRANSLATE**                         |
| Niśvāsa                       | major critical editions      | major portions gold-translated                          | **INGEST GOLD + finish holes**        |

That changes the execution order significantly.

The **highest-value new translation work from this historical extension is probably not Ṛgveda at all**. The Vedic material already has enormous translation coverage.

It's roughly:

**Śivadharma → early Skandapurāṇa → Pāśupata/Pañcārthabhāṣya rights-clean translation → remaining Niśvāsa holes**, while Veda/Brāhmaṇa/Upaniṣad/Mahābhārata become a heavily provenance-aware **ingestion and extraction job**.

And that is exactly what we want: don't burn model tokens producing the 47th Ṛgveda translation when there are genuinely important chunks of the **actual emergence of Śaivism** still lacking a complete aligned English layer. ([Digital Collections][16])

If we're updating the Git queue next, I would make these availability distinctions explicit as `INGEST_GOLD / TRANSLATE_HOLES / TRANSLATE_FULL / RIGHTS_BLOCKED`, because the current simple “work = target” model is going to waste a lot of agent effort.
