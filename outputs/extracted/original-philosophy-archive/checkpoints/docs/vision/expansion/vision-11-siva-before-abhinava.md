# Vision 11 — Śiva Before Abhinava: the genealogy of Śaiva ideas as the next major corpus

*2026-08-12. Imported from R2 (`sanskritree/historicalsiva`, renamed to a numbered vision doc). The next
major Pāṭala corpus: walk backward from Abhinavagupta to the earliest recoverable Śaivism, modeled as a
**braided history** (not a simple lineage). Proposes six chronological corpora (Rudra → Śiva Emerges →
Pāśupata → Early Tantra → Bhairava/Śākta/Kaula → Kashmir) + three cross-cutting graphs (concept
genealogy, cosmology, argument genealogy), to answer "How did the world of Abhinavagupta become
possible?" Ends at Abhinavagupta, connecting forward into the existing IPVV work. See `docs/vision/INDEX.md`.*

---

Yes. If the project is **“start from Abhinavagupta and walk backward until we hit the earliest recoverable formation of Śiva/Śaivism”**, I would make that the next major Pāṭala corpus.

The key correction is that there is **no simple straight genealogy**:

> Vedic Rudra → Śiva → Pāśupata → Tantra → Abhinavagupta.

What the evidence actually gives us is a **braided history**: Vedic Rudra traditions, emerging Rudra-Śiva theology, epic/Purāṇic religion, ascetic Pāśupata currents, lay Śivadharma, Siddhānta/Mantramārga, Bhairava/Śākta systems, Kaula/Krama/Trika, and philosophical Kashmirian exegetes repeatedly interacting and reorganizing one another. Early Śaivism itself was already plural. ([French Institute of Pondicherry][1])

For Pāṭala, that is ideal. We should build **the genealogy of Śaiva ideas**, rather than assume one lineage and force the sources into it.

# The earliest layer: Rudra

If your question is:

> **Where did Śiva actually come from?**

the first indispensable corpus is not Vedānta.

It is **Vedic Rudra**.

You want all the major Rudra passages from:

* Ṛgveda;
* Yajurvedic traditions, particularly the Rudra litany/Śatarudrīya material;
* Atharvaveda;
* Brāhmaṇa material where Rudra's identity and functions develop.

Already here you get features that remain extraordinarily persistent:

```text
terrible / dangerous
healer

lord of animals
wild places

archer
disease-bringer

disease-remover

marginality
auspiciousness

multiplicity of forms/names
```

One of the interesting long-term questions for Pāṭala would be:

> **Which properties of tantric Śiva are genuinely inherited from Rudra, which are transformations, and which enter through other currents?**

Do not let the graph answer that by lexical similarity alone.

Represent:

```text
RUDRA_PROPERTY
HEALER

source:
RV ...

later parallel:
Śiva as healer

relation:
POSSIBLE_CONTINUITY

NOT:
IDENTICAL_DOCTRINE
```

That distinction is essential.

Research on Indian divine multiplicity explicitly traces important representation patterns through Vedic Rudra into later Śaiva forms, though reconstruction of the transition is a historical question rather than something that can simply be assumed. ([Brill][2])

# First giant pillar: Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad

This should be one of the first works you ingest.

It is fascinating because here Rudra is no longer merely one dangerous Vedic deity among others.

You encounter strong moves toward:

```text
supreme Lord
cosmic causality
puruṣa
yoga
liberation
Rudra as supreme
```

And its conceptual environment interacts heavily with emerging Sāṃkhya/Yoga categories. Scholars have long noted that simply reading the text backwards through later Advaita can obscure its own theological and Sāṃkhya-Yoga-inflected structure. ([Cambridge University Press][3])

For Pāṭala this is gold.

You could construct a trajectory:

```text
RUDRA
Vedic dangerous divine agent

↓

RUDRA / ĪŚVARA
cosmic sovereign

↓

ŚIVA
supreme causal/soteriological reality

↓

ŚAIVA TANTRA
Lord + powers + mantra + cosmology

↓

PRATYABHIJÑĀ
ultimate consciousness / agency / recognition
```

But each arrow gets separately tested.

# Second giant pillar: Pāśupata

This is probably the earliest institutional Śaiva movement you should model in depth.

Modern scholarship treats the Pāśupatas as one of the earliest organized and geographically widespread Śaiva ascetic traditions; evidence places Pāñcārthika/Pāśupata sources around the fourth–fifth centuries CE, with related traditions spreading well outside India. ([Cambridge University Press][4])

Important:

### Lakulīśa

Not “Abhinavagupta-like” as a surviving philosophical author, because the historical/source situation is completely different.

But as a **lineage-founder figure**, he becomes a major node.

The early Skandapurāṇa preserves important material about Śiva's descent as Lakulīśa at Kārohaṇa and the origin geography of the Pāśupata movement. ([Brill][5])

### Kauṇḍinya

Now we get closer to the sort of exegetical pillar you want.

The **Pañcārthabhāṣya** attributed to Kauṇḍinya on the Pāśupatasūtra is crucial for reconstructing Pāśupata doctrine.

Conceptually:

```text
pati       Lord
paśu       bound soul
pāśa       bondage

causation
liberation
ritual conduct
relation of Lord and souls
```

This gives you a direct predecessor *problem-space* for later Śaivism, although later tantric systems transform it substantially.

If you're interested in:

Peter Bisschop's work on early Śaivism and the Skandapurāṇa shows that portions of the text are valuable sixth/seventh-century evidence for Śaiva sacred geography and Pāśupata origins. ([Brill][5])

This starts creating the **Śaiva world-model graph** you are after.

# Fourth pillar: Śivadharma

Don't overlook this.

The Niśvāsamukha research is particularly valuable because it helps reconstruct the world immediately around the emergence of tantric Śaivism, explicitly distinguishing:

1. Pāñcārthika Pāśupatas;
2. Lākulas;
3. emerging Mantramārga;
4. non-Mantramārga lay Śaiva practices. ([French Institute of Pondicherry][1])

That is exactly the historical knot we need.

This corpus helps answer:

> How did Śiva religion move from specialized ascetic groups into larger lay, ritual and social worlds?

And:

> What did “being Śaiva” mean before mature Tantra?

Very high priority.

# Fifth giant pillar: Niśvāsa

If you want the **moment Tantra becomes visible as a recoverable textual system**, this is probably the most important source.

The Niśvāsatattvasaṃhitā is described by its critical editors as probably the earliest surviving complete scripture of the Mantramārga; its earliest layers cover cosmology, worship, initiation and yoga. ([French Institute of Pondicherry][6])

This is an extraordinary Pāṭala corpus.

Because now you can compare:


You can begin asking:

> What genuinely changes when Śaivism becomes tantric?

That is a major historical question.

# Sixth pillar: early Śaiva Siddhānta

This is essential because Abhinavagupta does not arise against a blank tantric background.

Early Siddhānta was once pan-Indian and spread outside India, rather than originally being merely the later Tamil regional tradition people sometimes imagine. Scholarship has reconstructed its broad early-medieval presence and influence, including in Kashmir. ([Cambridge University Press][7])

You need the early scriptures:

```text
Niśvāsa layers
Kiraṇa
Mṛgendra
Mataṅga
Parākhya
Svacchanda? [non-Siddhānta; keep branch distinction]
other early Āgamas
```

The **Parākhyatantra** is especially useful because it preserves a pre-tenth-century Siddhānta thought-world before the major Kashmirian theological commentators, and its surviving material concerns doctrine and yoga. ([French Institute of Pondicherry][8])

# Sadyojyotis: first real intellectual giant

Now we're approaching figures much more analogous to Utpaladeva/Abhinavagupta.

**Sadyojyotis** is one of the foundational early theologians of Śaiva Siddhānta.

He is extremely important because he systematizes issues such as:

```text
Lord
soul
bondage
karma
initiation
liberation
causality
knowledge
```

This is the philosophical machinery against which later nondual Śaiva thinkers can be understood.

In Pāṭala I'd treat:

```text
SADYOJYOTIS
```

as one of the earliest major **AUTHOR-PILLAR** nodes.

Not because he invents Śaivism.

Because now we have someone doing systematic Śaiva philosophical theology.

# Bhaṭṭa Rāmakaṇṭha II: absolutely a pillar

If Abhinavagupta is one summit, **Bhaṭṭa Rāmakaṇṭha II** is another major Kashmirian summit from the dualist Śaiva Siddhānta side.

The IFP describes the tenth-century Kashmirian Rāmakaṇṭha's lineage as producing the most significant body of theological exegesis in the school's history; Goodall's edition of his Kiraṇa commentary shows the range of theology, yoga, observances and ritual addressed by the underlying tantra. ([French Institute of Pondicherry][8])

This becomes incredibly useful for your debate engine.

You eventually want:

```text
RĀMAKAṆṬHA
dualistic Śaiva ontology

vs

UTPALADEVA / ABHINAVAGUPTA
nondual recognition ontology
```

Same broad Śaiva universe.

Different metaphysical commitments.

That is an almost perfect **DebateFrame** use case.

# Seventh branch: Bhairava Tantra

Now the history starts moving toward the world Abhinava inherits much more directly.

One of the most important early textual witnesses is the **Brahmayāmala / Picumata**.

Its core is probably late seventh/early eighth century, it is among the earliest surviving goddess-oriented Śaiva tantras, and possibly the earliest known surviving example of the Bhairavatantra tradition. ([French Institute of Pondicherry][9])

This is a gigantic shift.

You start getting:

```text
Bhairava
goddesses
yoginīs
mantras
maṇḍalas
power
transgressive observances
ritual specialization
```

and eventually the matrix from which Kaula systems become intelligible.

If you want to know where Abhinava's **very strange tantric universe** comes from, you absolutely need this branch.

# The evolution of Śiva's form can itself become a dataset

Here's an example of what becomes possible.

Research into early tantric iconographic/mantric materials suggests a development from four-faced divine representations toward the standardized five-headed Sadāśiva; the five-headed form becomes characteristic of tantric/Āgamic Śaivism and even influences how earlier textual material is rewritten. ([Brill][10])

That would be an excellent example of textual + ritual + iconographic history becoming computable.

# Eighth branch: Krama

Now we hit one of the most important immediate ingredients in Abhinavagupta.

Krama is critical for:

```text
Kālī
temporality
sequence/nonsequence
cognition
phases of manifestation
ritual interiorization
```

Abhinava absorbed Krama deeply.

The problem historically is that the early Krama corpus and lineages are much less neat than later textbook descriptions suggest.

Important figures/nodes include lineages around figures such as:

```text
Jñānanetra / Śivānanda
Eraka
Keyūravatī
Kalyāṇikā
```

and later exegetical figures.

I would flag exact lineage details as needing careful primary-source verification rather than immediately encoding them as settled genealogy.

But as a corpus, Krama should be a major branch because it probably explains a surprising amount of what feels distinctive in mature Abhinavagupta.

# Ninth branch: Kaula

Another direct road into Abhinava.

Don't think of “Kaula” as one school.

You have multiple streams, goddess traditions and transmissions.

Eventually:

```text
early clan/kula goddess rites
       ↓
Bhairava / Yoginī systems
       ↓
Kaula traditions
       ├── Trika/Kaula synthesis
       ├── Krama intersections
       └── Kubjikā / western transmission complexes
```

Modern scholarship sees a major history of transgressive ritual traditions being absorbed, transformed and increasingly interiorized into yogic practice over the early medieval period. ([Cambridge University Press][11])

For your project this branch becomes the history of concepts like:

```text
kula
śakti
yoginī
cakra
sexual ritual
body
mantra
internal worship
```

from early ritual meanings to Abhinava's highly philosophical formulations.

That's incredibly valuable.

# The “Abhinavas” before Abhinava

If you specifically want **pillar figures**, I'd initially prioritize:

1. **Vedic Rudra** — not an author, but foundational deity-complex.
2. **Śvetāśvatara authors/tradition** — early Rudra-as-supreme theology.
3. **Lakulīśa** — founding lineage figure of Pāśupata tradition.
4. **Kauṇḍinya** — crucial Pāśupata exegete.
5. **Sadyojyotis** — foundational Siddhānta theologian.
6. **Bhaṭṭa Rāmakaṇṭha II** — sophisticated Kashmirian Siddhānta philosopher/exegete.
7. **early Bhairava/Śākta scriptural redactors** — anonymous, but conceptually pivotal.
8. **early Krama masters** — direct ingredients of Abhinava's synthesis.
9. **Vasugupta** — Śivasūtra tradition.
10. **Kallaṭa** — Spanda.
11. **Somānanda** — foundational nondual Śaiva philosopher.
12. **Utpaladeva** — Pratyabhijñā's philosophical giant.
13. **Abhinavagupta** — synthesis.
14. **Kṣemarāja** — systematizer/transmitter after Abhinava.

And on a parallel line:

```text
Siddhānta:
Sadyojyotis → Rāmakaṇṭha

Pratyabhijñā:
Somānanda → Utpaladeva → Abhinava

Spanda:
Vasugupta/Kallaṭa → later exegesis → Abhinava/Kṣemarāja

Tantra:
Niśvāsa → Bhairava/Śākta corpora → Trika/Kaula/Krama → Abhinava
```

This is much better than searching for one “teacher of the teacher.”

```text
CORPUS 6 — KASHMIR

```

And three **cross-cutting graphs**:

```text
G1 CONCEPT GENEALOGY
Śiva / consciousness / śakti / liberation / bondage / grace...

G2 COSMOLOGY

G3 ARGUMENT GENEALOGY

```

That would be an incredible use of Pāṭala because you're no longer merely translating texts.

You're answering:

> **How did the world of Abhinavagupta become possible?**

And because the earlier strata include texts like the Niśvāsa that preserve extraordinarily early tantric cosmology, initiation and yoga, and the Niśvāsamukha that explicitly sketches the plurality of Śaivism at the dawn of the Mantramārga, we actually have enough primary evidence to make that question computationally tractable rather than speculative. ([French Institute of Pondicherry][6])

My immediate first ingestion order would be:

**Śvetāśvatara → Pāśupatasūtra/Kauṇḍinya → early Skandapurāṇa → Niśvāsamukha → Niśvāsa → Sadyojyotis/Rāmakaṇṭha → Brahmayāmala → Somānanda → then connect forward into Utpaladeva/IPVV.**

That sequence gives you the maximum historical distance with the fewest foundational corpora, while letting the **Śiva / soul / bondage / cosmos / liberation / consciousness** trajectories emerge rather than assuming what they are.

And I'd separately ingest the major Vedic Rudra passages as a **source anthology** rather than beginning by trying to process the entire Vedic corpus. ([Cambridge University Press][13])
