# Krama — the Sequence of Cognition

## QUICK (the school in one idea)

Krama ("sequence," "order") is the highly internalised Kālī-oriented tradition that makes a
striking claim: **the order of worship (*pūjā-krama*) corresponds to the imperceptible
sequence of cognition (*saṃvit-krama*).** Sequence *is* cognition — the Kālīs are not merely
goddesses but the very moments of consciousness.

Where other systems posit a single static witness, Krama insists reality is *process*: it
unfolds in a sequence of cognitive moments, and this sequence — the coming and going, the
appearance and dissolution of awareness — is what liberation is about. To understand the
Krama is to understand that consciousness does not *have* a process; it *is* a process.

Its crown is the **Mahānaya/Mahārtha** path, which reads the whole of reality as the ever-
present, already-complete sequence of the Goddess's own cognition. The Krama supplies the
*process-based reading* of liberation that balances the static precision of Pratyabhijñā.

## DEEP (the full introduction)

### 1. Sequence is cognition

The name *krama* already states the thesis. In ordinary use it means "order" or "sequence";
the Krama tradition capitalises it into a *doctrine*: the perceptible order of ritual is the
external enactment of an imperceptible order of cognition.

The core move: **the pūjā-krama (the order in which the Goddess is worshipped) reflects the
saṃvit-krama (the order in which consciousness knows).** Ritual is not a mere performance laid
over a static reality. It is the visible grammar of how consciousness actually works — how it
emerges, expands, rests, and dissolves, moment by moment.

This is why Krama is the *process-based corrective* to a "single static witness" metaphysics:
reality is not one frozen eye; it is an ordered unfolding.

### 2. The Kālīs are moments of cognition

The most esoteric teaching of the tradition is the **Doctrine of the Twelve Kālīs**. As Wenta
notes, this is one of the earliest developments of the Kālīkula/Kālīkrama and the most
esoteric teaching in early-medieval Kashmiri Śaivism.

The Twelve Kālīs are not a pantheon of separate goddesses; they are **the twelve moments of
cognition itself** — the phases through which consciousness moves as it knows. The highest,
**Kālasaṃkarṣaṇī** ("She Who Draws Time to Herself"), gathers the whole sequence back into
itself and is understood by Abhinavagupta as *beyond Parā* — above even the three Trika
goddesses (Parā, Parāparā, Aparā). The Krama thus crowns the Trika's triad with a goddess of
the very principle of sequence.

This gives the tradition its distinctive feel: reality is not a state but a *movement*, and
its deities are the grammar of that movement.

### 3. The deep link to Pratyabhijñā

The Krama and the recognition school are not separate worlds. Wenta's study shows the lineage
running from the Krama teacher **Govindarāja** → **Somānanda** → **Utpaladeva** → Abhinavagupta.
The Doctrine of the Twelve Kālīs was passed down this very chain, and Abhinavagupta counts it
as the most elevated esoteric aspect of his own Trika.

This matters: the sequence-of-cognition (Krama) and recognition (Pratyabhijñā) are two halves
of one insight. If reality is a sequence of cognitive moments, then liberation is recognising
oneself as the *whole* sequence — as the Kālīs' return to Kālasaṃkarṣaṇī — rather than as a
single moment mistaken for a separate self. Process and recognition are complementary.

### 4. The Mahānaya/Mahārtha path (the crown)

The Krama's culmination is the **Mahānaya** ("Great Method") and **Mahārtha** ("Great
Meaning") teaching, embodied in the Mahānayaprakāśa and Mahārthamañjarī. Here the whole of
reality is read as the ever-present, already-complete cognition-sequence of the Goddess.

The Mahānaya is not a ladder to climb; it is a recognition of what is already the case: that
the sequence of the world is the Goddess's own sequence, complete from the first moment. The
Kālīs do not have to be assembled; they are already one movement. This is the process-based
equivalent of the recognition thesis — liberation as the re-cognition of an ever-present whole.

### 5. The key distinction: Krama is not disorder, and not a static system

- **Krama ≠ disorder.** "Sequence" is not chaos; it is *order*, the intelligible grammar of
  consciousness. The point is that the order is *active*, not static.
- **Krama ≠ a static hierarchy.** The sequence is not a fixed ladder to be memorised; it is
  the living movement of cognition, always unfolding and always returning to itself.
