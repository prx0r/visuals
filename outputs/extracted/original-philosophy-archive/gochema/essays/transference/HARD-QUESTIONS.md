# HARD QUESTIONS — transference file (2026-10-24)

> Asked against the banked sources. Each bites a specific passage. Unanswered =
> the video's honest-boundary beats. Nothing here is rhetorical; every one has
> a real defender's reply worth steelmanning — which is the film's job.

## I. CORPSE MECHANICS (bites: Karma Chakme training/application; TBOTB Ch.10; kuśa test)

1. **Viability line.** Death is a gradient (3-day window) — but what distinguishes
   a reanimatable corpse from a non-reanimatable one? Is the criterion stated
   anywhere, or is it circular (it worked = it was viable)?
2. **Brain death.** Minutes without oxygen = irreversible neuronal loss; the
   tradition's window is days. Both can't be true of one body. Has the
   prediction ever been tested — and by the tradition's own account (lineage
   dead), could it ever be?
3. **Failure conditions.** Kuśa grass, serum, hair-pull are a falsifiability
   protocol — but has any neutral or hostile witness ever been present? What
   does the literature record as a FAILED attempt?
4. **Guide-out vs move-in.** If lingering consciousness makes a corpse viable for
   3 days, why can't phowa-for-others be performed on ANY fresh corpse to bring
   them back? What's the principled difference between guiding out and moving in?

## II. IDENTITY / MEMORY (bites: grong 'jug claim; chariot; tulku testing; karma ownership)

5. **Blended hardware.** The incomer keeps their memories — but does it inherit
   the corpse's languages, skills, brain-habits? If yes, whose karma ripens in
   that body? If no, why does mind need an intact brain at all?
6. **Who pays.** Social identity (face, name, debts, marriage) stays with the
   body; continuity belongs to the incomer. Who answers for the corpse's past
   deeds — Milinda's mango-thief, but the thief changed bodies mid-trial?
7. **Asymmetric proof.** The tulku system demands memory tests; grong 'jug texts
   describe NO testing protocol. Why does the institutional version require
   proof and the DIY version none?
8. **What travels.** If the self is designation-only (chariot), designations
   can't travel. Either something real moves (eternalism charge) or nothing
   does (then what reanimates the corpse?). The tradition must pick.

## III. LINEAGE / PRACTICE (bites: Karma Chakme "no practice lineage"; Achard; restrictions)

9. **Dead or alive.** Karma Chakme: no practice lineage survives. Achard: Kagyü
   lost it, Nyingma/Bön retain it. Both can't be fully true. Name one practitioner
   in living memory — or admit the file is closed.
10. **Self-authorization.** Practice requires empowerment + teacher + training. If
    no lineage holder exists to empower, is every current claim self-authorizing
    by definition?
11. **Secrecy gradient.** Karma Chakme's full manual is public; Longchen Nyingtik
    root stays restricted. Same practice, opposite handling — what determines it?
12. **Consent.** Forceful phowa = deity-murder; coercive entry = evicting an owner.
    The tradition has transfer ethics — but where is the consent framework for
    the corpse's family? For the evicted owner?

## IV. HINDU SIDE (bites: TĀ 19 quarantine; vol10 parapraveśa; Szántó thesis)

13. **Sport or sociology.** Parapurapraveśa: siddhas entering living bodies for
    play — literal power, or mythic sociology authorizing the Kula (disembodied
    teachers = unchallengeable authority)? Jayaratha vs Kṣemarāja read it
    differently; which is primary?
14. **Quarantine meaning.** Abhinavagupta fences utkrānti OFF from yoga (TĀ 19).
    Is willed death then NOT a liberation-method in his system, just an exit?
    What happens to "yogic suicide as attainment" framing?
15. **Direction of borrowing.** Szántó: 'pho ba = adopted utkrānti. What did
    Buddhists CHANGE and why? Anattā guard: if no owner travels, what exactly
    transfers in phowa? Same paradox as rebirth — answer must match the rebirth
    film's.
16. **Cheating vs liberation.** Kālavancana (sacred site, starvation) extends
    life; utkrānti ends it well. The texts run both under "cheating death" —
    dying-well or dying-late? Pick one per passage.

## V. META (the video's own honesty)

17. **Witness problem.** Every success sign is reported by the practitioner or
    their disciples. One hostile or neutral witness anywhere in the literature?
18. **Defense problem.** If consciousness can be evicted from the living (coercive
    variant), what protects anyone? Does the tradition describe a defense — or a
    weapon it can't counter?
19. **Why train lower modes.** If dharmakāya ("nothing to transfer") is ultimate,
    are the other eight modes theater? (Their answer: capacity/gradation — same
    logic as stream-entry vs arahant. Note the parallel, don't resolve it here.)
20. **The money question.** Lineage dead, signs unwitnessed, metaphysics
    untestable. What does this material actually establish? The video's honest
    claim: a precise phenomenology of what its practitioners believed happens
    at death — plus the historical fact of the borrowing (Szántó) and the
    sociological fact of the fencing (both traditions ban the DIY version).
