## 2. THE DYNAMIC ASPECT — DURATION AS INVARIANCE

### The core equation (from the real text)
> "**Duration and Invariance under Transformation are one and the same.**" (Dynamic, §2)

A thing endures = a combination of unchange and change. The lamp + the ticking clock: if
either is missing, the image of duration fails.

### The eternity claim (Dynamic, §4)
> "To get duration, the difference between the unchanging and the changing must be
> absolute… If a thing endures, it endures for ever. A thing is eternal."

A thing changes only after an **infinity of moments** — and since the structure is
hierarchical, each moment endures for an infinity of lesser moments. But:
> "We must be careful not to introduce preconceived notions of time: just as the structure
> is not *in* space but *of* space, so the structure is not *in* time but *of* time."

### The weight/intensity of 'this' vs 'that'
> "The intensity or weight must be distributed among the four symbols… 'this' is necessarily
> more intense than 'that'… the weight of 'this' is always twice the combined weights of the
> three 'that'."

### Past, present, future
> "Past, present and future (as well as present) exist in the present; but they exist *as*
> past and *as* future… the past as over-determined, the present as determined, the future
> as under-determined."

**The time-structure connects to the felt and to active inference:** past = over-determined,
future = under-determined — the system's directional (teleological) character.
