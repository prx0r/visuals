# IPVV MEMORY vs. "WHAT THE HECK IS MEMORY" — the deep comparison

*A side-by-side: the IPVV's memory doctrine (read from the L2 chapters V1D/E/F, V2A/J/K/L/M/N)
vs. our modern "what the heck is memory" essay. The point: does the IPVV's memory argument align
with the essay's scientific decomposition? Where it does, where it diverges, and what each adds
to the other. Method: lay the two side by side, claim by claim, honestly.*

---

## PART 1 — THE SIX-FOLD SPLIT (the essay) vs. the IPVV's actual memory claims

The essay (Part 1) splits memory into: storage, retrieval, reconstruction, pastness, mine-ness,
phenomenal reliving. It says Torella's argument is strongest on the last three.

**What the IPVV actually argues (from the L2):**
1. **Memory is a knowledge, not a trace-revival** (V2A): "Memory is the Lord's own power — the
   un-broken manifestation by which the past and the present are held together in the single 'I.'"
   Memory "brings the previously-experienced object back before the mind; the object appears, again."
2. **Memory requires the reflexive-awareness to be laid and awakened** (V2J): the impression is
   laid only by the vimarśa ("I, thus, shine"); without it, no seed. The coconut-island example: the
   one who never saw fire does not remember fire from smoke.
3. **Memory requires an abiding self** (V1F, V2A): "A memory reaches across the break of experience
   and says 'I saw this before' — and only an abiding self could hold the before and the after
   together in the single 'I.' The stream of moments cannot do this."
4. **The self is eternal ("I was never not")** (V1D): the memory of the Rāmāyaṇa presupposes an
   experiencer who was there; the "un-precedented manifestation" rests on the eternal knower.
5. **The "I"-recollection is not a construction** (V2L): the pure ahaṃ-pratyavamarśa is the
   self-grasp that underlies all construction, not itself one.
6. **The inner appearance stands firm in memory, cognition, and construction** (V2N): the
   reflexive-awareness is "innate" across all three.

**The alignment with the essay's split:** The IPVV's strongest claims are EXACTLY on the essay's
last three (pastness, mine-ness, reliving) — not storage/retrieval. The essay said Torella is
strongest there; the IPVV confirms this precisely. Memory's "un-broken manifestation" (V2A) is the
essay's "mine-ness + reliving" — the experienced "I saw this before." The essay's claim that the
real frontier is "why is the reconstruction given as one first-person act" is EXACTLY the IPVV's
claim that memory is the un-brokenness of the manifestation.

> **PENETRATION 1 (alignment):** The IPVV's memory argument lands exactly where the essay says
> Torella is strongest — on pastness/mine-ness/reliving, not storage. Memory as "the un-broken
> manifestation by which the past and present are held in the single 'I'" (V2A) is the essay's
> "why is this given as one first-person act." **The two agree on WHERE the hard problem is.**

## PART 2 — THE BIGGEST ALIGNMENT: memory as present construction under constraint

The essay's Advance 3 (and Part 8) is striking: **"Memory and imagination use overlapping generative
resources. The critical distinction is source attribution. Memory is imagination constrained by a
claimed causal relation to the system's own past. That is extremely close to Utpaladeva's treatment
of both as vikalpa while preserving the 'already perceived' character of memory."**

**The IPVV confirms this precisely, in the L2:**
- V2J: the determination "this jar" is "beyond name and form — the param-Lord's power." The
  "this-ness" is the self's own manifestation, not a word pasted on a thing.
- V2L: construction (vikalpa) is the joining/splitting of the "this"; but the "I"-recollection is
  NOT a construction.
- V2K: the word is superimposed (adhyāsa) on the meaning; the cognition's order is only in the
  objects, "the awareness that knows them all is one, without sequence."

So the IPVV aligns with the essay's "memory and imagination are both vikalpa, differing by source":
both are present acts of construction, and the difference is the "already perceived" (source)
constraint. This is a genuinely deep alignment — the modern essay and the 10th-century IPVV reach
the SAME structure: memory = construction + source-attribution, over shared generative resources.

> **PENETRATION 2 (the deep alignment):** Both agree memory and imagination are the SAME present
> constructive act, differing only by source-attribution (the "already perceived" vs. the imagined).
> The essay's "memory is imagination constrained by a claimed causal relation to the self's past"
> IS the IPVV's "memory and imagination are both vikalpa, but memory preserves the already-perceived
> character." **They independently reach the same structure.**

## PART 3 — THE DIVERGENCE: what the IPVV adds that the essay cannot

The essay's final synthesis says: "This may explain content + pastness + mine-ness + reconstruction.
What remains unresolved is: WHY do those processes constitute one presently manifest act of 'I
remember'?" And it offers three frameworks (constructivism, inexternalism, Pratyabhijñā), saying
"the debate moves from whether traces can encode pastness to whether mechanistic source attribution
explains experienced first-person identity."

**The IPVV's answer to that exact question** (V2A, V2L, V2N):
- **The "I" is not constructed** (V2L): "The 'I'-recollection... is not a construction. It is the
  self-grasp of the manifestation free of the contraction by the body and the rest... the pure
  self-awareness that underlies every construction, the very thing that makes construction possible,
  without itself being one."
- **The inner appearance stands firm across memory/cognition/construction** (V2N): it is "innate"
  in all three — not a construction that appears in some and not others.
- **The "I" holds time together** (V2A): "Memory is the thread by which the 'I' holds time together."

So the IPVV DIRECTLY addresses the essay's open question: the "one presently manifest act of 'I
remember'" is the ahaṃ-pratyavamarśa — the pure self-grasp that is not a construction, the
"un-brokenness of the manifestation." This is the essay's "Inexternalism" option, stated with full
metaphysical commitment: the persisting process IS intrinsically lived as one temporally extended
field, and the "I" is its self-grasp.

> **PENETRATION 3 (the divergence/answer):** The essay ends with the open question "why is this
> given as one first-person act?" and offers Inexternalism as one option. **The IPVV answers it
> directly: because the "I"-recollection (ahaṃ-pratyavamarśa) is not a construction — it is the pure
> self-grasp that underlies all construction (V2L), innate across memory/cognition/construction
> (V2N), and it is what holds time together (V2A).** This is Inexternalism made full: the persisting
> field is intrinsically lived as one, and its self-grasp is the "I." The IPVV commits to what the
> essay only floats.

## PART 4 — WHERE THE ESSAY CORRECTS THE IPVV (the honest side)

The essay's most valuable contribution is its PART 10 (false memory/reconsolidation) and PART 11
(amnesia):

**False memory (essay Part 10):** "A memory can feel mine and past while being partly or wholly
false. Autonoetic form is separable from veridicality." — The IPVV's "the inner appearance stands
firm" (V2N) and "the 'I' holds time together" (V2A) do NOT address accuracy. The IPVV's argument is
about the POSSIBILITY of connection (the un-brokenness), not infallibility. **The essay correctly
notes the IPVV survives this (its argument concerns connection, not accuracy), but it means the IPVV
cannot use memory-certainty to prove metaphysical persistence.**

**Amnesia (essay Part 11):** "A person with severe episodic amnesia may remain conscious in the
present... Memory explains temporal self-extension, not the existence of momentary manifestness." —
The essay places the memory argument at the level of *diachronic* unity, NOT consciousness-as-such.
The IPVV actually AGREES (V2N: the inner appearance "stands firm for every living one" even in
memory-loss contexts, because it's the self-grasp, not the memory-content). So the IPVV's "I" is not
memory-dependent — it's the self-grasp that makes memory possible. **This is a genuine alignment, not
a correction: both locate memory at diachronic unity, and both keep the self-grasp prior to memory.**

> **PENETRATION 4 (the honest correction):** The essay correctly warns that the IPVV's argument is
> about the possibility of connection, not infallibility (false memory doesn't refute it, but
> certainty doesn't prove persistence). And the essay's amnesia point (memory = diachronic unity, not
> manifestness) is something the IPVV ALREADY agrees with — the "I" is the self-grasp prior to
> memory (V2L/V2N), not the memory-content. **The essay and the IPVV align: memory is diachronic
> unity; the self-grasp is prior to and independent of memory.**

## PART 5 — THE SYNTHESIS: the exact contribution each makes

**What the essay gives the IPVV:**
- The six-fold split (which claims are strong vs. weak).
- The modern mechanism (engram, hippocampal index, temporal context, source monitoring, bodily
  self-model, metacognition) that answers the Buddhist trace-objection the IPVV could not
  (retrieval/pastness are now explicable).
- The honest limit (the debate is now "mechanistic source attribution vs. experienced first-person
  identity").
- The three frameworks (constructivism / inexternalism / Pratyabhijñā).

**What the IPVV gives the essay:**
- The ANSWER to its open question: the "I"-recollection is not a construction; it's the pure
  self-grasp underwriting all construction (V2L), innate across memory/cognition/construction (V2N),
  holding time together (V2A).
- The "I was never not" (V1D) — the eternal knower as the ground of the un-precedented.
- The memory-requires-vimarśa point (V2J) — memory needs the reflexive-awareness to be laid at all.
- The word-joined-but-not-constructed distinction (V2L/V2K) — how the "I" can have a word-body yet
  not be a vikalpa.

**The combined thesis:**
> Memory is present reconstruction under historical constraint (the essay) that is GIVEN as one
> first-person act because the "I"-recollection is the pure self-grasp, not a construction, that
> underlies all construction and holds time together (the IPVV). The essay provides the mechanism
> and the honest frontier; the IPVV provides the answer to why the mechanism is lived as one "I."

## THE VERDICT

**They align deeply, more than either alone suggests:**
1. Both locate the hard problem at pastness/mine-ness/reliving (not storage).
2. Both agree memory and imagination are the same constructive act, differing by source-attribution.
3. Both place the "I"/self-grasp as prior to and independent of memory (diachronic unity, not
   manifestness).
4. The IPVV answers the essay's open question (why one first-person act) with the
   ahaṃ-pratyavamarśa — the non-constructed self-grasp.
5. The essay correctly guards the IPVV (connection ≠ infallibility; memory ≠ manifestness).

**The one real gap in the IPVV that the essay exposes:** the IPVV's memory argument proves the
POSSIBILITY of first-person connection, but cannot — by memory alone — establish a timeless
metaphysical self against the modern mechanistic account. The essay's final three frameworks remain
open; the IPVV commits to one (Pratyabhijñā/inexternalism) but the essay rightly shows it is a
commitment, not a consequence.

*This is the comparison. The IPVV's memory doctrine (memory as the un-broken manifestation holding
time in the "I"; the non-constructed ahaṃ-pratyavamarśa; the self-grasp innate across all cognition)
aligns with — and answers — the modern "what the heck is memory" essay's decomposition and its open
question. The essay adds the mechanism and the honest guardrail; the IPVV adds the answer to why the
mechanism is lived as one "I." Together they form the complete memory argument: mechanism +
source-attribution + the non-constructed first-person grasp that unifies them.*
