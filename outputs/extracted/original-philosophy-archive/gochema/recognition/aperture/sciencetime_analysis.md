## Part 1: Time — What It Says vs. What We Claim

### What science establishes

The document distinguishes four distinct time concepts:

| Variable | Name | Definition |
|----------|------|------------|
| t_phys | Physical time | Ordering and duration in physical theory |
| t_therm | Thermodynamic arrow | Irreversible entropy increase |
| t_bio | Biological time | Organismic rhythms, temporally extended regulation |
| τᵢ | Lived time | Succession, duration, presentness, flow |

### Key findings that support our thesis

1. **Physics has no objective "flow"** — the equations describe temporal order (e₁ < e₂ < e₃) but not a moving NOW. This means τᵢ (lived time) is NOT reducible to t_phys. The aperture must CONSTRUCT the experience of passage.

2. **The specious present (2-3 seconds) is constructed** — it's not a direct readout of physical simultaneity but an active integration mechanism. This gives τᵢ a specific neural timescale.

3. **Gisin's argument from the Bekenstein bound** — if finite volumes cannot contain infinite information, then real numbers are processes in time, not completed objects. This implies the future is genuinely open. This directly supports svātantrya as a real feature of the world, not an illusion.

4. **Kāla as a kañcuka** — the document supports the WEAK interpretation: kāla is finite access to temporal relations from within an embodied aperture. This IS our position.

### What it does NOT support

| Our claim | Supported? |
|-----------|-----------|
| τᵢ = lived time constructed by the aperture | **Yes** — consistent with specious present, temporal context models |
| kāla is a kañcuka (limitation freely assumed) | **Yes, weakly** — kāla as finite access, NOT as consciousness creating physical time |
| Physical time is created by individual consciousness | **No** — science provides no support for this stronger Śaiva claim |
| The flow of time is an illusion | **No** — the Better: flow is a constructed feature of τᵢ, not an illusion |

### What it adds that we didn't have

- Gisin's Bekenstein-bound argument for ontological indeterminacy (supports svātantrya)
- The specious present as a 2-3 second integration window (gives τᵢ a specific scale)
- Temporal context models from memory science (connects to our memory analysis)
- The hierarchy of time concepts (t_phys, t_therm, t_bio, τᵢ)

## Part 2: Agency — What It Says vs. What We Claim

### What science establishes

The document defines agency in six levels:

| Level | Name | Definition |
|-------|------|------------|
| 0 | Reactive causation | Billiard balls |
| 1 | Homeostatic agency | Self-maintaining systems (cells) |
| 2 | Flexible goal-pursuit | Multiple means to same end (organisms) |
| 3 | Goal learning | Learning which goals to pursue |
| 4 | Goal revision | Changing one's own goals |
| 5 | Self-revision | Changing the criteria by which goals are evaluated |

### Key findings that support our thesis

1. **Goal revision (Level 4) is the threshold** — this is where genuine agency begins. A system that can change its own goals is qualitatively different from one that merely pursues fixed goals. This maps onto our svātantrya-as-operational-autonomy.

2. **Computational irreducibility** means the system's future may not be predictable by any shortcut — it must be generated. This provides a scientific analogue of limited svātantrya without requiring metaphysical libertarian free will.

3. **Imagination is agency's most complete expression** — the document reaches the same conclusion as Ratié: imagination (generation of novel configurations) IS the experience of freedom.

### What it does NOT support

| Our claim | Supported? |
|-----------|-----------|
| Svātantrya = the worm's minimal freedom scales up to Śiva's absolute freedom (ĪPK I.6.11) | **Yes, as a graded spectrum** — but science stops at Level 4-5; it cannot confirm the "Śiva" endpoint |
| Imagination = svātantrya (ĪPK I.6.10) | **Yes** — "imagination is the only cognitive process that can 'break the causal chain' by generating genuinely novel content" (direct quote from the document) |
| Absolute freedom (uncaused self-determination) | **No** — science supports operational autonomy within constraint, not creation ex nihilo |
| The worm consciously experiences freedom | **No** — science can establish agency without establishing phenomenality |

### What it adds that we didn't have

- The six-level hierarchy of agency (gives us a way to talk about the worm-to-Brahmā spectrum)
- Computational irreducibility as a formal constraint on prediction (not freedom per se)
- Goal revision as the minimal criterion for genuine agency
- Policy selection ≠ freedom (active inference pursues fixed preferences)
