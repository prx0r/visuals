## Section 1: What the Texts Actually Say

### 1.1 ĪPK 1.3.7 — The Three Powers [T]

```
syād ekaś cid-vapur jñāna-smṛty-apohana-śaktimān
```

"There would be one reality whose nature is consciousness, possessing the powers of cognition, memory, and exclusion." (Torella 2002)

**Established:** Consciousness has three powers: cognition (jñāna), memory (smṛti), and exclusion (apohana). Exclusion is NOT a secondary linguistic operation — it is a constitutive power alongside cognition and memory. (Torella 2007a)

**NOT established:**
- That these three powers are the ONLY powers (the verse says "possessing" not "consisting of")
- That apohana = Λₚ (phenomenal partition). The verse discusses determination of objects, not individuation of subjects.
- That the TĀ is organized around this triad. This is our reconstruction, not stated in any text.

### 1.2 ĪPK I.6.10 — Imagination as Autonomous [T]

```
sa naisargika evāsti vikalpe svairacāriṇi / yathābhimatasaṃsthānābhāsanād buddhigocare
```

"The [conscious manifestation] is perfectly spontaneous in [the case of] a mental construction that wanders autonomously, since [it] manifests [this or that] configuration at will in the realm of the intellect." (Ratié 2010)

**Established:** Some mental constructions are NOT determined by residual traces (saṃskāra). They are autonomous (svairacārin), self-prompted (svapreraṇa), independent of external prompting. This is the clearest ordinary case of svātantrya (freedom).

**NOT established:**
- That this proves absolute freedom. The imagination still operates within constraints (prior concepts, perceptual materials, linguistic capacities, memory, bodily organization). "Novel configuration ≠ causally unconditioned creation ex nihilo." (Audit §1)
- That imagination = apoha. These are distinct operations: imagination generates novel forms; apoha determines through exclusion. Imagination likely USES exclusion but is not identical with it.
- That this establishes basal self-affection (ρᵢ). Creative spontaneity is not automatically felt self-relish.

### 1.3 ĪPK I.6.11 — Imagination as Path to Recognition [T]

```
ata eva yathābhīṣṭasamullekhāvabhāsanāt / jñānakriye sphuṭe eva siddhe sarvasya jīvataḥ
```

**Established:** Because imagination manifests objects at will, the powers of knowledge and action are evident in every living being — from a worm to Brahmā. This is a phenomenological argument: even the most limited creature experiences svātantrya in imagination.

**NOT established:**
- That every worm consciously recognizes absolute freedom. The verse establishes that knowledge-power and action-power are spontaneous in every living being. The INTERPRETATION of that spontaneity is the dispute between Śaiva and Buddhist/naturalist accounts.
- That this proves universal consciousness. It proves that the powers are universal — not that their ground is numerically one.

### 1.4 TĀ 10:12113-12125 — Dehabhedāt and Pratibimba [T]

```
saṁvit sarvātmikā dehabhedād yā saṅkucet tu ṣā / melake 'nyonyasaṅghaṭṭapratibimbād vikasvarā
```

"Consciousness, which is all things, contracts due to differences between the bodies... [and] returns to its state of ongoing expansion during a gathering, because of the mutual reflection taking place by their union with one another."

**Established:**
- Body differences (dehabhedāt) produce contraction of consciousness
- Mutual reflection (pratibimba) between embodied participants produces expansion
- Contraction and expansion are graded, not binary

**NOT established:**
- That the body IS the mirror. [R] Three models are possible: (A) body as mirror, (B) consciousness as mirror with bodies as differentiating conditions, (C) relation as mirror. The text does not decide.
- That mutual reflection produces numerical identity. It produces expansion of the contracted field without merging subjects. "During ritual, aesthetic, emotional, or social synchronization: attention becomes coordinated; bodily rhythms may align; intentions become mutually predictive; self-models become less defensive." This is NOT fusion.

### 1.5 TĀ 1:15177-15185 — Space/Time/Form Cannot Divide [T]

"Space, time and form cannot divide up Śiva, Who is the Light of consciousness and independent of (all) else, because (space and the rest) are conditioned by thought constructs based on unity and diversity (bhedābheda)."

**Established:**
- Space, time, and form do not divide consciousness into independent substances
- The appearance of division is conditioned by vikalpa (thought constructs)

**NOT established:**
- That phenomenal difference is unreal or merely epistemic. [R] The better distinction: "difference is phenomenally and causally effective" while "difference is not an ultimate severing of the ground." The text says consciousness is NOT divided — it does NOT say the experience of division is false.
- That this solves the partition problem. It establishes ontological non-division of the ground — not the mechanism by which local fields appear exclusive.

### 1.6 TĀ 16.242 — Anusandhāna Limited to One Body [T]

"bhogyajñānaṃ nānyadeheṣv anusandhānam arhati"

"Knowledge that is enjoyed in one body cannot perform connective synthesis with other bodies."

**Established:** Abhinavagupta explicitly limits anusandhāna (the connective synthesis that unifies cognitions into one stream) to one body. This directly constrains Utpaladeva's universalization argument in APS 17.

**Significance:** This is internal COUNTER-EVIDENCE to the strong universalization reading. If anusandhāna cannot cross bodies, then the "one knower of all cognitions" argued in APS 17 is the knower within one cognitive stream — NOT the universal subject across all streams.

## Section 2: What We Have Reconstructed [R]

### 2.1 The Body as a Regime of Determination [R]

A body is not merely a container but a dynamically maintained set of capacities and limitations determining: what can affect the process, what can be sensed, what can be acted upon, what must be regulated, what information is retained, what events belong to its history, which effects propagate through it.

**Formally:** Bᵢ = ⟨Sᵢ, Aᵢ, Vᵢ, Γᵢ, Cᵢ⟩

**Supported by:** TĀ dehabhedāt (body differences contract), ĪPK 1.3.7 triad (but the body as exclusion boundary is our extension)

**NOT established:** That the body boundary IS the phenomenal boundary. Skin ≠ causal boundary ≠ functional boundary ≠ phenomenal boundary. The body is probably the main carrier architecture, not a simple boundary line.

### 2.2 Rāga as Weighting Exclusion [R]

The TĀ defines rāga as generalized craving and niyati as that craving fixed on a particular object. This suggests an elegant transition: undirected lack → directed fixation → localized world.

**Supported by:** TĀ 3:2199-2220 definition. Strongly plausible as a link between affective and epistemic determination.

**NOT established:** That every exclusion is driven by desire. Many exclusions (distinguishing blue from red, identifying a triangle, hearing one phoneme rather than another) do not appear motivational. [R] Better model: desire weights or guides exclusion without constituting every act of it.

### 2.3 The Kañcukas as Configuring the Exclusion Space [R]

The five kañcukas (limited agency, knowledge, attachment, time, necessity) can be understood as establishing the alternative space within which apohana operates:
- Kalā excludes unavailable actions
- Vidyā excludes unavailable distinctions
- Rāga distinguishes actual from desired
- Kāla excludes non-present temporal access
- Niyati excludes causally inaccessible positions

**Supported by:** The general structure of the kañcuka system + ĪPK 1.3.7 apohana as determination. The specific mapping is our reconstruction.

### 2.4 The Five Operations [R]

The strongest revised system proposes five operations rather than one master exclusion operator:
1. Manifestation (𝒥ᵢ): x ↦ x appears
2. Retention/connection (ℳᵢ): past ↦ historically connected present
3. Alternative generation (ℐᵢ): Zᵢ ↦ {a₁,...,aₙ}
4. Value weighting (𝒱ᵢ): aₖ ↦ wₖ
5. Determinate exclusion (ℰᵢ): (aₖ, wₖ) ↦ a*

**Supported by:** ĪPK 1.3.7 triad (jñāna, smṛti, apohana) + ĪPK I.6.10 (imagination as autonomous generation) + TĀ rāga-niyati (value weighting) + TĀ kañcukas (constraint of alternative space).

**NOT established:** That this set of five is complete, or that the specific arrangement is correct. This is a working model.

## Section 3: Research Hypotheses [H]

### 3.1 The Aperture Operator [H]

```
Λᵢ = Closeᵢ ∘ E_worldᵢ ∘ E_contentᵢ ∘ Iᵢ
```

**Status:** Conjecture. Not supported by any single text. Combines elements from ĪPK, TĀ, and our reconstruction.

### 3.2 Three Exclusion Types [H]

- Content exclusion (E_contentᵢ): Ωᵢ → xᵢ — [T] supported by ĪPK 1.3.7
- World exclusion (E_worldᵢ): Ω → Wᵢ — [R] plausible extension through kañcukas
- Subject exclusion (E_subject): Ω → {D₁,...,Dₙ} — [H] NOT established by any text

### 3.3 Identity Between Dᵢ and Oᵢ [H]

```
Dᵢ ≡_inext Oᵢ
```

**Status:** Metaphysical hypothesis. The texts assert that consciousness is one and its manifestations are its powers. They do NOT assert the inexternalist identity between a physical process and its intrinsic aspect. This is our addition.

## Section 4: What the Audit Rejected [X]

### 4.1 Body = Mirror [X]

Too strong. The TĀ text does not identify the body with the reflective substrate. Three models remain possible: body as mirror, consciousness as mirror with bodies as differentiating conditions, or relation as mirror.

### 4.2 Subject Partition Is Merely Epistemic [X]

Too strong. The TĀ says consciousness is NOT divided — it does NOT say the experience of division is unreal. Better: difference is phenomenally and causally effective without being an ultimate severing of the ground.

### 4.3 Every Exclusion Is Driven by Desire [X]

Too strong. Many exclusions (distinguishing colors, recognizing shapes) do not appear motivational. Desire may weight or guide exclusion without constituting every act of it.

### 4.4 Each Sense Excludes Other Modalities [X]

Falsified by ordinary multimodal experience. Modality differentiation ≠ phenomenal exclusion. Vision does not remove sound from the field.

### 4.5 Beauty = Experienced Svātantrya [X]

Not textually supported. The vīṇa passage describes tanmayatā (absorption in music) — it does not identify beauty with the experience of freedom.

### 4.6 Musical Continuum = Phenomenal Aperture [X]

The "single extension of sound on the vīṇa" is more likely structured content within the musician's field, not the field boundary itself.

## Section 5: The Three Surviving Frameworks

All three remain viable after the audit:

**Process inexternalism:** Oᵢ* is intrinsically one field.
**Participatory inexternalism:** Oᵢ* locally actualizes a universal manifest ground.
**Pratyabhijñā:** Oᵢ* is one contracted self-articulation of Śiva.

The texts have not selected among these. They have shown that Utpaladeva and Abhinavagupta already possessed nearly the complete functional anatomy of the finite aperture — but interpreted its powers as contractions and expressions of one universal free consciousness.

## Section 6: The Actual Frontier

The frontier is no longer "why does matter become conscious?" It is:

```
Why is one embodied, temporally continuous regime of manifestation,
generation, valuation, and exclusion intrinsically one private field?
```

Pratyabhijñā now gives serious resources for every term except the final necessity:
- Manifestation → prakāśa/jñāna
- Self-determination → vimarśa
- Continuity → smṛti/anusandhāna
- Alternative creation → imagination/svātantrya
- Determination → apohana
- Finite regime → kañcukas
- Embodied individuation → deha-bheda
- Interpersonal expansion → pratibimba
- Affective direction → rāga

What remains absent is a demonstrated implication:

```
closed determination regime ⇒ one phenomenal token
```

That is what we still cannot derive from any text.
