## Batch 1: Timalsina — Theatrics of Emotion (19p, 737 lines)

**Source:** Philosophy East and West, Volume 66, Number 1, January 2016, pp. 104–121.

### Core Claim
Abhinavagupta's aesthetics provides a model where aesthetic experience (rasa) is NOT self-deception but a higher-order awareness in which the subject relishes emotion while remaining aware of its constructed nature. The term "theater" (raṅgabhūmi = "stage of colors" / "battlefield") is both the space of performance and the space of life — the world IS a theater, and recognition is seeing it as such while continuing to engage.

### Key Passage
"For Abhinava, relishing the rasa experience is an epiphany of wonder (camatkāra) that also applies to the bliss of being in the dynamic world (jagad) or living a good life. This foundational being that is equated with bliss is the awareness or meta-cognition of the blissful instances of somatic engagement found by a yogin in all instances of cognition, and occasionally by a layperson when watching a drama."

### Significance
**Rasa is not about objects — it's about HOW you relate to experience.** The key variable is not what emotion you feel but whether you are "merged" with it (self-deceived) or "watching" it while engaged (the transcendent gaze). This maps directly onto our σ_owner (ownership contraction) variable: aesthetic absorption relaxes the practical ownership of emotion → emotion becomes relishable. This is exactly what we proposed in the rasa thesis v0.2.

## Batch 2: Timalsina — Aham, Subjectivity, and the Ego (23p, 1179 lines)

**Source:** Journal of Indian Philosophy (2020).

### Core Claim
Abhinavagupta's "aham" (I/self) has THREE distinct topographies:
1. **Aham as empirical ego** — the limited self that identifies with the body
2. **Aham as subject of experience** — the "I" that objectifies the ego
3. **Aham as absolute I-consciousness** — the "I" that embodies the totality (pūrṇāhantā)

### Key Passage
"It is the I-sense that determines or delimits the parameters of the body, and in this sense aham also stands for the embodied self-experience."

### Significance
This tripartite structure maps onto our ∂Sᵢ (field boundary) variable:
- Level 1: ego contraction (samāna ownership — "THIS body is me")
- Level 2: witnessing (sākṣin — the "I" that observes the ego)
- Level 3: recognition (pūrṇāhantā — the "I" that IS the totality)

The movement from level 1 to level 3 IS recognition (T_rec). The paper provides a precise textual foundation for our claim that the self-model (SMᵢ) is not a single thing but a spectrum from contracted to expanded.

## Batch 3: Timalsina — Can Representation be Transformative? (25p, 1094 lines)

**Source:** Philosophy East and West, Volume 71, Number 1, January 2021, pp. 193–216.

### Core Claim
In tantric meditation, representation is not second-order presentation of something absent — it is "presentation again and again" (re-presentation = praty-abhi-jñā). Visualization is not imagining something that is not there — it is PRESENTING AGAIN the fundamental nature of reality.

### Key Passage
"Representation here is not a second-order presentation of something 'out there,' not a shadow truth lurking yet never totally given to us, nor a replica of something else. It is a 're-presentation' or 'presentation again'."

### Significance
This is the semantic version of apoha-as-world-carving. Just as imagination (Ratié) is NOT memory recombination but original creation, representation in tantric practice is NOT copying but RE-PRESENTING — the act of consciousness presenting itself again in a new form. This means the gap between "original" and "representation" is collapsed: every representation IS the original presenting itself differently. This is exactly our claim that Dᵢ = Int(Oᵢ) — the phenomenal field is not a representation of the process but the process presenting itself intrinsically.

## Batch 4: Timalsina — Reconstructing Abhinavagupta's Philosophy of Power (20p, 631 lines)

**Source:** South Asian Studies: Bridging Cultures.

### Core Claim
Śakti (power) in Abhinavagupta's system is not just theological or semantic — it is the freedom (svātantrya) that constitutes consciousness itself. This philosophy of power can be extended to address secular concepts of social, political, and semantic power.

### Significance
The paper grounds śakti in svātantrya rather than the other way around. This supports our claim that the freedom to carve a world (Eᵢ) is not a special power but the fundamental operation of consciousness. The connection to political and semantic power opens a new dimension: if apoha-as-world-carving is the mechanism of consciousness, then social and political exclusion are not violations of consciousness but EXPRESSIONS of it — which means the ethical question is not "how to avoid exclusion" but "how to exclude wisely."

## Batch 6: Timalsina — Embodiment and Self-realisation (15p, 680 lines)

**Source:** The Journal of Hindu Studies 2012;5:258–272.

### Core Claim
Non-dual experience is NOT disembodied transcendence — it IS embodied experience recognized AS consciousness. The polarity between Śaṅkara's transcendentalism and shamanic embodied practice is false: both are forms of embodied realization.

### Significance
Supports our claim that embodiment (∂Sᵢ) is NOT the opposite of liberation. The body IS the aperture — liberation is not escape from the body but RECOGNITION of the body as consciousness's self-manifestation. The Tantrāloka's "dehabhedāt" (body differences contract consciousness) is not a claim that embodiment is bad — it's a claim that the SPECIFIC CONTRACTION of embodiment produces the phenomenal field, and recognition relaxes the contraction without dissolving the body.

## Batch 7: Ratié — On the Distinction Between Epistemic and Metaphysical Buddhist Idealisms (23p, 1374 lines)

**Source:** J Indian Philos (2014) 42:353–375.

### Core Claim
The Śaiva nondualists argue that the Buddhist distinction between "epistemic idealism" (we can't know if external objects exist) and "metaphysical idealism" (external objects don't exist) is invalid — the epistemic version already entails the metaphysical one. This is because if we can have no epistemic access to external objects, we cannot even assert their POSSIBILITY.

### Significance
This strengthens the Śaiva position that the world IS consciousness's self-manifestation — not because we have a proof that matter doesn't exist, but because the very concept of "matter outside consciousness" is incoherent. The external world is not denied — it is REINTERPRETED as the form consciousness takes when manifesting as other.

## Cross-Cutting Synthesis

### The Structure of Subjectivity (from Aham paper)
The three-level structure of aham (empirical ego → subject of experience → absolute I-consciousness) is the same structure as our three-level aperture model:
- Level 1 (empirical ego) = contracted field (∂Sᵢ = body boundary + ego identification)
- Level 2 (witnessing subject) = the aperture's self-awareness (SMᵢ as observer)
- Level 3 (absolute I-consciousness) = recognition that the aperture IS the field (ρᵢ as the intrinsic self-affection of manifestation)

### Self-Deception as the Mechanism of Contraction (from Theatrics)
The Theatrics paper provides a model for how contraction occurs: the subject "enters its own dream" and becomes the dream subject, forgetting that it created the dream. Self-deception is not a cognitive error but the OPERATION by which consciousness becomes limited. Recognition is not learning new facts but REMEMBERING that you are the dreamer.

This is a significant addition to our model: Eᵢ (exclusion) is not just carving — it is FORGETTING that you carved. The boundary (∂Sᵢ) is maintained by ongoing self-deception. Recognition (T_rec) is seeing through the deception without needing to stop the play.

### Representation as Re-Presentation (from Representation paper)
The paper's claim that representation is "presentation again" solves a problem we have struggled with: if consciousness is one, how can it appear as many? The answer: the many ARE the one presenting itself again and again in different forms. This is not fragmentation but the one's infinite capacity for self-differentiation.

### Śakti as Svātantrya (from Reconstructing)
The philosophy of power paper grounds śakti in svātantrya, not the other way around. This means power is not something consciousness HAS — power IS what consciousness IS. The ability to carve a world (Eᵢ) is not an attribute of consciousness but its very nature.
