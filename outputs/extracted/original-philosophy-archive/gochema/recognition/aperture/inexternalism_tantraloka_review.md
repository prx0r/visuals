# Inexternalism and the Tantrāloka — Reviewed in Light of the Journey

## What Inexternalism Got Right

The core identity has held up:

```
Int(Pᵢ*) = Dᵢ
```

The phenomenal field IS the intrinsic aspect of a closed, self-maintaining process. This is not provable, but it has proven resilient — no alternative formulation has emerged that explains more with less.

The original decomposition of Pᵢ* into seven terms also holds up:

```
Pᵢ* = Closure[CEᵢ, Semᵢ, Vᵢ, Γᵢ, Mᵢ⁽²⁾, Kᵢ, Qᵢ]
```

Each term has been given a scientific counterpart and, where possible, a textual anchor.

## What Inexternalism Missed

### 1. No exclusion operator (Eᵢ)

The original Pᵢ* had no term for determination through exclusion. This was the single biggest gap. ĪPK 1.3.7 establishes apohana (exclusion) as a fundamental power of consciousness alongside cognition and memory. The aperture must exclude to determine. This is now confirmed.

### 2. No generative operator (Iᵢ)

The original Pᵢ* had no term for imagination or counterfactual generation. ĪPK I.6.10 establishes imagination (svairacārin vikalpa) as the experience of svātantrya — the autonomous generation of novel configurations. This is now the clearest ordinary evidence for freedom in the system.

### 3. No memory operator (Mᵢ) as active reconstruction

Smṛti was listed but not developed. The Torella App.II deepdive confirms that memory is NOT passive trace-retrieval but active reconstruction linking past and present through the invariant I (svātmāṃśe). The Buddhist saṃskāra model cannot explain pastness, mine-ness, or autonoetic reliving.

### 4. No distinction between basal self-affection (ρᵢ) and local valence (νᵢ)

The original framework had only Vᵢ (valence). The Rastogi extraction (Ch.5) revealed two formulations of camatkāra: existential (ρᵢ — joyful valuation of everything as oneself) and experiential (νᵢ — aesthetic savoring). These are not the same. The rasa thesis required this distinction to avoid claiming that all consciousness is pleasant.

### 5. No explicit body-boundary term (Bᵢ)

The original had Σᵢ (embodiment) but not as a formal boundary condition. TĀ 16.242 gives anusandhāna as body-bounded — connective synthesis does not cross bodies. This is the closest thing to a phenomenal exclusion rule in the texts: one body = one maximal domain of connective synthesis.

Where ρᵢ (basal self-affection) and ωᵢ (wonder/expansion) were missing from the original.

## What the Tantrāloka Contributes Now

### Confirmed contributions:

1. **Prakāśa (manifestation):** Manifestation has transcendental priority — every proof occurs within it. This is the strongest argument in the system and requires no metaphysics.

2. **Vimarśa (reflexive articulation):** Reflexive awareness IS activity, not passive registration. The ĪPK 1.5.11 Vṛtti renders vimarśa as camatkāra — reflexive awareness IS aesthetic relish.

3. **Spanda (pulsation):** Reality is dynamic self-articulation, not static substance.

4. **Ābhāsa (manifestation):** Qualities are how the process exists intrinsically, not properties of external objects.

5. **Camatkāra/rasa (aesthetic relish):** Consciousness IS self-affection. The PTv passage confirms this: supreme consciousness is "by its very nature essentially the aesthetic relish of consciousness."

6. **Dehabhedāt (body differences):** The closest thing to a partition rule in the system. Body differences contract consciousness into separate domains.

7. **Anusandhāna (connective synthesis):** The operation that unifies cognitions into one stream. TĀ 16.242 limits it to one body — it does not cross bodies.

8. **Pratibimba (mutual reflection):** Consciousness expands through reflection between bodies. The Ratié correction clarifies: another subject's effects appear WITHIN my field as reflections; the other subject is NOT directly present.

9. **Kañcukas (the five coverings):** The five axes of finitude — limited agency, limited knowledge, attachment, time, necessity. These structure the possibility space within which exclusion operates.

10. **Prasiddhi (certainty):** Certainty is intrinsic, not derived. The baby knows food nourishes without inference. This is the foundation of epistemology.

### What the TĀ does NOT contribute:

1. **No derivation of why one body = one field.** It gives the boundary condition (anusandhāna stops) but not the reason for phenomenal exclusivity.

2. **No proof that universal consciousness underlies all fields.** It asserts this — but as a metaphysical claim, not a derivation.

3. **No formal mechanism for how exclusion operates.** It describes the kañcukas as constraints but not as active exclusion operations (that requires ĪPK 1.3.7).

4. **No experimental confirmation.** The Vijñānabhairava methods are a "library of candidate first-person perturbations" — not controlled experiments.

## The Honest Verdict

The project has completed the functional anatomy of the finite aperture. The Tantrāloka and ĪPK give nine operations with clear textual anchors. Science gives functional counterparts for every operation except manifestness. Cross-cultural convergences confirm the structure across four independent traditions.

What the project has NOT done:

- Crossed the co-presence gap (integration ⇏ co-presence)
- Derived phenomenal privacy from body-boundary (deha-bheda ⇏ ∂ᵢ)
- Derived felt valence from regulatory weighting (rāga ⇏ νᵢ)
- Derived aesthetic experience from counterfactual generation (vikalpa ⇏ αᵢ)
- Decided between the three interpretations of the ground

**The Tantrāloka gives us everything except the final step.** It tells us what the aperture does, how it contracts, how it expands, how it reflects, how it integrates, how it values, how it limits. It does not tell us why this system of operations, closed in one body, is intrinsically one field of experience. That step is not missing from the text — it is missing from every text, every tradition, and every scientific framework we have examined.

The frontier has not moved on that point since day one. But the terrain around it has been mapped in extraordinary detail.
