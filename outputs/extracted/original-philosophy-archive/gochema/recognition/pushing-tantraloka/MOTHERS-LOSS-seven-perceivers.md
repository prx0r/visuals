# THE MOTHER'S LOSS — the experience of a child's death at every level of the Tantrāloka

*A major teaching reference. One situation — a woman's child dies — traced through every level
of the Tantrāloka's structure: the seven perceivers (pramātṛ) mapped onto the thirty-six
principles (tattvas). Each level shows the SAME event as it is experienced from that standpoint
of consciousness. Built from the primary text: TĀ 1/39-40 (the perceivers), TĀ 1/52-55 (the
Light), TĀ 1/131 (the "I" as consciousness), TĀ 1/73-74 (the real difference), TĀ 1/205-209
(the seven perceivers), the PTv p. 41 chain (the three impurities), TĀ 25589 (suffering as
camatkriyā), A.Bh (the rasa work). The point: the loss is ONE event, experienced at seven
depths — from the contracted grief to the recognition that is the ground of even the grief.*

---

## THE LEVELS (the text's own map)

The Tantrāloka teaches **seven perceivers (pramātṛ)** arranged in a hierarchy along the
thirty-six principles (TĀ 1/205-209, explained at 17612-17735). Each perceiver views its
corresponding section of the tattvas as its object. They are:

| # | Perceiver | Where it resides (tattvas) | Its defining character |
|---|-----------|---------------------------|------------------------|
| 1 | **Sakala** (Conditioned) | Earth → Individual Soul (36–13) | Subject to all three impurities (āṇava, māyā, karma); objectivity as separate |
| 2 | **Pralayākala** (Dissolution Deconditioned) | Individual Soul → Māyā (12–6) | In deep sleep; karma and māyā latent; no distinct phenomena |
| 3 | **Vijñānākala** (Consciousness Deconditioned) | between Māyā and the Pure | karma and māyā ended; āṇava tending to cease; absorbed but paralyzed |
| 4 | **Mantra** | within Pure Knowledge (Śuddhavidyā, 5) | objectivity as one with the perceiver in various modalities |
| 5 | **Mantreśa** | Īśvara (4) | higher; āṇava as a fading latent trace |
| 6 | **Mahāmantreśa** | Sadāśiva (3) | higher still |
| 7 | **Śiva** | Śiva (1) | āṇava totally gone; pure consciousness |

*(The classical tattva order: 1 Śiva, 2 Śakti, 3 Sadāśiva, 4 Īśvara, 5 Śuddhavidyā, 6 Māyā,
7 Kalā, 8 Vidyā, 9 Rāga, 10 Kāla, 11 Niyati, 12 Puruṣa, 13 Prakṛti, 14 Buddhi, 15 Ahaṁkāra,
16 Manas, 17-21 the five senses, 22-26 the five actions, 27-31 the five subtle elements
(tanmātras), 32-36 the five gross elements. The "Pure" principles are 1-5; Māyā is 6; the
kañcukas are 7-11; 12 Puruṣa; 13-16 the inner organ; 17-36 the outer world.)*

---

## THE EVENT: the child dies. The mother's experience, level by level.

### LEVEL 1 — SAKALA (Conditioned): the contraction, the grief

**The perceiver:** the ordinary self, subject to all three impurities — the felt incompleteness
(āṇava), the perception of duality (māyā), and the binding activity (karma).

**The tattvas in play (36 → 13):** the gross elements (the body that has died), the subtle
elements (the child's form as perceived), the inner organ (the mind, the ego, the intellect),
and the individual soul.

**The experience:** This is the grief as we most fully know it. The child is a real object in
a world of separate things (māyā — "the perception of duality"). The mother's self-apprehension
is turned outward into the lost object and stuck there — reaching for a child who will not
come. The felt is the "absence of all hope that arises when the inner joy... the arousal of
love a son gives... will never exist again" (TĀ 25589-25594). The suffering is real: it is the
camatkriyā, the wonder of consciousness denied its rest, because the self is identified with
the loss. At this level, the mother IS the grief — "I am this loss," "my life is broken."

**What the Light is doing here:** the Light of consciousness is fully present (it "shines but
once," never unmanifest, TĀ 1/54), but it is not seen. The mother is identified with the
content (the grief), so the wonder (the substrate) is hidden beneath the identification. This
is the contracted state: the "I" is a vikalpa ("I am this grief," with a counter-correlate — it
could be otherwise).

> **AT SAKALA:** the loss is a real, separate object; the grief is real; the mother is
> identified with it; the wonder is present but unseen beneath the identification. Suffering =
> rasa + identification.

---

### LEVEL 2 — PRALAYĀKALA (Dissolution Deconditioned): the numbness

**The perceiver:** still within the realms of māyā, but the active energies of karma have
ceased; only the latent ones remain. It is a state of deep sleep (suṣupti), of two kinds: with
objectivity (savedyā) and free of objectivity (apavedyā).

**The experience:** This is the shock-numbness after the loss. The mother is not yet fully in
the grief; she is "in a state of deep sleep" — the phenomena have withdrawn into latency. The
active grasping has stopped (karma's active energies have ceased), but the latent traces of the
loss persist. She may recollect "I slept comfortably" (savedyā — a bare sense of the lost
relationship) or "I knew nothing at all" (apavedyā — the dissociation, the refusal). The child's
death is present only as a latent trace, not yet risen into full conscious grief.

**What the Light is doing:** still present, still unmanifest in awareness. The numbness is the
Light's withdrawal of the active engagement — a protective latency, the karma not yet
reactivated. The mother is between the shock and the full grief; the loss is real but held at
the threshold.

> **AT PRALAYĀKALA:** the grief is latent, not yet risen. The active grasping has stopped; only
> the traces remain. The mother is in a protective deep-sleep of dissociation — "I knew nothing"
> — holding the loss at the threshold.

---

### LEVEL 3 — VIJÑĀNĀKALA (Consciousness Deconditioned): the absorption

**The perceiver:** both karma and māyā impurities have ended, along with their latent
impressions. Only āṇava (the felt incompleteness) remains, tending to cease. The yogi at this
level "enters a state of deep contemplative absorption (samādhi) but, as if paralyzed, does not
have the power to maintain it" (TĀ 17672-17676).

**The experience:** This is the state of being entirely absorbed in the loss — so completely
identified with the grief that the world (māyā) and even the activity (karma) drop away. The
mother is "in samādhi" on the loss — utterly taken by it — but she cannot sustain it (it keeps
collapsing into ordinary grief or numbness). She is "free but unconscious of being so" or
"conscious but has no freedom" (TĀ 17670-17672): the pure aham (the inward self) is beginning
to be glimpsed, but the mother cannot hold it. This is the grief as total absorption — the 
self-apprehension so fully turned outward into the loss that it is paralyzed.

**What the Light is doing:** at the threshold of the pure. Māyā and karma are gone; only the
felt incompleteness (āṇava — "my life is broken, I am incomplete") remains. The wonder is
beginning to be touched (the "samādhi"), but it cannot be sustained — the ego re-asserts the
grief. This is the pivot level: one step above is the Pure.

> **AT VIJÑĀNĀKALA:** the loss is total absorption — the world and activity drop away; only the
> felt incompleteness (āṇava) remains. The mother touches the pure (a "samādhi" on the loss)
> but cannot hold it — paralyzed between the grief and the ground. The pivot level.

---

### LEVEL 4 — MANTRA: objectivity as one with the perceiver

**The perceiver:** above Māyā, within Pure Knowledge (Śuddhavidyā, the first Pure principle).
Here "objectivity is experienced as one in various modalities with the perceiver" (TĀ
17729-17731). The āṇavamala survives only as a latent trace.

**The experience:** The child is no longer a separate external object. At this level, the
mother begins to experience the loss NOT as the death of a thing separate from her, but as a
modality of her own consciousness. The child, who was "recognized to be nothing but itself"
(the self's own reflection, from TĀ 1/52 and the pratibimbavāda) is now felt as within the
field of her awareness. The grief is no longer "my loss of an external object" but the taste of
the loss as a mode of the one. This is the beginning of recognition: the identification with
the external object (the child-as-separate) loosens; the child is known as a form of her own
consciousness.

**What the Light is doing:** the "I" is no longer a vikalpa (a separate ego grieving a separate
object); it is approaching the pure consciousness-"I" (bodhamātra, TĀ 1/131) that has no
counter-correlate. The child is one with the perceiver in various modalities. The grief is
becoming a tasting rather than a grasping.

> **AT MANTRA:** the child is no longer a separate object — it is experienced as a modality of
> the mother's own consciousness. The grief is loosening from grasping toward tasting; the "I"
> is approaching the pure consciousness-"I" that has no counter-correlate.

---

### LEVEL 5 — MANTREŚA: the Īśvara level

**The perceiver:** resides in Īśvara (the principle of determinate object-presentation). The
āṇavamala survives only as a fading latent trace.

**The experience:** The loss is now presented as determinate but no longer binding. The mother
experiences the child's death as a full, determinate event (the child did die; the love was
real; the loss is real — the reflections are real, TĀ 3/1-4) AND as a mode of the one. The
"trace" of incompleteness is fading: the grief no longer announces "I am incomplete without the
child"; it is becoming the pure tasting of the love in its absence. The mother can now hold
the grief as the love (the grief IS the love, tasted in absence) without being caught in the
incompleteness.

**What the Light is doing:** the determinate presentation (Īśvara) is now transparent to its
source. The mother is able to be fully present to the loss (not dissociated, not absorbed) and
fully aware of its ground. The grief is real, and it is the wonder.

> **AT MANTREŚA:** the loss is fully determinate (real, not denied) and transparent to its
> ground. The felt incompleteness is fading — the grief is becoming the pure tasting of the
> love in its absence, no longer "I am incomplete without the child."

---

### LEVEL 6 — MAHĀMANTREŚA: the Sadāśiva level

**The perceiver:** resides in Sadāśiva (the principle of subject-dominant directedness). The
āṇavamala is nearly gone.

**The experience:** The "I am" — the pure self-assertion of consciousness — predominates. The
mother experiences the loss from the side of the subject: she is the one in whom the loss
arises, not the one whom the loss defines. The grief is there, but it no longer touches the
core "I am" — the self-apprehension is firmly turned inward, and the grief is a flavor within
it. This is the "I am this grief" having become "this grief arises within the I am that I am."

**What the Light is doing:** the pure "I" (bodhamātra) predominates. The loss is a
self-articulation of consciousness, fully present, fully real, and fully within the subject.

> **AT MAHĀMANTREŚA:** the pure "I am" predominates. The loss arises within the subject; the
> grief is a flavor within the wonder, no longer defining the "I am."

---

### LEVEL 7 — ŚIVA: the ground, the recognition

**The perceiver:** Śiva (the first principle). The āṇavamala is totally gone.

**The experience:** This is not a state in which the loss has vanished — the loss was real and
remains part of the play. It is the state in which the mother recognizes that the loss, the
grief, the love, and the child are all modes of the one consciousness — the wonder that she is.
The grief is the camatkriyā, the "particular kind of wonder" (TĀ 25589); the child was "a true
reflection, not a mistake" (TĀ 3/1-4) — a real mode of the one; the mother's tears are the
karuṇa-rasa of the one (A.Bh: "all rasas are essentially pleasurable," because they are modes
of the delight). The recognition is NOT the denial of the loss (the child was real) and NOT an
escape (the grief was real); it is the seeing of the ground — the wonder that was always there,
beneath and within the grief.

**What the Light is doing:** fully seen. The mother, at this level, does not grieve the child
as a separate loss and does not escape it; she recognizes that the love for the child, the
grief at the loss, and the child itself are all the one consciousness's self-manifestation —
and she tastes the love-wonder in and through the grief. "Where there is no fear at all" (TĀ
1490) is approached: the love has seen that nothing it loves is finally lost, because all is
the one's own reflection. The mother keeps the child, as the love that the grief is; and the
love is the wonder of her own consciousness, tasted in and through the loss.

> **AT ŚIVA:** the loss is recognized as a mode of the one consciousness — the wonder that the
> mother is. The grief is the camatkriyā (the wonder taking the flavor of loss); the child was
> a true reflection (real, not a mistake); the tears are the karuṇa-rasa of the one. This is
> NOT the denial of the loss but the seeing of its ground: the love kept, tasted in and through
> the grief.

## THE SYNTHESIS: one event, seven depths

The mother's loss is ONE event, experienced at seven depths of the one consciousness:

1. **Sakala** — the grief, the identification, the suffering (suffering = rasa + identification).
2. **Pralayākala** — the numbness, the dissociation, the latency.
3. **Vijñānākala** — the total absorption, the pivot.
4. **Mantra** — the child no longer separate; the loss as a modality of the one.
5. **Mantreśa** — the loss fully determinate and transparent to its ground; the incompleteness fading.
6. **Mahāmantreśa** — the pure "I am" predominates; the grief as a flavor within the wonder.
7. **Śiva** — the recognition: the loss, grief, love, and child as modes of the one; the love kept, tasted in the loss.

**The deepest teaching:** the loss is real at every level (the child was real, the grief is
real, "a true reflection, not a mistake"). The change is not that the loss becomes unreal as
one ascends — it is that the self-apprehension turns from grasping (the identification, the
suffering) to tasting (the recognition, the wonder). The grief is the camatkriyā, the wonder
taking the flavor of loss. And the ground of even the deepest grief is the wonder that the
mother is — which is why recognition is not the denial of the tears but the seeing of what they
rest in. The mother does not lose her child twice — once to death and once to a callous
metaphysics. She keeps the child, as the love that the grief is; and the love is the wonder of
her own consciousness, tasted in and through the loss.

seven perceivers / thirty-six principles of the Tantrāloka — from the contracted grief (Sakala)
to the recognition that is the ground of even the grief (Śiva). Every claim grounded in the
primary text: the perceivers (TĀ 1/205-209), the "I" as consciousness (TĀ 1/131), the real
reflection (TĀ 3/1-4), suffering as camatkriyā (TĀ 25589), the rasa (A.Bh). The teaching: the
loss is one event, real at every level; the change is the self-apprehension's turn from grasping
to tasting; and the ground of the grief is the wonder that the mother is.*
