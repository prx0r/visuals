# LOGICVID SESSION — pushing the three impurities (āṇava, māyīya, karma) against the three powers

*Freestyle Logicvid. The text (TĀ ad 1/22, TĀ 1/152, 9/60 ff., 4/162cd) defines three
impurities; the earlier session (Q2/Q3) established the three powers are three modes of one
vimarśa. Question: are the impurities the *blocked* or *contracted* forms of the powers?
Method: ask why, use the text's own definitions, push deeper.*

---

## ROUND 0 — the text's own definitions

**Three impurities that bind the soul** (TĀ ad 1/22, 3734–3740):
1. **Āṇava** — "pertains directly to the soul (aṇu) as its fundamental ignorance and sense of
   incompleteness (apūrṇamanyatā)."
2. **Māyīya** — "the sense of duality."
3. **Karma** — "the soul's binding activity when subject to the other two Impurities."

**The three powers** (from the Q2 session): will (icchā), knowledge (jñāna), action (kriā) —
three modes of one vimarśa, ordered by degree of krama (time-succession).

**The three upāyas** (from the Q3 session): Śāmbhava (akrama), Śākta (subtle krama), Āṇava
(gross krama) — three intensities of one Śakti.

---

## ROUND 1 — the first penetration: do the impurities mirror the powers?

**The question:** The powers are will / knowledge / action. The impurities are āṇava /
māyīya / karma. Is there a structural correspondence? "Karma" appears in BOTH lists — as a
power (action) and as an impurity (binding activity). Is that a coincidence, or is karma-
impurity the *contracted form* of the kriyā-power?

**The text's answer:** The text says Karma-impurity is "the soul's binding activity *when
subject to the other two Impurities*" (ad 1/22). And TĀ 1/152 says the latent impressions are
"the products of the three impurities." So the impurities are *prior to* and *generative of*
the vāsanā, while the powers are the *liberated* functions. The text does NOT explicitly map
power↔impurity one-to-one. But the seed is there: karma-impurity is *binding action* — i.e.
action that binds rather than liberates.

**The new, deeper question:** If karma-impurity = binding action, and kriyā-power = liberating
action, then the same "action" function appears in two registers: contracted (binding) and
uncontracted (liberating). Is this the general structure? Is *every* power double — binding
when contracted, liberating when recognized?

> **PENETRATION 1:** The same function (action) appears as power (kriyā, liberating) and as
> impurity (karma, binding). This suggests the impurities are not *additional* things that
> bind an otherwise-free soul; they are the *contracted modes* of the very powers that
> liberate. Binding and liberation are the same three functions, differing only in whether
> they are recognized (Q4: recognition, not attainment) or misapprehended as exhausting the
> self.

---

## ROUND 2 — push harder: what is āṇava, really?

**The question:** The text defines āṇava as "fundamental ignorance and sense of
incompleteness (apūrṇamanyatā)." It "pertains directly to the soul (aṇu)" — the soul as a
*minute* particle. Why is the *root* impurity defined as incompleteness-and-minuteness
rather than as, say, error or sin?

**The text's answer (via the definition):** āṇava = aṇu-tva = "soul-ness" = being-a-minute-
particle = the sense of being *incomplete*, *partial*, *lacking*. This is not an error about
this-or-that fact; it is the *fundamental self-positing as a deficient particular*. It is the
root because māyīya (duality) and karma (binding action) presuppose a subject that already
takes itself as a deficient, separate particle. Dyczkowski: "it pertains directly to the soul
as its fundamental ignorance and sense of incompleteness."

**The new, deeper question:** If āṇava is the *self-positing as deficient particular*, then
it is precisely the "false self-positioning" — the σ-flip in `am0` terms, the "I am this
deficient bounded organism." And it is *rooted in the self itself* (the aṇu), not imposed
from outside. **So the root impurity is the contracted self-positing: vimarśa (self-
apprehension) turned into self-positing-as-lacking.** That is a stunning claim: the very
reflexivity that makes consciousness conscious is, when it seizes itself as a deficient
particle, the root bondage.

> **PENETRATION 2:** Āṇava is self-apprehension (vimarśa) *mis-seized as deficiency*. The
> root impurity is not an addition to the self; it is the self's own reflexivity contracting
> into "I am a lacking particle." This is the direct link between Q1 (reflexivity) and the
> doctrine of bondage: **reflexivity is both the condition of liberation and (when it
> seizes itself as the deficient aṇu) the root of bondage.** Same power, two directions.

---

## ROUND 3 — the uncomfortable consequence: is māyā the knowledge-power contracted?

**The question:** māyīya = "the sense of duality." In the Q2 session, knowledge (jñāna) is
vimarśa as *determinate content* — the act of distinguishing "this not that." Duality
(māyīya) is also about distinction (this vs that). Are they the same act, differing only in
whether the distinction is *recognized as within consciousness* or *misread as external
separation*?

**The text's answer (TĀ ad 1/52, the "blueness of blue" passage + the vimarśa line):** the
difference blue/not-blue "arises through an act of reflective awareness (vimarśa)." So
*distinction itself* is a vimarśa act. And māyīya = the sense of *duality*. The distinction
is not the problem; the *sense of duality* — that the distinction indicates two *independent
separate things* — is the impurity. So māyīya is the *misreading* of vimarśa's
differentiation as external separation.

**The new, deeper question:** Then māyīya is knowledge-power *contracted into externality*:
vimarśa-as-determination (correct: this not that within one field) misread as
vimarśa-as-separation (wrong: this and that are independent substances). This exactly
matches the `logicframework2` §10 point (exclusion ≠ informational scarcity; exclusion-as-
externalization is the bondage). And it matches the checkpointcharlie kañcukas-as-partition-
axes.

> **PENETRATION 3:** The three impurities are the three powers *inverted*:
> - **Āṇava** = will (self-apprehension) contracted into self-positing-as-deficient-particle.
> - **Māyīya** = knowledge (determination) contracted into duality-as-external-separation.
> - **Karma** = action (kriyā) contracted into binding activity under the other two.
> Recognition (Q4) is the reversal: the powers recognized as themselves, not as their
> contracted forms. This unifies Q1 (reflexivity), Q2 (powers), Q3 (upāyas), and Q4
> (recognition) into ONE structure: **impurities are contracted powers; upāyas are the
> reversal; recognition is the goal.**

## ROUND 4 — the frontier that survives

**Established by the text:**
1. Three impurities: āṇava (incompleteness/ignorance), māyīya (duality), karma (binding
   activity) — ad 1/22.
2. The latent impressions are products of the three impurities — 1/152.
3. Distinction arises through vimarśa — ad 1/52.
4. Impurity is ignorance (not a material substance), per the nondualist position — 9431 ff.

**Open / asserted but not fully argued by the text here:**
1. **The exact power↔impurity mapping** (P1–P3): that āṇava/māyīya/karma are the *contracted
   forms* of will/knowledge/action. The text gives the seed (karma-impurity = binding action;
   āṇava = self-as-deficient; māyīya = duality; distinction = vimarśa) but does not lay out
   the threefold mirror explicitly.
2. **The direction claim** (P2): that reflexivity itself, mis-seized, is the root impurity.
   The text's definition of āṇava supports it, but the "same power, two directions" framing is
   our synthesis, not a quoted claim.

## THE LOGICVID LESSON

The Tantrāloka's three impurities (āṇava, māyīya, karma) are not three external fetters added
to a free soul. Read against the three powers (will, knowledge, action) that the same text
derives from one vimarśa, the impurities emerge as the *contracted forms of the powers
themselves*: āṇava = self-apprehension seized as deficiency; māyīya = determination misread
as external duality; karma = action contracted into binding activity. This is the text's own
material, synthesized: **the impurities are the powers inverted, and recognition is their
reversal.** It ties the whole first chapter together — reflexivity (Q1), powers (Q2), means
(Q3), and recognition (Q4) are one structure with one direction: contraction → recognition.
