# LOGICVID SESSION — Branch X: the direction of self-apprehension is FELT — the bridge to Solms/valence

*Freestyle Logicvid. The master-key session showed bondage/liberation = the direction of the
one self-apprehension (outward into the object vs. inward into itself). The stirring question
now: is that direction FELT? If so, the felt register (the Solms/valence bridge) is not an
extraneous addition to the recognition thesis — it IS the phenomenal face of the
direction-reversal. Method: ask why, find the text's own account of the felt (camatkāra,
ānanda, the "wonder of the supreme bliss"), push deeper.*

---

## ROUND 0 — the text's own account of the felt

From the Q10 extraction (TĀ Vol 2, the Anuttara / bliss passage):

1. **The pure experience of one's own nature** (3546): "It manifests as the dense (unbroken)
   wonder of the supreme bliss, which is just the pure experience of one's own nature alone
   (svānubhūtimātra), of those who, free of the activity of thought (vikalpavṛtti), have made
   the reality of the supreme Self a direct experience."
2. **camatkāra = wonder** (3637): "'camatkṛtī,' which literally means 'wonder.' Torella
   writes: 'Abhinava (IPVV vol. 2...)'" — wonder is the felt of consciousness.
3. **The aesthetic delight of Śiva's power of emission** (the section header): rasa = the
   tasting of the bliss of one's own consciousness.
4. **Freedom as wonderful** (1702–1715): the wonderful varieties of manifestation; the
   (supreme) reality manifests "in a wonderful form."
5. **"Enjoy how everything is bliss"** (2418): "neither a renouncer, nor one who accepts —
   Enjoy (how) everything is bliss, just as you are."

## ROUND 1 — the first penetration: is the felt the DIRECTION, or a separate content?

**The question:** The master-key says the difference between bondage/liberation is the
*direction* of self-apprehension. The text here says liberation is "the dense wonder of the
supreme bliss." Is that wonder a *content* that appears in liberation (which would contradict
the Anupāya's "nothing added"), or is it the *felt quality of the inward direction itself*?

**The text's answer:** The text is precise: "the dense (unbroken) wonder of the supreme bliss,
which is just the pure experience of one's own nature ALONE (svānubhūtimātra)." The wonder
is not a new object; it is the *experience of one's own nature as such* — the felt of the
self-apprehension turned inward. It is "dense/unbroken" (continuous), not a passing content.
And it is "free of the activity of thought (vikalpavṛtti)" — free of the outward determination
that constitutes the ego. So the wonder is the felt of the *inward* self-apprehension.

> **PENETRATION 1:** The wonder (camatkāra/ānanda) is NOT a content added in liberation. It is
> the *felt quality of the self-apprehension turned inward* — "the pure experience of one's
> own nature alone." The outward-turning is felt as grasping/lack/need (the ego); the
> inward-turning is felt as wonder/bliss/ānanda. **The direction of self-apprehension has a
> felt quality, and that felt is the rasa.**

---

## ROUND 2 — the gem: this is the felt-is-primary thesis, IN THE TEXT'S OWN TERMS

**The question:** If the direction of self-apprehension is *felt*, then the felt is not an
output of recognition — it is *the very register in which recognition occurs*. Is that the
text's position?

**The text's answer (the whole of what we have):** Yes. Recall:
- **camatkāra is the mark of consciousness** (Q1, ad 1/53): wonder "is the primary
  characteristic of consciousness; it is indicative of its sentience, which distinguishes it
  from the insentient."
- **Determination = vimarśa = self-apprehension** (Q1).
- **Recognition = the reversal of direction** (master-key).
- **The reversal's felt = the wonder of the supreme bliss** (Q10).

So the felt is not a *secondary* report of a cognitive event; it is the *primary* register in
which the self-apprehension and its direction are given. This is exactly Solms' "the felt is
primary" — but now derived from the Tantrāloka's own metaphysics of self-apprehension. The
wonder is not an emotional reaction to recognition; recognition IS the felt wonder of the
inward self-apprehension.

> **PENETRATION 2 (the gem):** The Tantrāloka's account of the felt IS the felt-is-primary
> thesis. camatkāra (wonder) is the *primary characteristic* of consciousness (the mark that
> distinguishes it from the insentient, TĀ ad 1/53); the self-apprehension and its direction
> are *given as felt* (the wonder of the supreme bliss, svānubhūtimātra, TĀ Vol 2). This is
> the Solms↔rasa bridge, DERIVED FROM THE PRIMARY TEXT: the felt is the register of the
> direction-reversal. The outward-turning is felt as the ego's grasping; the inward-turning
> as wonder.

## ROUND 3 — push harder: what is the felt of the OUTWARD direction (bondage)?

**The question:** If the inward direction is felt as wonder/ānanda, what is the outward
direction (bondage) felt as? The system needs a felt for bondage too, or the asymmetry is
unexplained.

**The text's answer (the impurities session + the material):** The outward direction is the
contracted powers:
- **āṇava** = will turned into deficiency → felt as **incompleteness, lack, need**
  (apūrṇamanyatā — the sense of "I am incomplete").
- **māyīya** = knowledge turned into duality → felt as **separation, otherness, fear**.
- **karma** = action turned into binding → felt as **compulsion, the treadmill of vāsanā**.

And the text (Q4 extract) ties the felt of bondage to the vāsanā (latent impressions) and the
"wonder that is not there" — the *absence* of the bliss in the contracted state. "Enjoy how
everything is bliss, just as you are" (2418) — the imperative implies the bondage-state does
NOT feel that bliss; it feels the lack.

**The new, deeper question:** So the two directions have two felt qualities:
- **Outward (bondage):** felt as lack, separation, compulsion — the "negative" valence.
- **Inward (liberation):** felt as wonder, bliss, satiety — the "positive" valence.

This is a VALENCE structure. And it maps directly onto the `RASA-VALENCE-INTO-ESSAY`
framework (V = −dF/dt): the outward-turning (the ego's restless self-maintenance) is a
*drive* (negative valence, free-energy minimization); the inward-turning (recognition) is the
*resolution* (positive valence, the decrease of drive). The direction of self-apprehension IS
the direction of valence.

> **PENETRATION 3:** The two directions of self-apprehension carry two felt valences:
> outward = lack/separation/compulsion (the contracted powers as āṇava/māyīya/karma);
> inward = wonder/bliss/satiety (svānubhūtimātra). This is a valence structure: the
> outward-turning is the drive (V negative, free-energy minimization); the inward-turning is
> its resolution (V positive, camatkāra). **The direction of self-apprehension = the direction
> of valence.** This is the Solms↔rasa bridge made exact.

## ROUND 4 — the frontier that survives

**Established by the text:**
1. camatkāra (wonder) is the primary characteristic of consciousness, distinguishing it from
   the insentient (ad 1/53).
2. Liberation's felt = "the dense wonder of the supreme bliss, the pure experience of one's
   own nature alone" (TĀ Vol 2).
3. Determination = self-apprehension (Q1); the felt = the direction (master-key + Q10).
4. Bondage = the contracted powers (āṇava/māyīya/karma), with their felt of lack/separation/
   compulsion.

**Open / asserted but not fully argued:**
1. **The valence-mapping** (P3): that the felt of the outward direction is literally the
   "negative valence" and the inward the "positive" — our `RASA-VALENCE` register, inferred
   from the text's descriptions (lack vs. bliss), not quoted.
2. **The felt-as-primary read** (P2): that the felt is the *register* of recognition, not a
   report — strongly supported (camatkāra as the mark of sentience) but framed as our
   synthesis.
