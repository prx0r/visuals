# LOGICVID SESSION — Branch K: the status of krama (time) — is time a real addition or a mode of manifestation?

*Freestyle Logicvid on the Q5 extraction (TĀ Vol 1, the Krama/Eternal-Now passage). The text
itself addresses the status of time. Method: ask why, find the text's answer, push deeper.
This is a rare gem: the text directly says time is the source, not definable in temporal
terms.*

---

## ROUND 0 — the text's own account of time (the Q5 extraction)

From TĀ Vol 1 (the Krama passage, lines 2378–2460):

1. **The perpetual Present:** "The practice is to cultivate a stable state (sthiti) of
   awareness of the Present in the centre between the past moment that has ceased and the
   future moment that does not yet exist."
2. **The yogi absorbs time into eternal consciousness** "even as he experiences how all
   things stream forth from the immediacy of the Eternal Now and return to it."
3. **The source of time is not temporal:** "The sequence (krama) of emanation, persistence,
   and withdrawal are understood as three processes or cycles (cakra) of a single process or
   cycle, which is the eternal activity of consciousness. **The source of time, this activity
   is not definable in temporal terms as either a process, its absence, both or neither.**
   Inexplicable in these, or any finite terms, the one reality is called the Inexplicable."
4. **Persistence as the descent of eternity into time:** "Persistence, the intermediate
   phase between emanation and withdrawal, is experienced as a 'descent' (avatāra) of the
   oneness of Eternity into time, consuming it into its inner dynamism."
5. **Parā = Anākhyā (the Inexplicable):** "Parā Herself is the Inexplicable (Anākhyā)... who,
   by the power of her own innate freedom, manifests and destroys the cycles of emanation and
   the rest within her own nature."
6. **Trika and Krama converge:** "Parā, the Supreme Goddess of the Trika, is the same as the
   Inexplicable — Anākhyā — the Supreme Goddess of the Krama."

## ROUND 1 — the first penetration: WHAT does "the source of time is not temporal" mean?

**The question:** The text says the activity of consciousness is "the source of time" but "not
definable in temporal terms." How can something be the *source* of time without being in
time? Is this a coherent claim or a mystical evasion?

**The text's answer:** The tetralemma ("a process, its absence, both or neither") is
deliberate. Time is not denied (that would be "its absence"); it is not asserted as a
substance (that would be "a process"). The source is *prior to* the process/absence split —
it is the activity from which the cycles of emanation/persistence/withdrawal arise, and which
cannot itself be classified by those cycles. This is the logical move: **the ground of time
cannot be described by time's own categories.** It is the same move as TĀ 1/54 (the Light is
ādisiddha — self-proved, prior to all means of knowledge).

**The new, deeper question:** If the source of time is non-temporal, then *krama* (the
succession that defined the upāyas in the Q2/Q3 session) is NOT fundamental. It is a *form
taken by* the non-temporal activity. This confirms Penetration 3 of the Q2/Q3 session: action
= knowledge "assumed the nature of a succession." Time is the mode of manifestation of the
akrama vimarśa.

> **PENETRATION 1:** The text's tetralemma ("process, absence, both, neither") is the *logical
> signature* of a ground prior to its own categories. Time is not illusory (not "absence"),
> not substantial (not "process"), not a mixture, not a third thing — it is the *self-shaping
> of the non-temporal activity into succession*. This is the deepest statement of the
> "time-is-from-the-state" claim, IN THE TEXT'S OWN TERMS, centuries before Connes–Rovelli.

## ROUND 2 — push harder: WHY the Present, and why is it the point of power?

**The question:** The text privileges the Present — "awareness of the Present in the centre
between the past moment that has ceased and the future moment that does not yet exist." Why
is the Present the locus of practice? What makes it special?

**The text's answer:** The past has ceased; the future does not yet exist. Only the Present is
the actual moment of consciousness. The Present is the "centre" between the two cycles
(emanation/withdrawal), the point where the yogi "absorbs time into eternal consciousness."
And critically: **persistence (sthiti) is the descent of eternity into time.** So the Present
is the place where the non-temporal ground *appears as* time — the pivot where eternity
"descends" and "consumes" time into its own dynamism.

**The new, deeper question:** This is the recognition-event's temporal seat. The Present is
where the akrama (non-temporal vimarśa) is *accessible* — because it is the only moment not
yet absorbed into the krama (past) or not-yet-real (future). So **the Present is the crack
in time through which the non-temporal ground is met.** Practice (the upāyas) works by
settling into the Present, where the akrama vimarśa is already operative but hidden by
attention to past/future.

> **PENETRATION 2:** The Present is the *interface between krama and akrama* — the one moment
> where the non-temporal ground is directly met, because it is the only moment not already
> captured by succession. This is exactly the "point of power in the present" — and the text
> grounds it: the Present is where persistence (the descent of eternity into time) is
> experienced. Recognition (the σ-flip) happens THERE, not in the stream.

---

## ROUND 3 — the convergence: Trika and Krama are the same structure

**The question:** The text says Parā (Trika's supreme goddess) = Anākhyā (Krama's supreme
goddess). Why does Abhinavagupta equate them, and what does the equation reveal?

**The text's answer:** From the Trika (Śiva-oriented) view, Śiva's freedom does creation and
destruction. From the Krama (Śākta-oriented) view, the focus is on Śiva's *power* (Śakti)
as the one who absorbs time into eternal consciousness. **They are two perspectives on one
reality:** Trika sees the agent (Śiva), Krama sees the power (Śakti). The supreme is the same
— the Inexplicable source of time.

**The new, deeper question:** If Trika (agent) and Krama (power/process) converge on one
non-temporal ground, then **the upāyas' krama-ordering (Q2/Q3) is the *Krama* perspective, and
the akrama ground is the *Trika* perspective.** The three upāyas (Śāmbhava/Śākta/Āṇava) are
the Krama descent; the recognition of their non-temporal ground is the Trika return. The two
schools are the two directions of one structure: **Trika = the ground (akrama); Krama = the
descent (krama).**

> **PENETRATION 3:** Trika and Krama are not rival schools — they are the two directions of
> the one structure. Krama = the descent into succession (the upāyas, the powers, the
> impurities); Trika = the recognition of the non-temporal ground beneath the succession
> (recognition, reflexivity, the Present). The equation Parā=Anākhyā is the text's own
> statement that the ground (Trika) and the process (Krama) are one.

---

## ROUND 4 — the rare gem: the deepest claim

Pushing all three penetrations together yields a rare gem:

> **The Tantrāloka's account of time is: (1) time is the self-shaping of the non-temporal
> activity into succession (the tetralemma); (2) the Present is the interface where that
> non-temporal ground is met (the point of power); (3) the succession (Krama) and the ground
> (Trika) are one structure, and recognition is the return from the succession to the
> ground.**

This is the `TIME-PARALLEL-IPK-OCHEMA` claim (time-is-from-the-state; the present as the
point of power) **derived from the primary text itself**, not imposed. The text says: time
comes from the state's activity; the Present is the moment of the state's change; recognition
happens there.

**The frontier:** The text gives the claim (time is the self-shaping of the non-temporal; the
Present is the point of power). It does NOT give a *formal* account of how the non-temporal
"descends into" time — that is the "Inexplicable" (Anākhyā). The IPVV is where Abhinava
presses this further. But the *structure* is fully present in Vol 1.

## THE LOGICVID LESSON

The Tantrāloka Vol 1 contains a complete, argued account of time that anticipates the modern
"time-is-from-the-state" claim: the activity of consciousness is the *source* of time yet is
"not definable in temporal terms" (the tetralemma); the Present is the interface where the
non-temporal ground is met (the point of power); persistence is the descent of eternity into
time; and Trika (ground) and Krama (descent) are one structure. This is a genuine explanatory
gem: it grounds the `TIME-PARALLEL-IPK-OCHEMA` hypothesis in the primary text's own reasoning,
centuries before Connes–Rovelli. Recognition is the return from the succession to the ground,
performed in the Present.
