# LOGICVID SESSION — first-person: I am the mother, my child is gone. If I recognize myself as Śiva, how does my experience of suffering change — and WHY?

*The deepest drill, from the inside. Not the "dancer watching from above" — the MOTHER, in the
grief, who recognizes herself as Śiva. The text even names this exact case: suffering is "a
particular kind of wonder... brought about by the absence of all hope that arises when the
inner joy, which is the vitality and expansion of consciousness, consisting of the arousal of
love a son gives... when one thinks that the son has gone and all that has ceased and WILL
NEVER EXIST AGAIN" (TĀ 25589–25594). This session stays inside that experience and asks: what
actually changes, phenomenologically, and why? Method: the rasa text, held honestly — no
escapism.*

---

## ROUND 0 — where I actually am (the phenomenology before recognition)

Let me be precise about the experience of grief as it is, undistorted:

1. **The loss is a present, live fact.** The child is gone. This is not a thought; it is the
   texture of the whole world. Everything is suffused with the absence.
2. **The felt is the "absence of all hope"** (TĀ 25589): "the inner joy... will never exist
   again." The grief is not sadness-about-a-memory; it is the felt collapse of the future. The
   joy is not just absent — it is *irrecoverably* absent. That irrecoverability is the pain.
3. **The self-apprehension is turned outward, into the lost object.** Every thought, every
   perception, loops back to the child — the empty room, the silence, the cry for help that
   will not come. The self is *identified with the loss*: "my child," "my loss," "my life is
   broken."

This is the contracted state: the self-apprehension (vimarśa) is turned outward into the lost
object, and it is *stuck there* — denied rest. From the suffering-session: **suffering = rasa +
identification.** Right now, I am pure identification.

---

## ROUND 1 — what recognition does NOT do (the honesty, first)

Let me clear away the false pictures of recognition before touching the true one, because the
false pictures are themselves a kind of violence.

**Recognition does NOT:**
- Make the child come back. (Nothing is added.)
- Erase the grief, or "see through" it to a pleasant feeling underneath. (That would be
  denial, not recognition.)
- Tell me "it was all an illusion, the child was never real." (From the pratibimbavāda: the
  reflections are REAL — "a true reflection, not a mistake." The child was real, the love was
  real, the loss is real.)
- Detach me from the child into a blissful blank. (That is samādhi's absorption — and the
  rasa-session showed recognition is NOT samādhi; it keeps the felt.)

So recognition is not anesthesia, not derealization, not escape. If it were any of these, it
would be a lie about the loss — and the text does not lie about the loss.

> **PENETRATION 1 (the honesty):** Recognition does not erase the grief, bring back the child,
> or reveal the loss as illusory. The reflections are real ("a true reflection, not a
> mistake"); the child was real, the love was real, the loss is real. If recognition were
> anesthesia, derealization, or escape, it would be a lie about the loss — and the text does
> not lie.

---

## ROUND 2 — what recognition IS: the shift of WHERE the felt is positioned (not WHAT is felt)

**The question:** If the content of the grief (the loss, the irrecoverability) does not change,
what DOES change? The answer, from the rasa text, is the **positioning of the felt within the
field of consciousness** — the same content, re-seen.

The rasa text gives the key move (A.Bh I, 291):
> "In aesthetic experience it is one's own consciousness that consists of uniform bliss that
> is enjoyed. How can there be any suspicion of sorrow? The various permanent emotions like
> love, sorrow... only serve to **lend variety to the enjoyment of this bliss.**"

Now hold this against the grief. The grief is a *permanent emotion* (śoka) — and Abhinava says
the emotions "only serve to lend variety to the enjoyment of this bliss." How can this be, when
I am in the grief?

The answer is the alaukika/sva-saṁvedana move from the earlier session: **the rasa is the
felt of the SELF-APPREHENSION turned inward — the self-luminous, self-verifying wonder
(sva-saṁvedana) of consciousness itself.** The grief is the *content* (the flavor); the wonder
is the *substrate* that feels it. Recognition is the shift from being *identified with the
content* (the grief) to *being the substrate that the grief flavors*.

So the change is not that I feel less grief; it is that **the grief is no longer the whole of
me — it is a flavor within the wonder that is me.** The felt is repositioned: from "I AM this
grief" to "this grief is a mode of the wonder that I am."

> **PENETRATION 2 (the change):** Recognition changes WHERE the felt is positioned, not WHAT
> is felt. The grief stays — but it shifts from being the WHOLE of me ("I am this grief") to
> being a FLAVOR within the wonder that I am. The rasa text: the emotions "only serve to lend
> variety to the enjoyment of this bliss" — the bliss (the wonder, the self-relishing of
> consciousness) is the substrate; the grief is the flavor. **Recognition is the shift of
> identity from the content (the grief) to the substrate (the wonder that the grief flavors).**
> The grief does not disappear; it is re-seen as a mode of the wonder — camatkriyā, "a
> particular kind of wonder" (TĀ 25589).

---

## ROUND 3 — the WHY: why does this shift change the experience?

**The question:** WHY does re-positioning the felt change the experience of suffering? Why is
"the grief as a flavor within the wonder" less suffering than "I am the grief"?

**The derivation (the mechanics of suffering):** From the suffering-session, **suffering = rasa
+ identification.** The pain is not the grief-content itself; it is the grief-content PLUS the
identification (the outward turn stuck on the lost object, denied rest). The pain is the
*restlessness* of the self-apprehension caught in the loss — the felt of the outward turn that
cannot resolve, that keeps reaching for a child who will not come, that is denied its rest.

Recognition does not remove the grief-content; it **removes the identification that produces
the restlessness.** When the self-apprehension turns inward (from the master-key) — when I am
no longer the one *reaching outward for the lost child* but the one *in whom the grief arises*
— the restlessness ceases. The grief-content remains, but it is no longer a *grasping*; it is
a *tasting*. And the rasa text says: the tasting of the emotion, universalized, "yields pure
joy" (A.Bh I, 291).

**But the honesty again:** "pure joy" here is not a giddy happiness. It is the *rest* — the
cessation of the grasping. The text (from the earlier session): "happiness consists in complete
rest or repose without any kind of obstacle. Pain is nothing but lack or absence of complete
rest or repose" (A.Bh I, 282). So the change is: the grief moves from a *pain* (the lack of
rest, the grasping after the gone child) to a *restful tasting* (the grief as a flavor, no
longer a grasping). The love for the child is still there — the tears are still there — but
they are no longer a *reaching that cannot complete*; they are a *resting in the wonder that
the love and the loss both flavor.*

> **PENETRATION 3 (the why):** The shift changes the experience because suffering = rasa +
> identification, and recognition removes the IDENTIFICATION, not the rasa. The pain is the
> restlessness of the self-apprehension caught in the loss — the grasping after the gone child,
> denied rest. Recognition turns the self-apprehension inward: the grief is no longer a
> grasping but a tasting. The grief-content remains; the RESTLESSNESS ceases. **The grief moves
> from a PAIN (the lack of rest, "pain is nothing but lack of rest," A.Bh I, 282) to a RESTFUL
> TASTING (the grief as a flavor within the wonder). The love and the tears remain; they are
> just no longer a reaching that cannot complete.** This is why the change is real and not
> escapist: nothing false is added or removed; only the grasping is released.

---

## ROUND 4 — the deepest move: the grief becomes the RASĀYANA of the love

**The question:** What is the grief, finally, after recognition? Is it merely "tolerable," or
does it become something positive?

**The derivation (the rasa text, pushed to its depth):** Recall the TĀ passage again — the
suffering is the wonder "brought about by the absence of all hope that arises when the inner
joy... consisting of the **arousal of love a son gives**... will never exist again." The grief
is *made of* the love. The "inner joy" that the son gave IS the substance of the grief — the
grief is the love, turned into a flavor by the loss.

So the grief is not the opposite of the love; it is **the love, tasted in its absence.** This
is why the emotions "lend variety to the enjoyment of this bliss" — the grief is a MODE of the
love, and the love is a mode of the wonder. The mother who recognizes herself as Śiva does not
find the grief replaced by a neutral calm; she finds that the grief IS the love, and the love
IS the wonder of her own consciousness, and she tastes that love-wonder in and through the
grief.

**The rasa-move:** This is the karuṇa-rasa move, made on one's OWN life. In the theatre, the
spectator tastes karuṇa (the grief) as bliss because it is universalized — "divorced from
reference to personal interests... and from all reference to time and space" (A.Bh I, 291).
Recognition does to my OWN grief what the theatre does to the spectator's: it universalizes it.
My grief, recognized, is no longer "my" private loss but **grief-as-such — the universal
flavor of loss within the one consciousness** — and tasted as such, it is the wonder taking the
flavor of grief. The mother's tears become the karuṇa-rasa of the one; the one tastes the
mother's love, in its loss, as the wonder.

> **PENETRATION 4 (the deepest):** The grief, recognized, becomes the LOVE tasted in its
> absence — and the love is a mode of the wonder. The TĀ passage makes this literal: the
> suffering is the wonder "brought about by the absence of all hope that arises when the inner
> joy... consisting of the arousal of love a son gives... will never exist again." **The grief
> is made of the love; the grief is the love turned into a flavor by the loss.** And
> recognition universalizes my grief (as the theatre universalizes the spectator's — "divorced
> from reference to personal interests and from all reference to time and space," A.Bh I, 291):
> my grief becomes grief-as-such, the karuṇa-rasa of the one, and the one tastes the mother's
> love in its loss as the wonder. **The mother does not lose her child twice — once to death
> and once to a callous metaphysics. She keeps the child, as the love that the grief is; and
> the love is the wonder of her own consciousness, tasted in and through the loss.**

---

## ROUND 5 — the honest answer to the question

**Question:** If I recognize myself as Śiva, how does my experience of suffering change, and why?

**The change:** The grief does not disappear. But three things shift:
1. **My identity shifts** — from "I am this grief" (identified with the content) to "this
   grief is a flavor within the wonder that I am" (the substrate).
2. **The restlessness ceases** — the grief is no longer a grasping after the gone child (the
   outward turn denied rest) but a tasting (the inward turn resting).
3. **The grief reveals its nature** — it is the love, tasted in its absence; the karuṇa-rasa
   of the one, not a private catastrophe.

**The why:** Because suffering = rasa + identification. Recognition removes the identification
(the grasping, the restlessness), not the rasa (the love, the grief, the wonder). The pain was
the lack of rest; the rest is found; the grief, resting, is tasted as the wonder it always was.

**The deepest truth:** The mother who recognizes herself as Śiva does not find the grief
replaced by a blank or a giddy joy. She finds that the grief IS the love, and the love IS the
wonder of her own consciousness — and she tastes that love-wonder in and through the grief. The
tears remain; but they are no longer the tears of a broken reaching. They are the tears of the
one tasting the love of the child, in its loss, as its own wonder.

## THE BRANCHES (the next Logicvids)

> **Branch BO — The tears and the "yield pure joy":** if the grief, recognized, "yields pure
> joy," what are the tears? Push: is the weeping itself the tasting — the sāttvika-bhāva (the
> involuntary expression) of the recognized karuṇa? If so, the mother's tears are not the sign
> of her continued bondage but the very vehicle of her tasting.

> **Branch BP — The child as a mode of the one:** from the pratibimbavāda, "the face reflected
> in the mirror is my own" — the child, too, is a reflection of the one. Push: does recognition
> transform the grief because the mother recognizes that the child was never OTHER than her own
> consciousness — so the loss is not the loss of an external object but the return of a mode of
> the one? (This is the hardest and most delicate claim; it must not become a denial of the
> child's reality.)

> **Branch BQ — The universalized grief and compassion:** if my grief becomes grief-as-such
> (universalized), does it become the occasion of karuṇa (compassion) for all mothers? Push: is
> the recognized grief the very thing that makes me able to grace others (the liberated soul's
> "only purpose is to grace others")?

## THE LOGICVID LESSON (the mother's recognition, from the inside)

From inside the mother's grief, recognition changes the experience not by removing the grief
but by re-positioning it within the wonder that I am:

**What changes:** (1) identity — from "I am this grief" to "this grief is a flavor within the
wonder that I am"; (2) rest — the grasping after the gone child ceases; the grief moves from
pain (the lack of rest) to restful tasting; (3) nature — the grief reveals itself as the love,
tasted in its absence.

**Why:** suffering = rasa + identification. Recognition removes the identification (the
grasping, the restlessness), not the rasa (the love, the grief, the wonder). "Pain is nothing
but lack of rest" (A.Bh I, 282); the rest is found; the grief, resting, is tasted as the
wonder it always was.

**The deepest truth, held honestly:** The text even names my case — the suffering is the wonder
"brought about by the absence of all hope that arises when the inner joy... consisting of the
arousal of love a son gives... will never exist again" (TĀ 25589). **The grief is made of the
love; the love is a mode of the wonder.** Recognition universalizes my grief (as the theatre
universalizes the spectator's), and the mother's tears become the karuṇa-rasa of the one — the
one tasting the mother's love, in its loss, as its own wonder. **The mother does not lose her
child twice — once to death and once to a callous metaphysics. She keeps the child, as the love
that the grief is; and the love is the wonder of her own consciousness, tasted in and through
the loss.**

*This is the most precise and the most honest answer the whole project can give to the problem
of suffering: not that it is illusory, but that it is the love tasted in its absence, and that
recognition is the return of the grief from a grasping to a tasting — the tears becoming the
rasa of the one. Next: Branch BO (the tears as the sāttvika of the recognized karuṇa) or Branch
BQ (the universalized grief as the occasion of compassion). Continue hounding.*
